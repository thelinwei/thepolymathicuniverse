Collection 01
[The Parallel Straits]
(an exploratory atlas of a parallel equatorial port, its seven great schools, and the magic that came later)
From the archive

A city whose schools are rival theories of knowledge, and a pickle jar that explains it.

The idea that holds the Parallel Straits together was written down on a night at Funan, after a concert of P. Ramlee songs: the mixture was never pure. Around it grew a port like Singapore and unlike it, where seven great houses of learning each give a different answer to what knowledge is for, and where everything worth having was pickled together in one jar.

It grew in two movements. The first stayed close to the ground: the schools and their crests, their names and their quarrels, a cast of seventeen, a water crisis to drive a novel, and then the idea carried home to the real island as cantons and a commonwealth.

The second movement let magic in. The same seven houses became seven grammars for bending reality, each with its own hands, costs, relics and shadow, and the city found worlds lying beside it.

Each thread here begins with a main idea and gathers what was built around it. The cards carry the spine; the lists beneath hold the studies, variants and side-paths. Every volume is labelled with the hand it came from.

On the numbering

Volumes made by different hands carried their own atlas numbers, so the archive holds two Atlas VIIIs, two IXs and two magical series. The two-digit numbers here are the reading order; the original numbers stay on each title page.

On the names

Book One uses the houses' earlier names. The current register, used in Book Two, is Toa Keng, Al-Merikani, Balai Pusaka, Lan Fong, Nine Rain, Sané-Kora and Sund. Lan Fong takes its name from the Lanfang kongsi of west Borneo, 1777–1884.

Still to recover

Atlas IX·a, The Gloss (Claude, 17 September), lives only in its original conversation. Volumes 12–14 will take the Lan Fong names when it is rebuilt.
