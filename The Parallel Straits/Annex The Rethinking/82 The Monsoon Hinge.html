Thread I

The world at a glance

One fleet kept sailing, and the world stayed many.

A chartered Chinese fleet and the ports of the monsoon formed a Concord, turned the Portuguese back at Diu in 1509, and let Europe trade in the east only as a licensed guest. Without eastern wealth and silver, Europe never conquered the world; the Americas and Africa kept their own polities; the great powers stayed many and mid-sized. Magic arrived in 1791, at the moment coal would have, and the coal stayed in the ground. Because magic punishes the grasping hand, empire became the heaviest thing anyone could try to hold. In 1908 the first Door opened onto another world, and the old hunger for land found somewhere gentler to go.

      The divergence ledger 分岔账

      Each assumption beside its counterpart in our world, with the cause that makes it happen.

      Our worldPMUWhy

      01The hinge: the fleet sails on

      Our worldZheng He’s voyages end in 1433. The court scraps the fleet as a ruinous expense and bans ocean-going trade.
      PMUIn 1426 the Treasure-Ship Charter 宝船公约 hands the fleet to the merchant houses of Fujian and Guangdong in partnership with the admiralty, with Quanzhou as its home port. The voyages never stop.
      WhyMoving the cost off the throne’s treasury takes away the anti-voyage faction’s strongest argument. The coast now pays for the sea and profits from it, so it defends it.

      02The Monsoon Concord

      Our worldIndian Ocean trade is open, rich and unarmed: many ports, no common defence. It is easy prey for ships carrying guns.
      PMUIn 1435 at Malacca the ports bind themselves into the Monsoon Concord 季风会盟: Malacca, Calicut, Hormuz, Kilwa and the Swahili cities, Pasai, Brunei, Ryukyu and the Charter houses. They share a licence, a fleet and a court of arbitration.
      WhyThe fleet needs friendly harbours, and the harbours want protection from pirates. A shared licence replaces tribute: trade on terms, which becomes the Concord’s whole ethic.

      03Europe in the east

      Our worldPortugal wins at Diu in 1509 and takes Goa in 1510 and Malacca in 1511. The Dutch, English and French follow and build empires.
      PMUThe Concord, with Gujarat and Mamluk Egypt, wins at Diu. The Diu Settlement of 1510 allows Europeans to trade under licence from unfortified factories, and nothing more, east of the Cape.
      WhyOne lost battle at the right moment. Without eastern spice wealth, Europe’s crowns stay small, indebted and busy with each other.

      04Sund is allowed to stay

      Our worldThe Kalmar Union breaks in 1523. Denmark later holds a small, quiet post at Tranquebar.
      PMUThe union is re-chartered as the Sound Compact. In 1587 a Norden lodge is granted residence at the Straits, the only European house allowed to put down roots.
      WhyThe Nordic crowns live off the Sound Dues at the Øresund. Keepers of one strait recognise keepers of another. They come without guns, accept the licence, bring navigation and shipwrightry, and marry in.

      05The Americas keep their powers

      Our worldTenochtitlan falls in 1521 and Cusco in 1533. Epidemics and conquest take most of the population. The United States forms in 1776 and spreads west.
      PMUThe Castilians are expelled from Tenochtitlan in 1520 and never return. Tlaxcala makes peace and the Anahuac Compact forms. Tawantinsuyu settles its civil war. Epidemics still strike, and the states survive them battered and whole. In the north, the Longhouse Confederacy, the Horse Nations, Anahuac and five small Shore Leagues balance one another.
      WhyWith no eastern windfall, Castile has no money for second expeditions and turns to the Mediterranean. Contact stays thin, through trade rather than settlement, so populations recover across generations, as Eurasia did after the Black Death.

      06Africa without lines

      Our worldThe Atlantic trade carries about twelve and a half million people across the ocean. Songhai falls in 1591. Borders are drawn in Berlin in 1884–85.
      PMUThe Atlantic trade stays small. Songhai turns back the 1591 desert crossing. In 1885 Mbanza Kongo hosts the Congress of Routes, which affirms that sovereignty runs over peoples and routes, never over lines on a map.
      WhyThin American colonies never build the plantation complex that demanded mass labour. Concord ships guard the Swahili coast, and with no colonial race there is no scramble.

      07China: strong, many, unbroken

      Our worldThe Ming falls to the Qing in 1644. Then come revolution in 1911, warlords, civil war and the Cultural Revolution.
      PMUThe Shanhai Settlement of 1644 keeps the Ming south of the Wall and the Manchus as the realm of the north. The provinces hold charters. The Wuxu Settlement of 1898 is followed by the Federated Provinces 联省共和 in 1911, without civil war. The coast is liberal; some inland provinces stay gentry-ruled and close to feudal.
      WhyMaritime silver lets the provinces feed themselves through the Little Ice Age famines, and provincial militias make sharing power cheaper than fighting. The federalist idea that failed in our 1920s succeeds here.

      08No world wars

      Our world1914–18 and 1939–45.
      PMUThe Balkan crisis of 1914 is settled by arbitration at Vienna. Frontier skirmishes, trade quarrels and one Door dispute, and nothing on a world scale.
      WhyThere are no colonies to race for and no alliance blocs. By 1914 magic has made domination ruinously expensive (Thread III).

      09No fossil age

      Our worldCoal, steam, oil, electricity, computers, AI.
      PMUProto-industry grows slowly in many centres: the Yangzi delta, Bengal, Gujarat, the Low Countries, the Hanse cities. Magic arrives before coal can take over, and the coal stays in the ground.
      WhyOur industrial leap was fed by colonial cotton, sugar and silver gathered in one place. In PMU those flows stay spread out, so no single place builds up enough to leap.

      10Magic: the Kindling

      Our worldNo magic.
      PMUIn 1791, at a Concord gathering in the Straits port, practitioners of several traditions hold a working true and others reproduce it. Within a generation the same happens in Bengal, Fujian, Timbuktu, Uppsala and Texcoco.
      WhyFor three centuries the Concord ports kept teachers of many traditions side by side. Magic grew out of their mixing, which makes it achar: it was never pure.

      11Computation without computers

      Our worldCalculation moves into machines, then into networks, then into AI.
      PMUReckoning is done in relational workings, kept by trained reckoners and held in instruments of cord, bead, script and sound.
      WhyWorkings already compute relations. Once they can be held and reproduced, there is no pressure to build switching machines.

      12Other worlds instead of each other

      Our worldExpansion means taking other peoples’ land.
      PMUThe First Door opens in 1908. Dozens follow, all onto unpeopled worlds. The Threshold Accord of 1924 门约 makes every Door a commons that no single power may hold.
      WhyAbundance ends the zero-sum. The custodians are whoever’s land a Door opens on, which keeps power with peoples rather than crowns.

      VI
      Thread VI

      Why the urge to conquer fades 修身止戈

      Five strands, each growing from the one before.

      i

      Trade as hospitality

      From 1435 the Concord teaches the ocean that wealth comes on terms. A guest who accepts the licence is welcome, and the host protects the guest. Two centuries of this make hospitality a habit before it is a law.

      ii

      The ports keep the teachers

      Concord harbours shelter contemplatives, poets, astronomers and healers of every tradition in neighbouring streets. Inner work becomes respected knowledge, discussed in the same teahouses as tide tables and freight rates.

      iii

      The physics of grasp

      A working holds a relation true against World Resistance, and every held relation accumulates load. The relations that cost the most are those held hardest against their own nature. Holding a people under you is the heaviest working there is. The Silesian Breaking of 1827, when a prince tried to keep a conquered province by workings and was broken by the load, teaches this to the whole world in a single season.

      iv

      Cultivation becomes civic

      If restraint is the source of power, then restraint is a craft, and crafts are taught. The strongest practitioners are the most cultivated, and teaching houses become the places where a society trains its conscience along with its skill.

      v

      Enough

      When the Doors open onto unpeopled worlds, the old fear of not having enough loses its grip. The Gardener’s Rule spreads: take from a world only what it can give back.

      ∴

      Together

      Hospitality made the habit, the teachers gave it depth, the physics made it law, the houses made it teachable, and the Doors made it easy to keep.

      Empire became the heaviest thing a hand could try to hold, and the cultivated hand learned to let go.

      PMU-am

      After magic · 1791 onward
      1. 1791am 1
        The Kindling 点灯

        At a Concord gathering in the Straits port, a working is held true and reproduced by others. Magic becomes teachable.

      2. 1800to 1830
        The apprenticeship years

        Guild-schools spread through the Concord ports. Workings lift water, keep ships against the wind and turn mills, taking over from wind, water and animals.

      3. 1827
        The Silesian Breaking

        A prince tries to hold a conquered province by workings and is broken by the load. The world learns that domination is the heaviest relation.

      4. 1840s
        The coal stays in the ground

        Mines that were opening close. The fossil age never begins.

      5. 1850s
        The cultivation houses

        Schools that teach restraint as a craft spread through the Concord. The Straits schools take shape in this era; their own chronology lives in the Straits atlases.

      6. 1858
        The Mississippi Accord

        Anahuac, the Horse Nations and the Shore Leagues fix the great river as a shared range.

      7. 1868
        The Council of Domains

        Japan’s shogunate reforms into a council of its domains.

        Our worldThe Meiji Restoration.

      8. 1884
        Lanfang joins the Concord

        The kongsi republic becomes a full member.

        Our worldAnnexed by the Dutch.

      9. 1885
        The Congress of Routes

        Held at Mbanza Kongo, it affirms that African sovereignty runs over peoples and routes.

        Our worldThe Berlin Conference partitions Africa.

      10. 1898
        The Wuxu Settlement 戊戌之约

        A constitutional throne with provincial assemblies.

        Our worldThe Hundred Days’ Reform fails.

      11. 1908am 118
        The First Door

        Forest practitioners and visiting scholars open a passage by the Podkamennaya Tunguska onto an unpeopled world. Evenki keepers hold its custody.

        Our worldThe Tunguska event.

      12. 1911
        The Federated Provinces 联省共和

        Declared without civil war; the throne remains as keeper of the rites.

      13. 1914
        The Vienna arbitration

        The Balkan crisis is settled at the table.

        Our worldThe First World War.

      14. 1924
        The Threshold Accord 门约

        Every Door is a commons. Custody belongs to the peoples whose land it opens on, and no power may hold one alone.

      15. 1930sto 1950s
        The opening

        Dozens of Doors open. Settlement stays light under the Gardener’s Rule. One Door dispute in the southern ocean is settled after a brief standoff.

      16. 1960s
        The crossings

        Travel between worlds becomes ordinary. Door-ports grow up around the old harbours.

      17. 2026am 236
        The present

        A world of many powers, kinds of rule and worlds. The Straits remain the Concord’s seat.
