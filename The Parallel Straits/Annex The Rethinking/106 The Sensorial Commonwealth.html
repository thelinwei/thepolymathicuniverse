The Sensorial Commonwealth

What if an entire civilisation trusted ways of knowing that our institutions barely recognise?

Five historical windows. Nine invented social roles. Three speculative societies. A journey from perception to political authority, without simply adding song to familiar institutions.

The provocation

A studio critique sung is still a studio critique.

The earlier thought experiment translated the jeli into a studio storyteller, an epic for graduating students, and a poetic exchange between student and critic. These may be lovely ceremonies. But the institutions stay almost exactly the same.

Let's invert the question. Not: what familiar job could we perform in an unfamiliar way? But: what entirely different jobs, obligations, and seats of authority become possible when sensory knowledge is recognised as civic expertise?

The radical change is not the medium of a ritual. It is what a society accepts as evidence, who has standing to interpret it, and what institutions grow around that expertise.

Part I / Historical windows

The world already
knows differently.

These are documented practices, not ready-made fictional job descriptions. Each demonstrates a cultivated perceptual or material literacy.

Feeling the sea before seeing land

Marshall Islands

Marshallese ri-meto navigators used knowledge of swells and their interactions with islands. Stick charts taught abstract wave relationships; navigators memorised them before travelling, then read the water through embodied experience.

Smithsonian Ocean ↗

Giving smells their own words

Malay Peninsula

Studies of Jahai speakers document abstract vocabulary for smells. In an experimental comparison, Jahai speakers often named odours directly, while Dutch participants more frequently described their likely sources.

Majid et al. · Olfactory language ↗

Holding state records in cords

Andes

Andean khipu systems used knotted cords for record-keeping and administration. Trained specialists, often called khipu kamayuq, worked with a structured medium whose numerical functions are well established and whose other uses remain under research.

National Museum of the American Indian ↗

Hearing the condition of ice

Inuit regions

Inuit sea-ice expertise draws on multiple environmental indicators, including sound. Research documents how cracking, ridging, or unusually quiet ice behaviour can inform assessments of changing travel conditions.

FACETS · Inuit travel indicators ↗

Recognising the season, not merely counting the days

Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Australia

Indigenous seasonal calendars read combinations of plant, animal, weather, water and other signs. Their cycles are locally specific and connected to harvesting, travel and caring for Country. There is no single Indigenous Australian seasonal system.

CSIRO · Indigenous seasonal calendars ↗
Important distinction. These are living, situated knowledge traditions, not exotic prototypes for fantasy. What follows is independently invented worldbuilding prompted by broad questions about sensory expertise. It should not be read as claiming that any real community has the fictional offices below.
Part II / A speculative catalogue

Nine offices of perception.

No court poets in modern suits. Instead: witnesses, navigators, mediators, conservators and stewards whose skills change how society works. Explore the map, then open the roles below.

01 / truth & memory◌

The Scent Witness

OLFACTION · MEMORY · LEGITIMACY

The smell of a landscape becomes part of its civic identity; ecological change may be recognised before ordinary measures provoke action.

Open the thought experimentA scene

A settlement proposes abandoning an ancestral home. The wells and forests have changed. Scent Witnesses compare the present atmosphere against living archives of soils, resins, cultivated plants and fermented preparations.

A possible institution

Independent ecological witnesses who can contest a community's stewardship claims.

The judgment depends on trained, corroborated expertise, not infallible noses.

02 / truth & memory⌘

The Hand That Remembers

TOUCH · TEXTURE · COLLECTIVE MEMORY

Kinship, obligation and political agreement are maintained in a great textile that every generation repairs and extends.

Open the thought experimentA scene

Families contribute fibres, knots, tensions and repairs to a communal textile. Readers trace its histories by touch as well as learned material codes. A new agreement is completed by weaving it into the fabric.

A possible institution

A living constitutional archive whose preservation is itself a civic obligation.

The textile is a culturally learned record, not a magical material that independently remembers.

03 / truth & memory≈

The Taster of Waters

TASTE · CHEMISTRY · THE COMMONS

The water flowing between settlements has interpreters who are trusted to witness ecological relationships, not merely inspect a commodity.

Open the thought experimentA scene

Specialists compare subtle mineral and saline qualities of water with familiar samples, ecological records and other tests. Their testimony may reveal when a settlement has changed a source shared downstream.

A possible institution

An inter-island water commons whose guardians answer to multiple communities.

Taste is one learned signal among others, not a substitute for chemical analysis or safe water testing.

04 / time & territory∿

The Swell Keepers

BALANCE · PROPRIOCEPTION · NAVIGATION

The people who read movement through their bodies become custodians of routes, passages and relations between islands.

Open the thought experimentA scene

Before a trading passage is recognised, multiple Swell Keepers cross it and give their assessment. They understand island networks as sequences of motion and water behaviour, not only fixed points on maps.

A possible institution

A maritime council guaranteeing access, safe passage and reciprocal rights between shores.

Inspired at a conceptual level by embodied navigation; this fictional political office is not an attested Marshallese institution.

05 / time & territory✳

The Season Callers

ECOLOGICAL PATTERNS · TIME

An agricultural year begins through recognised conjunctions of ecological events, not solely by a calendar date.

Open the thought experimentA scene

The Callers attend to insects, bird calls, flowerings, soil moisture and winds. Different districts may enter the same named season at different times.

A possible institution

A distributed seasonal assembly coordinating planting, harvests, travel and mutual aid.

The fictional institution should not be confused with any specific Indigenous community's calendar or governance.

06 / time & territory↝

The Path Rememberers

KINESTHESIA · ORIENTATION · TERRITORY

Boundaries are journeys more than lines: changes of slope, wind, footing and effort are part of how territory is recognised.

Open the thought experimentA scene

When communities dispute a border, Path Rememberers walk its routes together. Their knowledge concerns the relation between places and the obligations of travellers who traverse them.

A possible institution

Territorial law based on maintained routes, right-of-passage and collective care of corridors.

This is an invented juridical system, not a general description of Indigenous territorial law.

07 / social cohesion◍

The Listeners of Hollow Things

HEARING · RESONANCE · ARCHITECTURE

A building is known through how it sounds. Acoustic knowledge can determine whether a civic chamber is fit to gather in.

Open the thought experimentA scene

Listeners know the signatures of occupied rooms, hidden cavities, timber, water and movement. Reconstruction of an important public room requires their assessment of what has been retained and changed.

A possible institution

Civic architecture with acoustic certification, resonant floor systems and inherited spatial memory.

Resonance is evidence about a space, interpreted alongside material and structural expertise.

08 / social cohesion♡

The Keepers of Unease

INTEROCEPTION · SOCIAL ATTENTION

The body's unease becomes a reason to reconsider how people are meeting, rather than a verdict about what anyone secretly thinks.

Open the thought experimentA scene

Through disciplined observation of their own responses and shared behaviour, these specialists notice when polite negotiation has become impossible. They can interrupt a gathering and propose a different encounter.

A possible institution

A civic office empowered to change the conditions of deliberation and reconciliation.

They cannot read minds, diagnose people from gestures or claim privileged access to another's interior life.

09 / social cohesion☼

The Readers of Heat

THERMORECEPTION · LAND STEWARDSHIP

Subtle thermal readings, alongside knowledge of weather, fuel and vegetation, inform how communities collectively tend their land.

Open the thought experimentA scene

The Readers coordinate seasonal stewardship based on extensive knowledge of fire behaviour, soil, plant life and thermal conditions. Their authority derives from organising care rather than controlling fire.

A possible institution

A communal land-tending body in which ecological renewal is also political cooperation.

This speculative office is not a representation of any particular culture's fire practices.

Part III / Change the entire society

Not just new jobs.
Different worlds.

A sensory profession is most interesting when its presence changes public space, law, archives and the very idea of authority. Select a world to see the consequences.

I
A city's most powerful monument may be a garden whose atmosphere has been tended for centuries.

The Atmospheric Polis

A society in which a place's changing character can be smelled, classified, debated and conserved.

Architecture
Fragrant courtyards, porous walls, carefully maintained ecological microclimates.
Archives
Living scent libraries of plants, woods, soils, resins and fermentation practices.
Law
Testimony about landscape alteration, environmental injury and stewardship.
Authority
Independent scent witnesses, alongside other ecological and material experts.
II
Its geography is an atlas of traversable relationships, not only a mosaic of owned surfaces.

The Society of Passage

Here, political geography begins with movement, safe routes and obligations along the way.

Architecture
Landing courts, paths of varying gradient, bridges and shelters as civic landmarks.
Archives
Route itineraries, embodied instruction, remembered sequences, navigational records.
Law
Passage rights, reciprocal hospitality, maintenance of shared corridors.
Authority
Keepers of swells and paths answerable to communities on both ends of a route.
III
An agreement cannot simply be filed. It must be joined, held, repaired and inherited.

The Woven Commonwealth

Continuity is made tangible. The social contract is maintained through material practice.

Architecture
Common weaving houses, chambers of contact, public works designed for ongoing repair.
Archives
Textiles and objects encoding genealogies, obligations and successive revisions.
Law
Amendments become material additions; repair and preservation are public duties.
Authority
Readers, makers, conservators and witnesses who must work together.
Part IV / Beyond the individual

What if nobody can sense it alone?

Imagine a profession that exists only when several trained people perceive together.

A swell reader, a scent witness, a thermal specialist and a keeper of ecological rhythms each perceive an incomplete aspect of an event. No individual judgment is authoritative. Only their joint reading can recognise an approaching transformation.

Their institution is a distributed sensory organ — part academy, part public service, part instrument for collective decisions. Its power rests less in a gifted person than in the relationships between differently skilled observers.

Perhaps the most unfamiliar sense is one that belongs not to a person, but to a community.

Reading this atlas

Historical windows are concise summaries of research or institutional resources. They should be investigated through their original contexts and living knowledge holders. Different traditions should not be flattened into a single category of “non-Western sensory culture.”

Invented offices and worlds are speculative proposals. Their names, governing arrangements and institutions are fictional. The page does not argue that sensory testimony is infallible, or that historical communities lacked writing, science, mathematics or other forms of knowledge.

These proposals can be developed as alternatives to familiar bureaucratic or courtly models without treating one civilisation as the opposite of another.
