01
The corrected starting conditions

The first demonstration.

The Straits schools already possess substantial scholarly renown. Their maritime correspondences connect them to schools and powers in other lands. They have developed magic far enough to demonstrate its usefulness, its larger possibilities, and the burden and resistance involved in more powerful workings. They have not yet remade the planet.

Established in this revision

Messages travel physically.

No telegraphs. Invitations, replies and news move with ships and overland messengers. Information reaches different places at different times.

Established in this revision

The long sea routes remain.

No Suez Canal and no Panama Canal yet. A continuous sea route between the Indian Ocean and Europe goes around southern Africa. Atlantic–Pacific sea passages cannot cut through Panama.

Established in this revision

No planetary works yet.

The Congress is the first revelation to invited foreign delegates. Later magical canals, worldwide transport and other large infrastructure belong to subsequent development.

What is settled, and what is elaboration? The invitation, demonstration, silence, conditional bids, selective agreements, invasion, defensive pairing and later student travel come from Lin Wei’s proposal. Detailed paperwork, example applications, travel allowances and ceremonial scenes below are proposed elaborations. They can be changed without changing the originating event.

The schools’ central opportunity is generative: magic can support healing, cultivation, comfort, construction, water management and entirely new forms of knowledge. Sharing it can establish relationships across the world. Their challenge is to build those relationships quickly enough to give its dissemination a dependable structure.

They can teach a method. They must also discover whom they can trust to carry it into another society.
02
Phase one · An invitation before a revelation

The sea-borne 英雄帖.

An invitation leaves the Straits under the authority of its schools. It travels through maritime networks to selected institutions and powers. Its declared occasion is an auction. The recipients already know the hosts as serious scholarly institutions; the unexpected form of the gathering gives them a reason to wonder what has been discovered.

An archaeological object? A newly devised instrument? An agricultural innovation? A manuscript? The initial mystery is useful. At this stage, the invitation asks familiar contacts to attend an unusual scholarly occasion. It does not yet require them to take a position on the governance of an unknown power.

The originating relationship

A name carried by another name.

An invitation can arrive with a merchant known to the recipient, a returning scholar, a translated letter from a trusted correspondent, or a captain who has carried books between the institutions before. These are possible delivery forms. The trust comes from the relationships already supporting the route.

The invitation’s character

A summons to curiosity.

The 英雄帖 analogy gives the letter presence: named hosts, recognised seals, a place and season, and an occasion worthy of a long journey. It invites capable people to witness something whose importance they must judge for themselves.

Proposed practical wording

Leave the object undescribed.

The notice might promise an exhibition followed by an auction, with the offered lots and terms disclosed on arrival. That preserves the mystery without inventing a false archaeological discovery or pretending that a conventional object is for sale.

The first attendees

Existing friends, new stakes.

The first circle largely consists of institutions already on good terms with the Straits. This explains why they attend. It does not guarantee that all will respond to magic in the same way—or that every friendly scholar can speak for the power supporting their school.

The invitation’s practical journey: a trusted carrier delivers the hosts’ notice to the institution. The notice identifies the hosts, the auction, the place and the date. Its undisclosed subject creates curiosity; the hosts’ established reputation makes the journey worth considering.

03
A provisional maritime model · Not a steamship timetable

How long does the invitation take?

Removing telegraphs and the two canals changes the planning substantially. Since the ships and propulsion systems of this PMU period are not yet specified, the working estimates here assume competent ocean-going sailing vessels, established trade routes and ordinary port calls. They assume no magical propulsion or weather control. No fossil-fuel steamship network is silently imported.

A historical reference gives the order of magnitude: Royal Museums Greenwich records Cutty Sark leaving London on 15 February 1870 and reaching Shanghai on 31 May—about 105 days. This was a capable clipper, not a universal delivery speed. PMU’s invitation must also survive transfer between ships and the final journey from port to institution.[1]

These are scenario estimates. They are not measured PMU voyages or verified schedules for every historical route. The farthest intellectual centre cannot yet be named confidently: we need the map of participating institutions, their ports, ship performance and sailing seasons. A distant American institution is a candidate; a less-connected inland institution elsewhere could take longer to reach.

The absence of Suez does not eliminate a Red Sea–overland–Mediterranean courier route. Such a relay could deliver a letter faster than a continuous sea passage, if trusted connections exist. Likewise, an overland crossing of an isthmus could transfer a message between oceans without a canal. These are optional routes to establish, not assumptions required by the table.

Season matters as much as distance. Indian Ocean monsoons involve seasonal reversals of prevailing winds; historical sailing directions treated these winds as a central navigational consideration.[2] A letter arriving just after an appropriate departure may spend longer waiting than sailing. Outward and return journeys need not take equal time.

A workable invitation calendar

Proposed allowance: dispatch the farthest invitations about two years before the Congress. That gives room for delivery, deliberation and the delegates’ journey back. Eighteen months may work for well-connected routes; twenty-four to thirty months is more forgiving for the least-connected participants. The first invitation need not demand that an acceptance reach the Straits before the delegates depart.

Months −24 to −15 · proposed

The letters travel.

Long-range copies leave first. Trusted intermediaries carry duplicates on different ships where possible. Nearby invitations can leave later so that attendance is not simply determined by proximity.

Months −15 to −6 · proposed

Institutions prepare.

Recipients select delegates, secure permission and funds, and wait for a suitable departure. Some send an acceptance with a different ship. Some delegates travel before their reply can reach the hosts.

Months −6 to 0 · proposed

The harbour receives them.

Early arrivals pursue ordinary scholarly exchanges. Others arrive near the appointed season. A broad reception window absorbs uncertainty; the central demonstration occurs on a stated date.

The two-year planning horizon is an editorial recommendation for the sail-based scenario. It does not fix the Congress to a particular Gregorian year.

04
Year 0 · The Congress

First wonder. Then comprehension.

1 · Arrival

The auction has not yet disclosed its lots.

The delegates arrive as scholars and representatives. Their hosts’ reputation supplies credibility. The meaning of the gathering is still open.

2 · Everyday possibilities

Without burning anything, there is light.

There is warmth, and then cold. Seeds grow. Water is diverted. Sound is silenced. The sequence reveals distinct applications rather than a single mysterious instrument. The smart people recognise that something fundamental is being demonstrated.

It need not answer every theoretical question. A delegate can understand the importance while remaining uncertain about mechanism. The demonstrated effects establish what the hosts can do; they do not automatically prove unlimited energy, universal healing or control at planetary scale.

3 · The larger working

Power has a practice behind it.

A more powerful spell follows. Burden and resistance become apparent. Within the current working system, burden is the practitioner’s metabolic exertion; resistance concerns the difficulty of the working, including its relation to existing conditions and consent. The demonstration makes these differences legible without needing to disclose every rule.

The exact spell remains to be chosen. A possible scene is water held in an unfamiliar course while its caster visibly labours. Another is a far larger version of one of the earlier effects. These are alternatives, not additional established events.

4 · The imposed silence

No conversation afterwards.

The demonstration ends. There is no discussion in the hall. The delegates return to their rooms. The silence allows the revelation to settle before the room supplies a common interpretation.

The precise duration is open. One proposed form ends the silence with the submission of initial sealed responses. Whether it also prohibits discussion within each delegation must be decided explicitly; this document does not assume a private discussion exemption.

Possible scene · The walk back

Someone who crossed an ocean expecting a rare object walks back through a corridor lit by the very thing they have just witnessed. At the door of their room, they stop. The practical question arrives before the grand theory: What could this do in the place I come from?

Optional elaboration: before the final demonstration and the silence, the hosts could permit limited observation or controlled repetition. This would give the delegates grounds for confidence. It remains a proposed procedure; the revelation does not depend on every attendee being allowed to handle every apparatus.

05
The auction · Bids within conditions set by the Straits

The conditions are already set.

The Straits possess the knowledge, the teachers and the ability to demonstrate magic. They set the conditions for access. A foreign school or power can accept those conditions and seek admission, or decline to participate. It cannot negotiate the conditions, purchase an exemption or exchange a higher payment for fewer obligations.

The bid concerns access to a demonstrated working, the offered exchange and the intended use. The rules governing that access are not part of the bid. Acceptance of the rules is a prerequisite for consideration. It does not oblige the Straits to accept the bidder.

The delegates must identify the exact magic they seek and state what they would use it for. They make their offers within the framework announced by the hosts. The Straits promise to teach; they do not promise that the students will be able to learn, master and reproduce the working. Those who participate accept that risk.

What a bidder submitsWhat the hosts assess
The requested workingWhich demonstrated magic the institution seeks to learn, and why that particular capability matters to it.
The intended applicationWhat the institution and its governing power intend to do with the magic, who would benefit, and whether that purpose is acceptable.
The offered exchangeThe payment or other contribution offered for access. Possible forms include money, materials, facilities or research contributions. None purchases a change to the conditions.
The responsible institution and authorityWho stands behind the application, who would control the practice, and whether the representative has authority to accept the conditions.

Acceptance is the entry condition. Selection remains with the Straits.

A participant accepts the conditions as a whole. A proposal to weaken the requirements for consent, withhold required findings, restrict the teachers’ freedom or obtain prohibited uses does not become an alternative set of terms for negotiation. It falls outside the offer.

The highest bid does not necessarily receive access. The process reveals how each school and its governing power would use the demonstrated capability. The Straits assess purpose, credibility, existing relationships and the institution’s ability to uphold the conditions. A sincere promise or a large payment alone is insufficient.

Illustrative application · not a fixed historical actor

A hospital seeks cooling.

It accepts the conditions, identifies the practitioners to be taught and proposes cooling for its treatment rooms. Its offer includes accommodation, teaching facilities and the required return of observations. The hosts can assess a specific application and the institution responsible for it.

Illustrative application · not a fixed historical actor

A wealthy institution offers much more.

It also accepts the conditions, but its proposal reveals that its governing patron intends to concentrate the new capability in a small circle of private facilities. Even if the application does not expressly breach a rule, the hosts may judge another bidder a better partner and refuse the larger offer.

The auction is therefore a means of eliciting intentions and choosing whom to teach. The Straits retain the right to refuse every offer. A bidder can ask what a condition means; clarification does not confer a right to amend it. An authorised institution’s eventual signature records acceptance of the Straits’ conditions.

06
The Straits’ framework · Conditions of participation

Shared knowledge, binding conditions.

The Congress opens access to instruction under rules established by the Straits. The institutions that join undertake those obligations from the beginning. The Covenant twenty-five years later celebrates their continued participation, the return of findings and the relationships cultivated through that practice. It does not reopen the conditions for bargaining.

The detailed clauses remain for us to develop as authors. Within the event, however, whatever conditions the Straits announce are fixed for the participants. Their leverage sets the structure of the exchange.

Knowledge sharing

The scope of access.

The Straits specify what may be learned, practised, recorded, translated and taught onward. Seeing a demonstration does not itself grant every form of access. Participants accept the permitted scope when they enter.

Return of findings

Discoveries must come back.

Required findings, adaptations and observations return through the agreed channels. Participants contribute to a growing body of knowledge. Regular correspondence sustains the exchange before the Year 25 gathering.

Consent and applications

Use remains governed.

The Straits define the requirements for consent and the permitted applications of the magic. Schools and governing powers accept those requirements. A higher payment cannot waive them.

Responsibility and authority

Acceptance must carry authority.

The institution and the power governing it must be able to answer for the undertaking. A delegate without authority can take the offer home for a decision; the offered conditions remain the same.

Protection and response

Teachers must be protected.

Conditions cover the safety of visiting teachers and the conduct expected of host institutions. The Straits determine the consequences of breaches and the circumstances in which access or instruction ends.

Scholarly contribution

Participants enrich the knowledge.

Foreign schools bring discoveries, methods and insights the Straits do not yet possess. They can question a theory, test a working and improve a technique. Their intellectual contribution develops the knowledge within the established conditions.

Two different kinds of growth: participants may transform what is known about magic. They cannot use that contribution, their wealth or their attendance at the Covenant to renegotiate the conditions of access.

07
After the Congress · A carefully engineered race against time

The revelation travels outward.

The veil has been pierced. Some witnesses now know that magic is possible, and others may eventually learn it independently. The Straits hold a first-mover advantage in methods, trained people and accumulated resources. They use that advantage to grow a trusted network.

With no telegraphs, the spread is uneven. A returning delegate, a letter and a harbour rumour can reach different places in different orders. News can travel by several routes at once. It remains ship- and messenger-borne; it does not become instant simply because the revelation is important.

A · The first agreements

Teaching begins with selected partners.

The Straits select institutions whose offers and intended uses they accept. Those institutions enter under the prescribed conditions. These Congress-era agreements govern the teaching from the outset; the Year 25 Covenant does not mark the first appearance of rules.

B · Later approaches

Those who did not attend begin to ask.

Other institutions and powers learn of the demonstration and approach the Straits with offers of their own. Later applicants face the same principle: accept the Straits’ conditions and submit their intended use and offer for consideration, or decline. Admission is not restricted forever to the first attendees.

C · The invasion

A foreign power attempts seizure.

A foreign power comes to invade. By then the Straits have accumulated sufficient magical resources and ability to repel it. The identity of this power, its route, its intelligence and the exact defensive workings remain unspecified.

D · Travelling teachers

Specialists travel with protection.

Every teacher sent abroad must be able to defend themselves to some degree. Each non-combat magic specialist is paired with a combat mage. This is an established detail of Lin Wei’s proposal; the training threshold, staffing and timing of its implementation remain to be worked out.

E · The major retaliation

A decisive demonstration of force changes the conditions.

The Straits later conduct a major retaliation that demonstrates overwhelming capability. Its immediate trigger and relation to the earlier invasion are still open. The invasion’s repulsion and this retaliation should not be silently collapsed into one incident.

F · Students go later

Student exchange follows the change in security.

Straits students are sent abroad only much later, after the major retaliation. Early specialist missions and later student exchange therefore belong to different stages of the network’s development.

The strategic tension

The Straits cannot guarantee that magic will remain theirs alone. They can make the most capable and dependable path into its study run through relationships that already know how to teach, exchange findings, protect their members and act together.

No exact duration is assigned to these stages. Some can overlap: incoming bids, departing teachers, defensive preparation and returned findings may all be happening at once. The sequence preserves the dependencies Lin Wei specified, especially the delay before sending students.

08
Year 25 · 发馨合盟

The Covenant.

Twenty-five years after the Congress, participants return. Their relationship now has a history. The opening revelation has become knowledge cultivated in different places, by people who have contributed their local experience, understanding and care.

The earlier culinary proposal supplies the ritual’s material form: at the originating agreement, preparations can be sealed, seeds entrusted, starters divided and future ingredients distributed. The Covenant brings these separated futures together. This connects the sharing of magic to the practice of tending a relationship.

Explore the three moments · Select a stage

The gift leaves with a future.

Magic is demonstrated. The Straits select participants who accept the fixed conditions and whose bids and purposes they approve. In the proposed ritual, delegations take seeds, living cultures or other preparations to be tended. A sealed portion remains for a later opening.

Different places add themselves.

Teachers travel. Findings and letters return. Seeds are cultivated, starters maintained and recipes adapted. Protective arrangements develop. The relationship acquires a daily practice between its ceremonies.

What was entrusted becomes shared presence.

Delegates return with discoveries and things their lands have helped make. Sealed preparations are opened. Contributions become a meal, and the participating institutions reaffirm their commitments under the established conditions.

A proposed order for the Covenant

1 · Recognition

Who has returned?

Original delegates stand beside successors, former pupils and those who tended the gifts. The ceremony recognises both institutional continuity and the people who sustained it.

2 · Returned knowledge

What have we learned?

Schools present discoveries, changed practices and questions arising from their experience. Important findings should already have circulated; this gathering makes their collective significance visible.

3 · The opening

What has time made?

Sealed preparations from the Congress are opened alongside living ingredients tended elsewhere. Their scents make the passage of time perceptible.

4 · The shared table

What can we make together?

Separate contributions complete a meal. The recipe can hold differences: related ingredients grown in different lands, or several descendants of an original starter.

5 · Renewal

What shall we undertake next?

The participants reaffirm their commitments to consent, the permitted uses of magic, the return of findings and the protection of teachers. The gathering makes those continuing obligations visible alongside the knowledge and relationships they have sustained.

6 · Further entrusting

What leaves this gathering?

New seeds, cultures, questions and teaching invitations depart. Continuing twenty-five-year cycles are a possible extension, still to be adopted.

Possible scene · What returns after twenty-five years

At the Congress, a delegation takes seeds home to cultivate. Twenty-five years later, its school returns with seeds harvested from the plants it has tended, a preparation made from their produce, and records of a magical technique developed through that work. The original sealed preparation has remained in the Straits throughout the interval; the hosts now open it for the shared meal. The visiting contribution and the stored preparation have distinct histories. Together they bring the first undertaking into the present.

At the Congress, they witnessed what magic could do.
At the Covenant, they encounter what one another have made possible.

The Chinese pair can be read poetically as a breath held in trust and a fragrance released into shared life. “Breath,” “fragrance” and the relation between the two assemblies remain interpretive possibilities, rather than a claim that these are literal English translations.

The Latin candidates retain the earlier preservation-and-seasoning idea; the Year 25 name does not itself mean “now opened.” The Arabic candidates pair entrusting with a festival of the trust; the Year 25 phrase should not be presented as a literal translation of “returning the trust.” Those linguistic distinctions remain attached to the candidates.

This revision supersedes the earlier English assignment. Year 0 is the Congress; Year 25 is the Covenant. The short phrase “Covenant of Magic” therefore belongs to the Year 25 title in this working document, rather than automatically naming the first demonstration. 10
Questions for later · The event can stand before these are settled

Where the next work lies.

Chronology and participation

Choose the exact Congress year within the working 1920s chronology, the relation to the BM/AM boundary, and whether the Covenant counts from the central demonstration or the first formal entrusting. Identify participating institutions, including the farthest recipient. Do not assume that every later school or power was present at the first auction.

Ships, seasons and routes

Establish vessel performance and propulsion, the main shipping connections and the role of overland relays. Replace the provisional delivery windows with route-specific estimates once the world’s network exists. The lack of telegraphs and the absence of both canals are fixed conditions for this event.

The demonstration and the silence

Select the exact stronger working; establish how burden and resistance become visible; decide what inspection is permitted. Fix the silence’s duration, whether it covers private delegation discussion, and when bids can be prepared and submitted.

The auction and agreements

Develop the exact conditions the Straits impose, the forms of exchange bidders can offer, the selection criteria and how authorised acceptance is recorded. These are authorial decisions. Participants accept or decline the announced conditions; those conditions are never auction lots or negotiable clauses.

Conflict and travelling specialists

Name the invading power and choose the defensive response. Establish whether the later retaliation responds to that invasion or a separate provocation. Determine when mandatory defensive competence and combat pairing begin. Preserve the established condition that student travel comes after the major retaliation.

The Year 25 gathering

Decide who returns, which foods and living materials were entrusted at Year 0, how successors participate, and whether later entrants share the same anniversary. Determine whether the twenty-five-year rhythm becomes recurrent or remains a singular founding reunion.
