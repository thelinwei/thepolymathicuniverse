The Polymathic Universe · The Chronicle · Chapter, 1908–1921
[From the Flash to the Congress]天漏 · 燃灯 · 英雄帖
(thirteen years in which the sky began to leak, seven people pierced it, and the Straits called the world to see what had come through)

The world had kept its peace for a century. In thirteen years it learned it could do almost anything.

Claude4 November 2026 · the Chronicle · from 83, 84, 115, 116, 118–121

The chapterThree movements

  1. 天漏, 1908–1918. The Evenki Flash, the founders’ first private workings, and a world at peace asking the right questions in prose.
  2. 燃灯, 1918–1919. The Kindling at the Monsoon Convocation, the Fortnight Circle, whispers from other shores, and the decision to call the world.

How to read itOne spine, two sides

Time runs down the spine. The Straits is on the left and the world on the right. Events that move both cross the spine and span the page.

The StraitsThe worldBothA blank to fill

For a hundred years the world had argued instead of fighting. The Concord’s harbours kept the oceans stitched together, the provinces of China and the courts of Europe settled their quarrels at tables, and every port had its contending schools, each sure it held the truer description of the world. Knowledge kept climbing while power stayed modest, drawn from water, wind and muscle. It was an age of questions, and the Straits was where the questions came to be compared.

Then, in 1908, a light flattened a forest in Siberia, and nobody could say what it was. In the decade that followed, the World’s resistance thinned. Almost no one noticed. The few who did were already the heads of the seven houses, and they began, privately and each in their own way, to coax small impossible things through the gap.

When the Concord called every school to the Straits to settle the argument over the Flash, those seven were in the room. On the ninth night of the lull between monsoons, working together on a single relation, they pierced the veil, and the whole world felt it.

What followed was three years of secrecy and a decision. The seven could not keep magic to themselves for long; others were already finding it. So they chose to show it, on their own terms, to the schools they trusted, and to watch closely what each one wanted. The Congress of 1921 is where wonder and fear arrived in the same room, and where the Straits began the longest negotiation in its history.

1908
The Straits and the world · b.m. 10

The Evenki Flash

The Straits

Every house argues over what it was. Toa Keng measures the reports, Sané-Kora collects the testimony, Nine Rain reads the weather records for a sign. For a decade none of them can account for it.

The world

A blast of light flattens the forest by the Podkamennaya Tunguska. No one can explain it. The Evenki keepers say the forest opened and closed.

The Tunguska event, 30 June 1908.

1908–1918
The Straits · b.m. 10–1

天漏 begins

The World’s resistance thins toward the threshold. The heads of the seven houses feel the gap and coax small, private workings through it, each in their own idiom. A fever ward cools for one night. A runaway cart slows to the tempo of an organ. Seedlings root overnight in a dry field. Nobody else can repeat any of it.

The world

Who else felt 天漏?

Gifted minds abroad who sensed the gap before the Kindling: in which ports, which schools, and what became of them.

1909–1915
The world

The contending schools at their height

In Hamburg a biologist argues that every species lives inside its own perceived world. In Kyoto a philosopher begins a new philosophy from Asian and Western roots at once. In Edinburgh a planner reads the city as a living region. The questions the Straits will answer with magic are being asked everywhere in prose.

Uexküll’s Umwelt (1909), Nishida’s Inquiry into the Good (1911), Geddes’ Cities in Evolution (1915).

1911
The world · b.m. 7

The Federated Provinces联省共和

China becomes a republic of strong, loosely united provinces without a civil war; the throne remains as keeper of the rites. The provinces send their best scholars abroad, and many of them go to the Straits.

The Xinhai Revolution, and the civil wars that followed.

1914
The Straits

The Straits keeps its correspondence

With the world at peace, letters, students and instruments keep arriving in the harbour. Every house is in conversation with a dozen ports at once.

The world · b.m. 4

The Vienna arbitration

The Balkan crisis is settled at the table, and the world keeps its peace.

The First World War begins, and ends the flowering of the 1900s.

1916
The Straits · b.m. 2

The Concord calls a convocation

After eight years of rival explanations for the Flash, the Concord invites every contending school to the Straits to compare evidence in one place, under its old rule of hospitality: any guest who accepts the licence may speak.

1917
The Straits and the world · b.m. 1

The arrivals

The Straits

Sri Temenggong opens its grounds; Al-Merikani pays the passages; Toa Keng prepares the interpreters; Lan Fang’s physicians and Sund’s yards host the visitors; Nine Rain reads the coming monsoon; Sané-Kora sings the names of every lineage present.

The world

Delegations sail by every road the houses once travelled. More descriptions of the world sit in one city than ever before.

Movement ii · 1918–1919

The Kindling and the Hidden Years 燃灯

Seven people pierce the veil, and spend a year learning what they did.

The StraitsThe world
1918
The Straits and the world · a.m. 0

The Kindling燃灯

The Straits

On the ninth night of the lull between monsoons, seven founders, one from each house, work together on a single relation, as they have for weeks without result. This time something gives. No one in the room, the seven included, can say exactly what they did.

The world

Almost everyone in the world feels it, each in their own way: a pressure in the ears, a light behind the eyes, a moment no language has a word for. The Gifted feel it most of all.

The influenza pandemic of 1918.

1918–1919
The Straits · a.m. 0–1

The Fortnight Circle半月会

The seven swear their closest colleagues to secrecy and meet every two weeks, in turn in each of the seven houses, to compare their failures. It takes months to repeat the working once, and longer to understand why it worked. With no grammar and no spell book, they begin to write the first.

The world

The delegates sail home

The Convocation ends in astonishment and confusion. Its delegates carry home a story they cannot prove and a feeling everyone shares.

1919
The Straits · a.m. 1

The reunion of the seven

A year after the Kindling the seven meet in full. They can repeat a handful of small workings reliably and have begun to map the rules beneath them. They cannot agree whether to tell the world.

The world

Whispers from other shores

Word arrives through the Concord’s networks. A lamp lit itself in a Bengal school. A weaver in Cusco tied cords that held back a storm. Others are finding the veil on their own.

1919
The Straits · a.m. 1

The decision

The seven choose to call the world before the world finds magic without them. If their trusted network grows faster than anyone else’s discovery, it can shape how magic is used. The race begins here.

The Invite and the Congress 英雄帖

The Straits sends for the world, shows it wonders, and watches what it fears.

The StraitsThe world
Dec 1919 – Jun 1920
The Straits and the world · a.m. 1–2

The Invite英雄帖

The Straits

The letters go out in two waves timed to the monsoons, westward in December on the northeast wind and north to China and Japan in May and June on the southwest. Each goes in three copies by three routes, carried by someone the recipient already knows.

The world

An auction, the letter says, with no object named, only effects without causes.

无火而明，无薪而暖，无冰而寒，非时而生，水自改道，击鼓无声。

1920
The world · a.m. 2

The letters land

Two weeks to Guangzhou, three months to London, four to six months to Quito, almost exactly on the far side of the globe. Most readers take the list for a conjuror’s programme or a new chemistry. A few read it twice and choose carefully whom to send.

1920–1921
The Straits and the world · a.m. 2–3

The waiting harbour

The Straits

Delegations arrive on whichever wind carries them, and are lodged, fed and taught while they wait.

The world

Scholars who have read each other for years meet for the first time, as merchants on the Straits always have, waiting for the monsoon to turn.

Oct 1921
The Straits and the world · a.m. 3

The Congress藏息会盟

The Straits

Nothing is brought into the hall. The lamps are carried out. Light without flame, warmth and cold without fuel, seeds grown, water turned aside, a drum struck without a sound. Then the great working: the tide held back from the quay for the length of one song, the casters grey and shaking, the World pushing back.

The world

The clever ones understand at once, and what they understand first is the danger. A drum silenced in a hall is an army silenced on a field. A tide held at a quay is a harbour emptied. Behind every gentle wonder they see its use at scale, and they are afraid.

Oct 1921
The Straits

The bid paper

  1. What did you see?所见为何
  2. Who will learn it?何人来学
  3. What will it do in your land?用于何处
  4. Who there can say no?谁可拒之
  5. What will you bring back?将还何物

The Straits will teach. Whether you learn is yours. Bid at your peril.授在海峡，学在诸君，慎之。

The world

The silent night

Walked to their rooms alone, the delegates have one night to think. The long peace had rested on stalemate, on compacts and restraints, on faith. Every one of them can see that magic changes the arithmetic. The world wants the light. It may want the silence more.

Late 1921
The Straits

The first circle

The Straits chooses its partners, and not the highest bidders. Each delegation leaves with several gifts to tend and bring back: seeds, waters, cured meats, a living starter. If one cannot come home, another still can. This is Year 0 of the rite.

The world

The refused

Delegations that were turned down learn it by letter, months after sailing home. Some resent it. Some sail back to bid again.
