The Parallel Straits · synthesis edition, 29 October 2026

[The Prospectus of the Seven Schools]

(One university ecology, seven ways of knowing. What each school teaches, how deeply, which school leads, and the real records underneath.)

The seven schools of the Straits share one university and divide its fields between them. Some fields warrant a full faculty, others a precise specialist school, a taught option or a student society, and some are learned by crossing to another campus. Each school’s shape follows its way of knowing, the record it stands on, and what the city asks of it.

The seven schools

Students first meet a school in its rooms and rituals, and only later in its faculties. Each portrait gives the school’s character, the order of its emphases, the fields it leads, the tension it lives with, and what lies underneath.

GPTClaude

TK · The Unfinished Institution

Toa Keng

A city hearing, a student newspaper and a public theatre share the same courtyard.

Academic character
Civic institutions, public reasoning and metropolitan systems.
Order of emphasis
Public policy → law → economics → urban systems
Leads in
Law and public policy, economics, management and finance, and civic drama.
Productive tension
Professional status and formal procedure can crowd out informal knowledge.
Underneath

Its working habit is the survey and the register: every lot, title and licence entered in one hand. This prospectus is drawn in its categories, which is its quiet power over the other six.

AM · The Turning Court

Al-Merikani

A student moves from an optics bench to a translation seminar and an evening composition studio.

Academic character
Mathematics, instruments, translation and experimental inquiry.
Order of emphasis
Mathematics → engineering → translation → composition
Leads in
Humanities and translation, computing, mathematics and the physical sciences, composition and acoustics.
Productive tension
An elegant formal system can miss how people actually live.
Underneath

Named for merikani, the American cloth East Africa once priced its goods in, and carried by navigators who steered by star altitudes. Trust across distance is its subject, so its business school is a specialist in ethical finance.

ST · The Woven Garden

Sri Temenggong

Fieldwork in a tidal garden becomes a material workshop and a movement rehearsal.

Academic character
Living landscapes, maritime worlds and embodied making.
Order of emphasis
Ecology → landscape → performance → maritime enterprise
Leads in
Architecture and landscape, biology, agriculture and food, theatre, dance and movement, textiles and costume, and maritime enterprise.
Productive tension
Claims of harmony can conceal conflicts over land, labour and access.
Underneath

The oldest house on the shore, already granting river letters to planters when the charter arrived. It learns through the body: custom survives by being enacted in front of witnesses, so ground, ceremony and stage form one curriculum.

LF · The Rewritten Courtyard

Lan Fang

A repair bench, an animation studio and a small venture test occupy adjacent rooms.

Academic character
Making, production, materials and entrepreneurial transformation.
Order of emphasis
Materials → design → production → enterprise
Leads in
Business and enterprise, fine art and design, engineering and production, fashion, film, graphic design, craft and curating.
Productive tension
Speed and commercial success can outrun care, reflection and labour rights.
Underneath

Heir to a kongsi that elected every head, founded by a man who failed the imperial examination and became a teacher. It admits without examination and treats every made thing, from type to timber to cloth, as parts that can be set again.

NR · The Rain Terraces

Nine Rain

Students check a rain gauge, compare long observation series and build an installation that changes with the season.

Academic character
Earth systems, water, time and long-horizon observation.
Order of emphasis
Water → earth systems → astronomy → spatial arts
Leads in
Earth, water and climate, and spatial, land and time-based art.
Productive tension
Long horizons can become a reason to postpone urgent human needs.
Underneath

Its records are knotted cords and centuries of sky tables, and its silver was the Straits’ money until 1903. It leads few fields, and one of them is the field the city cannot live without.

SK · The Listening Village

Sané-Kora

An ensemble rehearsal becomes an oral-history session and a discussion with a neighbourhood health team.

Academic character
Listening, oral knowledge, music, education and community health.
Order of emphasis
Music → oral humanities → education → community health
Leads in
Music in nearly every form, education, archives, oral literature and writing, community health and cultural enterprise.
Productive tension
Consensus and respected voices can silence dissent or less audible members.
Underneath

Two memories live under one roof: the jeli who sing, and the copyists who keep a manuscript tradition of astronomy, mathematics, medicine and law. Its leadership in archives holds both.

SU · The Common House

Sund

A student repairs a shared component, tests an accessible service and joins a cooperative budget meeting.

Academic character
Public welfare, cooperative systems and repairable design.
Order of emphasis
Social design → welfare → education → circular engineering
Leads in
Product and service design for common use, and cooperatives and social enterprise.
Productive tension
Agreement and standardisation can suppress singularity and productive friction.
Underneath

Sund means strait. Its keepers tolled the Sound for four centuries and kept the books; when the toll ended in 1857 they came looking for another strait, and built a commons.

Why Sri Temenggong leads performance

Its knowledge is practised through movement, ceremony, making and place. Landscape becomes stage and teacher; cloth, puppetry, architecture and sound come alive through bodies; dance connects to martial movement, procession and ritual; theatre ties storytelling to public memory.

The city’s popular stage was mixed from the start. Parsi theatre troupes from Bombay played the Straits in the 1870s, and the first local company formed in 1885 when a merchant, remembered as a Merican, bought a failing troupe’s curtains, costumes and instruments and staged stories of Malay palaces. In the Straits, Balai Lakon’s popular stage began with an Al-Merikani merchant and a bankrupt touring company.

Music: three anchors

Sané-Kora runs the broad conservatory: performance, voice, composition, improvisation, music education and living lineages, Western classical included.

Al-Merikani specialises in composition and sound, joining musical structure, instrument-making, acoustics and computation.

Sri Temenggong joins music, movement, theatre and material scenography.

A conservatory is an institution with a curriculum, individual and ensemble tuition, practice facilities and professional pathways. A campus choir or gamelan on its own is an activity.

Lineage shapes method

Sané-Kora teaches Western classical performance. Lan Fang sustains experimental work with little commercial return. Sund hosts the difficult, individualistic artist. A school’s genealogy shapes how it teaches, and every student may study anything.

Fashion and dress

The full fashion school sits at Lan Fang: the Lan Fang School of Fashion, Dress & Material Culture, called 裁衣堂, the Cutting Room. Pattern cutting suits a school whose method is taking a made thing apart and setting it again. The rest of dress is distributed unevenly across the city.

  • Lan FangThe full fashion school: experimental, technical and industry-facing.
  • Sri TemenggongTextiles, costume, drape and clothing in movement.
  • SundCircular fashion, repair, accessibility and cooperative production.
  • Sané-KoraWeaving, adornment, cultural livelihoods and performance dress.
  • Al-MerikaniGeometric cutting, ornament, wearable instruments and material mathematics.
  • Toa KengUniforms, institutional dress, fashion criticism and public identity.
  • Nine RainClimate-responsive garments, natural pigments and environmental wearables.

Theories of enterprise

Several business schools, each a different idea of what an enterprise is for.

GPTClaude

Toa Keng studies the corporation and the institution, capital that answers to a registry. Lan Fang studies production and enterprise as shares among those who do the work, inherited from the kongsi. Seven records

Toa Keng

The long record
The survey and the register: every lot, title and licence entered in one hand, the working habit of a colonial port.
The crossing
Arrives with the charter that made the port, and never leaves the waterfront.
In the Straits
Its categories are the ones this prospectus is written in.
The stain
Plantation science, and a register that turned shared ground into titled lots.

Al-Merikani

The long record
Merikani, the unbleached American cotton so common on the East African coast that goods were priced in lengths of it, with a navigator’s handbook from around 1490 behind every voyage.
The crossing
By monsoon, family by family, carrying letters of credit instead of land.
In the Straits
Trust across distance becomes Dar al-Amana, and its oldest ledgers still count in bolts.
The stain
The same cloth was spun from slave-grown cotton and issued to enslaved porters on the caravan roads.

Sri Temenggong

The long record
The river letters: permits the Temenggong granted to Chinese planters, each naming a river and a term, kept in Johor’s registers from 1844 to 1902.
The crossing
None. Sri Temenggong was on the shore first.
In the Straits
Dewan Adat still issues river letters, so the island keeps two registries and an old quarrel over which came first.
The stain
River masters fell into debt and sold their letters to city magnates, who held rivers they never worked.

Lan Fang

The long record
Centuries of Chinese star records, Zheng He’s star-measured crossings, and the Annals of Lanfang, the only written account any west Borneo kongsi left.
The crossing
1884, when the Dutch ended the kongsi.
In the Straits
Lan Fang admits without examination, elects every head, and keeps the Annals in its oldest case of type.
The stain
A president who helped the Dutch seize a rival kongsi’s leader. Lan Fang teaches the vote as the guard against that kind of bargain.

Nine Rain

The long record
Knotted cords that kept accounts, tables that tracked Venus for centuries, and silver: for three and a half centuries Mexico mined most of the world’s supply.
The crossing
Across the Pacific on the Manila galleons, as coin.
In the Straits
Its silver was the city’s money for a century, until Toa Keng’s registry demonetised it. The Silver Question never quite closes.
The stain
Andean silver at Potosí was dug under the mita, a forced labour draft. Nine Rain knows whose hands made the coin.

Sané-Kora

The long record
Timbuktu’s manuscripts, hundreds of thousands of them, on astronomy, mathematics, medicine and law; and Kaabu, where tradition says the kora was born and the Sanneh were one of two royal lines.
The crossing
Two falls: Timbuktu’s scholars exiled in the 1590s, and Kansala taken in 1867.
In the Straits
The jeli of Jeliya sing and the copyists of the Ajami House write, and the house argues over which memory is truer.
The stain
Kaabu’s nobility grew rich on war captives. Kuma recites that too, because a genealogy that leaves it out unpraises the captives.

Sund

The long record
The Sound Toll Registers: about 1.8 million ship passages recorded at Helsingør between 1497 and 1857, beside an assembly whose lawspeaker carried the law in his head.
The crossing
1857, when the toll ended.
In the Straits
Its folk school dates itself to 1844 and its assembly keeps a law-speaker.
The stain
A commons paid for by a toll on everyone else’s ships.

Read across, the seven share a shape. Each school is anchored in a record that really exists, each crossed the water after something fell, and each carries a stain its own record documents. A school’s depth in any field is what survived the crossing, plus what the city asked of it.

Other ways to read it

The faculty map is the orientation layer. These lenses explain why the divisions exist, how students move between them, and what each school would rather not say.

1One subject, many ways of knowing

GPT

Two schools may study water through different practices: Nine Rain through observation and modelling, Sri Temenggong through living landscapes, Al-Merikani through instruments, Sané-Kora through community governance and health. They produce different kinds of knowledge about the same subject.

2Home school and teaching host

GPT

A student belongs to one school and studies across all seven. A Toa Keng student can take composition at Al-Merikani or an auditioned performance pathway at Sané-Kora, with open prerequisites, shared timetables and funded travel between campuses.

3Lead, steward, collaborator

GPT

A lead school maintains the degree. A steward maintains shared facilities or archives. Collaborators supply methods, staff and field sites. Sané-Kora leads music degrees, Al-Merikani stewards the acoustics laboratories, and Sri Temenggong hosts performance residencies.

4Shared foundations, scarce facilities

GPT

Statistics, writing, computing, ethics and business literacy are shared by all. Costly provision is built once: one recording complex, one advanced materials facility, one clinical partnership network, each open to every school.

6Who recognises learning

GPTClaude

Every degree in the Straits becomes legal tender once Toa Keng’s registry enters it. Sané-Kora’s masters refuse, because a song written into a register loses its nyama, so some of the city’s greatest musicians hold no recognised qualification. A later layer of this prospectus records who can enter each field, how learning is recognised, and whether informal masters are paid and credited as teachers.

7Precedence as responsibility

GPTClaude

A lead school sustains its field for the whole city. It supports access, shares resources and welcomes critique, and answers for failures in its field wherever they happen. Nine Rain leads water, so Basin Seven is its to explain. Sané-Kora leads archives, so a genealogy lost anywhere is its shame. Leadership orders move as staff, student work and the city’s needs change.

9What stays unprogrammed

GPTClaude

Some workshops, ensembles, gardens and discussion groups stay outside assessment, so students can make music without turning it into a qualification. Food and preserving belongs to no school at all. Every school runs a kitchen society and none runs a faculty; the city’s cooking is taught in markets, on void decks and at wakes. This is the achar principle as curriculum: the one subject everyone learns and nobody owns.

How this edition was made

Process notes, for whoever opens this next.

  • The base is GPT’s faculty atlas and its follow-up on performance and fashion: the allocations, portraits, prospectus names and most lenses. Claude’s Seven Curricula supplies the records underneath, the names in each school’s own tongue, the student names, and the lenses on transmission, recognition, joint degrees and the shadow curriculum.
  • Fashion becomes a full school at Lan Fang. Sri Temenggong keeps textiles, costume and drape in a smaller institute.
  • Performance is divided by kind: dance and movement at Sri Temenggong, civic theatre and public speech at Toa Keng, music at Sané-Kora, composition and acoustics at Al-Merikani, film at Lan Fang, environmental work at Nine Rain, participatory work at Sund.
  • Health is community-based across the schools, with no full medical school. Al-Merikani’s business school becomes a specialist in ethical finance. Sané-Kora’s leadership in archives now includes a manuscript tradition. Sund keeps its boathouse inside circular engineering.
  • Settled names throughout: Toa Keng, Al-Merikani, Sri Temenggong, Lan Fang, Nine Rain, Sané-Kora, Sund.
