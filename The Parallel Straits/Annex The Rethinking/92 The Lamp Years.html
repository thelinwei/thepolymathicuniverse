[The Lamp Years]灯年 · 1918–1983
(sixty-five years between the Kindling and the First Door, a.m. 1 to 66, in which a wonder seen by seven houses became the everyday craft of the world)

The port is lit all night for the first time, and people walk until dawn simply because they can.

Claude1 November 2026 · companion to The Loom and the Kindling

The outcomeSixty-five years of wonders

The Lamp Years run from the Kindling of 1918 to the First Door of 1983. Here is what became possible, age by age.

  1. Every wonder of our industrial age gets a magical twin: moving photographs on tile, songs that cross deserts in a day, kite-ferries, cold lamps, weather theatre.
  2. The turn of the era is the bearer, in 1934. Once a cord or a seal can hold a working, anyone can use magic.
  3. Life opens in every direction: seasons bottled, fevers turned, whales consulted, rivers given a seat on the council, old caravan cities blooming again.
  4. Five ages, from a locked room above a dried-goods shop to a door between two larches.
  5. The frictions wait in Part Three, for when we turn to attitudes.

Part One · Possibilities

[What Became Possible]

(the wonders of sixty-five years, first)

i
Before you begin

Words of the age

Ten words, so the rest reads easily.

The Kindling燃灯

The night in 1918 when seven practitioners pierced the veil together at the Monsoon Convocation. Everyone on earth felt it in their own way, the Gifted most of all.

a.m.魔后

After magic. Years counted from the Kindling: 1918 is a.m. 1, 1983 is a.m. 66, and 2026 is a.m. 109.

A working术

A piece of magic: a spell, cast from a recipe.

A ring环

People casting one working together, sharing its weight.

The Gift天赋

How much magic a person can draw, from 0 to 5. About half the world is Gift 0, and still finds ways in.

A bearer载具

A made thing that holds a working’s weight: a knotted cord, a carved seal, an iron ring, a glazed tile. Through a bearer, anyone can use magic.

Burden and sink负担 · 汇

Every working leaves a weight behind, and it lands somewhere you can see: frost at your feet, a steaming harbour, tired legs. Where it lands is the sink.

The paired schools结对

Each Straits house twinned with schools of its own lineage abroad, students and teachers crossing both ways.

The Straits Keynote海峡基调

One shared tuning, so that any ring in the world can join any other.

A Pattern Cord纹绳

A cord that remembers a whole working and plays it back.

ii
Thread ii

Every wonder has a twin

Each marvel of our industrial age, met by its magical double.

In our world · The photograph

Kept Light 藏光

Al-Merikani entrusts a moment of light to a glazed tile, and it plays back whenever someone looks at it. Family albums move: grandmother laughs again on every viewing.

In our world · The telegraph

The Long Song 长歌

A melody started in Timbuktu passes ring to ring along the paired schools and reaches the Straits by evening. News travels as music, and every port has an hour when it sings what it has heard.

In our world · The phonograph

The Singing Strings 歌弦

Kora strings hold a whole concert. Orchestras tour as one boxed instrument, and a grandmother’s lullaby is kept for great-grandchildren.

In our world · Electric light

Cold lamps 冷灯

Lamps of glazed tile that glow without flame, in house colours, so you can tell which quarter you are in by its light. The port is lit all night for the first time, and people walk until dawn because they can.

In our world · The railway

Wind-roads 风路

Sea lanes where the wind is borrowed and returned, so ships sail upwind on schedule. The ocean gets timetables.

In our world · Flight

Kite-ferries 风筝渡

The Mohist master’s wooden bird was said to fly for three days. In 1925 a ring of Lan Fang and Nine Rain kite-makers carries a person across the Straits on monsoon air, and kite-ferries follow.

In our world · Refrigeration

The Cool Hour 凉时

Pocket Winter carts bring ice cream to the tropics, then whole homes are cooled at two in the afternoon. The tropical day rearranges itself around a new hour.

In our world · The printing press

The overnight Gazette 一夜报

Fair Copy lets a single editor print a city’s newspaper overnight, in her own hand.

In our world · Cinema

Weather theatre 天气剧场

Nine Rain stages plays in the sky: rain on cue, a storm that bows, a sunset held for the final scene.

In our world · The world’s fair

The Straits Exhibition 海峡博览会

A hall of glass and living banyan where every house shows its newest wonder, and the whole world comes to see.

iii
Thread iii

Life opens up

Food, bodies, cities, play, old roads, weather and the feeling of the age.

Food and seasons

  • Mangoes in December: seasons bottled in Nine Rain chuspas and sold at a Season Market.
  • Floating farms drift between ports, harvesting as they go.
  • A second steep from every tea leaf, and fruit that keeps its day of ripeness for a month.

Bodies and medicine

  • Fevers lose their terror within a generation.
  • Old dancers dance again once their knees remember.
  • Childbirth eased by midwives who ask the body first; surgery without a cut.

Talking with other species

  • Ships consult whales on the weather, and migratory birds guide fleets home.
  • Elephants become partners in the Riau forests.
  • City councils keep a seat for the river.

Cities that breathe

  • Courtyards keep time with fountains, a splash at every hour.
  • Houses grown from living trees and shaped over a generation; neighbourhoods that float to a cooler bay in the hot months.
  • Buildings sung into shape, each quarter with its own key you can hear when the wind passes through.

Play and learning

  • Children learn their first working the way ours learn to whistle.
  • Kite duels, tide football, rope races across the harbour.
  • A twelve-year-old from the Straits spends a term in Cusco or Timbuktu.

Old roads wake up

  • Magic needs camphor, salt, frankincense, cinnabar and kola, so the old caravan and spice cities bloom again: Timbuktu, Tarim, the Borneo camphor coast.
  • The Kindling revives the ancient routes instead of replacing them.

Weather and land

  • Rains that arrive when the rice needs them.
  • Terraces climbing new mountains, deserts greening at their edges, mangrove coasts knitting back.
  • Rivers that share themselves fairly between both banks.

Feeling and festival

  • Everyone alive in 1918 felt the veil pierced: a whole generation, across every language, shares one memory.
  • On the ninth night of the lull each year, the whole world lights one lamp at the same hour.
  • New feasts join the old calendars: Boiled Crab Night, the Loom festival, the Season Market.

Part Two · The ages

[Five Ages]

(from a locked room to an open door)

I
Age I · 1918–1921 · a.m. 1–4

The Hidden Years 隐年

Nobody, not even the seven, could do it twice at will. For three years the most important discovery in the world lived in an upstairs room above a dried-goods shop, behind a door that locked from the inside.

1918a.m. 1The Kindling at the Monsoon Convocation, on the ninth night of the lull. Every Gifted person on earth feels it.
1918a.m. 1Weeks later the fever season reaches the port. The seven treat the sick in secret, in the back rooms of shophouse clinics.
1918a.m. 1The Fortnight Circle 半月会 begins: the seven meet every two weeks and work out the rules from scratch.
1919a.m. 2The seven reunite a year on with a handful of reliable workings.
1920a.m. 3Whispers from other shores: a schoolroom lamp in Bengal, a Cusco weaver’s cords, an Uppsala choir that can lean a flame.
1921a.m. 4The Congress of the Open Lamp 启灯大会.
In the street

A Lan Fang physician and a Sané-Kora healer cast together in a back room, still unsure what they are doing, and the port buries fewer of its people that season than any harbour on the coast. Nobody can say why. Everybody starts to talk.

What opened up

A handful of workings that hold, seven traditions talking to each other every fortnight, and a port that came through the fever season.

In our world · Arkwright’s mill at Cromford, behind its gate.

II
Age II · 1921–1931 · a.m. 4–14

The Licensed Decade 执照十年

The Congress opened the craft to the world, through apprenticeship, licence and the paired schools, and magic went first wherever need was sharpest.

1921a.m. 4The paired schools 结对: each Straits house twinned with schools of its lineage abroad.
1922a.m. 5The Straits Register: every recipe written down with its house, its teacher and its parent.
1924a.m. 7The first rings work the shipyards. Anyone, Gifted or not, can stand in one and share its weight.
1925a.m. 8The first kite-crossing of the Straits.
1926a.m. 9The Gazette prints its first edition by Fair Copy.
1927a.m. 10The Concordat: many grammars licensed under no single sovereign.
1931a.m. 14The Silesian Breaking closes the decade.
In the street

A rivet boy at the Keel Yard drives his first glowing rivet with frost forming round his boots, and the whole gang downs tools to throw snowballs.

What opened up

Yards, clinics, fire watches and irrigation boards take up the craft. Students cross the seas both ways, and each house writes its first grammar and songbook.

In our world · Cotton first: the jenny and the water frame.

III
Age III · 1931–1950 · a.m. 14–33

The Bearer Years 载具之年

From a.m. 17 the weight of a working could be stored in a made thing: a knotted cord, a carved seal, an iron ring, a glazed tile. Magic stopped needing the Gifted in the room, and it went everywhere.

1932a.m. 15Anneke Vos of the European Rooms carries forty recipes in her head from Toa Keng to Rotterdam. Hedge schools open from Kilwa to the Ruhr.
1934a.m. 17The first bearers, found twice in one year: a Nine Rain cord-keeper knots a working into a cord, and a Lan Fang seal-carver cuts one into stone.
1935a.m. 18The old roads wake: camphor, sea salt, cinnabar, indigo, copal and frankincense move again, and Timbuktu and Tarim bloom.
1937a.m. 20The Long Song links Timbuktu to the Straits in a single day.
1938a.m. 21Burden Notes: the Al-Merikani Trust Houses make great workings possible on credit.
1940a.m. 23Kept Light: the first moving photograph, on a glazed tile.
1946a.m. 29The first Floating Ward, a clinic on the water.
In the street

A fishwife at Kilwa who has never cast a working in her life hangs a knotted cord over her stall, and her fish stay cold till evening.

What opened up

Magic leaves the Gifted’s hands. A fishwife, a farmer or a child can use a working through a bearer, and the old caravan cities bloom on the trade in expendables.

In our world · Coal and steam, which made energy something you could store and carry.

IV
Age IV · 1950–1970 · a.m. 33–53

The Ordinary Years 寻常之年

Magic became household. Every kitchen had a bearer, every school taught small workings alongside letters, and the tropical afternoon changed its shape.

1950a.m. 33Bearers in half the world’s homes.
1951a.m. 34The Straits Exhibition: the world comes to see its workings under one roof of glass and banyan.
1952a.m. 35The Straits Keynote gives every ring in the world one tuning. The Cool Hour reaches the tenements.
1954a.m. 37The Island Speaks: a whole island tells the Land Court where a new harbour town may be built.
1959a.m. 42The Choir of a City turns the fever summer in the Straits.
1963a.m. 46The Turning Year: the Bengal monsoon arrives two weeks later, when the rice needs it.
1965a.m. 48The first Listening Council of the world: rivers, forests and herds given voices at the Concord.
In the street

Two in the afternoon on a tenement street. The Cool Hour arrives, every shutter opens at once, and children who used to sleep through the heat run out to play in it.

What opened up

Every kitchen, every school, every afternoon. The tropics cool, the night markets glow, the monsoon comes on time and the rivers get a voice.

In our world · Mass production, household electricity, air-conditioning and the Green Revolution.

V
Age V · 1970–1983 · a.m. 53–66

The Threshold Years 门槛之年

The loom’s line came home. Cords that once held rain learned to hold a whole working and play it back. And the keepers at the Evenki site, who had been listening to the Flash for sixty years, began to hear something answer.

1970a.m. 53Pattern Cords at the Nine Rain Cord House, and pattern cards at Lan Fang’s Dye Works.
1974a.m. 57A cord replays a working without its composer present, for the first time.
1977a.m. 60The first Pattern Ring: three ports joined on the Keynote, playing one cord.
1979a.m. 62Cords tuned to the resonance of the Evenki Flash.
1981a.m. 64The Evenki keepers’ council agrees to an attempt, on its own terms.
1983a.m. 66The First Door opens at the site of the Flash.
In the street

The Evenki keepers have listened here for sixty-five years. The cords are strung between larches. The eldest raises a hand, the cords play, and between two trees there is suddenly a slope of grass under a different sky.

What opened up

Workings outlive their makers, three ports play one cord together, and a door opens on another sky.

In our world · The computer, born from the loom’s punched cards, arriving on desks in the same years.

VII
Thread VII

The frictions

Kept for the next layer.

Held for later

Every age also argued with itself. These threads are gathered here, unopened, for when we turn to how people felt about magic.

The half without the Gift

Carriers who stand in rings for wages, the Carriers’ Union (1939), the Gifted societies called the Lit (1941), and the Declaration of Common Hands (1945).

Coal and Silesia

Forge heat by working halves the price of coal (1928); a coal prince tries to hold his striking province by working and is broken (1931).

Credit falling due

Burden Notes oversold by the Long Account, and the Falling Due of 1947.

Sinks and their neighbours

The Workings Act (1936), the Summer Frost over the Rhine delta (1956), the Sink Accords (1961), and the crooked year after the Turning Year (1964).

Minds and cords

The Whisper Houses, who learn that a cord can carry a working into a mind.
