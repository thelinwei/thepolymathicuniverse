00 · Working decisions

A deeper origin story

The search began with TKI: could its history grow from a richer account of scholarly travel, rather than rely on a single European founding figure?

Irish monastic scholarship offered a model of sustained learning and travelling communities. Jesuit networks opened another possibility: intentional movement of languages, instruments, texts and teachers. Our attention then shifted towards the local institutions that would give those travellers a reason to remain.

“Once we have a critical mass of scholarly activity in Singapore, others might be attracted to come.”

Wei Lin · from this conversation

Revisionary archive · 04 November 2026

Earlier PMU dates and origin stories do not constrain this exploration. This document records the revised direction; synthesis with the wider archive will follow later. It is an edited record, not a verbatim transcript.

01 · Historical reference points

Travelling through is different from taking root

Three scales need to remain visible: Singapore island, passage through its straits, and the wider Melaka–Johor maritime network. A recorded traveller in the straits does not by itself establish an island settlement, a permanent mission or a school.

14th century

Temasek before European arrival

Singapore was already a trading port within regional and Chinese maritime networks. This provides a historical setting for exchange; it is not evidence of a documented Sri-like school.1

1511

Portuguese Melaka

The Portuguese conquest established a European commercial and political presence in the wider Straits. It should not be described as the founding of a Portuguese settlement on Singapore.2

1552

Francis Xavier passes through

Singapore’s National Heritage Board records the founding Jesuit passing through the Straits of Singapore on his way to China. This precedes both East India companies, but does not establish an island school or mission.3

1565–1815

A trans-Pacific connection

The Manila galleon trade linked Asia and the Americas. It offers a real transport framework for imagining later connections; Nine Rain’s particular scholarly journey remains fictional.4

1600–1602

English East India Company

Chartered in 1600; its first expedition departed in 1601. Lancaster reached Aceh and established a trading post at Bantam in 1602. Southeast Asian activity substantially predates the British post at Singapore.56

1602

Dutch East India Company

The VOC consolidated earlier Dutch commercial ventures. Its charter also authorised diplomatic and military action: commerce, state power and information gathering were intertwined.7

1603 / 1636

Conflict and travellers off Singapore

Dutch forces captured the Portuguese Santa Catarina off Singapore in 1603. In 1636, Jesuit Marcello Mastril narrowly escaped Dutch ships in the area. These are records of a contested maritime corridor.8

1641

Johor and the Dutch take Melaka

Johor helped the VOC defeat Portuguese Melaka. Local powers actively shaped the network within which Europeans travelled.2

1819

British trading post at Singapore

Raffles’s arrival and the February treaty mark the British establishment within a much older maritime history.9

1825

Portuguese Mission in Singapore

Father Francisco da Silva Pinto e Maia established the mission. He belonged to the Vincentian/Lazarist congregation, not the Jesuits.10

1951–1954

The permanent Jesuit community

Patrick Joy became superior in Malaya in 1951; Kingsmead’s Jesuit community and student hostel began operating in 1954. This institutional history is much later than early Jesuit passage.11

Implication for PMU

A visiting Jesuit connection for early TKI is historically situated in a plausible travelling world. A resident scholarly partnership in Singapore would be our fictional development. The school’s local hosts, endowment and intellectual work explain why a traveller becomes a participant.

These dates describe our world. PMU can use its routes and encounters as reference material without reproducing their colonial outcomes.

02 · Revised institutional sequence

Seven schools, gathering by about 1820

Sri → Al Merikani → TKI → Lan Fang → Nine Rain → Kora → Sund.

This is a working sequence of institutions in Singapore. It does not rank the age or sophistication of the knowledge traditions they carry. A younger local school can inherit a very ancient intellectual lineage.

Chosen anchorSuggested window

01 · 14th century

Sri Temmengong

Suggested window

The first durable teaching community grows from local coastal expertise: mangroves, tides, freshwater, plants, seasonal change and responsibilities surrounding their use. Recognised teachers, apprentices and field sites can precede a formal charter. Its knowledge is already a reason for outsiders to seek the Straits.

02 · 15th–16th centuries

Al Merikani

Suggested window

A merchant-supported house develops teaching in calculation, navigation, languages, contracts and trust. South Asian and wider Indian Ocean relationships sustain its exchanges. Endowments, accommodation and passage make repeated scholarly visits possible.

03 · Late 16th–17th centuries

TKI

Suggested window

A locally sponsored translation and comparison house takes root before Lan Fang. It brings interpreters, navigators, mathematical teachers and visiting scholars into recurring work. Jesuits could be one consequential strand among its European connections. Its public standing grows through useful translations, reliable measurements and the ability to adjudicate disagreements.

04 · 1786

Lan Fang

Chosen anchor

The school is established in connection with the decision to reach out to the Qing court. The wider kongsi world provides a setting for organising people, resources and learning. Craft workshops and medical teaching are foundational strengths; the medical school contributes herbal knowledge, preparation practices and collections to the developing scholarly concentration.

05 · c. 1790–1805

Nine Rain

Suggested window

A small visiting programme becomes a resident institution. Sri’s coastal observations and Lan Fang’s developing medical work offer complementary reasons to stay. The initial community can be modest: a teacher, students, shared facilities and a continuing programme of observation. This window is proposed to retain its place before Kora and Sund.

06 · c. 1805–1815

Kora

Suggested window

Invited scholars establish sustained teaching and exchange around oral transmission, historical testimony, language, sound and performance. Their work enriches other schools’ understanding of evidence while encountering new traditions of its preservation. The founding journey and the people who sustain it still need to be imagined.

07 · Around 1820

Sund Commons

Chosen anchor

Latest of the seven. A maritime workshop and cooperative teaching community forms around ship repair, tools, materials, heat and maintenance. Shared work with local practitioners provides the initial foothold. Its later industrial buildings, engineering breadth and contemporary identity develop through subsequent generations; its 1820 foundation need not already resemble its present campus.

What moving Sund changes

The seven-school concentration emerges through a relatively rapid late-18th- and early-19th-century expansion. Early Nine Rain and Kora need not arrive as fully equipped campuses. Residencies, shared rooms and small teaching communities can become recognised schools before their later physical growth.

03 · The 1786 hinge

Lan Fang: diplomacy creates an educational opening

Wei Lin’s selected anchor connects institutional founding with an outward political decision: reaching towards the Qing court. That gives Lan Fang a motive to organise translators, teachers, technical practitioners and medical knowledge as part of a wider network.

Selected in this revision

1786 is Lan Fang’s founding year. Qing outreach is the proposed founding context. The medical school belongs within this origin story.

Still to imagine

Who initiates the outreach? Who funds the school? Does it begin as a medical and craft teaching house, a kongsi-supported institution, or a coalition of several existing practices?

The scholarly opportunity

A continuing connection could bring teachers, books, instruments and correspondence into circulation. Local experience would also travel outward: unfamiliar plants, environmental observations, workshop techniques and questions that metropolitan scholars cannot answer from afar. These are possible PMU developments, not consequences already fixed by the date.

Historical distinction: scholarship on the West Borneo kongsis discusses Luo Fangbo’s wish to attach his polity to Qing China, while noting the absence of formal Qing recognition. The research checked here does not securely establish 1786 as the date of that outreach. This document therefore records 1786 as the chosen PMU anchor rather than a verified historical diplomatic date.12

04 · Reciprocal scholarly attraction

One school becomes another’s reason to come

The founding mechanism is a continuing question that needs people to remain together.

RelationshipWhat the exchange makes possible
Sri ↔ Nine RainCompare water across climates and biomes: rain reaching land, river water approaching the coast, tidal cycles, mangroves and saline transitions. Each tradition brings observations that expose gaps and open new questions in the other.
Lan Fang ↔ SriBring medical preparations and herbal collections into conversation with local habitats, cultivation, seasonal availability and knowledge of plants in place.
Lan Fang ↔ Nine RainCompare plant knowledge, preparation, storage and the effects of different growing conditions. Medicine becomes a shared field of inquiry rather than an incidental service.
Kora ↔ the other schoolsStudy how communities preserve knowledge in voice, performance, memory and testimony. Kora learns from unfamiliar traditions and contributes methods for understanding their precision, authority and transmission.
TKI ↔ the other schoolsTranslate concepts and compare measurements, calendars and claims. Working across traditions also changes TKI’s own standards of evidence and its understanding of what a record can be.
Al Merikani ↔ the networkSustain travel, accommodation, endowments and trusted correspondence; develop the mathematics and agreements that make long-term exchange workable.
Sund ↔ the networkDevelop and maintain tools, vessels and shared facilities. Encountering unfamiliar materials and tropical conditions gives its resident practitioners a continuing research programme.

From a visit to a school

  1. An invitation answers an existing need.A particular teacher or community has something valuable to contribute—and something it wants to learn.
  2. The work outlasts the visit.Seasonal observation, translation or teaching requires repeated contact.
  3. People and materials accumulate.Students stay; collections grow; patrons fund accommodation and facilities.
  4. A resident institution takes shape.Its founding date may mark a shared commitment or endowment, well before a monumental campus appears.
  5. The new school attracts further exchange.The scholarly concentration becomes a reason to travel in its own right.
05 · Knowledge and the relationships it carries

Several purposes can share the same route

Our earlier discussion identified three layers. They remain useful for imagining particular journeys without assigning every scholar the same motive.

01 · Origin

Who enables travel?

Religious communities, merchants, courts, kongsis, local patrons and scholarly families provide different starting conditions.

02 · Inquiry

What do they pursue?

Languages, medicine, water, navigation, instruments, oral history, materials and questions generated through exchange.

03 · Consequence

What else travels?

Diplomatic access, conversion, intelligence, commercial advantage, influence, solidarity and challenges to authority.

A tidal record may serve a researcher, navigator and military patron. A translation house may become a diplomatic meeting place. An invitation can strengthen an alliance, while a guest’s teaching develops in directions the patron never anticipated. These layers offer stories to explore alongside the excitement of the learning itself.

06 · What remains open

Enough structure to keep imagining

  • Founding event versus older practice. Give each school a distinctive way of remembering its beginning: a teaching lineage, endowment, shared workshop, first residency or charter.
  • TKI’s first coalition. Identify the local sponsors and the practical question that makes sustained translation necessary. Decide later which visiting networks become influential.
  • Lan Fang in 1786. Develop the people and institutional arrangement connecting Qing outreach, craft education and the medical school.
  • Nine Rain and Kora before 1820. Their suggested windows keep Sund latest. Their particular journeys, invitations and supporting communities deserve further exploration.
  • Sund’s early form. Explore a small maritime and cooperative institution in 1820, then its later industrial expansion. Later educational models can influence a school founded before those models emerged.
  • The later magical transformation. These foundations concern scholarly institutions and expertise. How they subsequently become the magical schools can be developed in the wider revision.

The Straits become a place where scholars arrive with questions—and discover reasons to stay.
