The Seven Schools
A Cultural Ecology

GPT27 October 2026 · naming genealogies

Seven institutions. Seven philosophies of knowledge. One society that none of them could build alone.

The proposition

The final world first.

Imagine the schools as functioning institutions: each with a founding community, beliefs, pupils, patronage, scholarly work, political influence, revenue sources and a physically transformed landscape.

Their cultural origins remain recognisable and alive in practice, and any student can learn to think in any of them. The real identity of each school is what it does for the Parallel Straits.

An alternate history of seven institutions.

Names and broad visual directions come from the PMU studies of 26–27 October. The demographic percentages, institutional economics, founding scenarios and philosophical commitments are new propositions in this chapter.

4 + 3: a starting geography.

Four Straits-rooted schools and three internationally founded schools, all seven mixed. The CMIO inspiration is historical scaffolding; each way of knowing is learned.

III / The naming genealogies · new study, 27 October 2026

First, somebody called it something.
Then a school decided what that meant.

Names are archaeological deposits. A toponym, a ledger number, a patron’s honorific, a workshop sign, a nickname: all may precede an institution’s beliefs. Later generations can keep the sound while changing its meaning.

Proposal for the current PMU

Keep the seven names we have.

These are the established public names in the lookbooks and environmental studies. Inventing earlier names adds depth without prematurely invalidating the identities already developed. The historical anecdotes below are fictional possibilities awaiting your selection, not previously agreed canon.

01 / Place name → civic charter
Inherited · provisional origin
The Court at Toa Keng
→
Present · established public name
Toa Keng Institution
Inner name · proposed philosophical epithetThe Open Court

01 / Circumstance

A municipal hearing court, schoolroom and debating society shared a site called Toa Keng. People called the whole compound “the court at Toa Keng” because that was the easiest direction to give a stranger. The name records a place rather than an ethnic constituency.

02 / Conscious choice

A later charter retained Toa Keng as a public address, but adopted “Institution” to bind the school to civic responsibility beyond examination results. “The Open Court” is what its students call its ideal: rules that can be argued with.

03 / The tension

Can a body called an Institution remain genuinely open to challenge?

04 / Consequence for the landscape

Civic authority passes from a building associated with hearings to a campus where the public can question how rules are made.

01 / Circumstance

Port records mention a chart house informally called Merikani. In the fiction, nobody now agrees whether the word began as a family name, an inaccurate transcription, or a name given by sailors. That disagreement is part of its origin story, not a puzzle to solve with an invented etymology.

02 / Conscious choice

When it became a college, the community standardised the inherited form as Al-Merikani rather than replacing an ambiguous waterfront history with a newly polished identity. The internal title “College of Bearings” expresses the choice to orient rather than dictate.

Who has the authority to correct an inherited name whose original meaning is uncertain?

The earliest teaching pavilions stood on lands associated with a temenggong’s household and maritime jurisdiction. Neighbours called it “Temenggong’s Garden.” The title records a political relationship, not the single essence of Malay or Nusantara culture.

Why seven mixed schools?

A school is sustained by teachers, students, labour, landowners, donors, alumni and a changing public. Even an institution rooted in one heritage develops through many others. A dominant founding tradition is therefore compatible with a thoroughly mixed present student body.

What the numbers should not do

They should never explain talent, intelligence, faith, personality or rightful ownership of a discipline. They exist to prompt fiction about migration, language, access, patronage and how the schools changed over generations.

Where Al-Merikani’s streams come from

In the current PMU it has moved toward Arabic and Andalusi artistic and scientific histories. I would preserve the South Asian founding role and let the school become a translation-and-navigation institution formed by Indian Ocean encounters. Religious, linguistic and racial identities are deliberately not collapsed into one category.

How Nine Rain holds its Maya and Andean streams

Maya traditions supply the main inquiry into weather, long time and celestial observation. Andean works enter as comparative engineering—a later intellectual meeting invented for the PMU, not a claim that the two traditions historically founded one organisation together.

Why Kora should remain specific

Rather than “African,” its proposed centre is Mandé/Senegambian knowledge transmission. Its contemporary curriculum is not restricted to music; acoustics, computational media, oral-history ethics and mediation make it a modern institute rather than an exotic performance village.

Why Sund is still here

Nordic cultures are part of the broader Western world. Sund survives the original “not Western-epistemic” test because its focus is on stewardship, working-class production and repair, rather than on presenting Europe as the default universal canon. That distinction is deliberate and open to challenge.

VII / Interdependence

One city requires
all seven kinds of mind.

A school becomes believable when it needs the others. Consider one public project: preparing a vulnerable district for a decade of extreme rain.

Intellectual disagreements

Where the schools should quarrel.

Distinct epistemologies only matter if their differences create meaningful debate.

Toa Keng × Sané-Kora

Codified deliberation versus lived testimony

Can a procedure be fair if the people most affected do not recognise themselves in its terms?

Sri Temenggong × Nine Rain

Negotiating with ecosystems versus forecasting them

What happens when a predictive model asks for an intervention that local stewards resist?

Lan Fang × Sund Commons

Reinvention versus repair

When is replacing an obsolete material an act of care—and when is it just another waste cycle?

Al-Merikani × Nine Rain

Calibrated certainty versus deep-time uncertainty

Which measurements deserve authority when the climate and the archive refuse stable baselines?

Toa Keng × Al-Merikani

Public legitimacy versus technical expertise

Sané-Kora × Sund Commons

Living memory versus material trace

Is the authentic record the remembered event, or the object that survived it?

VIII / What the proposition does not settle

Do not canonise
the first coherent answer.

The two schools that remain architecturally less resolved are opportunities—not failures. They can now be generated from their own philosophical engines.

Experiment A / Sané-Kora

Design without a famous African building.

  • Start with seven different situations of listening: testimony, music, conflict, solitude, instruction, mourning, celebration.
  • Derive plan and section from the audience–speaker relationship, acoustics, air and proximity.
  • Allow Sahelian material knowledge to inform how the building works; do not copy monumental forms by default.
  • Ask what kinds of rooms become necessary when the ownership of memory is contested.

Architectural grammar: resonance, thresholds, gathering, protected voices.

Experiment B / Nine Rain

Design without a Mayan pyramid.

  • Start with nine rain conditions: drizzle, first storm, violent downpour, overflow, drought and more.
  • Derive courtyards, screens, section cuts and thresholds from the movement of water.
  • Use the brick-pixel language from our current studies as a calibrated surface, not a historical costume.
  • Make a building that records time through sediment, wear, vegetation and changing light.

Architectural grammar: catchment, terracing, measurement, long duration.

Questions for a subsequent cultural-research pass

Sané-Kora. Which actual Mandé/Senegambian communities, languages and living scholars should inform the school? How would its governance prevent archives from becoming extractive?

Nine Rain. Is the fictional diaspora origin convincing? Which specific Maya lineages matter, and how do we avoid collapsing present-day Maya communities into archaeological ancestry?

Al-Merikani. How do South Asian traditions remain consequential as the Andalusi and Arabic research grows more visible?

Toa Keng. Who first held political power at the institution, and by what struggle did it become genuinely public?

Sund. Does an alliance between Nordic educators and local shipyard workers redistribute control, or merely aestheticise industrial labour?

All seven. What proportion of their funding makes them dependent on wealthy patrons? How do students cross schools? Who can enrol without inherited advantage?

Closing proposition
Seven living institutions, arguing about what a civilisation ought to know.

One useful next step

Take each campus's strongest existing architectural result, then test whether it still makes sense after removing its famous reference. If its belief, economy, teaching and daily life generate the same essential spatial logic, we've begun to invent a school rather than borrow an image.
