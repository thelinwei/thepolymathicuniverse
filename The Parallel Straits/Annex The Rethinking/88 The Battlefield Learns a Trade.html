01 · The idea

Not seven combat classes.
Four moments in the life of a technique.

A shipwright's skill is first useful at work. In danger it becomes a trick. In war it becomes a doctrine. At its frontier it may become a miracle. The common domains and operations explain how; the curriculum explains why anyone invented it.

0
At workA zero-Exertion cantrip. The daily job.
1
In a fightOne sharp, repeatable basic attack or evasive move.
2
In the fieldA specialised technique refined through training.
3
On a grand scaleA strategic invention, not merely a bigger blast.
From the PMU curriculum

The institutions and programmes named below come from The Seven Curricula and The Prospectus of the Seven Schools, indexed as numbers 77 and 78 on the PMU site. They are earlier worldbuilding notes, not fixed rules.

Invented for this fieldbook

All spells, effects, progressions and four-part recipes are new experiments. “At work” is defined here as zero recorded Exertion, not zero physical energy. No precise costs, caster Ranks or exclusive domain ownership are imposed.

03 · What changes at scale

Grand magic is not just a larger punch.

Basic combat: one decisive instant

A foot slides. A buckle opens. A sword misses by a measured angle. A second shadow draws the wrong counterattack. The power is small; its timing changes the fight.

Grand combat: a new environment

A harbour becomes a shield. A city rearranges evacuation routes. A coastline walks. A storm of light draws every incoming arrow into a harmless curve. The power reorganises the conditions of conflict.

Professional mastery adds specificity

Not every Force specialist can hold a fleet together. That requires naval architecture, fatigue analysis, shared operating procedures and a thoroughly rehearsed recipe.

Cross-training adds surprise

A physician might borrow a shipwright’s load distribution. A percussionist might learn astronomical synchronization. The combinations are not preassigned privileges of their schools.
