Thread II

The atlas 五图

Pick a year, zoom to a region, and select any territory to follow the same ground through all five maps.

Borders are drawn by kind of rule: solid for crowns, dashed for leagues, dotted for merchant charters, fading radiance for mandala courts, and open hatching for peoples who rule by movement or kin. Rose dots are Concord harbours, the violet diamond is Quanzhou, and the dotted lines across the Sahara are caravan roads, governed as routes rather than ground. Pale, unmarked land is inhabited ground whose polities are not yet drawn. Coastlines are Natural Earth; every border is approximate.

III
Thread III · 1450

The Concord’s first generation

The Concord ports, ringed in rose, already span three seas.

The Concord is fifteen years old. Its ports sit like beads along the monsoon coasts, from Kilwa to Ryukyu, and the Charter fleet sails out of Quanzhou. Everywhere else the map looks much as ours did: Ming China, a Timurid Persia, a Mamluk Egypt, a young Ottoman realm that has not yet taken Constantinople, and on the far side of the Atlantic a Triple Alliance and an Inca state still growing. The great hatched spaces are not empty: they are the herding, hunting and voyaging peoples whose rule follows people and seasons rather than lines.

Watch for
  • The Concord ports, ringed in rose, already span three seas.
  • Novgorod is still a republic; the north is not yet Moscow’s.
  • The Mexica and the Inca are regional powers, not empires yet.
  • Africa’s crowns are cores, not containers: the Sahara and the Rift are ruled by movement.

Thread IV · 1600

The licensed seas

Egypt stays its own realm and a Concord member: the Mamluk alliance at Diu keeps it out of Ottoman hands.

A century and a half after Diu, Europe trades in the east only from licensed, unfortified factories. The Concord now reaches from Egypt and Ethiopia to Ryukyu and Luzon, and its seat at Malacca has never fallen. In the Americas the Anahuac Compact and a Tawantinsuyu of two capitals have survived the epidemics. Europe is a crowded field of mid-sized crowns, leagues and merchant republics, with the Sound Compact holding the north and a Commonwealth of Two Nations in the east.

Watch for
  • Egypt stays its own realm and a Concord member: the Mamluk alliance at Diu keeps it out of Ottoman hands.
  • Gujarat and Bengal keep their ports outside the Mughal Empire, as Concord members.
  • Songhai is at its height; the 1591 crossing has just been turned back.
  • The Sound Compact is not yet a Concord member, but Sund has had a lodge at the Straits since 1587.

Thread V · 1790

The eve of the Kindling

The Shore Leagues are five commonwealths, hemmed in on every side.

The last year without magic. The long balance holds: no power anywhere is large enough to swallow its neighbours. Great Anahuac is the strongest state in the Americas, flanked by the Longhouse Confederacy, the Horse Nations and five small Shore Leagues that broke from their crowns and never federated. The Mughal Empire has loosened into a commonwealth of princely realms, the Ming rule by sharing power with chartered provinces, and in west Borneo a Hakka kongsi has just founded a republic.

Watch for
  • The Shore Leagues are five commonwealths, hemmed in on every side.
  • The Dzungar Khanate still holds the steppe.
  • The Sahel has become a compact of cities, lineages and confederations.
  • The Sound Compact is now a Concord associate, the one European power inside it.

VI
Thread VI · 1914

The year of the Vienna arbitration

The Federated Provinces: one name, two very different halves.

The summer our world went to war, PMU went to the table. A Balkan quarrel is settled by arbitration at Vienna. China has become the Federated Provinces three years before, without civil war, and the map shows why it is strong and not united: a liberal coast and conservative inland provinces under one compact. Crowns are giving way to leagues and assemblies on every continent. Six years earlier, in the Evenki forest, the First Door opened.

Watch for
  • The Federated Provinces: one name, two very different halves.
  • The Sublime Commonwealth holds the Levant whole; no distant hands draw its borders.
  • Africa’s great leagues: the United Sahel, the Forest Crowns and the Kongo Confederation.
  • The First Door, marked in Siberia, is held by Evenki keepers.

Thread VII · 2026

The present, am 236

Leagues and compacts, dashed, now outnumber every other kind of rule.

Today the map is a patchwork of compacts. Two crowns rule outright where sixteen once did; everywhere else power is shared, federated, chartered or carried on the move. The Great Lakes Concord, the Cem Anahuac Federation, the Union of the Four Quarters, the Mande–Songhai Federation and the Federated Provinces are the great shapes, and none of them could swallow its neighbours if it tried. The Straits remain the Concord’s seat, and dozens of Doors, charted elsewhere, open beyond the map.

Watch for
  • Leagues and compacts, dashed, now outnumber every other kind of rule.
  • The Atlantic Concert joins the Isles, France and the Low Countries without merging them.
  • The Plains Commons and the Steppe Kurultai still rule by movement.
  • The Federated Provinces keep their two halves.

VIII
Thread VIII

Lines, routes and radiance

The edge of a territory says how it is ruled.

Because the colonial scramble never happened, most of the world’s edges were never surveyed into lines. A league’s border is agreed among its members; a mandala court’s reach simply thins out until another court’s begins; a herding people’s range moves with the seasons. The map shows these honestly as different kinds of edge, which is why Africa, the steppe and the Plains look so open even in 2026.

Thread IX

Two harbours: Quanzhou and the Pearl River

The Charter sails from Quanzhou; the Lanfang Hakka sail from the Pearl River.

The Treasure-Ship Charter needs a home port, and Quanzhou is the natural one. In PMU it is the Charter’s harbour from 1426, the city where the merchant houses keep their books and the fleet refits between monsoons.

The real roads underneath
  • Quanzhou was the Zayton of Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta, the greatest port of Song and Yuan China, home to Arab, Persian and Tamil merchant communities. It declined under the Ming sea bans and the silting of its harbour.
  • Zheng He’s fleets gathered and waited for the monsoon at Changle, near Fuzhou, also in Fujian. A stele at Quanzhou’s Lingshan tombs records his visit in 1417.
  • Luo Fangbo, founder of the Lanfang kongsi, was a Hakka from Jiaying prefecture (today’s Meizhou) in eastern Guangdong. He led more than a hundred migrants, mostly from Jiaying and Dabu, out of the Pearl River estuary.

So in PMU Quanzhou never declines: the Charter keeps its harbour dredged and its foreign quarters full. And the Lan Fang road begins in the Hakka hills of Jiaying, reaching the sea at the Pearl River, a different road from the Charter’s. Two Chinese streams reach the Straits, one merchant and coastal, one Hakka and upland.
