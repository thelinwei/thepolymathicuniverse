The Polymathic Universe · An exploratory fieldbook · 31 October 2026

Magic with
a Day Job.

Forty-two new things people do with magic when nobody is saving the world. Apprentices, midwives, welders, pranksters, performers, and the occasional rather inventive duellist.

Axioms stay beneath the surface. What we see first is the boiling kettle, the crooked staircase, the missing note, the impossible seam. All spell costs and taxonomies here are provisional, not canon.
How to readBrowse spellsCross-school inventionsOne spell, seven accentsWhat to test next

Start with the delightful thing that happens.

01 · The ordinary job

Someone needs to fix a joint, carry a sofa, dry a sleeve, tune a drum, or find a door. The magic belongs to a lived profession.

02 · The second use

The same working becomes a prank, a stage trick, a rescue method, or a defensive move. Invention happens by borrowing from another trade.

03 · The hidden structure

Domain and operation remain readable, and every spell has a four-part recipe. But the magical grammar is the engine, not the destination.

All seven schools can learn any of these. The school named here is where the technique is imagined to have been cultivated; affinity means fluency, not ownership. Cost 0 = zero recorded Exertion, not zero heat, matter, effort, or consequences.

The next discoveries

What this opens up.

A memorable magic system is not a classification of extraordinary effects. It is a civilisation full of people discovering ordinary uses for extraordinary relationships—and then discovering unexpected uses for those ordinary techniques.
  • Make professions the testing ground. Follow a shipwright’s six working days, a medicine student’s clinic rotation, or a Nine Rains brick-maker’s workshop.
  • Build spell ancestry. Which tiny schoolroom cantrip was eventually adapted into a rescue method, stage stunt, or combat technique?
  • Keep ranks, scale and costs independent. A tiny spell may be conceptually sophisticated; a large spell may be a familiar recipe performed with more resources.
  • Test the environmental substrate. What changes when the street is dry, the walls are timber rather than brick, or the song is played outdoors?
  • Leave room for surprises. Axioms explain why a technique works. They should never predict every ingenious thing people will do with it.

These are exploratory spell proposals. The shared nine-domain/eight-operation vocabulary and four-part recipe are retained for comparison; individual spell costs, durations and emergent effects remain provisional.
