The proposition

A school’s beliefs, given weight and surface.

A shield, an instrument, a textile triangle, a carved seal, a hanging record, a listening seat and a reciprocal joint. Each emblem makes a different claim about where knowledge comes from, and what gives an institution the right to hold it.

The format tells us where a school locates authority. The workmanship makes that claim tangible.

The ceremonial designs are the schools’ legacy crests. Decades later, each school reinterprets its crest through a symbol already carried on its uniform, and the mark moves from inherited authority onto the student’s body. The mottos and founding identities follow the PMU atlases.

School 01 · The shield · Chartered authority

Toa Keng Institution

School motto
Ex Limo, Lumen

From mud, light.

Core belief

Knowledge begins in the ground we actually stand on.

Toa Keng places its confidence in inquiry, archives and public institutions. Its inherited ceremony is continually tested against the material city: drains, weather, water, evidence and the inconvenient facts beneath respectable stories.

Where authority resides

Charter, record and accountable inquiry.

Knowledge in practice

Archives, laboratories, cartography, perceptual experiments and civic investigation.

Material language

Oxidised teal and ivory enamel; aged brass; ember-rust rays; mud-umber recesses.

Rising sun

Illumination emerges from the ditch itself. Learning grows out of difficult conditions rather than escaping them.

Teal tidal band

The port’s water and drainage remain central to the school’s account of itself.

Mud and branching channels

The ground is an active foundation. The institution acknowledges the compromised conditions from which it grew.

Shield and parapet

The chartered school retains its inherited heraldic format; the small drainage outlet ties its grandeur back to infrastructure.

The question the emblem leaves open
Who gets to decide what counts as evidence—and whose account enters the archive?

School 02 · The astrolabe seal · Entrusted knowledge

Al-Merikani College

School motto
Fides · Ratio · Amanah

Faith · Reason · Entrusted responsibility.

Core belief

Knowledge travels through relationships that must be worthy of trust.

Al-Merikani is a school of oceanic routes, obligation and intergenerational stewardship. Measurement gives a promise precision; reputation gives it consequence. Wealth carries duties of hospitality, patronage and keeping.

Where authority resides

Oath, circulation, measurement and reputation.

Knowledge in practice

Navigation, commercial law, mathematics, manuscript culture and arts sustained through patronage.

Material language

Indigo enamel; engraved brass; a silver-toned pointer; restrained green accents.

Astrolabe and pointer

Finding one’s bearings becomes the organising idea of the emblem. The instrument connects observation with judgment.

Two-course sail

Several routes meet in one vessel: exchange depends on a shared undertaking.

Eight-pointed star

Celestial orientation and geometric order anchor the upper field.

Waves and engraved ring

The sea is both route and uncertainty; the enclosing seal recalls agreements made binding.

The question the emblem leaves open
Trust can enable exchange, but inherited networks can also decide who is allowed to participate.

School 03 · The tumpal · Custodianship

Sri Temenggong Institution

School motto
Akar Menjunjung Langit

Roots uphold the sky.

Core belief

Continuity is an obligation to care for what sustains life.

Known within its own walls as Balai Pusaka, Sri Temenggong understands learning through place, inheritance and responsibility. Forest, river, cloth and ceremony carry knowledge across generations. Authority is justified through custodianship.

Where authority resides

Living inheritance, territorial memory and care.

Knowledge in practice

Landscape knowledge, textile lineages, ceremonial arts and the stewardship of living places.

Material language

Deep forest-green enamel; old gold; earth-brown recesses; cream inscription inset.

Ascending tumpal

A textile-border form gives the emblem its own format of authority. Cloth and ceremony precede the institutional gate.

Downward keris

The blade’s orientation expresses power restrained into duty.

Roots and canopy

The lower structure supports the upper growth: inheritance must remain alive to sustain aspiration.

Crescent and woven border

Courtly continuity and textile workmanship frame the central obligation.

The question the emblem leaves open
Custodianship must confront whose inheritance is protected, and who has been displaced from it.

School 04 · 兰芳书院 · The recomposed seal

Lan Fang College

School motto
通古今，达中外

Connect past and present; reach across cultures.

Core belief

Belonging can survive departure, translation and reform.

Lan Fang treats the archive as material for an unfinished future. Its scholarly and mercantile city life brings reading, printing, travel and criticism together. The settled name is Lan Fang, in hanyu pinyin; 兰芳书院 remains an integral name of the institution.

Where authority resides

Translation, scholarship and the capacity to revise.

Knowledge in practice

Print culture, design criticism, cinema, translation, architecture and scholarly-commercial exchange.

Material language

Cinnabar lacquer; ink-black bronze; pale-gold relief; parchment-toned details.

Swallow in flight

Departure and return belong to one life. Movement need not sever belonging.

Recomposed square

The authority of the carved seal is retained while its arrangement is opened to revision.

Folded horizon

Page, landscape and route meet beneath the travelling bird.

Chipped corner

Loss remains visible. An inherited record carries omissions as well as achievements.

The question the emblem leaves open
Reform renews an archive, but it can also destroy the records that would challenge its account.

School 05 · The stepped band · Recorded relations

The House of Nine Rain

School motto
Measure the heavens. Feed the valley.

Observation carries an obligation to sustain life.

Core belief

Sky, water, land and livelihood are one connected problem.

Nine Rain brings cosmological observation into everyday ecological provision. Terraces, channels, seasons and woven records make relationships visible. A record may be a physical object, held and read through material structure.

Where authority resides

Cosmological pattern joined to material provision.

Knowledge in practice

Astronomy, agricultural systems, water engineering, ecological habitats and textile records.

Material language

Terracotta and ochre enamel; cacao-dark bronze; turquoise water; woven record cords.

Condor and serpent

Sky and earth share one field. Their relationship gives the emblem its central composition.

Stepped terraces

The geometry of landscape and infrastructure continues into the spread wings.

Water and nine rain marks

Observation becomes the distribution of a life-sustaining resource.

Five knotted cords

The record is part of the emblem itself. The 2·1·3·1·2 sequence is the house’s own register motif.

The question the emblem leaves open
A system can make resources legible while concealing the labour of those who maintain its records.

School 06 · The listening seat · Dialogic authority

Sané-Kora Institute

School motto
No throne hears itself clearly.

Authority needs a voice that can answer it.

Core belief

Knowledge is carried between voices and tested through response.

Sané-Kora treats performance, memory and listening as scholarly infrastructure. Its ceremonial seat is an office with obligations: whoever occupies it must hear contradiction. The emblem gives listening a structural presence.

Where authority resides

Ceremony, memory and the obligation to be answered.

Knowledge in practice

Oral archives, music-poetry-history, responsive chorus, acoustic architecture and public dialogue.

Material language

Warm copper; dark plum enamel; near-black patina; finely engraved rhythmic lines.

Ceremonial seat

The office is defined by its responsibilities rather than the identity of its occupant.

Paired listening forms

Hearing another voice is built into the silhouette of authority.

Open loops and three strings

The open spaces suggest room for response; the strings connect the mark to resonance and performed memory.

Blank dedication plate

No individual’s name occupies the base. The institution leaves the office available to future voices.

The question the emblem leaves open
A tradition of listening still has to answer who is invited to speak, and who appoints the listeners.

School 07 · The reciprocal joint · Shared structure

The Sund Commons

School motto
Ingen står alene

No one stands alone.

Core belief

A shared structure endures through the contribution of every member.

Sund makes civic cooperation visible through construction. The school values repair, public beauty and the ordinary infrastructures of a good life. Its ceremonial emblem gives precision and care the visual weight that other schools give to inherited splendour.

Where authority resides

Reciprocity, assembly and continuing maintenance.

Knowledge in practice

Cooperative workshops, repair, civic design, shared provision and the design of everyday public life.

Material language

Brushed silver-toned metal; slate-blue and grey-green enamel; pale ash-wood inserts.

Three joined members

Equal structural elements form one reciprocal arrangement; none is presented as a sovereign centre.

Open central field

The shared space remains open rather than occupied by a ruler or figurehead.

Exposed joints and fasteners

The means of assembly are visible and can be understood, maintained and repaired.

Datum rail

The inscription sits on a working-drawing-like baseline, carrying the school’s civic character into the badge.

The question the emblem leaves open
Equality depends on continuing maintenance—and on recognising who is doing it.

Chapter II · Decades later

The contemporary identities

The uniform becomes the bridge. Each school develops its own graphic voice through a symbol already carried on the body.

Different visual traditions arrive at different kinds of modernity. Architectural cuts, instrument geometry, folded foliage, seal carving, stepped fields, textile loops and modular joints keep their distinct characters, set side by side on one board.

Chapter III · The identity worn

Colour & single metal

Fourteen pins: two ways for each school’s contemporary mark to catch the light.

Colour keeps the recognisable school palette through enamel and contrasting surfaces. Single-metal relief translates those distinctions into depth, grain and reflection. Both versions keep the mark’s silhouette at the centre of the object.

Chapter IV · Material as meaning

Beyond enamel & metal

Four schools reconsider what the badge itself is made of. Grain, clay, carved stone and concrete become part of the identity.

The exploration moves into materials: bamboo or wood for Sri Temenggong, a brick-like body for Nine Rain, a carved seal for Lan Fang, and concrete joined to metal for Sund. Each becomes a small wearable object.

Sri Temenggong · Laminated bamboo

The leaf folds become carved grain. Pale honey laminations and a silk-matte surface connect the badge to growth, craft and living inheritance.

Material direction

Laminated bamboo, with a solid-wood version as its sibling: species, grain direction and finish open to exploration.

Next design question

Fine channels and slender tips would need wear trials and a discreet support backing.

Nine Rain · Fired terracotta

A brick-red ceramic body turns the stepped mark into a small piece of landscape infrastructure. The turquoise-glazed channel makes water the clearest colour accent.

Material direction

A refined fired-clay, brick-like surface, with shallow moulded terraces and kiln variation.

Next design question

Relief depth, edge strength and weight would need testing at actual pin size.

Lan Fang · Carved seal stone

The modern swallow seal returns to the act of carving. Cream and russet stone, knife-cut recesses and cinnabar tones connect the contemporary mark to an inherited material practice.

Material direction

The rendering reads as a pale swallow left in relief against red recesses.

Next design question

A later iteration could explore a true incised seal face, including a mirror-reversed version intended to print.

Sund Commons · Concrete and metal

Pale concrete and brushed stainless steel make interdependence visible. Two differently finished members share the same constructed mark.

Material direction

A thin, fine cementitious composite within protective metal support is the design intent.

Next design question

A prototype should resolve fragile corners, the concrete-to-metal connection and how the badge can be repaired.

Toa Keng, Al-Merikani and Sané-Kora keep their metal-and-enamel marks. The four material studies widen the range without asking every school to follow.

From image to wearable object

The badges sit at roughly 25–35 mm. The next physical pass resolves thickness, backing, fastening, weight, comfortable edges and legibility at actual size, then sets each badge on its school’s chosen uniform.

On the collection

One port. Seven ways of knowing.

The legacy crests establish each institution’s account of authority. The contemporary marks bring that account into graphic form; the pins give it a place on the student’s body; the material studies let substance carry meaning. Their relationship belongs to the city that holds them together.

Nine Rain’s motto is recorded here; it is not engraved on the current plate.
