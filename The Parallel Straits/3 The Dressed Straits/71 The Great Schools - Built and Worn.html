Thread XIII · The Outcomes

Built & Worn
Campus, cloth, belonging.

GPT26 October 2026

Seven imagined campuses. Seven ways of belonging. Architecture, cloth and the life between them.

Architectural cross-pollinations, with the students in place: each school met first through the people who wear it, then through the buildings that hold them.

The proposition

What does a school’s
way of knowing feel like?

The uniforms gave each institution a relationship between cloth and body. The crests gave its beliefs weight and surface. This exploration asks how those relationships might become courts, roofs, libraries and places to gather.

We meet each school through its students first: clothed bodies inhabiting a place already dense with routines, conversations and inherited character. Its architectural genealogy is revealed only afterwards.

Seven coherent places, encountered through the ordinary life that makes each one a school.

School 01 · Toa Keng Institution

The Unfinished Institution

Ceremonial order becomes a framework that students can alter.

A / Students in place

The uniform in use.

Displaced charcoal tailoring, ivory layers and teal cloth fans. Assembly carries the articulated shoulder; everyday dress reduces it to a smaller fold, diagonal closure or asymmetric waistcoat. Pleated divided garments and trousers coexist.

The identity worn.

Contemporary split lozenge: charcoal and teal fields, silver-toned outline and a small ember-orange sun.

Exchanging notes and carrying a model through the workshop court turns institutional formality into everyday student activity.

B / The school as place

The architectural proposition

Formal brick courts, deep stone gateways and carefully measured arcades establish an inherited institutional rhythm. Adaptable steel galleries, sliding workshop walls and teaching platforms introduce another rhythm: projects can expand, rooms can open and each cohort can leave a considered change.

What happens when an institution builds change into its own traditions?

Spaces for school life.

A hall that opens along its side into the court; inhabited workshop bridges; project tables beneath arcades; adjustable teaching bays.

Material language

Warm red brick · pale stone · charcoal steel · teal details · timber handrails

What the place holds.

Ceremonial order becomes a framework that students can alter.

C / Architectural character

Two tempers

A grammar of proportion, arrival and civic ceremony,

met by a capacity for adjustment, experimentation and changing programmes.

Prompts · Edwin Lutyens · Cedric Price

School 02 · Al-Merikani College

The Turning Court

Earthen enclosure meets the precision of an inhabited astrolabe.

A / Students in place

The uniform in use.

Ivory and indigo preserve robe and kaftan capacity. Controlled sleeve folds distinguish assembly; shorter curved tunics and generous divided lower garments carry the family into daily life. Head coverage remains a personal expression.

The identity worn.

A small fixed astrolabe pin: concentric rings, a pointer, sail and star in indigo and brass tones.

A shared notebook and a rolled chart connect the students’ study to the instruments and shifting shade above them.

B / The school as place

The architectural proposition

A low vaulted approach opens into a circular court beneath slender branching ribs and articulated copper-toned shade panels. Libraries and teaching rooms sit within substantial masonry arcades. Orientation, shade and the passing day become part of the school’s spatial experience.

What if a quiet courtyard college contained an enormous moving instrument?

Spaces for school life.

The circular gathering court; shaded upper loggias; vaulted reading rooms; small gardens and places for instrument study.

Material language

Sand-toned protected masonry · lime plaster · ivory structural ribs · patinated copper · dark timber

What the place holds.

Earthen enclosure meets the precision of an inhabited astrolabe.

C / Architectural character

Two tempers

Substantial enclosure, vaults, community and climatic attention,

lifted by ribs, articulated structure and the movement of the central canopy.

Prompts · Hassan Fathy · Santiago Calatrava

School 03 · Sri Temenggong Institution

The Woven Garden

An intimate garden approach opens into a collective clearing beneath woven timber.

A / Students in place

The uniform in use.

The revised forest, olive, cream and earth palette replaces the earlier emerald-and-gold direction. Shoulder-hung panels, leaf-like folds and generous divided wraps retain air around the body. Covered and uncovered heads belong within the same family.

The identity worn.

A small folded-leaf clasp in laminated bamboo, with grain and carved creases carrying the identity.

Flowing garments and the woven canopy share a language of suspension and folded edges, seen as students cross the court together.

B / The school as place

The architectural proposition

Low pavilions, shaded verandas and planted courts establish a sequence of enclosure and release. The assembly roof lifts above this quieter fabric as an interlaced timber canopy. Classrooms retain deep thresholds and places to sit between lessons.

What if a school’s roof behaved like a woven landscape?

Spaces for school life.

The low arrival veranda; garden court; soaring open-sided assembly canopy; screened classrooms and shaded benches.

Material language

Warm timber · laminated bamboo · clay tile · grey stone · forest-green ceramics · living landscape

What the place holds.

An intimate garden approach opens into a collective clearing beneath woven timber.

C / Architectural character

Two tempers

Structural ingenuity, interlaced members and legible connections,

set within a sequence of gardens, verandas, framed views and movement through the site.

Prompts · Shigeru Ban · Geoffrey Bawa

School 04 · Lan Fang College · 兰芳书院

The Woven Courtyard

A masonry mountain is threaded with an inhabitable timber forest.

A / Students in place

The uniform in use.

Silver-grey, parchment and ink-black form a shared scholar wardrobe. Wide sleeves, diagonal sashes, generous pleated divided garments and small red fastenings remain available across genders. Everyday tunics simplify the layers while retaining the diagonal construction.

The identity worn.

A small carved seal-stone badge with cinnabar recesses and a pale swallow, worn separately from the garment fastening.

Students compare drawings and books beneath the timber gallery. The shared silhouette carries Lan Fang’s openness to recomposition into everyday use.

B / The school as place

The architectural proposition

Layered reclaimed brick and tile form a grounded backdrop. Delicate timber galleries, screens and woven ceilings bring shade and changing views into the courtyard. Cinnabar appears on inner shutters and sheltered surfaces, discovered through movement rather than applied everywhere.

How can accumulated material memory meet a lighter, more porous way of building?

Spaces for school life.

A planted court crossed by timber galleries; a luminous double-height library; sheltered study verandas; roof gardens and screened teaching rooms.

Material language

Reclaimed grey brick and tile · ordered timber lattice · grey stone · restrained cinnabar · foliage

What the place holds.

A masonry mountain is threaded with an inhabitable timber forest.

C / Architectural character

Two tempers

Mass, reclaimed material layers and continuity with landscape and memory,

refined by fine-grained timber repetitions, porous screens and a gradual filtering of light.

Prompts · Wang Shu · Kengo Kuma

School 05 · House of Nine Rain

The Rain Cloisters

Geometric clarity meets the sound, texture and changing light of rain.

A / Students in place

The uniform in use.

The established girls’ families lead this scene: navy Terraced Column with rust inserts, and burgundy/cream Open Fan with orange and cream inner pleats. The stepped construction continues from shoulder and bib into the divided lower garments. The boys’ designs are still to come.

The identity worn.

A small fired-terracotta stepped-field badge with a turquoise-glazed central channel.

A sheltered conversation and field notes connect observation, ecological provision and material records to the rain moving through the court.

B / The school as place

The architectural proposition

Substantial brick piers and deeply recessed galleries enclose terraced courts. Circular and square openings frame sky and foliage. Rain chains, small basins and narrow turquoise channels bring water close to sheltered places for study and conversation.

Can rain become an intimate presence within a monumental school?

Spaces for school life.

The sheltered rain cloister; terraced communal court; tall brick library; small roof-lit rain courts and recessed reading benches.

Material language

Layered terracotta brick · pale concrete lintels · dark timber · stone basins · turquoise-glazed channels

What the place holds.

Geometric clarity meets the sound, texture and changing light of rain.

C / Architectural character

Two tempers

Monumental order, deep openings and a legible spatial hierarchy,

softened by material intimacy, atmospheric thresholds and close attention to sound and light.

Prompts · Louis Kahn · Peter Zumthor

School 06 · Sané-Kora Institute

The Listening Village

The gathering circle recurs in the courtyard, performance hall and discussion room.

A / Students in place

The uniform in use.

Mulberry wrapping, cream inner layers and woven geometric borders carry a soft shared silhouette. Full divided trousers, calf-length culottes and lighter diagonal panels provide different degrees of volume. The crossing remains secured; ears stay clear.

The identity worn.

A small plum-and-copper double-loop clasp framing an open space between the ribbons.

A music notebook, recorder and instrument case make listening visible as ordinary student practice, with two students seated and two joining the conversation.

B / The school as place

The architectural proposition

Clusters of learning houses gather beneath lifted roofs and deep covered galleries. Informal conversation courts lead toward a hall with audience terraces arranged around a central ensemble. Smaller rooms offer quieter settings for oral archives, study and response.

What if listening determined the plan of a school?

Spaces for school life.

Circular conversation benches; the central listening hall; storytelling rooms; shaded learning clusters and buffered quiet spaces.

Material language

Warm earth masonry · perforated ceramics · timber soffits · branching supports · mulberry acoustic textiles

What the place holds.

The gathering circle recurs in the courtyard, performance hall and discussion room.

C / Architectural character

Two tempers

Communal construction, warm masonry, ventilated roofs and shaded gathering,

arranged asymmetrically, with listeners gathered around a shared focus.

Prompts · Francis Kéré · Hans Scharoun

School 07 · The Sund Commons

The Common House

The school’s greatest luxury is room to participate, adapt and belong.

A / Students in place

The uniform in use.

Retained ivory and storm-blue references remain the basis: paired vertical planes, short tabards, joined clasps and generous ivory inner pleats in divided culottes. Everyday shirts and workshop clothing reduce the layers while preserving the shared geometry.

The identity worn.

The contemporary interlocking open rectangles, explored in fine concrete and brushed stainless steel; the render retains a simplified blue-and-light clasp.

A chair repair, open books and a shared table bring civic cooperation into an ordinary school day.

B / The school as place

The architectural proposition

A generous common hall links library, dining and repair. A pale concrete frame receives lightweight additions, warm timber surfaces and adjustable screens. Smaller reading corners give intimacy to the large shared room, while broad verandas connect it to a tropical civic garden.

How much architectural dignity can we give to ordinary shared life?

Spaces for school life.

The long shared table; curved library mezzanine; student council reading room; repair workshop and generously shaded verandas.

Material language

Fine pale concrete · exposed steel connections · warm ash timber · translucent panels · storm-blue screens

What the place holds.

The school’s greatest luxury is room to participate, adapt and belong.

C / Architectural character

Two tempers

Civic intimacy, curved library spaces and carefully modulated light,

joined to the generosity and adaptable occupation of an ordinary structural frame, extended.

Prompts · Alvar Aalto · Lacaton & Vassal

Chapter II · The process record

Directions that changed.

Two pairings were revised during the exploration. The earlier images remain here as a record of the choices that led to the current campuses.

Lan Fang · The Rewritten Courtyard

Earlier study

Reclaimed masonry met precise bronze bridges, stone thresholds and visible interventions. The revised direction develops a more continuous timber lattice, porous galleries and filtered light.

Nine Rain · The Rain Terraces

Earlier study

Coloured planes, exuberant planted terraces and conspicuous water shaped the first proposal. The revised direction retains clay, turquoise and rain while giving greater weight to enclosure, material texture, deep daylight and intimate shelter.

How the campuses settled

Sri Temenggong’s campus opened the architectural pass. Lan Fang and Nine Rain were each redirected once, toward the courtyards and cloisters in their chapters. The seven inhabited scenes then brought the lookbook’s student garment families into those environments.

Chapter III · Sources & continuity

One port. Seven ways to belong.

Each campus is built from a pairing of two architects’ languages. The campuses, students and scenes are new to this atlas; the uniforms and emblems they wear come from the existing PMU design work.
