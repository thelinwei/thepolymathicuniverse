01 · Research / in progress

Before the colour,
what does the body do?

A fold can carry a duty. A hem can record previous wearers. A garment can ask its wearer to stand apart, listen quietly, work beside another person, or reveal something only when trust is given.

A first broad sweep, not an exhaustive world dress history. Norse and Sámi entries remain explicitly marked research leads. Historical reconstruction, fictional canon and new proposals are kept distinct.

00 · Scope and method

Ways of making a body public.

Start with national and regional dress, then look beneath the national label: place, period, community, class, gender, work, ritual and climate all change what an outfit means.

A comparative method

For each case, identify the body’s outline, how the garment is held together, where cloth expands, what the head contributes, and how the foot meets the ground. Separate a museum object’s actual date from the much longer history of its garment family.

Then ask a fictional question: what might happen if this construction principle developed in a humid port with the school’s own institutions, technologies and magical practices?

A working evidence ledger

Attested A named source supports the historical description.
Research lead A relevant reference exists, but more object-level evidence is required.
Canon Already written in the live Great Schools atlas.
Proposal New design reasoning, not historical fact or adopted canon.

Dates below are study windows, not asserted invention dates. “Unknown” in hair or footwear is retained rather than filled with familiar costume imagery.

The shared constraint: all seven schools inhabit the same equatorial island. Distinction therefore needs to arise through construction, custom, trade and institutional belief as well as inherited imagery.

The later magical world can transform these principles further. This volume establishes a material baseline; it does not assume that each school corresponds to one real civilisation.

02 · Parallel timelines, not a ladder

Several histories at once.

These overlapping windows organise comparison. They do not imply that every region passed through the same stages, or that draping is less advanced than tailoring.

c.3000 BCE–500 CE
Ancient worlds

Linen, drape and civic cloth. Compare Egypt, Greek tunics and Roman toga without conflating their social codes.

c.300–1500 CE
Late antique and mobile worlds

Woven-to-shape tunics, overlapping robes and clothing built around movement. Surviving evidence is uneven.

c.1400–1800
Courts and maritime networks

Inca textiles, Ottoman cloth, Indian jama, kimono, regional wraps: authority travels through very different constructions.

c.1700–1900
Tailoring and industrial change

Structured court silhouette, kilt tailoring and changing military visibility. Handmade systems continue alongside industry.

c.1900–1970
National modernities

Qipao, áo dài, formal Chut Thai and post-independence fashion show that national appearance can be revised deliberately.

c.1970 onward / fictional
Living and alternative futures

Revival, reinterpretation, repair and technical cloth. The Straits asks how inherited construction might develop under other political and magical histories.

A research bias to correct: museums preserve fine ceremonial clothing more readily than the repeatedly washed shirt, worn-out sandal or work wrap. The finished school wardrobes need evidence of labour, laundry, cost, childhood growth and repair alongside prestige.

04 · Historical reference register

Cloth across places and periods.

01 · Nile Valley

Linen, pleat, exposed limb

Ancient Egypt · selected ancient forms

Attested reference

Light linen can hang close to the body, wrap the waist or gather into folds. UCL’s account connects dress with cosmetics and elite display; surviving images do not represent every social group.

Head / hair evidence
Elite wigs change the head’s outline independently of the garment. Do not turn the wig of an elite figure into a rule for all Egyptians.
Foot / ground evidence
Barefoot practice and sandals coexisted; the cited sandal record associates sandals with special occasions.

Possible lesson · inference
A short wrap and a ceremonial shoulder structure can produce very different degrees of formality with little fabric. Useful for studying Temenggong and Nine Rains, without asserting ancestry.

02 · Mediterranean

Chiton, peplos, himation

Archaic–Classical Greece · c.700–323 BCE

Attested reference

Rectangular cloth is pinned, belted and folded; a tunic and cloak build the silhouette. The Met explicitly notes colour and pattern: the white-marble image is an unreliable design shorthand.

Head / hair evidence
Pins and ties organise the garment, but a specific hairstyle needs a dated sculptural or vase-painting reference.
Foot / ground evidence
Select sandals or barefoot figures from a specific scene; the garment source does not establish one universal rule.

Possible lesson · inference
Belting can change hem height and body volume. Ceremony may add drape, while activity secures it closer to the body.

03 · Mediterranean

Toga over tunic

Late Republic / early Empire · c.100 BCE–100 CE

Attested reference

The toga’s sweeping cloth creates an asymmetrical civic silhouette over a simpler tunic. The Met describes Augustus promoting it as state dress in the forum.

Head / hair evidence
Head covering within a particular ceremony should be researched as ritual behaviour rather than a permanent hairstyle.
Foot / ground evidence
Footwear and citizenship status need an object-specific companion study.

Possible lesson · inference
A cumbersome garment may be prestigious precisely because it is not fieldwear. This offers TKI an institutional tension to explore.

04 · Nile Valley

Woven-to-shape tunic

Byzantine Egypt · 4th–7th century CE

Attested reference

The cited Coptic tunic was woven in a cross shape, folded and sewn. Linen or wool tunics could be short or long, sleeved or sleeveless, with simple layers beneath ornamented ones.

Head / hair evidence
No hairstyle is established by this garment object.
Foot / ground evidence
No barefoot or shoe rule can be inferred from the tunic alone.

Possible lesson · inference
Design can begin with the loom width and joining method. A plain base with replaceable identity panels is a strong uniform architecture.

05 · Inner Asia

Deel: belted mobile volume

Medieval precedents; living Mongolian tradition

Attested reference

A long, overlapping robe closes at the side and gathers under a sash. UNESCO stresses its adaptation to weather and ways of life. This is a clothing family with ethnic and regional differences.

Head / hair evidence
Hats must be chosen by community and occasion; avoid a generic “nomad” hat.
Foot / ground evidence
Boot profiles require a separate regional object study. A robe reference alone does not establish their construction.

Possible lesson · inference
Overlap, waist storage and freedom of movement are useful engineering principles. Translate the closure into a tropical weight rather than importing a cold-weather outfit.

08 · South Asia

Attested reference

A wrapped upper body opens into a generous skirt. The V&A’s Bikaner jama has a substantial embellished skirt requiring conservation support; material weight matters as much as outline.

09 · South Asia

Sari: a garment executed by folding

Long draped traditions; living regional forms

Attested reference

An unstitched length is organised into body wrap, pleats and an over-body section. The V&A treats the sari within regional textile histories. No single drape stands for the subcontinent.

Head / hair evidence
The shoulder or head section changes the upper silhouette. A bun or braid would need its own locality and occasion evidence.
Foot / ground evidence
A sari does not inherently prescribe barefoot practice or a particular shoe.

Possible lesson · inference
A uniform can prescribe a cloth and a few operations while allowing bodies to determine the final fit. A compelling alternative to fixed-size tailoring.

10 · Maritime Southeast Asia

Baju kurung: space around the body

Living Malay tradition; variant dates unresolved

Attested reference

The NHB description identifies a loose, knee-length upper garment. For development, distinguish women’s blouse-and-skirt ensembles from men’s related shirt-and-trouser forms and specific neck constructions.

Head / hair evidence
Do not assume a single head covering across centuries or wearers. Teluk Belanga and other variants require closer local study.
Foot / ground evidence
Temenggong’s removal of shoes at the Balai Lama is fictional canon; it should not be presented as a universal baju kurung rule.

Possible lesson · inference
Loose volume, a wrapped lower layer and a legible border fit Temenggong’s body language especially well.

06 · The complete figure

Hair is not headwear.
Barefoot is not an absence.

A school’s outline includes what happens above the collar and below the hem. Both can carry identity, discipline, belief, status or a practical response to place.

Movement tests for later drawings

Standing · seated at a desk · floor sitting or kneeling · arms overhead · running · stepping onto a wet surface · wearing a bag · changing clothes without an attendant. Add accessibility and different body proportions before calling a uniform complete.

07 · Part II preview / design proposals

Seven schools,
four occasions each.

The following are initial assignment options and wardrobe briefs. They have not been adopted as canon. Historical analogues contribute particular construction lessons; no school is assigned a complete real-world national costume.

Formal and everyday wear share a material family. Sportswear must answer a named activity. The fourth column explores possible service or military wear: the fiction still needs to decide which schools maintain cadet, civic-response or defensive roles.

01 · Parallax · proposals

Toa Keng Institution

What becomes visible when the body changes its frame?

Existing written constraint
Tropical worsted; four buttons; a disputed tie; Founding Day silt-dyed sash. Earlier material atlas also imagines long tropical field-scholar jackets.

Option A

Chartered field scholar: a long, vented coat over a light base; institutional asymmetry expressed through pocket and closure placement.

Option B

Ceremonial drape: an additional offset stole over the established jacket. Keep the sash legible; do not turn it into an unrelated toga.

Ceremonial / formal

Four-button jacket or its longer ceremonial relative; narrow silt sash; removable academic cord. Preserve accumulated staining as biography.

Everyday

Unlined or lightly structured tropical jacket, shirt and adjustable trousers/skirt options; pockets accessible while seated.

Sports / movement

Short sleeve or sleeveless training top; divided lower garment; a small asymmetrical panel tested for snagging. Tie stored away.

Possible service / military

Survey and response coat with secured tools and weather protection. Parade uses ceremony layers; field duty uses compact hems.

Head and hair

Proposal: open policy, secured clear of instruments during work. A ceremonial frame clip could hold hair without imposing one hair texture.

Foot and ground

Proposal: quiet everyday lace-up or slip-on; terrain-specific field shoes; sport-specific soles. No blanket boot requirement.

08 · The open shelf

Where the investigation goes deeper.

A comprehensive atlas needs more than this initial selection. These are explicit next research shelves, not historical descriptions already verified by this edition.

South and West Asia

Regional dhoti, salwar and kurta systems; Persian and Central Asian robes; Arab Peninsula and Indian Ocean working dress; matched turbans, hair and shoes.

East and Southeast Asia

Specific Han, Tang, Song and Ming garments; Vietnamese predecessors; Thai and Khmer wrapped lower garments; Javanese and Balinese court/work ensembles; beaded slippers and clogs.

Africa, plural

Asante and Ewe weaving; Yoruba construction beyond the selected gele; Ethiopian and Eritrean highland dress; Sahelian and East African coastal forms. Follow named makers and communities.

Americas and Pacific

Community-specific Maya ensembles; separate Andean periods and regions; named Pacific barkcloth traditions; Indigenous North American clothing systems; ordinary and working footwear.

Europe beyond court fashion

Medieval and Byzantine layers; Mediterranean workwear; civic and academic gowns; Balkan and regional dress; deeper Sámi-led study rather than a generic Nordic composite.

Movement, sport and service

Swimming, rowing, martial practice, running and team sport; adult athletic histories as context; civil defence, maritime rescue, protective dress and parade. Date and task each ensemble.

Ancient Greek nude male athletics is historically relevant to the limits of the modern category “sportswear”. It belongs in the research context, not in school student costume plates. [SP]

09 · Low fidelity to final image

A rendering pathway
that keeps the discoveries.

Make decisions in the order they become visible: outline, construction, use, material, then atmosphere. The later JPG/PNG collection should grow from a traceable design register.

I

Historical sweep · this edition

An initial comparative register, construction diagrams, evidence gaps and a first wardrobe matrix. Expand targeted shelves as a silhouette direction becomes interesting.

II

Assignment boards · the next design pass

Two candidate directions per school: 14 proposal boards. Each board names its construction references, canon constraints, silhouette, hair/headwear, feet and proposed material. Include formal, everyday, sport and possible service variants in simple flats.

III

Low-resolution silhouette trials

Compare all seven everyday outfits in monochrome on consistent neutral figures. Repeat for ceremony. Test front, side and rear outlines and seated/running poses. Keep colour and ornament from concealing similarity.

IV

Construction and material plates

Resolve closure sequence, seams, layers, headwear attachment, shoe transition, pockets and weather. Add labelled details and separate historic evidence from invented mechanisms. Check several bodies and hair textures.

V

Intermediate character renderings

Use image generation for exploratory illustration after the garment brief is specific. Keep a stable design sheet alongside the render so image drift does not silently become canon. Two variants need not mean two new identities.

VI

Final JPG / PNG and atlas integration

Deliver individual full-body plates and detail views with consistent framing, plus school comparison sheets. Use PNG for cutouts when needed and JPG for scene or editorial plates. Embed selected images in the final HTML; retain actual pixel dimensions and source notes.
