01 / The question beneath the examples

Not strange costumes on familiar institutions. Different institutions altogether.

丐帮 is startling not because beggars are good spies, but because people classified as marginal can operate a disciplined, transregional organisation with authority of its own. 隐士 is startling because distance from the court may increase rather than diminish someone's influence. A jeli's trained memory similarly matters because a form of knowledge has a real place within social and political organisation.

Instead of asking how a historical role could decorate a present-day institution, this atlas asks how social structures emerge when prestige, authority, membership, property, obligation and legitimacy follow very different logics.

01
Fante · coastal Ghana

Fante Asafo

Historical / documentedMilitary / civic companies

Asafo companies maintain their own officials, flags, shrines and loyalties. Historically military organisations, they also participate in civic life and the installation of chiefs; several companies can coexist in a town. Their appliqué flags communicate identity, history and pointed proverbs.

THE SHIFTThe paramount chief is visible, but organised counterweights have their own chain of command and public voice.

Implication & reading +
A question it opens

What if every district contained several associations whose banners carried real political weight?

02
Southeastern Nigeria · 1929 Women’s War

Igbo women’s political institutions

Historical / documentedCollective sanctions / protest

Women’s networks wielded collective public authority through market activity, mobilisation and practices described in English as “sitting on a man.” In the 1929 Women’s War, thousands challenged colonial warrant chiefs and policies that threatened their position.

THE SHIFTThe colonial administrative chart did not adequately recognise an existing political constituency.

Implication & reading +
A question it opens

Which institutions become invisible when outsiders decide what government is supposed to look like?

Northeastern North America

Haudenosaunee Clan Mothers

Living traditionMatrilineal authority

In the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, Clan Mothers select chiefs for inherited council titles and retain authority to remove a chief who fails in his responsibilities. The celebrated public office depends on the authority of the clan.

THE SHIFTA leader can be highly visible while the power to confer legitimacy belongs elsewhere.

A question it opens

What if installing and withdrawing public office were the responsibility of another social body?

04
Ethiopia

Oromo Gadaa

Living traditionRotating generational governance

Gadaa organises political and social responsibility through classes and grades that advance toward leadership. Governing authority rotates on an eight-year cycle, with assemblies and institutions of law, instruction and social obligation. Participation and gender roles are specific and not equivalent to modern universal voting.

THE SHIFTPolitical time can be organised through cohorts preparing to govern—and relinquish governance.

Implication & reading +
A question it opens

What changes if generations take turns carrying distinct public responsibilities?

05
Japan · 15th–16th centuries

Ikkō-ikki leagues

Historical / documentedReligious and village mobilisation

Leagues associated with Jōdo Shinshū Buddhism mobilised religious adherents, farmers and other supporters. In Kaga, the movement overthrew a military governor in 1488 and remained a formidable provincial power into 1580.

THE SHIFTBelief, local solidarity and disciplined association could challenge hereditary military rule.

Implication & reading +
A question it opens

When does a network of believers stop being merely a community and become a governing power?

Mande-speaking West Africa

Mande Donso Tòn

Living traditionInitiated hunters’ fraternities

Hunters’ associations are fraternities of apprenticeship, ethical discipline, specialised knowledge and mutual obligation. Scholarship describes advancement toward master-hunter status as not simply a matter of hereditary descent, giving initiates an affiliation that cuts across other social categories.

THE SHIFTA trade can become a second identity and a separate hierarchy of belonging.

Implication & reading +
A question it opens

What professions might acquire reputations, codes and power independent of birth or the court?

China · 456–536

陶弘景 · 山中宰相

Historical / documentedReclusive adviser

Tao Hongjing retired to Mount Mao yet remained sufficiently influential that Emperor Wu of Liang consulted him. The epithet “prime minister in the mountains” captures the paradox of an adviser whose legitimacy was tied to his distance from court.

THE SHIFTRefusing office may increase the moral prestige that makes a person worth consulting.

A question it opens

Highland Southeast Asia

Zomia / state-avoidance thesis

Interpretive modelContested historical interpretation

James C. Scott argued that highland mobility, dispersed settlement and certain livelihood practices could partly be understood as strategies for avoiding incorporation into lowland states. Researchers have challenged the breadth of this explanation and documented highland forms of governance and hierarchy.

THE SHIFTNot becoming legible to a state can sometimes be understood as deliberate political action.

Implication & reading +
A question it opens

What if the question is not “why no state?” but “why accept this particular state?”

Kachin gumsa and gumlao

Interpretive modelPolitical transformation

Edmund Leach’s influential account described contrasting more hierarchical gumsa and more egalitarian gumlao arrangements, tracing political change rather than fixed “tribal” types. His model has also been revised and criticised.

THE SHIFTPolitical orders can swing between organisational forms rather than advance along one inevitable ladder.

Bali, Indonesia

Balinese Subak

Living traditionWatershed cooperation

Subak coordinates irrigation through farming collectives linked to water temples and ceremonial life. Its water-sharing networks integrate ecological management, local social organisation and philosophy across fields and watersheds.

Māori rāhui

Yoruba communities and diaspora

Yoruba Ifá

Living traditionPatterned interpretation and judgment

Ifá practitioners interpret an elaborate formal system of signs and verses, including 256 odu, in addressing consequential questions. It is a knowledge and divination tradition with an extensive inherited—and evolving—interpretive corpus.

THE SHIFTAuthoritative judgment may be organised through patterned analogies and trained interpreters, not only statute or statistics.

Implication & reading +
A question it opens

What if a society’s advisory institution carried a vast repertoire of interpretive stories?

Kongo nkisi / Mangaaka

Historical / documentedMaterial witness / obligation

Some nkisi figures, activated through collaboration between artists and ritual experts, were involved in protection, healing, dispute settlement and enforcement of agreements. The Met’s Mangaaka figure bears metal insertions associated with vows and public obligations.

THE SHIFTAn object can participate in the social act of witnessing, not simply illustrate abstract power.

Korean classical fiction

洪吉童传 · Hong Gildong

Fictional workOutlaw → alternative kingdom

Born into an exclusionary status hierarchy, Hong Gildong becomes the leader of an outlaw band and eventually establishes Yuldo, an alternative kingdom. The novel converts social marginality into organisational and political invention; its authorship history is debated.

THE SHIFTThe outlaw does not simply defeat injustice; he imagines a different polity.

江湖 · 丐帮 · 华山论剑

Fictional workParallel moral and political geography

Jin Yong’s 江湖 possesses sects, loyalties, reputations, rival claims to mastery and circulation of prized manuals. 丐帮 imagines beggars as a disciplined association with extraordinary reach. 华山论剑 gives martial reputation a venue independent of the imperial court.

Five cautions worth keeping

One. A living practice is not a prototype waiting to be imported into a fictional school. Learn its particular history before recombining.

Two. A parallel institution can be exclusionary, hierarchical, benevolent, coercive or several at once. This atlas is about possibility, not automatic virtue.

Three. “Western” and “Eastern” are poor containers for intellectual traditions. Modern bureaucratic assumptions are the particular frame being unsettled here.

Four. Fiction and anthropology make different kinds of claims. 丐帮 is a remarkably useful social thought experiment, not evidence for a historical beggars’ polity.

Five. Some traditions (especially Zomia and Leach’s gumsa/gumlao model) are scholarly proposals that have attracted substantial debate.

A note for the next world
One person belongs to a family, a guild, a river, a ritual fraternity, a generation, a movement—and perhaps a state. None of these has to be the final authority.

The institutional imagination starts here: not from costumes, ceremonies or single magic powers, but from overlapping loyalties, contested legitimacy and forms of expertise that become forms of governance. This atlas is deliberately exploratory. It does not canonise these into any one fictional world.
