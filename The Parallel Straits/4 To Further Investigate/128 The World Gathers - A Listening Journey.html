THE CURATOR’S NOTE

Why these people?

Because Veyr is a story about an impossible problem: how can strangers, thousands of miles apart, make one spell together without having to become the same?

The historical artists here reveal different answers. Some carry memory through performance. Some transform a written framework on the spot. Some coordinate without a conventional conductor. Some compose intricate forms that leave room for many voices. Others turn an audience into participants—or let a composition keep changing after its maker has gone.

These are listening invitations. Each artist’s real work is rich, messy and worth hearing for itself, with the PMU parallel as one way in. Follow one recording, notice something, and only then move on.

CHAPTER 01

When a voice carries a people

Before orchestras or spell diagrams, music can carry memory, exile, solidarity and a community’s sense of itself. The opening question: “Why would anyone cross the world to answer this call?”

Miriam Makeba

South Africa · 1932–2008
What might intrigue you

Listen for the effortless shift between intimacy and public address. “Pata Pata” is joyful; then read about her political exile. The same performer can carry celebration and historical loss.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: a singer arrives not as a weapon but as a witness—someone whose voice makes strangers feel addressed.

Suggested start: Pata Pata

Toumani Diabaté

Mali · 1965–2024
What might intrigue you

Hear the kora’s flowing, interlaced lines. A single instrument can sound conversational, as though several people were answering one another. His hereditary jeli lineage also opens questions about memory as a profession.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: a keeper of ancestral repertoire may know how to weave countless independent contributions without flattening them.

Suggested start: Kaira
▶ Find recordingSpotify

Mercedes Sosa

Argentina · 1935–2009
What might intrigue you

Listen to “Gracias a la Vida”. Notice how an apparently simple melody can become collective property: other people adopt it, reinterpret it and sing it in different circumstances.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: the song must belong to those who need it, not merely to those who first cast it.

Suggested start: Gracias a la Vida

CHAPTER 02

What happens when a soloist refuses to stay inside the score?

Your night almost fails. There is a proposed framework, but one remote port needs longer, another begins late, another musician hears a new possibility. Improvisation becomes a form of intelligence, not a mistake.

Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

Pakistan · 1948–1997
What might intrigue you

Begin with “Allah Hoo”. Follow the intensification, the exchange with the ensemble, and the extraordinary way repetition continually changes its emotional force.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: the soloist who can keep a passage alive long enough for distant ports to catch up.

Suggested start: Allah Hoo

Umm Kulthum أم كلثوم

What might intrigue you

Start with a live “Enta Omri” performance. Don’t rush it. Pay attention to the long musical arc, audience response, and elastic treatment of a phrase.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: the soloist is not merely singing to a timetable; she is reading a living assembly.

CHAPTER 03

How do faraway ports stay together without becoming identical?

Broadcasting and livestreaming imply more than transmitting a melody. Each port needs an arranger, a local soloist and an ensemble capable of responding in real time. Here are three profoundly different approaches to coordination.

Danongan Kalanduyan

Philippines/USA · 1947–2016
What might intrigue you

Start with kulintang performances. Hear how repeated gong patterns, changes of emphasis and ensemble knowledge coordinate performers without a symphonic conductor.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: each port has a master of local timing who can adapt the shared framework to its own musical grammar.

Suggested start: Kaluntang
▶ Find recordingSpotify

Rahayu Supanggah

Indonesia · 1949–2020
What might intrigue you

Explore his gamelan and theatre work, including “I La Galigo”. Pay attention to layered cycles and the way music supports large-scale movement and story.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: a casting distributed across many cities may be nested, cyclical and locally paced, not one universal metronome.

Suggested start: I La Galigo Rahayu Supanggah

Zakir Hussain

India · 1951–2024
What might intrigue you

Find a solo tabla performance and listen to rhythmic recitation, acceleration and return. The astounding virtuosity matters, but so does the intelligible structure underneath.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: a rhythm keeper provides rendezvous points when ports drift apart.

CHAPTER 04

How can the largest spell ever cast still have room for difference?

More people do not automatically produce a larger spell. Someone must design a musical form that allows distinct contributions to remain distinct—and gives soloists room to surprise everybody.

Johann Sebastian Bach

Germany · 1685–1750
What might intrigue you

Start with “The Art of Fugue”. Follow how a recognisable idea travels between autonomous voices. It’s a way to think about coordinated independence.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: the written framework is sturdy enough to support parts its author never expected.

Suggested start: Art of Fugue
▶ Find recordingSpotify

Duke Ellington

USA · 1899–1974
What might intrigue you

Hear “Mood Indigo” and then a full band performance. Notice the personality of individual instrumental voices inside an ensemble identity; his arranging often used the specific sound of particular musicians.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: the conductor does not erase legendary artists’ signatures; the spell depends on them.

Suggested start: Take the A Train
▶ Find recordingSpotify

Tan Dun 谭盾

China · b. 1957
What might intrigue you

Listen to his “Water Concerto”. Instruments, environment, gesture and water itself become part of composition.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: Nine Rains and other schools discover that a worldwide musical spell can draw on local material and environmental conditions.

Suggested start: Water Concerto

CHAPTER 05

What happens when the world answers back?

The night is unprecedented, but the greatest discovery may come afterwards: ordinary people adapt the spell into relief, comfort, warning and celebration. A song no longer has to remain the property of its makers.

Bobby McFerrin

USA · b. 1950
What might intrigue you

Watch a live audience improvisation or pentatonic demonstration. What matters is not just his voice, but how quickly strangers learn to become participants.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: whole communities unexpectedly join the spell even though they were never on the official programme.

Suggested start: Bobby McFerrin audience pentatonic
▶ Find recordingSpotify

Jacob Collier

UK · b. 1994
What might intrigue you

Watch him conduct an audience choir. Listen for thousands of individuals finding a harmonic destination together, often with very little instruction.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: global casting becomes a participatory living system, not just a transmission from the Straits.

Suggested start: Jacob Collier audience choir
▶ Find recordingSpotify

Brian Eno

UK · b. 1948
What might intrigue you

Listen to “Music for Airports”. Its slow, repeating, overlapping phrases challenge the assumption that music must have one definitive performance.

PMU possibility. For Veyr: the original song becomes an adaptable musical environment; each later community makes it work differently.

Suggested start: Music for Airports

The spell succeeds because nobody could have rehearsed the whole world.

The organisers prepare a structure. Ports arrive with their own musical knowledge. Legendary soloists improvise. Conductors listen. Somewhere a voice enters unexpectedly, and somewhere else a community answers. The result is larger than its creators—and later generations will turn it into relief magic of their own.

That is what this atlas invites you to hear.
