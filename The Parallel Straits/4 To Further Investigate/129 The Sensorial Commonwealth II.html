01 / What changes from the first atlas

From aptitude
to institution.

The first Sensorial Commonwealth asked: what if unusual perceptual expertise were accepted as evidence? This sequel asks what that recognition does to society.

The transformative move is finding where making, perception and trained interpretation become conditions of political life.
An exploratory protocol

01 · The actual practice. What did people do? With what tools, performances, materials and learned distinctions?

02 · The social arrangement. Who could train? Who granted standing? Was the skill hereditary, purchased, collectively held or contested?

03 · The institutional consequence. What else depended on the practice—law, markets, inheritance, diplomacy, ecology, architecture?

04 · The speculative departure. Recombine the relations into new institutions.

Six forms of sensory power

Every sense
hides a verb.

The institution begins where a recognized expert gains a socially consequential verb. These six are the atlas’s own analytic categories.

To witnessA body examiner’s testimony can make a matter legally legible.
To rememberA material keeper sustains treaties, genealogies and public obligations.
To authorizeA guild can control the right to transform a body, image or civic space.
To hostThe shared meal or exchange may renew a relationship more effectively than a declaration.
To regulateAssessing quality or flows becomes jurisdiction over trade, water and safe passage.
To interruptLicensed counsel, satire or ritual can change what a community is willing to hear.

ArchivesDiplomacy

Wampum keepers

Haudenosaunee · North America

A belt is not a picture of a treaty. It helps hold a treaty in existence.

THE ARTShellwork · touch · speech · memory
EVIDENCEDirect institutional precedent
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Onondaga Nation describes wampum as a living record: agreement words are entrusted to strings and belts and subsequently recalled by authorized speakers. Strings have also signified chiefly and Clan Mother titles, helped convene councils, and accompanied truth-telling in ceremony. The authority lies neither in the object alone nor in any individual reader; it is sustained by community obligations and interpretation.

The unexpected connections

Diplomacy ↔ material making ↔ collective memory ↔ succession ↔ repatriation. Custody of the record is itself a political question: the return of belts from museums is part of the continuing struggle over who may hold, interpret and speak from them.

The generative question

If an agreement requires a keeper and recurring recitation, who can declare it forgotten, altered, or no longer binding?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A PMU accord has three lives: the negotiated wording, a crafted relational token, and a standing assembly of people authorized to renew its meaning. A missing custodian may suspend an agreement without invalidating it.

Reading note: Wampum is a living Haudenosaunee diplomatic protocol, with its own keepers and its own ways of being read.

02 / FIELD WINDOW
BodiesLaw

Steady hands, legal witnesses

Ming–Qing China · 稳婆

A society may need an expert’s testimony while denying her the status of expert.

THE ARTTouch · examination · care · testimony
EVIDENCEDocumented; roles varied by period
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Historical research on late-imperial Chinese midwives reaches beyond delivery to intimate examinations, assessments of sex, bodily evidence and the care of female detainees. Such work drew upon access to bodies and gendered spaces not readily available to male officials. A Duke Kunshan lecture summary highlights this paradox; scholarship on 稳婆 also traces their negotiated legitimacy across court, household, and state.

The unexpected connections

Birth ↔ kinship ↔ gendered privacy ↔ law ↔ forensic investigation ↔ status. Embodied access becomes an administrative capability without necessarily producing professional recognition.

The generative question

Can someone be officially necessary but socially disavowed? Who controls access to intimate truth?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A Straits city depends on a semi-autonomous guild of embodied witnesses for disputes that cannot be resolved from documents. They may refuse testimony without consent, bringing legal machinery to a halt.

Reading note: These duties are recorded for particular midwives in particular Ming and Qing settings, working to the evidentiary standards of their own courts.

03 / FIELD WINDOW
DiplomacyEconomy

The brewery as an embassy

Wari · Cerro Baúl, ancient Peru

Fermentation can be political infrastructure, not simply provisioning.

THE ARTTaste · fermentation · hospitality
EVIDENCEStrong archaeological interpretation
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Excavations at Cerro Baúl identify a monumental chicha brewery associated with a Wari administrative centre on a frontier with Tiwanaku. Archaeological interpretation connects brewing, feasting, reciprocity, and durable political relationships; shawl pins found at the facility suggest the involvement of high-status women. The brewery’s dramatic ceremonial closure highlights its special institutional role.

The unexpected connections

Flavor ↔ control of ingredients ↔ labor ↔ elite women ↔ hospitality ↔ diplomacy ↔ state endurance. The host organizes who owes what to whom, and in what spirit.

The generative question

Could a political order depend less on fixed tariffs than on its ability to feed and receive others?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

The Straits’ exchange houses are politically significant kitchens. Brewing lineages negotiate access, maintain hospitality obligations, and signal a breakdown in diplomatic relations by declining to host.

Reading note: Archaeology recovers the vessels, the brewing and the scale of the feasts; “embassy” is an interpretive analogy laid over them.

04 / FIELD WINDOW
BodiesLineages

The tattooist is also a ritual master

Sāmoa · tufuga tā tatau

A practitioner works on a body, but helps make a social person.

THE ARTTouch · rhythmic tapping · image · endurance
EVIDENCEDocumented craft institution
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Sāmoan tufuga tā tatau are renowned technical-ritual specialists whose authority can involve lineages, apprenticeship, tools, protocols and ceremonial respect. The broader category of tufuga also encompasses expertise in canoe-making and housebuilding. Tatau has communal meanings tied to responsibility and adulthood; its making involves patterned bodily action and the sound and rhythm of tools.

The unexpected connections

Skin ↔ kinship ↔ status ↔ ceremony ↔ architecture ↔ ocean craft. The provocative link is that tattooists, housebuilders and canoe specialists occupy related fields of sanctioned expertise.

The generative question

What if professional categories were organized around transformative obligations rather than materials?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A PMU lineage is entrusted with three transformations: preparing a person, preparing a vessel, and preparing a public hall. Their professional council spans bodies, voyages and buildings.

Reading note: Tattoo patterns and ceremonial protocols belong to their communities, and permission to reproduce them is theirs to give.

RightsLineages

Images that carry rights

New Ireland · Papua New Guinea

An artwork is a public display of rights, achievements and succession.

THE ARTCarving · visual literacy · ceremonial choreography
EVIDENCEDocumented rights-bearing art
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Malagan is both a complex ceremonial system and a field of carving. The Metropolitan Museum notes that rights to specified malagan images and associated rites are valuable and confer prestige. Memorial sculptures can function as visual records of the deceased’s achievements in obtaining these rights; ceremonial production and exchange take substantial time and resources.

The unexpected connections

Visual invention ↔ exclusivity ↔ inherited and acquired rights ↔ funerary ceremony ↔ status economy ↔ making. Here an image can be inseparable from the right to perform it.

The generative question

What might “intellectual property” mean when the right to make or show an image is also a civic rank?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A PMU civic title can only be activated through a specific living form—architecture, performance or carving—and the custodianship of that form can transfer separately from land or money.

Reading note: “Copyright-like” is a modern comparison used by museums; the rights themselves run on their own law of lineage and privilege.

Public voiceBodies

The masked society that can criticize

Yoruba-Nago · Benin, Nigeria, Togo

Satire in costume may negotiate social power that plain speech cannot.

THE ARTMasks · dance · rhythm · satire
EVIDENCEDirect ritual-social precedent
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

UNESCO describes Gèlèdé as a ceremonial complex of carved masks, song, dance and storytelling that honors primordial motherhood and women’s roles in social organization. Performances use irony and mockery; participating groups have male and female leaders. UNESCO describes the tradition as a rare masked society also governed by women. Its authority is ceremonial and relational, not identical with state executive power.

The unexpected connections

Woodcarving ↔ public performance ↔ gender relations ↔ satire ↔ social pressure ↔ ritual protection. The mask permits a critique that may be difficult for an individual to make alone.

The generative question

When does the right to ridicule become a recognized public responsibility?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A PMU polity grants an annual protected season for masked correction. The performer does not sit in a parliament; the square itself becomes a temporary institution of licensed social challenge.

Reading note: The figures and masks carry living, contested meanings held by the communities who perform them.

07 / FIELD WINDOW
CounselPublic voice

The interpreter of patterned possibility

Yoruba · Nigeria and diaspora

A decision-making expert is trained to navigate a corpus, not simply “see the future.”

THE ARTPattern casting · poetic recitation · interpretation
EVIDENCEDirect advisory tradition
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

The Ifá system, documented by UNESCO, joins signs produced through divination instruments to an extensive corpus of odù and recited èse verses. Trained babalawo interpret the patterns for consequential personal and collective decisions. The practice carries narratives about ethics, history, medicine and cosmology; its practitioners earn standing through learned interpretation.

The unexpected connections

Material signs ↔ mathematics-like classification ↔ poetry ↔ ethics ↔ counseling ↔ public choice. Knowledge is enacted by an interpreter familiar with accumulated precedents.

The generative question

What if important policy choices require interpreting a structured, living body of narrative cases?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

At PMU assemblies, delegates may request counsel from competing schools of case-reading. Their arguments are evaluated for interpretive depth, precedent, and consequences rather than claimed certainty.

Reading note: Ifá is a living religious and philosophical tradition, practised through years of training in a vast recited corpus.

08 / FIELD WINDOW
ArchivesLineages

The palace is a metal archive

Kingdom of Benin · Edo peoples

A workshop can produce political memory as surely as a chancellery.

THE ARTCasting · relief carving · visual order
EVIDENCEDocumented royal production
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

The Benin Ìgùn Ẹ́rọ̀nwwọ̀n brass-casting guild made royal heads, plaques and other works under court patronage. The Met documents ancestral brass heads as part of accession obligations and plaques depicting the oba, courtiers, hierarchy and foreign interlocutors. Visual production served palace authority and remembrance; after the 1897 British conquest many works were violently taken and dispersed.

The unexpected connections

Guild specialization ↔ import trade in metal ↔ royal legitimacy ↔ diplomacy ↔ public imagery ↔ colonial dispossession. Art, economics and dynastic memory converge at the foundry.

The generative question

What happens to a kingdom’s institutional memory if the objects and their custodians are separated?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A PMU guild controls civic casting rights but is answerable to multiple communities; destroying or stealing its registers becomes a political attack on legitimacy.

Reading note: The reading of the plaques as an index of court etiquette is a museum’s scholarly hypothesis.

PrestigeLineages

To listen to incense

Japan · kōdō and court incense mastery

Scent can become literacy, competitive expertise, and courtly capital.

THE ARTOlfaction · literary pattern · ceremony
EVIDENCEDocumented elite cultural office
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Japanese kōdō developed from incense practices linked to courts, temples and elite taste. Incense games trained participants to distinguish fragrant woods and connect their combinations with literature. The Met describes its role among aristocrats and warriors; another source documents courtly incense-master offices. The Japan Creative Centre in Singapore describes a contemporary demonstration using five scents and combinations associated with The Tale of Genji.

The unexpected connections

Scent ↔ long-distance resin trade ↔ literature ↔ etiquette ↔ distinction ↔ inheritance of techniques. A smell is not just pleasure: it signals cultivated membership in a world.

The generative question

Could one acquire political standing through trained perceptual literacy that few outsiders possess?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A PMU diplomatic house recognizes particular scent compositions as calls for confidentiality, reconciliation, or witness; the “language” exists only through trained participation.

Reading note: Kōdō is an art of cultivated appreciation; the diplomatic semiotics here are this atlas’s extrapolation.

EcologyKnowledge

A language that can name a smell

Jahai · Malay Peninsula

You cannot deliberate collectively about differences you have not learned to name.

THE ARTOlfaction · vocabulary · environmental knowledge
EVIDENCEStrong linguistic evidence; no formal office claimed
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Linguistic research by Burenhult and Majid shows that Jahai speech includes abstract everyday smell terms, rather than relying primarily on comparisons to source objects. Olfaction also participates in broader relations among people, surroundings and cosmology. This is evidence of different cultural and linguistic attention—not proof of a hereditary court of noses.

The unexpected connections

Vernacular categories ↔ attention ↔ forest knowledge ↔ social learning ↔ cultural value. What a society can debate follows partly from what distinctions it has available.

The generative question

Which ecological changes become politically visible when citizens share a much richer odor vocabulary?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A multi-ecology PMU settlement maintains scent lexicons across languages so that disputes about habitats can actually be argued rather than dismissed as “it smells wrong.”

Reading note: The findings concern Jahai vocabulary and cultural attention to smell; the olfactory legal institution is this atlas’s departure.

EcologyLineages

The sea-reader and the chief

Marshall Islands · ri meto

A navigator knows routes, but the authority to teach them may belong elsewhere.

THE ARTBalance · wave motion · memory · vision
EVIDENCEDocumented embodied navigation and chiefly control
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Marshallese ri meto navigators learned to interpret wave patterns and island-reflected swells through embodied practice. Stick charts were teaching and mnemonic aids, not standard maps carried aboard. An account of contemporary revival identifies the navigator’s title as bestowed by a chief and reports historical chiefly control over navigation knowledge, a fact with consequences for apprenticeship and access.

The unexpected connections

Embodied sea-reading ↔ island relations ↔ trade ↔ chiefly status ↔ knowledge restriction ↔ colonial displacement. The gatekeeper of a route is not necessarily its owner.

The generative question

Can movement between two places be a politically granted privilege rather than a neutral navigational capability?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A PMU passage charter belongs jointly to communities and navigators; route expertise confers leverage but chiefs cannot sell a sea as if it were a parcel.

Reading note: The traditional title and the restriction of knowledge are attested; the maritime council and passage charter are this atlas’s departure.

The watershed that deliberates

Bali · subak water-temple systems

An irrigation system is simultaneously a social system and a temporal one.

THE ARTWater levels · seasonal patterns · cultivation · ritual
EVIDENCEDocumented cooperative water governance
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Bali’s subak associations coordinate water, cropping rhythms and agricultural ecology through canals, weirs, temples and cooperative deliberation. UNESCO documents the system’s long history and its links to the philosophy of Tri Hita Karana. The water-temple network integrates practical allocation decisions and ritual life across linked agricultural communities.

The unexpected connections

Rainfall ↔ irrigation timing ↔ pest ecology ↔ assemblies ↔ temples ↔ built landscape. Water management is not merely engineering; it creates a political scale larger than one farm.

The generative question

What is a legislature whose jurisdiction is a watershed, not a rectangle on a map?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

PMU basin assemblies convene when water conditions change; members owe obligations both upstream and downstream. A canal is at once infrastructure, commons and forum.

Reading note: Subak joins temple calendars to practical collective organisation, with many local actors sharing the work of distributing water.

Knots at the administrative centre

Inka Andes · khipu kamayuq

Writing, calculation and memory need not be built from marks on a flat page.

THE ARTHandwork · color · spatial order · number
EVIDENCEStrong numerical record evidence; other codes debated
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Khipus use spun cords, colors, attachments and knots to encode information. Numerical/accounting uses and roles of trained khipu specialists in state record-keeping are well established. Scholars continue to research other kinds of information stored in these objects. The structured medium supported administration, resource accounting and political organization across enormous distances.

The unexpected connections

Fiber production ↔ numerical expertise ↔ census and tribute ↔ administration ↔ control of labor and resources. The archive is a craft tradition and a state technology simultaneously.

The generative question

Who holds power when the record is legible only through skilled manipulation of its material form?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

In PMU a woven ledger must be read publicly by two schools with different training; the requirement makes reconciliation of records a civic event.

Reading note: Khipus were read by sight and by hand, and researchers are still working out their narrative meanings.

A building that is an ancestor

Aotearoa New Zealand · wharenui / whare whakairo

A council can meet *inside* a record of who the people are.

THE ARTCarving · spatial memory · assembly
EVIDENCEDocumented architecture of genealogy and gathering
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

Carved meeting houses embody and recount whakapapa, the ancestry and history of their people. Government heritage research notes that larger wharenui developed in the nineteenth century as places to meet, deliberate and respond to colonial pressures. The history of Te Hau-ki-Tūranga also reveals that the taking and return of a house can become a major political and legal matter.

The unexpected connections

Carving ↔ genealogy ↔ community meeting ↔ territorial claims ↔ colonial history ↔ restitution. Architecture is more than the container for decisions: its meanings participate in those decisions.

The generative question

If the chamber itself embodies ancestral relationships, what does it mean to move, damage, or confiscate it?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A PMU hall contains legible histories of alliances; changes to its structure require public consent because renovation alters what future assemblies remember.

Reading note: Each Māori community’s carved houses have their own histories, interpretations and protocols.

EconomyLaw

The market examiner as civic guardian

Medieval Islamic cities · muḥtasib

Quality judgment can carry powers that cross markets, public safety and morality.

THE ARTInspection · touch · smell · weights · public observation
EVIDENCEDocumented civic office
Open the historical account and cross-relations ⌄

What is documented

The muḥtasib was an official associated with the ḥisba institution, often tasked with supervising marketplaces, measures, food quality and public conduct. Historical studies describe an office whose boundaries overlapped law, civic regulation, private households and the authority of rulers. Its work mixed embodied inspection with instruments, rules and public enforcement.

The unexpected connections

Market food ↔ fair trade ↔ skilled inspection ↔ public streets ↔ law ↔ sovereign authority. A visit to the bazaar was not just a consumer experience; it implicated city governance.

The generative question

What would an office become if it could challenge fraudulent quality before a formal lawsuit existed?

SPECULATIVE DEPARTURE · PMU

A PMU market witness can certify living craft processes and call a temporary public audit, but cannot judge their own patrons. The office regulates trust, not just prices.

Reading note: The office joined sensory inspection to weights, rules, morals and political jurisdiction, each as weighty as the others.

03 / Cross-relations are the research

Each sense leads somewhere.
Follow the consequences.

Here are six collision paths. Each joins separate historical precedents into a new design question. The arrows are our analysis.

PATH 01 · CONSTITUTION

Wampum × Khipu × Benin brass

crafted record → authorized interpreter → public legitimacy

The state archive need not be neutral paper. Each medium requires trained makers, custodians and readers—and therefore creates conflicts over who may certify truth.

What if every constitution has multiple material editions that no single guild can amend alone?

PATH 02 · DIPLOMACY

Wari brewing × Gèlèdé × Kōdō

hospitality → atmosphere → social correction

Hosting, smell and theatrical criticism occupy distinct domains, yet all change the conditions under which relations continue. Diplomatic power might belong partly to people who shape the encounter.

What if a summit can be called unsuccessful even when the delegates sign, because the hosts refuse its renewal?

PATH 03 · BODIES

稳婆 × tufuga tā tatau × malagan

intimate access → sanctioned transformation → restricted rights

The right to touch, alter, attest or commemorate a body is not interchangeable. Different types of authority may be indispensable and yet excluded from formal political rank.

What if sovereignty must be witnessed by people who can refuse to perform its ceremonies?

PATH 04 · LANDSCAPE

Ri meto × subak × Jahai lexicons

embodied reading → ecological naming → shared regulation

Sea swell, water season and forest odor are different forms of situated attention. Each makes new patterns discernible—and different territories governable.

What if a polity has overlapping jurisdictions of voyage, watershed and living atmosphere?

PATH 05 · CRAFT POWER

Benin guild × Japanese incense master × Sāmoan tufuga

lineage → skill gatekeeping → dependence of elites

Courts may commission expert crafts, but that does not mean courts own the knowledge. Patronage, ceremonial standing, restricted teaching and material supply create reciprocal leverage.

What if a ruler cannot complete an accession without the independent consent of three craft lineages?

PATH 06 · PUBLIC VOICE

Ifá × Gèlèdé × the Chinese midwife

interpretation → privileged speaking → contested legitimacy

The source of authority might be a trained verse-reader, a licensed satirist, or someone admitted into private bodily knowledge. Neither formal education nor office guarantees recognition.

Who can declare a decision incomplete because the wrong voices were excluded?

04 / Five open PMU thought experiments

Where the institutions
cease to look familiar.

Five provocations that open different governance logics, built worlds and tensions, with each practice still free to find its school.

01 · The missing maker at the Covenant ＋

A long-awaited international agreement is ready. Delegates have settled all terms, but the maker entrusted with the treaty’s material form refuses the commission. Do officials have authority to proceed? This draws on the separation between political office and the rights of skilled custodians, not on any specific historical treaty practice.

02 · The feast that counts as a payment ＋

An island receives aid after a flood. Twenty-five years later, its neighbors still regard it as having an unsettled obligation—not because money is owed, but because the reciprocal hospitality that recognizes restored equality has never occurred. It is possible for a ledger to show zero debt while relationships remain unreconciled.

03 · The law of passage ＋

In an archipelago, safe routes are certified jointly by sea readers, coastal communities, and water stewards. No one “owns” the currents. Communities can have overlapping rights to passage, care, warning, rescue and hospitality. A political boundary changes with a shifting channel, though the social obligation endures.

04 · The hall with several memories ＋

A school’s ceremonial hall is reconstructed after a disaster. Stonecarvers, scent cultivators, sound specialists and hereditary record keepers disagree about what must be restored. A visually perfect replacement is rejected because it does not preserve the relationships that made the old hall recognizable.

05 · The necessary but unofficial witness ＋

A legal dispute depends on embodied expertise passed down outside formal academies. The state recognizes the testimony but denies the witnesses any say in how their field is trained. When they collectively withdraw their services, the conflict turns from a legal question into a crisis of institutional legitimacy.

The most generative question: “what does everyone else become unable to do without a sensorial expert?”
