The proposition · three traditions

Three ways of knowing, one warming world

This thread grew out of the Three Tables experiments in the Polymathic Learner, where one problem was put to Greek, Vedic and Chinese mathematicians and each answered in its own native way. Doubling the Square and Squaring the Circle drew out three kinds of answer, and three verbs to carry them.

to boundthe Greek table

Archimedes traps the circle between a polygon inside and a polygon outside. The answer is an interval, proved from both sides.

to balancethe Vedic table

The Śulba cord-makers turn a square into a circle of equal area, and the circle back into the square. The answer is a rule that runs in both directions.

to cut finerthe Chinese table

Liu Hui cuts the polygon finer and finer: 割之弥细，所失弥少. The answer is a procedure that can always be refined.

Here the three tables leave mathematics and sit down before a warming planet. The thread runs in three volumes, from the real science, to the deep past, to a future with all of modern physics in hand.

The rules, loosened

Same three worlds, a much larger toolbox

The three traditions still never meet. This time, though, each one inherits the whole of modern physics. That means thermodynamics and relativity, quantum theory, information and chaos. Each one picks up first the idea that feels like home. Part One sets out the physics on the table. Part Two opens the three laboratories, each with its own wonders. Part Three goes past physics itself.

Every construct below carries a small honesty tag. physics means established science. built small means it has been demonstrated, at least in a laboratory. proposed means it is a serious paper that no one has built. dream means it is past the edge, offered for the wonder of it.

Part OneThe physics on the table GPT

Five extraordinary permissions

The richest inventions come from knowing exactly what physics allows, and exactly where the dream begins. Five permissions sit on every table.

I · Planetary thermodynamics physics
Sunlight in − heat radiated out = energy stored

The planet is warmed by sunlight and cooled by radiating to space. Clouds, greenhouse gases, ice, oceans, plants and circulation all tilt that balance, and even a small lasting tilt adds up. So the great works alter reflection, emission and storage: filters in orbit, roofs that radiate, oceans and lands that move heat.1

II · Chaos, feedback and attractors physics
A tiny push can matter enormously, at the right moment and place

Weather and climate are coupled nonlinear systems. Some nudges amplify, and the same sensitivity sets a horizon on prediction. A civilisation that learns this spends its effort at the moment a system is most responsive, shaping the conditions storms grow from.23

III · Orbital mechanics and optics proposed
At the Sun–Earth L1 point, a movable partial shade

Space sunshades have been studied in earnest. Shading the Sun cools the planet and also reshapes regional rainfall; the chemistry of the oceans needs its own works. In a world with magic the shade becomes sails of programmable shadow, swarms of photonic scales, lattices that redirect chosen wavelengths.45

IV · The open infrared window built small
A surface can pour its heat straight out to the cold of space

Between about 8 and 13 micrometres the air is nearly transparent. A surface that rejects sunlight and glows in that band cools below the air around it; humidity and cloud set how much. Coastlines of ceramic forests, roofs that change emissivity with the seasons.6

V · Chemistry and living systems built smallproposed
Carbon, water, nutrients, ecosystems and climate shape one another

Rock weathering and ocean alkalinity can draw down carbon and are being tested now. Feedbacks and tipping points tie the living world to the physical one, so restoring one is restoring the other.78

Where PMU physics begins

Present-day physics has no way to send waste heat elsewhere through entanglement, steer a monsoon, rewind an ecosystem or draw free energy from the vacuum. The constructs that do those things run on PMU’s own new laws, and each carries its tag.

Laboratory I
The geometers
to bound
ἀεὶ ὁ θεὸς γεωμετρεῖ.“God always does geometry.” · attributed to Plato by Plutarch
I · The physics they’d love physics

A universe made of limits

Hand the Lyceum modern physics and it will skip past the machines and go straight to the limits. Here is a whole science built of no more than.

No heat engine can beat Carnot’s efficiency, fixed by two temperatures and nothing else. Nothing travels faster than light. And Emmy Noether’s theorem of 1918 would make them weep: every symmetry of the laws of nature carries a conserved quantity. If the laws are the same today as tomorrow, energy is conserved. Conservation turns out to be geometry.

Then there is Einstein, who turned gravity itself into the shape of space and time. For a tradition that answered every question with a figure, this is coming home. The cosmos is a proof drawn in four dimensions.

They would not ask what a machine can do. They would ask what no machine can ever do, and then build right up to that line.
II · The wonder physics permits built small

The Window to the Dark

Here is the limit that fascinates the geometers most. Every warm body glows. Earth glows in infrared, and above it, past the thin shell of air, waits the coldest thing there is: deep space, about 2.7 degrees above absolute zero. Heat flows from hot to cold. The whole universe is a heat sink, if only the heat could reach it.

The air mostly blocks the way, with one exception. Between wavelengths of 8 and 13 micrometres the atmosphere is nearly transparent. It is a window, and it happens to sit right where a body at everyday temperatures glows most strongly.

The window · where the air lets the Earth’s heat escape
The green curve is the infrared glow of a surface at about 27 °C. The lavender band marks where the sky is clear to it, between 8 and 13 micrometres. Pour heat through that band and it never comes back.

So the geometers design a surface with two faces of perfection. It reflects nearly all sunlight, like polished Parian marble. It also glows strongly, and only, through the window, so its heat sails straight out to the cold between the stars. Such a surface can sit in full noon sun and grow colder than the air around it.

This is no myth. In 2014, a layered photonic surface on a California rooftop held itself about five degrees below the air temperature in direct sunlight. 2 In the geometers’ world, every roof, road and stoa in the Greek cities is clad in it. The temples gleam whiter than they ever did, and each one quietly pours a little of the world’s heat into the dark.

III · The wonder past the edge proposed dream

Archimedes’ lever, at last

And then the geometers reach for the oldest boast in their tradition. Give me a place to stand, and I will move the earth.

The Sun is slowly brightening. Over hundreds of millions of years it will warm the Earth past anything a window can carry. The only permanent answer is a bound on the Sun’s light, and the only way to set that bound is distance. Move the Earth outward.

In 2001, three astronomers published exactly this as a serious calculation. Send a large body from the outer solar system grazing past the Earth, and it hands over a little of its orbital energy. Then let it swing past Jupiter to pick up that energy again, and send it back. Repeat about a million times over the life of the Sun, and the Earth’s orbit walks outward to stay in the habitable zone. 3

The lever · energy borrowed from Jupiter, lent to the Earth
The messenger body grazes the Earth on its way in, giving the Earth energy, then swings past Jupiter on its way out to take energy back. Each pass moves the Earth’s orbit outward by a hair. Not to scale.

The geometers would love it for its honesty. The first proof is of the bound: how far the Earth must go to stay as cool as it is now. Every throw of the stone has to stay within the bound, close enough to give energy and never close enough to strike. The lever is a sequence of proofs, a million long.

Children of the geometers’ world know a strange festival. Once in a few thousand years, a new star crosses the night sky. It is the messenger, and when it passes the whole world feels, by the width of a hair, a little further from the fire.

Greek construct 01 · optics + orbital mechanics proposeddream

The Diadem of Helios

The engineer of sunlight

At noon the shadow of the harbour tower slips eastward. For two minutes the harbour receives less sunlight than it should.

The first experiment of the Diadem has succeeded. Thirty thousand mirrored petals drift at a gravitational station between the world and its sun. None covers the sun. Together they change the brightness arriving at the planet.

The astronomers are ecstatic. The shipbuilders ask how often the petals need repair. The farmers ask whether the change will alter the winter rains.

A child asks a simpler question: “Who gave them permission to move the sun?”

Sunward of the Earth, near the first balance point, floats a shifting crown of countless mirrored plates: the Parasol of Syracuse from the Three Skies, grown up. Its petals form bands, spiral apertures and translucent mosaics. Through a telescope it looks like a geometric argument drawn around the Sun.

The Diadem eases the incoming light without sealing the sky. Different patterns change the intensity, the season and the colour of the light that arrives. Its astronomers are architects of the Sun’s boundary condition. City-states fund clusters of petals and a federated council votes on the planetary pattern, so a great discovery in optics also gives the Greeks a new politics of shared sunlight.

Real seed orbital sunshade studies, solar sails, photonics and radiative forcing.4
The leap feather-light programmable petals, coordinated by fields, with fine control of the spectrum at enormous scale.
Greek construct 02 · fluid dynamics + nonlinear control physicsdream

The Aeolian Cathedrals

On the island ridges stand immense hollow towers of stone. Their chambers are carved as resonators, and their upper mouths hold blades of shimmering air. The towers change the pressure and temperature relations that decide how winds begin, bend and combine.

Thousands of cathedrals play a planetary fugue of circulation, giving moist winds a road to a thirsty interior, or coaxing a storm away from a vulnerable coast. Their best technicians are part mathematician, part sailor, part musician, and their forecasts are statistical. One cathedral alone does little; a network must learn the rhythm of the whole atmosphere, and that need founds a new kind of academy.

Laboratory II
The ritualists
to balance
पूर्णमदः पूर्णमिदं पूर्णात्पूर्णमुदच्यते । पूर्णस्य पूर्णमादाय पूर्णमेवावशिष्यते ॥“That is whole, this is whole. From the whole, the whole arises. Take the whole from the whole, and the whole remains.” · the Upaniṣadic invocation of wholeness
I · The physics they’d love physics

Nothing is lost

Give the ritualists modern physics and they will go straight to the ledgers. Conservation of energy, of charge, of momentum. A universe that never loses anything, only moves it.

Then, deeper, two ideas that read as if written for them. First: at the level of quantum mechanics, information is never destroyed. Every state can, in principle, be run backwards to where it came from. The laws of physics are a rite that can be performed in either order.

Second, and nearer to the fire: Rolf Landauer showed in 1961 that forgetting has a price. Erasing one bit of information must release at least a tiny, exact quantity of heat. Charles Bennett then showed that a computation done reversibly, keeping every step so it can be undone, need not pay it at all. 4 Heat is what irreversibility costs. Balance is cool, and imbalance burns.

To the ritualists, the warming world is a rite run in one direction only. The cure is to learn to run it back.
II · The wonder physics permits built small proposed at scale

Agni unkindled

Fire is one of chemistry’s simplest rites. Fuel and oxygen go in. Carbon dioxide, water and heat come out. Every plant on Earth already performs it backwards, taking in light, carbon dioxide and water and giving back fuel and oxygen.

The two-way fire · the same equation, read in both directions
Burning runs the upper arc and releases heat. Photosynthesis, and the solar-fuel furnaces of the ritualists, run the lower arc with sunlight. The ledger closes only if both arcs turn.

So the ritualists build unkindling altars. Great mirrored fields focus sunlight onto furnaces that pull carbon dioxide from the open air and run the fire backwards, into fuel for the next fire or into solid carbon. Each furnace is laid out as an altar and keeps its ledger in public: so much carbon kindled this year, so much unkindled.

And the solid carbon becomes brick. The next agnicayana, with its ten thousand eight hundred bricks, one for every moment of the year, is built from carbon taken out of the sky. The altar that once sent offerings up as smoke is now made of the smoke, brought back down.

III · The wonder past the edge dream

The Pratiloma rite

Sanskrit has a word for “with the grain”, anuloma, and one for “against it”, pratiloma, reverse order. The ritualists’ final dream is a pratiloma rite on a civilisational scale: the industrial age performed backwards, step by step, until the ledger reads as it did before the first furnace.

Two hundred years of burning would be unkindled over two hundred years of light. The carbon would not go back as coal, because coal invites the fire again. It would go back as the one form of carbon nobody burns: compressed, in deep seams of the earth, into diamond. Pure carbon, packed as tightly as it will go, stable for longer than any civilisation will last.

Above the diamond seams, the rites themselves change. The ritualists build computers on Bennett’s principle, machines that think reversibly and so barely warm. They are temples of calculation that leave the air as cool as they found it, because they never forget anything.

The priests say the old coal country still glitters. Deep under the fields where the forests have come back lie the seams of an age that was taken back, as bright as anything that age ever made.

IV · More from the ritualists’ laboratory GPT

The ritualists’ heirs include the later analytic schools of South Asia. Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika worked out fine theories of substance, quality, motion and atoms,10 and a tradition can be rigorous and performative at once. One academy studies pressure and the storing of seasonal water, another the smallest grains of matter, a third how the burden of a great working can be shared among many hands and instruments.

Vedic construct 01 · chaos + coupled oscillators physicsdream

The Monsoon Constellation · varṣa-maṇḍala

Across mountains, river basins and coasts rise thousands of circular terraces, each with resonant chambers, rain gauges, seed libraries and instruments that feel cloud electricity, airborne water and the wetness of the soil. The whole network pulses, sometimes in sound, sometimes in colour, sometimes in phases nobody can see.

Its makers learn to tell when the monsoon is nearing one of several possible patterns, and time their workings to guide it toward rains that let rivers, farms and forests recover. Its great breakthrough is polyphony. A rice valley, a millet dryland, an estuary and a forest each want their own rains, so every terrace keeps its own voice, and the song becomes a calculation the public can hear.

Real seed synchronisation in complex systems, atmospheric oscillations, data assimilation.23
The leap controllable phase-coupling between ritual instruments and the atmosphere, at planetary scale.
Vedic construct 02 · chemical thermodynamics + ocean systems built smallproposed at scale

The Samudra-Churning Works

The sea’s half of Agni unkindled. At the ocean’s edge float vast circular mineral gardens, petals of stone above and galleries below, where seawater runs through tuned mineral pathways while carbon, salts, sediment and life are measured without pause. The works store carbon for the long term, in minerals and in the dissolved chemistry of the sea, and their engineers think in centuries.

Poets call it the Churning of the Ocean, after the old story of gods and demons churning the sea for its treasures. Its prestige comes from chemistry and rite together, and its measure of success is the life that gathers around it.

Real seed ocean alkalinity enhancement, carbonate chemistry, enhanced weathering.711
The leap finely governed reactions and permanent storage at a scale no trial has reached.
Vedic construct 03 · ecological networks + adaptive materials physicsdream

The Forest of Many Seasons

A belt of forest a thousand miles long begins to answer the climate like one organism. Its roots move water through capillaries of magic; its leaves turn to the light and the damp; its fungal networks remember fire, disease and drought. Each region keeps its own species and seasons.

Biologists, seed-keepers, hydrologists, storytellers and musicians tend it together. It rebuilds soil, softens the local climate, holds the slopes and keeps the regional rains turning, and ecological functions long gone from the land begin to return. One faction wants a single superforest; another a mosaic of distinct ecologies. The forest thrives by holding both.

Real seed evapotranspiration, ecological feedbacks, restoration.8
The leap a deep shared ecological memory and living hydraulic links over great distances.

I · The physics they’d love physics

The butterfly and the zoom

Give the calculators modern physics and they will fall for two discoveries, both about procedure and scale.

The first comes from weather itself. In 1963, the meteorologist Edward Lorenz boiled the churning of the atmosphere down to three equations and found that their solution never settles and never repeats. Two starting points differing by a hair end up anywhere. Plotted, the path traces a shape like a butterfly’s two wings. It is the original butterfly effect. 5

The second is renormalization. You understand a system by zooming out one scale at a time, asking at each step which details still matter and folding away the rest. It is Liu Hui’s cutting run in reverse: step by step, finer and coarser, until the essential law appears.

II · The wonder physics permits built small weather-scale: proposed

The Four-Ounce Office

The taiji masters had a saying for this before anyone had equations: 四两拨千斤, four ounces deflect a thousand pounds. A force you cannot resist can be turned aside with the right touch at the right moment.

Physics caught up in 1990. Edward Ott, Celso Grebogi and James Yorke showed that a chaotic system can be steered onto a chosen path with only tiny, well-timed nudges, because chaos is exactly the condition in which small pushes have large effects. Within months, it was done in a laboratory. 6

So the calculators found a 四两司, a Four-Ounce Office. Where the Greek geometers shade the Sun and the ritualists unburn the fire, the calculators do something gentler and stranger. They watch the whole atmosphere at the scale of single clouds, through a lattice of sensors as fine as Liu Hui’s last polygon. A planetary board of counting rods, cut until it can be cut no more.

They don’t fight the weather. They wait for the moments when a monsoon or a storm system sits at a fork, when the atmosphere is about to choose between two wings. At those moments they apply the four ounces: a little seeded cloud, a little brightened sea-spray, a little shade at exactly the right place and time. The thousand pounds of weather turn by themselves.

III · The wonder past the edge dream

The sky that answers

Two thousand years earlier, Dong Zhongshu taught that Heaven responds to human conduct, the doctrine of 天人感应. In the calculators’ world, the Four-Ounce Office makes it literally true. The sky does answer the people below, because the people below have built the answering.

But this tradition also keeps a warning older than Dong Zhongshu. In the Zhuangzi, the Emperor of the Centre, Hundun, 混沌, “Chaos,” has no openings in his face. His two friends, wanting to repay his kindness, bore one hole a day so he could see and hear and eat like them. On the seventh day, Hundun dies. 7

So the Office writes its first law into its own charter: touch as little as possible. Its pride is measured in the interventions it did not need to make. Its best year is the year the sky balanced itself and the four ounces stayed in their box. The great calculator’s most refined procedure is to know when not to run.

In the calculators’ world, people still say Heaven answers. They mean it fondly, the way you speak of an old friend who listens well and rarely needs to say anything.

Chinese construct 01 · feedback control + network science physicsdream

The Ten Thousand Valves of Heaven and Earth

The Four-Ounce Office’s hands. Imagine the greatest irrigation network ever built, extended through cloud, soil, river, estuary, upper air, plants and currents of heat. Its nodes are scattered like a constellation over the world; each acts locally, letting in moisture, storing water, shifting a narrow corridor of wind, guarding an aquifer, shading a nursery forest.

No node commands the climate. Local keepers watch, act, compare and adjust: scholars, farmers, salt-workers, fishers, specialists who know the land by practice. The network’s great reformers make one rule central: a report from outside the known categories can change the system itself. The farmer who notices the missing insects is sometimes worth more than the official who reports the target met.

The leap reliable local reach into the large processes of air and life.
Chinese construct 02 · radiative physics + ceramics built smalldream at scale

The Heaven-Breathing Porcelain

Across hot plains and crowded basins rise slender ceramic structures shaped like open flowers, flying eaves and porous ridges. Their glazes reflect the sun and breathe heat upward as infrared; cool air drifts through the streets and orchards, and at dusk the porcelain shimmers as it cools faster than the stone around it. On the coasts they become ceramic archipelagos linked by walkways and canals, no two alike.

It begins in the kilns and soon belongs to everyone: potters, thermal scholars, planners and the watchers of the land. In PMU’s own Straits, Tang Ying carrying porcelain into Sané-Kora is the same idea from another side; see 138 What Artists Built.

Chinese construct 03 · paleoclimate + ecological reconstruction physicsdream

The Orchard of Forgotten Climates

Fossil wood, pollen in old soils, cave deposits and the remembered growth of ancient forests show that the world has lived in many climates. Shen Kuo’s bamboo at Yanzhou was the first clue. The calculators ask whether a damaged region can recover the relations that once sustained it.

The Orchard is a research garden the size of a continent, where soils, seed lines, watersheds and microclimates are patiently rebuilt. Its magic keeps traces of lost climates as clues to possible futures. Its finest keepers learn to grow possibilities, welcoming the creatures that have changed and the ones that have newly arrived.

Real seed paleoclimatology, ecological succession, long environmental archives.814

Three moments of wonder GPT

The day each world discovers wonder

Greek world · The Diadem’s first harvest

The astronomers had promised a fraction of a season’s shade. They delivered a cool summer across the western islands. The olive harvest was saved.

Then a navigator sailed into the assembly carrying a sack of salt. Across the sea, evaporation had changed, and with it the mist that fed the cliffs. The engineers were asked to improve their calculations.

They did. The next year’s Diadem gained a second pattern: a geometry that could be argued over and revised.

Vedic world · The first many-voiced rain

The Monsoon Constellation awoke at dusk, each terrace answering a different landscape. A quiet pulse came from the foothills. The delta replied. The high plateau waited until dawn.

Rain fell first where seeds had just been planted. It passed over one forest gently, and reached the drylands three days later. No single performer could hear the entire composition.

The architects called it a triumph. Its keepers called it a beginning. For the first time, the work had listened to more than the people who designed it.

Chinese world · A report no instrument had asked for

The Heaven-Breathing Porcelain cooled the city. The temperature boards confirmed it. The fountains ran longer into the dry season. The people slept comfortably.

A travelling gardener reported that a certain moth had stopped arriving at the riverbank. Its absence was too small to appear in the great indicators.

The gardeners were invited to alter the instrument’s seasonal schedule. The following spring the moths returned. The engineers added a new line to their manuals: When the world changes its answer, change the question.

The invention worth keeping GPT

The dream has three levels. At the first, a civilisation protects its settlements: it cools a city, turns a flood, stores water, rebuilds soil. At the second, it works on the planetary conditions behind local disasters: heat exchange, incoming sunlight, the circulation of the air, the long cycles of carbon. At the third, it builds the relationships through which a planet can hold open a wide range of flourishing futures.

These civilisations are capable of extraordinary works: orbital architecture like moving jewellery, stone and water instruments that compose a monsoon, cities that radiate their warmth to the stars, forests whose memory spans continents, and whole scholarly traditions transformed by making them. Perhaps the greatest planetary instrument is the one that makes a civilisation better at hearing how its world is changing.

The geometers would find a universe made of limits and build up to them. The ritualists would find a universe that keeps its books and learn to run them backwards. The calculators would find a universe that turns on a feather and learn the restraint to hold it lightly. Three ways of knowing, and modern physics needs every one.

Physics notes Claude

  1. 01 · Limits & symmetryCarnot (1824): no engine between temperatures Thot and Tcold can beat efficiency 1 − Tcold/Thot. Noether (1918): each continuous symmetry of physical law implies a conserved quantity, so time-symmetry gives energy conservation. General relativity (1915) describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime.
  2. 02 · The windowThe atmosphere is largely transparent between 8 and 13 µm, where a body near room temperature emits most strongly. Raman, Fan and colleagues (Nature, 2014) built a multilayer photonic surface that reflects sunlight and emits through this window, cooling to about 5 °C below ambient air in direct sunlight. Deep space sits at about 2.7 K.
  3. 03 · Moving the EarthKorycansky, Laughlin & Adams, “Astronomical engineering: a strategy for modifying planetary orbits” (Astrophysics and Space Science, 2001). Repeated gravity assists move orbital energy from Jupiter to the Earth through an intermediate body. Roughly 10⁶ passages of Kuiper-belt-sized bodies, about 1.5 Earth masses of cumulative flyby mass, would bring the Earth to 1.5 AU over the Sun’s main-sequence life.
  4. 04 · The price of forgettingLandauer (1961): erasing one bit releases at least kT ln 2 of heat. Bennett (1973): computation can in principle be made logically reversible, avoiding that cost. Quantum evolution is unitary, so information is conserved in principle.
  5. 05 · LorenzLorenz, “Deterministic Nonperiodic Flow” (1963): dx/dt = σ(y − x), dy/dt = x(ρ − z) − y, dz/dt = xy − βz, with σ = 10, ρ = 28, β = 8/3, a drastically simplified model of convection. The figure integrates these equations live.
  6. 06 · Controlling chaosOtt, Grebogi & Yorke, “Controlling Chaos” (Physical Review Letters 64, 1990): a chaotic attractor can be converted to a chosen periodic motion with only small time-dependent perturbations of a system parameter. It was demonstrated in a physical system the same year. Renormalization, the method of zooming across scales, earned Kenneth Wilson the 1982 Nobel Prize.
  7. 07 · Texts“God always geometrizes” is attributed to Plato in Plutarch’s Table Talk. The pūrṇam verse opens the Īśa Upaniṣad and appears in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka (5.1.1). 治大国若烹小鲜 is from 《道德经》 ch. 60. 四两拨千斤 comes from the taijiquan classics. Hundun’s death is told in 《庄子·应帝王》.
  8. 08 · MethodThe traditions remain independent. Status tags mark the line between established physics, laboratory demonstration, proposal and dream. Invented institutions (the Four-Ounce Office, the unkindling altars, the Pratiloma rite, the Diadem, the Monsoon Constellation, the Ten Thousand Valves and the rest) are imaginative.
