Relics of the Seven Lineages

Crown treasures, lost instruments, weapons, garments, seals and civic machines from a magical world in which power is mediated through relationship, obligation, place, memory and collective action.

Worldbuilding Document Museum Catalogue Edition Fully Expanded 26 September 2026
Curatorial Premise

Museum object first. Magical object second.

The relics are most convincing when they feel like objects that could sit quietly in a museum vitrine: darkened bronze, rubbed silver, cracked lacquer, silk, garnet, shell, meteorite iron, worm-eaten timber, old repairs, replacement rivets and inscriptions added by later custodians. Their power is quiet.

The greatest relics are treasured because humanity has forgotten how to make objects that establish relationships at their scale.
Material logic

Power has a body

Every artifact has weight, grain, corrosion, brittleness, repair history and craft genealogy. Its materials are part of the magical grammar.

Ritual logic

Power has conditions

A relic may refuse ownership, demand reciprocal obligation, require several wielders, or fail when used to confirm a pre-existing conclusion.

Institutional logic

Power has custody

Crown treasures are embedded in schools, archives, courts, temples, engineering guilds and public trusts. The institution matters as much as the object.

Deep-time logic

The relic may be only a handle

The surviving object may be a key, interface, control plate or broken component of an older planetary infrastructure whose complete form has been forgotten.

Catalogue I

The Seven Crown Relics

I
Meridian Orders / Parallax

The Astrolabe of the Unfixed Heaven

A survey instrument for realities that disagree about their centre.
MaterialsDarkened brass, meteoric iron, silver wire, rock crystal
ScaleApprox. 70 cm diameter
Wielding conditionThe user must declare their frame of reference and limitation
StatusCrown treasure; only three of seven crystal plates survive

Seven nested retes represent seven frames of reference. When calibrated, they reveal mismatches between political, ecological, historical, social and spatial realities.

Its most feared operation is The Removal of North: for a short period, a supposed constant—ownership, hierarchy, border, identity or cause— can be removed from a magical system so that all remaining relationships become visible.

Anyone claiming complete objectivity causes the retes to lock. The central axle fits descriptions of the legendary World Measure, suggesting that the surviving object may once have been a detachable control instrument for a planetary surveying machine.

II
Covenant Roads / Amanah

The Seal That Signs Twice

A device that makes obligation reciprocal.
MaterialsBlackened silver, red carnelian, flawless rock crystal
ScalePalm-sized, double-ended seal
Wielding conditionMutual comprehension and informed consent
StatusConstitutional treasure under three-key custody

The Seal binds each party to the full structure of the obligation. A ruler who guarantees a village's water remembers, irrevocably, that the water belongs to the covenant.

A lender can make repayment enforceable, but exploitation becomes equally impossible: the creditor carries the debtor's suffering as measurable weight.

The crystal clouds when legal language is deliberately designed to obscure obligation. Micro-inscriptions around its core list vanished ports, suggesting that it may once have been a terminal in the legendary Ocean of Trust.

III
Keepers of Ground / Communion

The Vessel That Cannot Be Moved

A ritual bronze containing no liquid, but place itself.
MaterialsCopper, tin, iron, pulverised ceramics, impossible geological trace minerals
ScaleApprox. one metre wide; depth unknown
Wielding conditionThe user must hold it as custodian
StatusImmovable; museum constructed around it

Touching the inner surface reveals successive occupations of the ground beneath: river, forest, settlement, cemetery, battlefield, rail line, tower—held simultaneously.

Its operation, Rooting, can make a threatened place temporarily impossible to erase from collective reality.

Anyone attempting to claim the land for themselves becomes unable to remove their hand from the rim until another person releases them. Seismic readings imply the object continues beneath the surface for kilometres. The visible vessel may be only an exposed node of the Root Below Continents.

IV
Houses of Setting / Recomposition

The First Forme

A printing machine whose type is made of operations.
MaterialsBronze, mercury-black compound, ceramic matrices, lost alloys
ScaleCabinet-sized press with 384 type pieces
Wielding conditionThe cost of every transformation must be locatable
Status71 known characters survive

Divide. Bind. Hollow. Mirror. Harden. Remember. Reverse. Branch. Dissolve. Each piece of type encodes an operation.

A Setter composes a sentence, inks the surface and presses it against matter. For several minutes, matter obeys the syntax. A collapsed bridge may remember its previous geometry; a wall may become porous without ceasing to stand; armour can be recomposed around a body.

Setting cannot create matter. Every transformation produces an account elsewhere. The oldest injunction is therefore: Never print a sentence whose costs you cannot locate. A type slug recovered from the Port That Was Carried Away fits the mechanism perfectly.

V
Weaving Calendars / Reciprocity

The Carpet Upon Which Rain Returns

A woven hydrological model that can negotiate with a watershed.
MaterialsIndigo wool, silk, reed fibre, silver filament, horsehair, human hair, mineral beads
ScaleApprox. 25 metres long
Wielding conditionNo single human may know the complete pattern
StatusLiving crown treasure; re-woven annually

Every knot represents a relationship: rain, aquifer, crop, evaporation, river, migration, storage, population.

Spread correctly during monsoon rites, the Carpet begins altering infiltration, runoff, cloud formation and groundwater recharge until imbalance is gradually corrected. It cannot simply "make rain": increase elsewhere creates an account somewhere else.

At least forty-nine people must operate different regions simultaneously. Nine Rains teaches that the power lies in object + ecology + distributed knowledge + maintenance. Border motifs correspond to descriptions of the lost Planetary Loom.

VI
Singing Lineages / Third Voice

The Lyre of Those Who Were Not Recorded

An instrument that does not reveal truth; it reveals absence.
MaterialsWood, gold, shell, lapis, bitumen, unknown string material
ScaleCeremonial lyre, seven surviving strings
Wielding conditionThe player must not seek confirmation of a predetermined conclusion
StatusOne string lost; notation possibly survives elsewhere

During performances recounting historical events, extra tones emerge: the rhythm of fleeing civilians beneath a victory song, an omitted branch beneath a genealogy, pauses corresponding to questions nobody asked.

The Lyre does not adjudicate a single authoritative account. It makes absence audible.

Museum records identify an eighth string made from an unknown material. The Forbidden Verse recovered from the Port That Was Carried Away may be notation for this missing string.

VII
Ring Assemblies / Commons

The Bell That Cannot Be Rung Alone

A machine for making burdens visible and shareable.
MaterialsLayered bronze, many repair alloys, old timber suspension frame
ScaleMonumental bell with seven ropes
Wielding conditionSeven distinct social roles must act together
StatusPublic crown treasure; extensively repaired

One person can pull a rope. Nothing happens. Two produce a vibration. Seven acting together make the Bell ring.

When it rings, load redistributes: structural stress, magical expenditure, logistics, labour, shortages and other burdens can be spread across a network. It never eliminates cost. Someone still carries it.

Sund law requires the burden-bearers to be publicly named before the Bell is rung. A seemingly insignificant timber peg in its frame is older than the Bell itself. That peg matches the surviving timber fragment from the Port That Was Carried Away. The Bell may simply be the latest apparatus humanity has attached to the true relic.

Catalogue II

Weapons, Armour & Instruments of Conflict

Weapons in this world express the metaphysics of their lineage. Each one imposes a conceptual cost on the one who wields it.

Parallax

The Parallax Helm

A dark iron helm plated with tinned copper, silver wire and garnet oculars. It reveals several plausible interpretations of an opponent's next movement at once. Extended use erodes confidence that any single perception is real.

Amanah

Both Edges of the Promise

A sword that can be drawn only against someone who has violated an oath. If the wielder's own side has broken the same covenant, the sword cuts the wielder first.

Communion

The Cuirass of Home Soil

Hundreds of fired-earth plates made from the defender's homeland. It becomes extraordinarily strong while defending that ground and unbearably heavy during conquest.

Setting

The Recomposition Gauntlet

Allows blade, chain, shield and spear to be recomposed from the same material during combat. Every transformation permanently consumes a small amount of matter.

Reciprocity

The Monsoon Mantle

Thousands of weighted silk tassels shape air, moisture and pressure across a battlefield. It fails when local wells and soils are already exhausted.

Third Voice

The Mask of the Unheard

Causes everyone within hearing to perceive testimony from casualties, prisoners, bystanders and others normally excluded from command decisions.

Commons

The Sevenfold Shield

Seven mediocre shields when separated. When interlocked by seven defenders, impact striking one is distributed across all.

Deep-Time Hypothesis

The Seven Keys of the Port That Was Carried Away

The humblest surviving objects may be the most dangerous: a seal fragment, a type slug, a timber peg, a line of notation, a shard of crystal, a strand of textile, a buried metal fitting.

They may not be relics of the lost port.
They may be seven interfaces removed from the same planetary machine.
Modern interpretation
Seven unrelated antiquities from different magical traditions.
Scholarly suspicion
Repeated dimensional correspondences suggest technical compatibility across institutions.
Forbidden hypothesis
The Seven Lineages may have originated as custodial specialisations around one lost planetary infrastructure.
Unanswered question
Nobody alive remembers what happens when all seven surviving interfaces are brought together.
Real-World Material Anchors

Museum traditions informing the visual language

The relics are grounded in real traditions of metalwork, textile making, instrument construction, reliquary craft, ritual bronze casting and mechanical objects, and grow from them into their own forms.
