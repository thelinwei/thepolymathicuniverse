How a spell is built

Four layers, from the street down

An onlooker sees a fireball. A scholar sees a domain and an operation. A house sees its own grammar. And the caster's body feels the hand: the stance, the unit of power, and the account that comes due.

Effect = Domain × Operation × Grammar × Hand

The first three layers come from the grammar volume: nine domains of relation (Force, Matter, Life, Mind, Space, Information, Pattern, Identity, Law) and eight operations (Perceive, Move, Create, Transform, Bind, Restore, Sever, Exchange). The fourth comes from the Seven Hands: how each house actually leans, what it counts as power, and where the account lands. Grammar tells you what was touched. Hand tells you what it looked like and who paid.

Part one

Seven grammars, with their hands

Each house in one card: the question it asks of the world, how it leans, what it is strong in, what it leaves behind at a scene, and how it fails.

Toa Keng

Parallax and the Survey

“From which description is this impossible?”

The Survey fixes one frame; Parallax holds two. A Toa Keng master does both at once: she snaps a bright line to commit, and keeps a second line faint beside it, in case the first description turns out to be the wrong one.

Power counted in
Decimal places, and frames held at once
The hand
Brass rule, chalk line snapped into the air
Strong domains
Information, Space, Mind, Pattern
Favoured operations
Perceive, Transform, Exchange
Residue at a scene
Chalk dust hanging in still air. Objects nearby cast two faint shadows for about an hour.
How it fails
Frame dissolution: too many valid descriptions, and the caster can no longer act in any of them. At the other extreme, the brass orchid.

Al-Merikani

Amanah

“A relation witnessed is harder to break than a relation merely desired.”

Wards in this house are permissions, never walls. The patterns in the lining specify who may cross, what may pass, and for how long, and they wake only when something is placed in the wearer's keeping.

Power counted in
Keepings held in trust
The hand
Palm flat over the lining, then the double seal
Strong domains
Law, Identity, Space
Favoured operations
Bind, Exchange, Restore
Residue at a scene
A smell of oud and warm iron. A faint double impression on the nearest hard surface, visible under raking light.
How it fails
Covenant debt. The stronger the witnessed relation, the worse its breach; and the old temptation of sealing the unborn.

Balai Pusaka

Communion and the Rooting

“This land remembers what you have forgotten.”

A place is a dense history of relations, and a family that has maintained one can lean on all of it. Balai Pusaka power grows by staying, which makes it the slowest craft to learn and the hardest to move.

Power counted in
Generations on the ground
The hand
Shoes off, the low kuda-kuda, the two-line verse
Strong domains
Life, Identity, Place, Force
Favoured operations
Perceive, Restore, Bind
Residue at a scene
Soil lifted a finger's width, roots turned toward the spot, and a smell of first rain on old earth.
How it fails
False custodianship: claiming a relation nobody maintained. The site itself resists, sometimes violently. And exile, which is disarmament.

Lan Fong

Recomposition and the Setting

“There may be no original spell, only transformations that have not yet admitted their ancestry.”

Anything set once can be set again. Wuxing gives the transitions, the press gives repeatability, and the kongsi gives the ballot, so Lan Fong became the most engineered craft in the city, and the one most often accused of taking without crediting.

Power counted in
Characters one can set
The hand
Cinnabar brush, type in a forme, fu arrays
Strong domains
Matter, Force, Life, Law
Favoured operations
Transform, Create, Move
Residue at a scene
A red stain that will not wash out. Printed signs nearby are missing a character or two, and nobody can remember which.
How it fails
Recomposition without lineage, which turns into crises of authorship; and the character in no dictionary.

Nine Rain

Reciprocity and the Weave

“Nothing happens alone, and every miracle enters an account.”

The Andean half of the house weaves and records; the Maya half chooses the moment. Together they learned to push least by acting when the world is already about to move, and to write down what they borrowed.

Power counted in
Strands held at once
The hand
Arms raised like loom heddles, knots tied in air, the shoulder band
Strong domains
Life, Force, Pattern, Space
Favoured operations
Exchange, Restore, Perceive
Residue at a scene
Condensation beaded in knots on glass. A dry ring where heat was borrowed. Weeks later, a small rain somewhere nobody expected.
How it fails
Feedback: a local success whose debt returns years later somewhere else. The snarl.

Sané-Kora

The Third Voice and the Jeliya

“A missing voice still changes the harmony.”

Deletion does not always erase structure. The jeli hears the empty beat where a voice used to be, holds several accounts at once without collapsing them, and carries the nyama that speech sets loose.

Power counted in
Voices held at once
The hand
The kora's twenty-one strings, the seat, the praise-name
Strong domains
Mind, Information, Identity, Pattern
Favoured operations
Perceive, Restore, Exchange
Residue at a scene
A gap in the room's echo where one voice should return. Anyone who enters afterwards feels a faint dread and a ringing in one ear.
How it fails
Ventriloquism: hearing a trace is not the right to speak for it. And recording, which leaks nyama nobody carries.

Sund

The Commons and the Joining

“Ask not who cast the enchantment. Ask who has been carrying it.”

Ten coupled casters are more than ten casters: the relation among them is itself the operator. Sund castings last because generations inspect, repair and show up, which makes maintenance the house's highest magic.

Power counted in
Hands joined
The hand
The ring, forearms clasped, the work-song, runes cut into timber
Strong domains
Law, Force, Life, Matter
Favoured operations
Exchange, Bind, Restore
Residue at a scene
Matching bruises on the shoulders of several people. Every joint in nearby furniture very slightly loosened.
How it fails
The coerced commons: forced participation and hidden labour produce stable magic while resistance quietly accumulates. And the glued joint.

Part two

The old catalogue, seven ways

Nine classic powers from the gamebooks, each run through all seven houses. The street sees one effect. The houses see sixty-three different spells.

Fire

Force × Create or Transform

Every child can name a fireball. Seven houses would each tell you it is a different event.

Toa KengFocal ProofTwo frames of the same sunlight are made to agree on a single point, and fire exists exactly where both descriptions meet. It is precise enough to light a pinhead and useless as a weapon.
Al-MerikaniThe Lamp in TrustA fire that cannot burn anything it was not entrusted to burn. It passes over the innocent and catches the named. Hearth fires in the merchant quarter have never spread.
Balai PusakaOld FireA place is called back to a fire it remembers. A hearth that has burned for a century lights itself at dusk, and a site of old burning is dangerous to stand on.
Lan FongWuxing CascadeThe city's true fireball. Wood feeds fire, fire makes ash, and every step is optimised. A written 火 draws on the characters of whatever is nearby, so an expert cascade can run for an hour.
Nine RainBorrowed NoonHeat taken from the midday street and paid back at night as a cold snap. Weavers cook by noon light at midnight, and the lane they borrowed from shivers.
Sané-KoraThe Song of the BurningSung fire, which is the memory of a fire. It warms and frightens and lights a room, but consumes nothing unless the singer names whom it is for.
SundThe Forge RingA ring passes warmth hand to hand into one smith's hammer. Forty palms feel warm and one anvil goes white. Sund cooks for festivals this way.

Healing

Life × Restore

The fastest way to start a fight in a hospital corridor is to ask what healing actually repairs.

Toa KengAnatomical ParallaxThe healer sees the body from the frame in which it is still healthy, then leans the patient toward that frame. Brilliant diagnosis, and a famously cold bedside manner.
Al-MerikaniHeld LifeFor one night, a patient's life is placed in a trustee's keeping, and death cannot collect what is under seal. It costs the trustee a year of a hot lining.
Balai PusakaHealing by BelongingBrought home among kin, a body mends faster. The healer calls back semangat with a two-line verse. Some wounds only close on the patient's own ground.
Lan FongMeridian SettingThe body's characters are reset: a broken bone set as type is set. Surgical, fast, and the healer's hands are red to the wrist by evening.
Nine RainSeasonal RepairA fever is moved to a season when the body can afford it, and the cure is borrowed from next year's health. Clinics keep khipu of recovery still owed.
Sané-KoraThe Name Held UpThe healer sings the patient's lineage until the self stops coming apart. Best for delirium, deep coma and minds that are losing their names.
SundThe Carried FeverThe ring takes on the illness, each member a degree of it. Every hospital ward in the city has a Sund rota pinned by the door.

Telepathy

Mind × Perceive or Exchange

Reading a mind turns out to be seven different kinds of intrusion, and the city's privacy law has seven chapters.

Toa KengDouble ExposureTwo viewpoints overlapped without merging. You see the room as she sees it and as you see it, both at once, and the headache lasts a day.
Al-MerikaniWitnessed ThoughtOnly thoughts offered in trust can be read. It is the one form of telepathy admissible in court, because it requires the witness's consent.
Balai PusakaWhat the Room RemembersTelepathy of place. The house tells you what was felt inside it: a quarrel last year, a birth in the back room, the fear from the night of the fire.
Lan FongMarginaliaReading the characters a mind is composing before they are spoken. Editors love it, negotiators fear it, and poets resent it.
Nine RainWeather of MoodsA crowd's feeling read as pressure systems. Weavers forecast riots and festivals with the same instruments.
Sané-KoraPolyphonic ListeningMany minds heard at once, without collapsing them into one voice. Contradiction is kept as part of the signal, which is why Kora mediators settle disputes nobody else can.
SundThe Common ThoughtA ring briefly thinks as one, and reaches a decision in a single breath. It is very efficient and dangerous for dissent, so the house limits it to emergencies.

Teleportation

Space × Move

Everyone in the Straits can tell you how their grandmother got home in the flood of 1962. No two stories match.

Toa KengFrame ShiftThe surveyor redefines which point counts as here. It is exact, and only works between surveyed benchmarks: the brass bolts in every Toa Keng doorstep are waypoints.
Al-MerikaniCaravan RoadsTravel along routes made short by centuries of honest exchange. Hadhramaut to the Coromandel to the Straits is nearly instant; a new route needs a century of fair dealing first.
Balai PusakaGoing HomeYou can always return to your own ground at once, from anywhere. You can never teleport away from it.
Lan FongThe Swallow's WalkBe in two places at once, then let go of one. The migrant's art: leave without severing belonging.
Nine RainCeque LinesSites become nodes and ritual lines become edges. A weaver travels along the lines between shrine, reservoir and terrace, and nowhere else.
Sané-KoraCalled AcrossA jeli sings your name and you arrive where you are being sung. Families use it to bring the living home for funerals.
SundThe RelayYou are handed across a ring like a bucket along a fire line. A thousand people can make a bridge of hands a kilometre long.

Invisibility and illusion

Information × Sever or Transform

Seven ways not to be seen. Several of them are also how the city treats its poor.

Toa KengOut of FrameStand where no measurement lands. The caster lives in the gap between two surveys, and instruments pass over her.
Al-MerikaniThe UnremarkedPeople who hold nothing of yours in trust cannot notice you. The gate-porridge children discovered this by accident, and it took the college a century to admit it.
Balai PusakaBelonging WellThe place hides its own. A kampung child can vanish into her lane in plain sight; a stranger standing beside her cannot.
Lan FongErratumA person's characters are left off the page. Readers skip over them, the way the eye skips a misprint.
Nine RainMist WeaveLiteral fog, woven thick and knotted in place. Old-fashioned, reliable and wet.
Sané-KoraThe UnsungNobody sings you, so nobody hears you. It is the house's darkest art, and the one it most wants to forget.
SundShared ShadowA ring spreads one person's presence across all its members. You see forty people and cannot find the one.

Toa KengLevel GroundThe surveyor declares the air level and walks on a surveyed plane. You can only go where she has drawn the line.
Al-MerikaniThe Monsoon SeatSitting in the trade wind as in a chair. Slow, reliable and strictly seasonal.
Balai PusakaThe Canopy RoadPassed from branch to branch by the trees themselves. Swift inside a forest and useless outside one.

Shapeshifting

Life and Identity × Transform

Each house has a different answer to the question of what a self is, and so a different way of becoming something else.

Toa KengSecond DescriptionYou become yourself as described in another language. The port's translators look a little different in each tongue they speak.
Al-MerikaniBorrowed FormA shape held in trust for its owner. You may wear someone's face only under their seal, and must return it.
Balai PusakaThe Inherited BeastSome pusaka lines carry an animal, and the old stories of the weretiger are about them. The shape comes with the land, and cannot be taken off it.
Lan FongSelf-RecompositionA caster rearranges her own characters. It is dangerous and addictive, and the city has ink-clinics for those who cannot stop.
Nine RainNahualThe co-essence animal born on your day-sign. On that day each year, a weaver can run as it.
Sané-KoraThe Ancestor's MaskA jeli sings herself into an ancestor's shape for exactly the length of the song, and not a bar longer.

Summoning and spirits

Time and probability

Pattern × Perceive or Transform

Nobody in the Straits controls time. Seven houses have learned seven ways to lean on it.

Toa KengError BarsThe spread of likely futures seen as a literal haze around people and things. Surveyors walk through crowds squinting.
Al-MerikaniInsured LuckMisfortune underwritten across a pool of consenting parties. The unlucky are paid out in luck, and premiums are brutal.
Balai PusakaWhat Always Happens HerePlaces have habits. An elder reads a site's recurrences in verse and knows what the lane will do on the night of a full moon.
Lan FongThe Revised EditionA failed moment reset once, as a second edition. Never a third: the ballot forbids it, and so does the world.
Nine RainCalendar MagicActing when the moment is already ripe. It is the heart of the house and the reason Nine Rain rarely pushes.
Sané-KoraThe Epic KnowsStories foretell by pattern. A griot hears history rhyme a verse before it happens.
SundThe Long RotaStability by maintenance. A future made likely because somebody keeps showing up to it, every week, for a hundred years.

The impossible one

Resurrection

Life × Identity × Pattern × Restore

Bringing back the dead means restoring Life, Identity and Pattern at the same time, three deeply coupled relations with enormous resistance. Each house can do about a third of it.

Toa KengCan find the frame in which she is still alive, for one instant. The house keeps a single photograph from that instant.
Al-MerikaniCan keep the dead person's trusts open, so she remains a party to her obligations. Her debts and promises survive her.
Balai PusakaCan make her present at her own ground: a warmth on the step where she used to sit.
Lan FongCan recompose the body perfectly. It opens its eyes and there is no one inside.
Nine RainCan borrow a season of life for her, owed back double.
Sané-KoraCan restore her voice and her name, and she will answer questions.
SundCan carry the whole weight of a life for one night, across a hundred people.

The city has one recorded resurrection, in 1917. It took all seven houses, one night, and a woman nobody will name. The snarled cord Nine Rain keeps in a sealed room is the receipt.

Part three

Powers no rulebook has named

These arise from the houses’ theories rather than the old catalogue. Nobody in a gamebook could have invented them, because they only make sense once you know who pays.

Absence-sight Sané-Kora
Seeing the exact shape of what was erased: the chair nobody sits in, the name missing from a list, the voice that should answer back.
Resistance underwriting Al-Merikani
Insurance for castings. The account of a great working is pledged across a pool of consenting parties, and magical finance is born.
Quorum lift Sund
Superlinear force: a hundred coupled casters lift far more than a hundred times what one can. The first person to prove it mathematically was a shift-school teacher.
Benchmark anchoring Toa Keng
A place made immune to rewriting. Nothing may be cast within a metre of a brass benchmark bolt, which is why the city's vaults and courtrooms are studded with them.
River recall Balai Pusaka
Buried rivers, vanished shorelines and felled forests called back toward how they were. In the Straits, historical ecology is a force.
Unwriting Lan Fong
Taking the characters out of a thing until it was never set. Illegal after 1904, except by shareholder vote.
Debt forecasting Nine Rain
Seeing the future bills of past castings: which storm will return, where, and to whom.
Creole casting Toa Keng
Translating another house's spell into your own grammar, badly at first. It is why Toa Keng leads every mixed team, and why the other houses find it insufferable.
Grief carrying Sund and Sané-Kora
Mourning redistributed. The bereaved sit in the middle of a ring while the chorus sings, and every member takes a little of the grief home.
Honest roads Al-Merikani
Places connected by long fair dealing become physically closer. Cheat on a route and it lengthens by a day.
The stencil copy Sekolah Petang
Any casting in the city can be copied onto a duplicator sheet, badly, for a cent. Weak, universal, and the thing every house most fears.
The drift Rumah Arus
Travel along what is already moving, with no account paid and nobody to blame. The reason Arus was struck off.

Part four

Achar spells

The mixture was never pure, and neither is the magic. These castings need two houses, and each belongs fully to neither.

Measured rain Nine Rain with Toa Keng
The weaver lays the storm down in steps and the surveyor counts each one against a line. First done on the Mole, the night of the King Tide.
The chorus ring Sund with Sané-Kora
A ring that sings while it carries, so the load and the grief move together. Used at every mass funeral since 1962.
Sealed type Lan Fong with Al-Merikani
A contract set in type that can be edited only by consent of every party. The only document in the city that both houses respect.
River recall at the right season Balai Pusaka with Nine Rain
A buried river called back on the day the calendar says it is ready to come. The Kallang ran above ground for a week in 1978.
The creole counter-song Toa Keng with Sané-Kora
A dispel translated into song and a song translated into proof. It unpicks almost anything, and nobody can say whose it is.
The underwritten ring Al-Merikani with Sund
A ring whose account is insured across a pool, so that the members who cannot afford the bruises are covered by those who can. Sund calls this justice; Merikani calls it a product.
Heirloom recomposition Lan Fong with Balai Pusaka
An old keris reset as new steel without losing its tuah. Technically brilliant, and the most bitterly argued case in the waqf and kongsi courts.

Part six · a case

The Stone in the Concord Courtyard

A three-tonne boundary stone, floating one metre up, at dawn, with nobody claiming it.

The stone had marked the edge of the Concord courtyard for a hundred and forty years, and at six in the morning it was floating a metre above its socket, turning very slowly, like a thing thinking. By seven there were seven investigators in the courtyard, one from each house, which was the rule, and none of them agreed.

The Toa Keng inspector photographed it under a single lamp and found two shadows. "Parallax," she said. "Someone redefined the ground." The shadows were very faint, which she wrote down and did not mention.

The Merikani trustee smelled oud at the socket, and ran his thumb along the rim until he found a double impression, old and worn. "It was placed in trust," he said. "It's being held up for someone." Nobody asked for whom, because the answer would take a week of registry work.

The Balai Pusaka elder took off her shoes and stood beside it. The soil in the socket had lifted a finger's width and the roots of the old angsana were all leaning in. "The place wants it to float," she said. "It remembers something." She did not say what.

The Lan Fong proofreader walked the signs around the courtyard and found that the brass plaque on the gate was missing one character from the word boundary. She was very pleased about this until she realised she could not tell which character it had been.

The Nine Rain weaver found knots of condensation on the courtyard glass and went straight to the Loom, where there was no knot for the night at all. That was more alarming than anything else anyone had found.

The Kora listener clapped once, and heard the courtyard's echo come back with one return missing. "Somebody was here who isn't in any account," he said.

The Sund investigator lined up the night staff and compared shoulders. Nobody was bruised. That settled it for her: whatever this was, nobody had carried it. She found that the most troubling fact in the courtyard, and wrote it at the top of her report.

· · ·

It took three days and all seven reports to find the purple smudge on the underside of the stone.

A cleaner on the night shift, a Sekolah Petang graduate who had worked the Concord floors for nineteen years, had found a duplicator sheet in the hall bin. It was a rota from a Sund rehearsal upstairs, with the words of a lifting song typed along the bottom, and someone had doodled a Toa Keng chalk-line in the margin during a boring meeting. She had copied it onto a stencil, the way Petang copies everything, and pressed it to the stone because she was tired of mopping around it. Keep up, the stencil said.

Everyone had been right. The stencil carried a Sund lift with no ring behind it, so there were no bruises and no one had paid. A Toa Keng line in the margin gave it a second frame and two faint shadows. The courtyard had been a Merikani gift in 1886, sealed in trust to the city, and the place had been waiting a long time for someone to ask it for anything. The missing echo was the cleaner, who appears in no house's account. And the account for the lift, the whole three tonnes of it, had gone nowhere yet. It was simply owed.

The Board met in emergency session and could not score it. The stone is still floating. The cleaner has been offered places at four houses, and has accepted none, because the shift pays better.

The Nine Rain weaver still checks the Loom every morning, waiting to see where the bill for the stone will land.
