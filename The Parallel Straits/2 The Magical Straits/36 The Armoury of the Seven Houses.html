Seven weapons, seven grammars. None of them works the same way twice.

Before you pick one up

How a magical item works in the Straits

An enchanted sword is a casting that has been given a handle. The item holds the relation so the wielder doesn't have to, and that makes it powerful. It also means the account still has to land somewhere, and every item in this armoury says where.

Every item is built on one house's grammar and responds to that house's way of bonding. A Toa Keng rapier in Sund hands is a sharp piece of brass, and a Sund axe swung by one person alone is a piece of lumber.

Rarity

Uncommon items are working tools, carried by thousands. Rare items are made by masters and passed down. Legendary artifacts exist once, are mostly lost, and appear in stories more often than in hands. Each house has one.

RapierRare

Datum

Space × Bind

after the brass surveying instruments of the Meridian Orders

A thin brass blade that leaves a white chalk line hanging in the air wherever it cuts. The line is a boundary. Whatever is on one side of it cannot cross back while the line holds, so a fencer with Datum fights by drawing the room into pieces.

To use it
A snap of the wrist at the end of each cut.
The account
Numb fingers on the sword hand, and a taste of brass. Every line drawn is logged in the house register that evening.
Lore
Toa Keng duellists are the only fencers in the Straits who win by making the arena smaller.

Parallax Mail

Information × Transform

after the ring-mail of the Met's arms and armour galleries

Mail of tiny brass rings, each set at a slightly different angle. Attackers see the wearer from the wrong frame. Blows land where the wearer appears to be, a hand's width from where she actually is, and the wearer casts two faint shadows.

To use it
Always active while worn.
The account
The wearer sees double for an hour after taking it off.
Lore
Toa Keng inspectors wear it under their jackets on raids. Criminals in the Straits know to aim between the two shadows.

The Levelling Staff

Space × Bind

after the graduated staffs of every surveyor

A tall wooden staff painted in graduated divisions. Planted upright, it throws a coordinate grid over the ground around it. Fire will not cross a grid-line, water stops at the mark, and any arrow, stone or falling beam that enters the grid can be arrested at an exact coordinate.

To use it
Plant the staff and call out the reading.
The account
Cold that climbs from the hands to the elbows. Staff-bearers wear gloves in the tropics.
Lore
Every Toa Keng fire marshal carries one. It is the most common magical item in the Straits, and the one that has saved the most lives.

Spectacles of the Fourth Place

Information × Perceive

after the brass-rimmed instruments of the Hall of Standards

Round lenses in a brass frame. Through them, every person is surrounded by a faint haze of likely futures, and anyone telling a lie casts a visible shadow behind them.

To use it
Wear them and look.
The account
Eyes too bright for a day afterwards, and a headache for anyone who wears them longer than an hour.
Lore
Given to a Fellow of the Fourth Place on appointment. They are not supposed to be worn at dinner, and are.

The Rule of Alexandria

Law × Transform

after the lost standards of the Library of All Frames

A brass rule engraved with every unit of every nation that ever measured. Laid against a thing, it makes the thing exactly that measure. A wall can be made a hand's width tall; a flood can be made a cup of water; a year can be made a day.

To use it
Lay it flat against the object and read one line aloud.
The account
It is the weapon of Redefinition, the house's forbidden art. The 1931 gallon was shrunk with a copy. Anyone who uses it loses the ability to estimate anything by eye, for life.
Lore
Lost with the Library. Toa Keng insists it holds only a copy, and that the copy is weaker. Nobody outside the house believes the second half of that.

A plain ring set with a double seal. It commands old contracts, the ones held so long they have begun to behave like persons: the jinn of the Straits. An old deed will open for it; an ancient curse will kneel.

Tombak Penjunjung

Place × Bind

after the tombak spears of the Malay royal regalia

A long spear with a pattern-welded blade. Driven into the ground, it roots: fibres grow from its butt into the earth, and its bearer cannot be moved from that spot. Thrown, it always returns to its home ground, however far that is.

Taming Sari

Life × Identity × Bind

after the legend of Hang Tuah's keris

The keris of legend, said to make its bearer invulnerable. In the Straits, it is understood as the perfect Rooting: its bearer becomes so completely part of their ground that nothing from outside it can harm them.

To use it
Worn, never drawn in anger. It has been drawn only four times in five hundred years.
The account
The bearer becomes invulnerable only on their own ground, and can never again leave it. Some tellings say the keris returns to the water when no worthy bearer remains.
Lore
A keris claimed as Taming Sari sits in royal regalia on the peninsula. The Balai Pusaka says, politely, that the real one went into the river.

Pattern × Sever

after the obsidian-edged war clubs of the Mexica, known from the chronicles

A wooden club edged with twenty obsidian blades, one for each day-sign. On any given day only one blade is sharp; the others are dull as wood. On the wielder's own day-sign, every blade is sharp at once.

To use it
Strike on the right day.
The account
On the opposite day-sign, the club is heavy as a log and cannot be lifted.
Lore
Nine Rain's duellists always challenge by calendar. Its opponents always check.

The Brine-Quilted Coat

Force × Exchange

after the quilted cotton armour of the Mexica, soaked in brine

A thick quilted cotton coat, soaked in salt water. Blows and heat are not stopped but exchanged: they spread through the weave into the water and are carried off as evaporation. The wetter the weather, the stronger the coat.

The Sling of Illapa

Force × Life × Exchange

after the Andean thunder god, who breaks the sky's water jar with a sling-stone

A sling of braided alpaca wool and a single polished stone. In the old story, the thunder god's sister keeps the rain in a jar, and he breaks it with his sling to make the rain fall. The Sling of Illapa breaks droughts the same way: one stone, one cracked sky.

Skíðblaðnir

Space × Matter × Bind

after the ship of Norse myth that folds like cloth

The ship of the old stories, built so that it could carry every god and still be folded small enough to fit in a pouch, and that always had a fair wind. In the Straits it is the perfect demountable: a whole ship that comes apart into its joints and goes back together without a single loss.

Beyond the houses

Crossings and strays

A few items belong to no single grammar. Some were made by crossers and answer to two houses at once. Two were made by the schools the houses struck off, and answer to anyone.

The Two-Script Blade

Toa Keng × Al-Merikani

A sabre whose blade carries an inscription in two scripts. It cuts only what both descriptions agree should be cut, which makes it the only weapon both houses allow into a treaty hall.

The Sword That Says I

Lan Fong × Sané-Kora

A Namewright's blade, set in type and sung over until it could speak. It remembers being made, argues with its bearer and refuses unjust orders. It took one character from its maker's memory: the character for her own name.

The Monsoon Standard

Nine Rain × Sund

A banner woven by a whole district. Carried at the front of a ring, it shares a season's weather across everyone who can see it: heat spread thin, rain given to those who need it.

The Rooted Grid

Balai Pusaka × Toa Keng

A levelling staff cut from a kampung's oldest tree. It holds the old shoreline, not the present one. Walk with it and the old coast appears around you.

The Stencil Blade

Sekolah Petang

A purple-inked stencil knife. Pressed against any enchanted weapon, it copies the enchantment onto whatever it next touches, badly, for a single strike. The most common weapon in any shift-school girl's pencil case.

The Drift Oar

Rumah Arus

A plain wooden oar. It holds no relation of its own; it only finds the current already moving and follows it. A boat steered by it pays no account, and cannot be tracked by any grammar.
