I · Method

1. What This Atlas Assumes

No School has one uncontested home world. Each is a compatibility family: a human institution formed from several causal ancestries that proved mutually translatable inside the Straits.

many ancestral axioms → translation → one School-family

Every entry therefore separates five things that school mythology tends to collapse:

Claimed origin

What the School officially teaches about its ancestry.

Candidate realities

Living, extinct or imagined worlds whose physics match the School-family.

Material evidence

Relics, architecture, language, ritual and bodily adaptation patterns.

Comparative evidence

What neighbouring worlds say about the School—and whether they recognise it as kin.

Institutional edit

What the Straits changed to make the foreign law survivable and teachable.

Buried rupture

The discovery capable of destabilising the School's identity.

The atlas treats ancestry as causal. A person inherits a School through language, practice, ritual, bodily calibration, attention and social formation.

V · The Politics of Homecoming

5. Ancestry and Home Are Different Things

When a School discovers a causal kinship with a living adjacent world, students may imagine return. The locals may disagree.

“You are our descendants.”

Some worlds embrace Straits Schools as diasporic relatives and build political or cultural ties around ancestry.

“You are not us.”

Others regard 1,000 years of divergence as more important than shared causal ancestry.

“You stole our law.”

Some accuse the Straits of appropriating techniques, patenting translations and returning as experts.

“We learned from you.”

A supposed ancestral world may turn out to have been changed by early Straits travellers instead.

Causal ancestry therefore produces politics.

VI · Extinct Homes

6. Some Schools Cannot Go Home

At least one School should eventually discover that no living source-world survives.

Its grammar exists nowhere else.

The School is a new home, built in the Straits.

It is the last known habitat of an extinct physics.

extinct world-law → surviving human practice
The School is not preserving heritage.

It is keeping a dead universe alive inside human bodies.

Conversely, another adjacent world may survive while its Straits School vanished centuries ago. On the far side, a receiving station could have been maintained for generations by people waiting for emigrants who never returned.

VII · Phylogeny of Realities

7. Worlds May Have Ancestry Too

Comparative axiomatics eventually notices that world-laws form families.

ancestral axiom A₀ → descendant axioms A₁, A₂, A₃

A small change in one foundational relation may produce a stable descendant world. Worlds can therefore share ancestry much as biological species do.

Seam probability rises with shared axiomatic ancestry

But the diagram may not be a clean tree. Seams allow causal structures to move between realities. Laws can hybridise.

The pluriverse may have reticulate evolution: a network of realities exchanging pieces of physics.

The Straits may itself be an ancient hybrid zone—an axiomatic ecotone where several branches overlapped before recorded human history.

VIII · The Great Academic Dispute

8. Why Exactly Seven?

Primordialist

There were seven foundational migrations from seven deep world-families.

Historical

Dozens of traditions arrived; institutional consolidation reduced them to seven.

Structural

Seven is the minimum basis required to span the Straits' translation space.

Unbound

Seven is political: an administrative settlement that rendered other grammars illegible.

The most interesting answer is that all four may contain truth.

historical multiplicity → seven stable compatibility classes → political Schools

Seven is simultaneously discovered, produced and institutionalised.

IX · The Question That Refuses Closure

9. Is There an Eighth Ancestral Family?

The obvious temptation is to hunt for an eighth world-family.

That may be exactly the wrong question.

If the Seven are stable grammars, then the Eighth may not be another grammar at all.

Eighth ≠ G₈

It may be the transformation between grammars.

Eighth ≈ how Gᵢ changes while becoming legible to Gⱼ

In mathematical language, scholars might compare it to a connection between basis vectors.

The Seven stabilise realities. The Eighth describes what happens when realities enter relation and both are changed.

If so, the Eighth cannot have a home world in the conventional sense.

Its “home” is wherever incompatible laws meet.

That may make the Straits itself the closest thing it has to an ancestral reality.

The Seven brought their worlds to the harbour.

The harbour taught them how to change in one another's presence.
