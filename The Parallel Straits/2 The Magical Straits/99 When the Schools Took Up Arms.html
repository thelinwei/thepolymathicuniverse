I

The world before the emergency

Three settled assumptions turn education, artefacts and maintenance into the central infrastructure of magical power.

Universal foundation

Everyone can learn basic magic

Magic resembles literacy. Nearly everyone can perform zero-Exertion workings; fewer sustain advanced recipes, great ensembles or original composition. Capacity grows with education, practice, institutions and infrastructure.

Artefact economy

Every artefact must be renewed

Early artefacts hold for a month; later ones for six months, one year or three. School industry arms create, sell and maintain prestigious artefacts without possessing a monopoly.

Constructive inheritance

The schools taught life before war

Their recipes heal bodies, manage water, build cities, navigate, perform, preserve memory and share burdens. Destructive magic emerges by changing consent, release, target, scale and where the cost lands.

The crisis does not reveal a hidden book of evil spells. It reveals that every sufficiently deep constructive knowledge contains a way to wound the thing it knows how to sustain.

II

From the sports hall to civil defence

The schools preserve differences of age, skill and judgement even as the world becomes dangerous.

Children · foundation

Fall, stop, release

Balance, safe landing, breath, signalling, ending a working cleanly and recognising when another child is frightened.

Young teens · sport

Consent and counterplay

Controlled duels, padded instruments, stopping rules, capture without injury and immediate care after a match.

Senior students · defence

Hold the school

Evacuation, first aid, boundary watches, communications, counter-artefact drills and protecting younger pupils.

Graduates · field work

Protect the city

Mobile rings, refugee corridors, infrastructure defence, capture teams and coordinated campaign workings.

Teachers · composition

Change the conditions

Design new strategic recipes, accept responsibility for their release and decide which knowledge must never become routine.

III

The Empty Desks

Veyr at the Narrows is a working placeholder: a once-respected exchange port captured by an arsenal faction that believes advanced education is a Straits cartel.

In legal records
The Veyr Exchange Murders

The factual name used in trials, treaties and archives.

Inside the schools
The Empty Desks

The memorial name: chairs remain unfilled for one full academic year.

In military histories
The Closed Harbour War

The invasions, mobilisation and campaign that follow.

Invitation

An exchange delegation enters Veyr

Teachers and older students arrive to teach healing, construction, water management, artefact renewal and civic defence. Their materials are constructive because that is what their schools possess and value.

Detention

Inspection becomes imprisonment

Passports and artefacts are seized. Teachers are separated from students. Veyr demands harbour-breaking, crop denial, cognitive invasion and the removal of another population’s access to advanced magic.

Murder

The delegation refuses

Torture produces no secret arsenal because no such arsenal exists. Some students offer harmless fragments to protect others. Most of the delegation is killed; a few juniors survive long enough to carry witness.

Inversion

Veyr mutilates constructive knowledge

Its weapon-makers remove releases, force unwilling Blends, reverse Threads and transfer burden to prisoners, occupied settlements and captured land. Healing becomes interruption; survey becomes collapse; irrigation becomes denial.

Expansion

The first neighbouring port falls

Its artefacts fail before renewal, senior practitioners cannot coordinate and its ground begins rejecting large workings. Veyr enters with few direct battlefield deaths and calls the conquest humane.

V

The Closed Harbour campaign

The coalition tries to remove Veyr’s capacity for conquest rather than its population. The distinction proves morally necessary and practically incomplete.

Phase 01

Open the roads out

Evacuation routes, floating wards and hidden coastways move civilians, prisoners and captured students beyond the coming workings.

Phase 02

Close the fleet in

The Harbour Without Grip separates military hulls from traction, current and coordinated wind.

Phase 03

Empty the arsenal

Stored workings leave weapons and enter witnessed keepings. Renewal houses and military Pattern Cords are severed.

Phase 04

Interrupt command

The Unfinished Command and Quiet Crown prevent the arsenal faction from coordinating a counterattack.

Phase 05

Make war unrecognisable

The Port Forgets War collapses the institutional relationships that allow the city to behave as a single military machine.

Last resort

Embitter the ground

When hidden military workings continue, the schools deny the arsenal district every great magical sink. They win—and leave the wound that makes regulation unavoidable.

No city dependent on magical medicine, cooling, water and structural artefacts can be “disarmed” without affecting civilians. The war establishes that nonlethal intention does not guarantee harmless consequence.

VI

The Falling Due

The second catastrophe begins with legitimate artefact renewal and ends when future magical capacity has been promised several times over.

Useful beginning

Renewal contracts

Homes and cities pay specialists to renew artefacts safely.

Financial invention

Burden Notes

Future renewal payments and sink capacity become tradable.

The boom

Long artefact lives

Three-year artefacts spread while their true future costs disappear from sight.

The fraud

Unwitting sinks

Villages, children and ecologies are pledged through buried clauses.

The trigger

One sink refuses

A weak monsoon leaves the first guarantor unable to carry its promised load.

Contagion

Everything falls due

Automatic transfers saturate every connected sink and artefacts fail together.

What people see

Cold lamps burn hot. Season-holders ripen and rot at once. Buildings remember older shapes. Citizens wake exhausted by contracts they never read. Rivers frost and forests lose colour.

What the ledgers show

The same wetland guarantees five cities. Renewal firms insure one another. Rating houses are paid by issuers. New artefact sales have funded old renewals. Nobody knows where the final burden ends.

VIII

The Straits Articles

Rules for conflict and commerce grow from the same discovery: power lies in deciding who sees a cost, who consents to it and who must carry it.

Protected learning

Exchange teachers, students, clinics, archives and knowledge houses cannot be detained, coerced or targeted.

No forced composition

Compelled magical research, torture for recipes and workings powered through prisoners are crimes against the whole Concord.

Civilian continuance

Medicine, water, food, shelter and essential artefact renewal remain protected during conflict.

The mind returns

No working may permanently alter will, identity, memory or magical capacity. Temporary interruption requires a named release.

The ground survives

No method may be expected to cause widespread, lasting magical or ecological barrenness.

Cost follows consent

No person, community or ecology may be pledged as a sink without specific, informed and revocable agreement.

Every artefact declares itself

Maker, recipe family, renewal date, expected burden, sink and failure behaviour must remain visible.

No anonymous burden

Bundled obligations must remain traceable to their artefacts and final sinks through every resale.

Divided judgement

Manufacturers cannot secretly rate, guarantee and insure the same artefacts they sell.

Many keys for great weapons

Legendary destructive recipes remain in multi-school custody and cannot be deployed through one grammar alone.

Emergency suspension

Independent inspectors may freeze burden transfers and require open ledgers during systemic stress.

The Straits hosts; the world governs

Headquarters remain in the Straits, while non-Straits members hold the majority and leadership rotates among regions.

IX

Working canon ledger

A clear separation between what the conversation has accepted and what this chapter has invented to make it visible.

Accepted framework

  • Exchange teachers and students are murdered after refusing to produce destructive magic
  • The foreign port steals constructive spellbooks and weaponises them
  • The schools mobilise martial curricula and research strategic magic
  • The coalition targets infrastructure, leadership, magical access and land while avoiding killing where possible
  • A separate crash transfers magical cost to unwitting victims and environments
  • Straits networks stabilise both crises and host the resulting global order

Strong proposals here

  • Veyr at the Narrows as the aggressor port
  • The Empty Desks and Closed Harbour War as layered names
  • Constructive recipes become weapons through altered consent, release and sinks
  • The Port Forgets War, Empty Arsenal, Unfinished Command and Bitter Ground
  • The Long Account and Burden Notes as the crash machinery
  • The World Holds One Night as the stabilising global working

Still open

  • Dates and relationship to the Silesian Breaking
  • Final identity, cultural genealogy and internal politics of Veyr
  • Which spell cards survive into present-day curricula
  • Whether the Bitter Ground fully heals
  • How many leaders fail to recover from the Quiet Crown
  • Whether the Straits Articles become justice, soft empire—or both
Historical precedent shelf

Structural precedents include the coerced movement of conquered artisans to Timurid Samarqand; the Manhattan Project’s coordination of universities, industry, military institutions and a vast labour force; modern humanitarian protections for civilians, medical systems, water, cultural knowledge and the environment; the opaque securitisation chains implicated in the 2008 financial crisis; and cross-border contagion during the 1997 Asian financial crisis.
