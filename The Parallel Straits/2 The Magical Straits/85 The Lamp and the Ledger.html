Part One · The recipe

[The Recipe]

(a spell is a dish already perfected: know it, hold the rank, bring what it needs, and cook)

I
Thread I

A spell is a recipe

Known spells simply work. Skill shows in the finish.

A spell is a recipe that someone has already perfected. Casting it is cooking it: if you hold the rank the recipe asks for and bring its expendables, it works as written. An instrument, if you carry one, makes it go further. You pay its cost and it happens. No dice. Skill shows in the finish: a master’s version is fuller, longer, lovelier. Dice come out only for the dishes no one can be sure of: things made to last forever, workings of great reach or duration, and recipes that have never been cooked before.

和羹 · The harmonised broth

Yanzi, in the Zuo Zhuan, explained harmony as a cook makes a broth: water and fire, vinegar, sauce, salt and plum, each balancing the others until the taste is whole. Harmony is difference brought into balance; sameness is water added to water. A PMU spell is built the same way.

Four parts, none above another

调 Keynote · the one relation the spell changes. 和 Blend · what joins with it and makes it stronger. 解 Release · what lets it end cleanly, so nothing rebounds. 引 Thread · what carries it to where it must go.

II
Thread II

Casting a known spell

Five steps, and no dice.

  1. Know the recipe

    You learned it from your house’s songbook, or someone taught you.

  2. Hold the rank

    Your rank must be at least the spell’s rank.

  3. Have what it needs

    Its expendables, which are used up. Above street size, also its keys. An instrument is welcome and never required: it amplifies the working.

  4. Cook it

    It happens as written. Mark its cost on your Exertion track.

  5. Show your skill

    For each rank you hold above the spell’s rank, add one flourish: a little wider, a little longer, a little lovelier, or a small second grace.

Making a caster

Gift is the ceiling; training is the climb; rank is the road; skill is the craft.

  1. Who can cast

    Everyone can work magic. Everyday workings are open to all, the way reading is, and the rungs above are reached by training. Each person’s Gift, from 0 to 5, is how far that training can take them: the ceiling their capacities can be trained up to. Gift is partly inherited, sometimes awakened late (near Doors, after a fever, at the death of a teacher), and never bought. Training raises your capacities toward your Gift; it cannot pass it. Your rank can rise to twice your Gift, and at least to 1. Instruments help a spell along but never raise a Gift.

    GiftHow commonWhat it allows
    0EveryoneEveryday workings, once taught: like reading and writing
    1–2One in five in the first years; three in five todayA working life of Real and Bold spells: the healer, the surveyor, the weaver
    3–4One in twenty in the first years; about one in four todayAdept and master work: Grand and Mighty spells, with keys
    5About one in a thousandGrand masters and composers; the only people who can lead legendary workings
    NarrowMany dyslexic, autistic and differently wired peopleA Gift that runs in one domain or operation only, often at 4 or 5 within it. Once thought non-magical; found through the Seven Doors
  2. Three capacities

    Each starts at 0 and is raised by training, up to your Gift.

    CapacityWhat it isIn play
    感律 · SensePerceiving the stratum: the grain of a place, a working under way, a flaw in a spellLets you read a working before casting; Reach for readings
    触律 · ReachTouching and shifting a relationThe core of your dice when a roll is needed
    承抗 · BearingCarrying what a spell costsEach point adds 2 Exertion boxes
  3. Eight attributes

    Each starts at 2, with 6 points to add, none above 4 at the start: Strength, Coordination, Endurance, Reason, Awareness, Resolve, Presence, Empathy. They matter for everything that is not magic, and for bearing magic’s cost.

  4. Skills

    12 points, none above 3. The eight Operation skills are the crafts of magic: Perceive, Move, Create, Transform, Bind, Restore, Sever and Exchange. Ordinary skills include Medicine, Navigation, Craft, Lore, Teaching, Persuasion and Listening.

  5. Rank and skill are different things

    Rank is how far you have come in your house’s grammar, and it decides the largest spells you may cast. Skill is what you are good at. Two Rank 5 casters may cast spells of the same size, but a healer with Restore 4 and an engineer with Bind 4 cast very different spells well, and roll very different dice when a roll is needed.

  6. Derived tracks

    Work these out from the sheet.

    Recovery1 + half Endurance per hour of rest; a night’s sleep clears most of the track

  1. Preparing expendables

    Expendables are prepared by the house’s own methods: a day’s work and a little Exertion for common ones, a season and rare materials for great ones. No dice; only time, care and supply.

  2. Instruments

    Instruments amplify, and never decide whether a spell works. Their rules are in the next thread.

  3. Substituting

    Missing an expendable? A caster may improvise with something close. Then the dish is uncertain: roll, as for a great working.

V·b
Thread V·b

Instruments

Preferred, never required. Each one adds steps.

Magic lives in the caster, and expendables are what the recipe needs. An instrument is what the caster prefers: a carabiner, a brush, a ruler, a ring, a leaf, a flask of rain, a voice. It makes a known spell reach further, last longer, cost less or land more beautifully.

  1. Never required

    Every known spell works bare-handed, exactly as written, if you hold the rank and bring its expendables. An instrument adds steps on top of that.

  2. One in hand

    A caster draws on one instrument per working. In a ring, each member may draw on their own.

  3. Grades give steps
    GradeStepsWho carries one
    Bare handsNone. Masters are the exception: see below.Everyone, always
    School issueOne step in one bonusEvery student, from the first day
    PersonalTwo steps, in one or two bonusesJourneymen and up; attuned to one caster
    MasterworkThree steps, in any mixAdepts and masters; named after its maker
    LegendaryThree steps, plus its own property; lifts one size; stands in for one keyA few in each house, all registered (see 94 The Instrument Hall)
  4. Six bonuses
    BonusOne step gives
    Reach 及远Double the range, up to the edge of the spell’s size
    Hold 持久Double the duration, up to the edge of the spell’s size
    Focus 专注One more Reach die, when the working falls within the instrument’s affinity
    Ease 省力One less Exertion, never below half the spell’s cost
    Grace 精妙One more flourish
    Chorus 合鸣Your ring counts two more people toward its keys, and lends one more die
  5. Size is the edge

    Reach and Hold stretch a spell within its size and never past it; a wider or longer working is a different recipe. Only a legendary instrument crosses the edge: it lets its wielder cast one size above their rank, and it can stand in for one key.

  6. Attunement

    A personal or finer instrument takes a season of use to attune. In anyone else’s hands it gives half its steps, rounded down. School issue works for anyone, which is why schools hand it out freely.

  7. Spent instruments

    Nine Rain’s spit and water and a Sri Temenggong leaf are used up in the casting. They still count at their grade, and a fresh one is always the strongest.

  8. Masters and bare hands

    At Master rank (7) a bare hand counts as a school-issue instrument; at Grand Master (9), as a personal one. Many masters carry nothing for this reason, and collect anyway: owning an instrument does nothing until it is attuned, and a collector attunes one a season.

  9. Instruments and the Gift

    Instruments amplify what a caster can do. They never raise a Gift, and never let a caster hold a rank they have not earned, beyond the one size a legendary instrument lends.

Medicines

Each house heals with its own pharmacy.

Every house brews medicine for the body: Lan Fang’s 丹药, Sund’s cod-liver oil, Sri Temenggong’s jamu, Nine Rain’s cinchona bark. A dose clears Exertion, restores Health, or helps a healing spell along.

  1. Taken, not cast with

    A medicine works on the body that takes it. It is neither an expendable, which a recipe uses up, nor an instrument, which amplifies a caster.

  2. Three grades
    GradeA dose givesMade by
    CommonClears 2 Exertion boxes, or restores 1 HealthAny trained hand in the house’s kitchen
    FineClears 4 Exertion boxes, or restores 2 HealthA journeyman physician or brewer
    GreatClears the whole Exertion track, or restores half of HealthA master, over a season, from rare materials
  3. One dose an hour

    A body takes one dose an hour; a second waits its turn.

  4. Medicine and healing spells together

    A patient who has taken the right medicine heals twice as fast under a healing spell, and the healer gains one flourish.

  5. Preparing medicine

    Medicines are prepared like expendables: time, care and supply, no dice. Each house brews its own, and every market sells the common grades.

Ranks, keys and what spells cost

Basic skill is assumed once you meet a recipe’s rank. Greater dishes need keys.

  1. Rising in rank

    Rank grows through three kinds of work, as in any craft: performing (casting your house’s spells well, in real need), teaching (passing a spell to an apprentice who then casts it), and composing (bringing a new spell through its trials). Each rank asks for a little of all three.

  2. What spells cost

    Cost doubles with each size, so a spell’s cost is set by the size it was composed for.

    Longer or wider than the recipe? That is a different recipe, composed for a larger size.

  3. Strain

    Spells mark their cost on Exertion; a night’s sleep clears most of it. Above your boxes, cost spills into Health. A caster with Endurance 4 and Bearing 2 has 20 boxes: a handful of Bold spells a day, or one Grand spell that nearly empties them.

Part Six · When the dice come out

[When the Dice Come Out]

(dishes no one can be sure of: the permanent, the great, the improvised and the new)

VII
Thread VII

Making, great workings, improvising and opposition

Most cooking needs no dice. These dishes do.

  1. Four occasions

    Roll only when making something that lasts forever (an artifact, a permanent setting), when a working is great in range or duration (Mighty and above, or held beyond a season), when composing a new spell or improvising with a missing expendable, and when someone actively opposes a spell.

  2. The roll

    The dice are ordinary six-sided dice. Reach = your 触律 + your skill in the spell’s Operation + your instrument’s Focus steps (a legendary instrument always adds 2) + any dice lent by people joined in the working. For readings, use 感律 in place of 触律. World = dice by size, from the table; one fewer when the spell runs with the world’s grain, two more against it, and one more for every ten people joined. Roll both. Pair high against high: each World die matched by one of your dice showing an equal or higher number is held.

    SizeWorld diceExamples
    Small1A light without fire, a warmed hand
    Real2A bone, a calmed horse
    Bold3A house, a field
    Grand5A harbour wall, a village
    Mighty7A city, a coast
    Legendary10A nation’s monsoon
    Mythic15A continent’s summer
    World-making20A Door
  3. Reading it

    All held: it is made as intended, and spare sixes become flourishes. Half or more: it is made with a flaw the game master names. Fewer: it burns. The expendables are lost, and a great working leaves a Scar on the place.

  4. Opposition

    A known spell needs no roll until someone resists it. Then the resister’s dice join the World: another caster adds their 触律 + Sever or Bind + instrument; a ward adds dice equal to its size; an ordinary person resisting a spell on their own mind or body adds their Resolve. The caster rolls as above. The spell’s listed cost is paid either way.

Composing a new spell

The real cost of magic is invention.

  1. Composing a new spell

    Composing is research, like a new medicine or a new alloy: materials, conditions and trials until the recipe is stable. Each phase takes a season of work, its own expendables, and a roll.

    PhaseQuestionSize of the rollIf it burns
    I · First tasteDoes it sound at all?BoldExpendables lost
    II · SafetyDoes it end cleanly, without rebound?GrandA small mishap: a minor Scar
    III · ScaleDoes it hold at its full size?The spell’s own sizeA real mishap: the game master decides
    WrittenInto the house’s songbook—From now on, anyone of the right rank can cook it without dice
  2. Why 1918

    Before 1918, every working was an improvisation, and almost all of them burned. At the Monsoon Convocation, seven houses brought their kitchens together and, for the first time, a spell passed all three phases. It was written down. Everything after that was cooking from a recipe.

Part Seven · The bill

[The Bill]

(where the cost of a spell can go when one body is not enough)

IX
Thread IX

Paying for the meal, and the ensemble law

Usually your own body and a good night’s sleep; for great spells, many shoulders.

  1. The ensemble law

    Shared magic is the most efficient magic there is. A spell’s cost is divided among everyone joined in it, and each person pays one extra Exertion for the joining itself. Forty people turning the monsoon pay about two each. One person could never pay thirty-two.

  2. Why it is so

    This is a property of the stratum itself: relations held by many hands hold more lightly than relations held by one. It is why PMU is a world of rings, lags, tings, guilds and compacts, why a lone caster, however gifted, stays small, and why the great workings need keys rather than giants.

  3. What it means for daily life

    A Rank 2 healer can knit a bone in an hour, eight or ten times a day. By the present, bone-setting is as ordinary as dentistry, and every town has its healers’ ring. Labour, medicine and building were all changed by the Lamp Years; the world is richer and slower to break, which is part of why it keeps its peace.
