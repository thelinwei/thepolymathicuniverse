The Unwritten Circle

The Revisionist does not stand alone. Around the project of the Second Concordance gathers a coalition of historians, engineers, survivors, mourners and former guardians—each convinced that actuality has forfeited some part of its moral privilege.

I · The Founder

1. The Revisionist

They begin not as a destroyer of the Concordat, but as one of its most respected interpreters.

Their original scholarship asks a conservative question: why did the Concordat work? They study incomplete systems, competing magical grammars, and the ways civilisations prevent one epistemology from becoming sovereign.

“The genius of the Concordat lies not in reconciliation, but in disciplined incompleteness.”

The first controlled Hinge changes everything. A cracked bowl and an intact bowl remain simultaneously valid for three seconds. In the relation between those states grows a line of moss belonging to neither.

$$R(A,B)=C,\qquad C\notin\{A,B\}$$

The discovery proves that the Hinge does not merely reveal unrealised alternatives. Relations between alternatives can generate genuinely new states.

The Unwritten Archive

Further work produces coherent alternative histories—“drafts.” One such draft contains a magical people erased from official history. Their civilisation has language, ritual, architecture, humour, kinship and memory. In the protagonists' world, the Concordat foreclosed the conditions under which they could survive.

$$\text{stability}=\text{preservation}+\text{suppression}$$

The Revisionist reports this upward. Senior authorities already know enough to confirm the underlying history. Their justification is blunt: the alternative was unacceptable.

“Who gave us the right to convert necessity into innocence?”

After years of comparative work, the Revisionist reaches the proposition that defines the movement:

$$\boxed{\text{actuality is evidence of occurrence, not evidence of legitimacy}}$$

The Doctrine of Minimum Revision

Fearing that Revisionism could become another Clear project, the Revisionist formulates a restraint: never replace a world, never optimise globally, never import an entire alternative history. Change only the smallest relation required to reopen a possibility artificially foreclosed.

$$\min \|\Delta R\|$$

Early interventions appear miraculous: a lost estuarine relation restores extinct ecologies; a suppressed magical dialect becomes legible again; relics begin responding to forgotten names.

The Revisionist's deepest restraint: they are willing to disappear if a more just world requires it. Their project is not self-preservation.
II · The Inner Circle

2. Six People Around the Revisionist

The inner circle should not agree on what Revisionism means. Their disagreements make the movement alive.

A
The Archivist

The Historian of the Eighth

A scholar from an erased or absorbed lineage who spent decades finding absences in official school histories. They do not care primarily about alternate worlds. They care that the current world has lied about its own construction.

“Before we revise anything, name what was taken.”

They insist on restitution, provenance, recovered names and public memory. They are the moral brake of the circle.

B
The Engineer

The Intercessor Cartographer

Formerly employed by a major magical infrastructure body, this person maps transitions between grammars. They think in interfaces, conversion losses and reversible operations.

$$G_a\xrightarrow{T_{ab}}G_b\xrightarrow{T_{bc}}G_c$$

They are the one who makes Revisionism operational. Without them, the movement is philosophy. With them, it becomes infrastructure.

C
The Mother

The Woman Whose Child Exists Elsewhere

She once had a child who died young—or perhaps was never born because of a historical intervention. In one coherent draft, the child lives. She has spoken to the child through a Hinge.

“Do not tell me he is hypothetical. He asked whether I still sing badly.”

She makes Revisionism emotionally unbearable. Every abstract rule about non-intervention now has a face.

D
The Defector

The Former Conservator

A senior safety official who helped classify early Hinge events and suppress public knowledge. They join after discovering containment policy has long included destruction of records, forced separation of mixed practitioners and interventions into family lineages.

“The state is not against Revisionism. It merely insists on being the only authorised revisionist.”
E
The Survivor

One of the Previous Seven

Older now. Partially damaged by an earlier Concordance event. They can perceive more than one relational settlement at once, but with memory loss, sensory dislocation and periods in which they cannot determine which version of a person they are speaking to.

“You are not the first seven. You are only the first seven they failed to separate in time.”
F
The Successor

The Counterfactualist

The youngest major thinker in the circle. Brilliant, disciplined, and initially the Revisionist's most rigorous student.

“If we can measure the harms preserved by actuality, why should actuality retain default priority?”

This person becomes more dangerous than the Revisionist precisely because they remove the founder's sentimental attachment to the present.

III · The Radicalisation

3. The Counterfactualist

The dangerous figure is not less ethical than the Revisionist. They are more consistent.

The Revisionist still carries one unspoken assumption: the current world possesses a weak presumption of continuity. The Counterfactualist challenges this.

$$W_0=\text{actual world}$$

Why should \(W_0\) receive privileged weight merely because it happened?

They construct a comparative measure across drafts—not a crude “best world” score, but unresolved harm, erased agency, coerced closure, inherited magical debt and foreclosed cultural possibility.

$$\mathcal{L}(W)=\alpha H_{\text{coercion}}+\beta H_{\text{erasure}}+\gamma H_{\text{externalised cost}}+\delta H_{\text{foreclosed possibility}}$$

The Revisionist is alarmed. The formula resembles a new road to the Clear.

“You taught me that actuality is not legitimacy. Why do you keep treating it as a tie-breaker?”

Weighted actuality

The Counterfactualist proposes that the present receives weight—but not absolute precedence.

$$\text{Claim}(W_0)=w_0,\qquad \text{Claim}(W_i)=w_i$$

If another settlement preserves substantially more agency, repairs historical erasure and reduces displaced cost, they argue that reality may be ethically obliged to migrate toward it.

$$\text{Revision}\rightarrow\text{Migration}$$
“Move reality toward a less unjust basin.”

The continuity argument

If identity is relational, the Counterfactualist argues that changing relationships does not necessarily destroy the person. Perhaps sufficient continuity preserves identity.

$$P_t\sim P_{t+1}\quad\text{if relational continuity exceeds threshold }\theta$$

This is existentially terrifying. A person might be “preserved” while childhood, language, family structure, grief, citizenship and memory are progressively replaced.

The Counterfactualist calls this healing. Others call it erasure.

The Unbound ally with some branches but reject others. Intercessors sell Hinge technology to all sides. Conservators infiltrate the movement. Continuists fracture between reform and refounding.

Revisionism stops being an ideology and becomes a civilisational condition.

V · The Seven Enter the Movement

5. The Seven and the Unwritten Circle

The Revisionist becomes the first senior figure who treats the seven protagonists as people rather than assets.

“You owe none of us your permeability.”

This immediately complicates allegiance. The institutions that raised them want access to their abilities. The Revisionist advises them to walk away.

TKI ↔ Counterfactualist

Intellectual attraction. Both can hold multiple valid frames. The conflict is whether multiplicity requires eventual selection.

Al-Merican ↔ Former Conservator

A confrontation over authority: when does obligation survive the legitimacy of the institution that issued it?

Temenggong ↔ Archivist

Both understand that places remember what institutions delete. They may recover suppressed geographies together.

Lam Keng ↔ Engineer

They become obsessed with the mechanics of cross-grammar transformation—possibly too obsessed.

Nine Rains ↔ Revisionist

The protagonist keeps asking whether every repair simply redistributes cost into another draft.

Kora ↔ Mother

Kora can perceive the shape of unrealised lives, but must decide whether perceptibility makes them claimants on actuality.

Sund ↔ Survivor

The Survivor forces Sund to confront a burden no fair distribution can solve: remaining permeable for everyone else's benefit.

VI · The Break

6. When the Founder and Successor Divide

The decisive split comes when the Counterfactualist identifies a convergence point: one historical decision from which enormous quantities of measurable suffering descend.

$$\Delta R_{\text{upstream}}\Rightarrow\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta R_k$$

The Counterfactualist argues that the Doctrine of Minimum Revision logically requires upstream intervention. One small change can prevent millions of downstream harms.

Counterfactualist: “You taught us to change the smallest relation.”

Revisionist: “Small is not the same as early.”

The Revisionist wants to reopen the founding settlement so suppressed traditions can be recognised in the present. The Counterfactualist wants to reopen it so reality can migrate toward a better historical branch.

$$\text{recognition}\neq\text{substitution}$$
Revisionist: “A world can be unfinished without being a draft.”

Counterfactualist: “That is what every bad draft says before someone edits it.”

The conflict is no longer preservation versus change, but two forms of change.

Relational repair

Let unrealised worlds indict and transform the actual without replacing its inhabitants.

Relational migration

Move actuality toward a less harmful settlement, preserving continuity where possible.

The dramatic inversion: the original “antagonist” becomes an uneasy ally, while the true danger emerges from the most rigorous extension of their own idea.

The next threshold

The Counterfactualist needs one thing for large-scale migration: a stable seven-way Hinge.

$$H^{(7)}=R(G_1,G_2,\dots,G_7)$$

Not seven masters. Not seven relics. The seven protagonists.

They are not the heirs to the Seven.

They are the first viable interface between the Seven.
