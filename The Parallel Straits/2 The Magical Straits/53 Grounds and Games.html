Grounds

Your seven definitions, read together, describe a building.

Physics sits at the bottom holding up chemistry, which holds up biology. Mathematics floats free above, the arts stand to one side, PE stays with the body, and design moves through the gaps doing repair. It is a beautiful account, and it is one particular account: the modern reductive stack, with physics as the ground.

In the Straits, that stack belongs to Toa Keng. It is also the stack of the Settled, the world where one account won. Readers of the novel will recognise it as their own school timetable, which is exactly why it can be made strange.

Each house has a different ground. Put a different discipline at the bottom and every subject above it changes meaning: physics becomes chemistry’s slowest reactions, or the universe’s ledger, or what things say when struck. Your list also has a gap that one house would notice immediately. There is no discipline of place.

Seven grounds

What each house stands on

The first term in each stack is the ground; the rest follow in the order the house believes they rest on one another.

Toa Keng

Ground: Physics, as frames and invariants

PhysicsMathematicsChemistryBiologyPEArtsDesign

Your stack, with one change of emphasis. Physics is the study of what stays the same when every frame changes. Everything else is that question asked at a smaller or warmer scale.

Knowing where your body is from outside it. The limit is the edge of your own vantage.

Children measure their own reach and pulse in the first week, as instruments. Art class is perspective drawing. Biology treats every organism as an observer with its own frame.

Al-Merikani

Ground: Mathematics, as axioms held in trust

MathematicsPhysicsChemistryBiologyPEDesignArts

An axiom is a statement everyone agrees to hold without proof. That makes it a trust. Mathematics is the house’s ground because it is the purest case of shared keeping: a whole civilisation standing on promises nobody can verify.

The body is an entrusted instrument. Limits are set by oath, and a training covenant can be broken.

Axioms are introduced as vows, with the class agreeing to hold them. Chemistry begins with the bond: two atoms sharing what neither owns. Conservation laws are taught as the universe keeping its books.

Balai Pusaka

Ground: Geography, the discipline of place

GeographyBiologyChemistryPhysicsPEArtsMathematics

The discipline missing from the list. For Balai Pusaka every law is local weather: physics is what this slope, this tide and this flood do; chemistry is this soil and this salt. Mathematics sits at the top, the most portable and therefore the most suspect.

The terrain sets the body’s limit. Walking the bounds teaches you what the ground will let you do.

Measurement uses the place’s own units: paces, fathoms, harvests. The species of one place are studied together as one body.

Lan Fong

Ground: Chemistry, as transformation

ChemistryPhysicsBiologyArtsPEMathematicsDesign

Everything is a reaction at some speed. Physics is chemistry’s slowest reactions; biology is chemistry that remembers its own recipe; art is the transformation of the maker by the making.

Refining: the body cultivated through stages. The limit is the next stage you have not reached.

Every science class is held at a stove. Mathematics is taught as combinatorics, the grammar of how many things can be made from these parts.

Nine Rain

Ground: Biology, as life returning across generations

BiologyChemistryPhysicsMathematicsPEArtsDesign

Life is the model of return: seasons, heredity, succession. Chemistry is metabolism, the body’s ledger of exchange. Physics is the study of the returns of non-living things: orbits, tides, weather.

Breath and recovery. The limit is how fast you return to yourself.

Mathematics begins with cycles and periods. Every biology lesson traces an organism across three generations.

Sané-Kora

Ground: the Arts, as witness

ArtsMathematicsPhysicsBiologyChemistryPEDesign

Your definition of the arts, as what reaches beyond cognition, is the ground here. Every science is a genre of witness. Physics is acoustics: what things say when struck. Mathematics is rhythm, rest and zero.

Dance. The limit is the silence between beats.

Biology asks what each species cannot say. Chemistry is taught through the tuning of bells and drums, where a change in the metal changes the voice.

Sund

Ground: PE, as coordination of many bodies

PEBiologyMathematicsPhysicsChemistryArtsDesign

Your definition of PE ends with “collectively”. Sund begins there. The crew is the unit of the body, and every other discipline studies how many parts move as one.

What the crew can carry that no one could carry alone.

Biology is colonies, flocks and mycelia. Physics is load paths through a structure. Chemistry starts with catalysis, reactions that need a partner.

The unowned discipline

Design

Look at the last column. No house puts design at its ground, and five houses put it at the very top of their stack, as the thing everything else eventually serves. Your own definition explains why: design is the mediation of everything in between, for better outcomes for all. That is the Straits’ native law, the capacity to keep unlike systems open to one another. It belongs to the harbour, and no single ship can own it.

So the Concordat reserves it. Design is the one subject no house may teach in its own schools. It is taught only in crossing hours, in studios run by the Concordat, and every design review is held with seven chairs, one for a critic from each house. A project passes when it survives all seven questions: from where, on whose behalf, where it belongs, what else it could become, what returns, who is missing, and who will carry it.

The seven-chair crit is the Concordat in miniature. Students who are good at it are good at the Hinge. It is also where the misfits first recognise one another: each of them is the student who keeps answering the critic from a different house.

Part two

The Concord Games

会通

Once every seven years, on the anniversary of the Sevenfold Harbour, the houses meet in open competition. The Games began as a truce, like the one that let Greek cities send athletes to Olympia through wartime, and they still open with one: for the seven days of the Games, no house may act against another.

There are three leagues, echoing the three games of the Mongolian Naadam: Body, Making and Axiom. Each has events with strict parameters. The strictness is the point. When the rules are fixed and identical for all, the only thing that varies is the ground each house stands on.

The charter

  1. The load budgetEvery competitor receives a fixed allowance of load for each event, recorded by Concordat clerks. Casting beyond it disqualifies. The budget is small on purpose: the Games reward understanding over force.
  2. The clean billWhatever load a competitor incurs, they must bear themselves. Exporting load to anyone else, anywhere, is the gravest offence in the Games.
  3. No home groundNo event is held on a site where one house’s grammar comes easily. Venues are chosen by lot from neutral ground, and the harbour itself is the only permanent venue.
  4. Mixed judgingEvery event is judged by three houses, never including the favourite’s. Each judge scores by their own house’s question, and the three scores are published separately.
  5. The crossing memberEvery team event requires one member from another house. The rule was meant as a gesture. It has decided more finals than any other.
  6. The Grey and the ClearNo practice of either pole may be used in competition. Dissolving a rival’s work and forcing a rival into one’s own frame are equally banned.

The Carry负

Parameters
Seven bearers carry one granite block across the harbour bridge. The block may not touch the ground. Load budget: almost none.
Favourite
Sund, nearly always.
How houses play
Sund crews move as one body, breathing together. Nine Rain teams time their steps to the tide’s swell under the bridge. Toa Keng once won by recalculating the block’s centre of mass from seven frames at once.
Famous moment
A Lan Fong team recomposed the block into a long stretcher shape mid-bridge. The rule now reads that the block’s form must be unchanged.

The Stillness定

Parameters
Competitors sit in a tidal pool as the tide rises. The last to move wins. No casting permitted.
Favourite
Balai Pusaka.
How houses play
Balai Pusaka athletes ask the pool’s permission and seem to become part of it. Sané-Kora athletes listen to the water and move only in its silences, which the judges cannot see.
Famous moment
A Balai Pusaka elder once sat through two full tides. Crabs had moved into her lap.

Parameters

Favourite

How houses play
Nine Rain divers treat the breath as a return and descend only as far as they can come back from. Al-Merikani divers go in pairs, each holding the other’s life in trust.

The Dragon Boats龙舟

Parameters
Mixed crews of twenty-two paddlers, one drummer and one steerer race the length of the harbour. Every boat must carry at least one paddler from every house.
Favourite
Whichever crew can make seven rhythms into one.
How houses play
The drummer is almost always Sané-Kora. The steerer is often Toa Keng. The winners are the crews whose Sund paddlers stop trying to lead.
Famous moment
The Games’ most-loved event, and the only one where houses cheer for mixed boats. The dragon’s head on each boat is a compression of the monsoon, and old sailors still say the harbour watches.

Invention under constraint. Every competitor receives identical materials, identical time, and the same load budget.

How houses play
Lan Fong throws, cuts and rejoins until the bowl carries every earlier form inside it. Balai Pusaka digs a pinch of harbour mud into the clay so the bowl belongs somewhere.

A Sané-Kora potter won with a bowl whose walls were barely there, quoting the Daodejing to the judges: the use of a vessel is in its emptiness. The Lan Fong team appealed and lost.

The crown event通

The Translation

Parameters
Pairs from two different houses stand at a Seam to an adjacent world. Each receives that world’s law. Together they must hold one relation that works under both the Straits’ law and the foreign one. The relation must stand for 384 seconds, one full reading of the changes.
Judged by
A registrar of the Straits and a representative of the adjacent world, who must agree.
Favourite
None. No pair has won it twice.
Why it is the crown
It is the only event in which design is the whole of the discipline: mediation between laws, for an outcome both worlds can live with. The winners receive no medal. Their names are carved on the harbour wall, beside the Concordat.

The closing

On the last evening, every competitor from every house builds one structure together on the harbour, from the day’s leftover materials. It is not scored. It is taken down at dawn. It is the part of the Games that spectators remember longest.

Scandals

Load dopingThe Games’ oldest scandal and its worst. An athlete casts far beyond the budget and quietly exports the load. The clerks’ books balance. Somewhere in the lower estates, a burden roll grows. The last great case was traced to Basin Seven, and the families there have never received a medal’s worth of anything.
The single scoreEvery generation someone proposes one universal scoring system for all events, so results can be compared across houses. It is always defeated. Its proposers are always sincere, and always Clear in everything but name.
The favoured venueA drawn lot that happened to fall on a Balai Pusaka keeping-ground. The house withdrew from every event held there. Its elders said the place would not let them compete on it fairly.
The crossing memberA dominant team recruited its crossing member and then had them sit out every race. The rule was amended: the crossing member must be the steerer, the drummer, or the one who finishes.

The novel

The seventh team

Deep in the charter, from the first Games, is a clause nobody has used in forty years. A team with no home house may enter if all its members were forwarded to the Concordat registry as unclassifiable. The team must be seven.

The misfits enter under that clause. They have no colours, so they compete in grey, which half the crowd reads as an insult and the other half as a threat. They are terrible at the Carry, because nobody leads. They are thrown out of the Bowl for making a vessel that holds water only when all three judges look at it at once. They come last in the Ledger, and the villagers vote for them anyway.

They have entered for one reason. The Translation is held at a Seam, the only lawful chance in seven years to stand at one openly. And the Survivor, from the last seven, has told them that the Seam chosen this year is the one beneath Exit H.

At the dragon boats, the Sund boy notices the load clerks’ books balancing too well. His mother’s cough has been worse all week.
