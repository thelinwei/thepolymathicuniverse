How Power Grows

The grain, the gift, and the debt.

Some masters grow strong enough to hold a falling mountain. Some can hear the instant when the mountain is ready to fall elsewhere. Others devise a way to make a mountain into a bridge. And one famous sword asks someone far away to carry the weight.

I · The premise

Power is not one number.

The world is full of gifted pugilists, inventors, disciplined cultivators, reckless prodigies and people who have simply been handed formidable instruments. They can all be powerful, but not for the same reason.

Some endure greater strain. Some perceive resistance with impossible sensitivity. Some invent a better way to shape a spell. Some make other people, buildings, rivers and machines participate. Some borrow or redirect the price—sometimes openly, sometimes without anybody knowing.

A spectacular effect does not reveal how it was achieved. The question is not only how powerful? but how was this power made possible?

ResistanceHow hard reality is to change by this particular route. Skill, situation and design can genuinely lower it.
ExertionWhat the practitioner expends in the act. Capacity, recovery, finesse and instruments affect how it feels.
DebtA cost or consequence deferred, stored or carried elsewhere. Its existence and ownership require investigation.

Working distinction: Low Exertion is not proof of high efficiency. But neither is it proof that a hidden victim exists. Some workings are simply better designed; others are paid for in advance; some have concealed debts.

II · The intent

Let the conflict open the imagination.

PMU need not begin with harmony. It can begin with spectacular duels, jealous masters, dazzling inventions, institutional rivalries, betrayals, wars and the temptation to win at any price. These are engaging ways to enter the world.

But the drama has somewhere to go. A duel can disclose that mastery is not the same as domination. A triumph can expose its hidden price. A rivalry can accidentally produce a better technique. A magical weapon can be celebrated and questioned.

We are not declaring that the world has become kinder. We are giving characters the chance to imagine—and perhaps build—different ways of being.

The hook

Make it thrilling

Flying steel, kinetic grappling, impossible timing, moving coastlines, grand assemblies and charismatic enemies.

The engine

Make it intelligible

Every wonder has a discoverable route through matter, force, life, perception, tools, people and accumulated knowledge.

The horizon

Make alternatives imaginable

Not every master must conquer. Not every artifact must exploit. Cooperation and genuine ingenuity can be spectacular too.

Five routes to greatness

These are overlapping journeys, not character classes. A single grandmaster may travel several of them.

01 / POWER

The Vessel

Make a body capable of bearing and shaping more.

Training, adaptation, breath, resilience.

A fighter stands beneath a collapsing gate and holds it until the last child passes.

02 / POWER

The Listener

Find where reality already wants to move.

Perception, sensitivity to grain, timing and pattern.

One fingertip redirects a falling wall at the precise moment its weight begins to turn.

03 / POWER

The Architect

Invent a cheaper or entirely new route to an effect.

Recombination, instruments, engineering and art.

A sword unfolds into bridges, wings and a thousand needles without inventing a gram of steel.

04 / POWER

The Conductor

Make many actions become a phenomenon no one could produce alone.

Coordination, rhythm, shared instruments, trust.

A fleet turns like one creature; a city finds its own heartbeat.

05 / POWER

The Creditor

Control when, where and by whom a burden is paid.

Storage, contracts, substitution, postponement, inheritance.

A sword seems weightless until someone discovers whose knees buckle when it is drawn.

Innate gift may affect the starting point, but training, mentorship, perception, invention, collective effort and historical discoveries can alter the ceiling. No single route is the proper route for everyone.

III · The spells

From a small trick to a legend.

Each school offers one street-level discovery, one duel technique, one masterwork and one grand form. The spell is described first as something a person sees; its underlying mechanism and four-part recipe are there to explore afterward.

These lineages are examples of how someone might grow in power. A masterwork need not be a giant version of the first trick: it may be a better idea born from it.

The conflict is an experiment

Three encounters that might change the rules.

Duel I

The Steel Bird and the Listening Hand

A Lan Fang sword breaks into thirty glittering pieces. Sund’s master grabs the bridge railing and sends a chill racing into the metal. The sword freezes into one brittle shape—until its wielder quietly changes the joints rather than the steel.

What it opens: Neither opponent is stronger in every dimension; the encounter discovers a new relation between metallurgy and recomposition.

Duel II

The Flood and the Hour

Nine Rains sends a ribbon of water around a tower to unseat an intruder. Al Merikani’s defender has already stored a single step for the moment the water reaches the stairs. But the river returns along a second path neither predicted.

What it opens: Re-used force challenges a perfect prediction. The encounter creates a fresh technique: listening to the consequences of your own deferred spell.

Duel III

The Weightless Sword

A renowned champion wins every bout with a blade that never tires them. After one spectacular victory, a young medic notices dozens of exhausted spectators. The champion must decide whether to fight the approaching army without the sword.

What it opens: A moral conflict grows from the spell’s mechanism. The story does not need a villain in the first scene.

IV · Caveats and concerns

Questions worth keeping alive.

These are places to investigate after the creative exploration, not reasons to stop imagining.

Genuine efficiency must be possible

If every clever spell secretly burdens someone, everyday magic becomes grim. Some discoveries really should reduce total resistance through better physics, alignment or design.

Avoid a single morality of power

The Vessel need not be brutish, the Listener virtuous, or the Creditor villainous. Consent, concealment, desperation and responsibility matter more than labels.

Costs remain provisional

We have not established a universal conversion between resistance, Exertion, magical storage, delayed consequences and environmental change. The spells suggest phenomena to test.

Scale is not the same as skill

A millimetre-precise cantrip might require genius. A huge ritual might require a large workforce, large infrastructure and ordinary proficiency.

Keep shared access and plural strengths

Every school may explore every domain and operation. Their curricula and discoveries produce ease, repertoire and surprise—not monopolies on fire, healing, navigation or combat.

Artifacts need discoverable histories

A hidden cost should have a traceable mechanism, not exist only as a last-minute moral twist. Equally, a perfectly efficient instrument should remain a legitimate possibility.

People and ecosystems are not free resources

Grand spells reshape systems with their own futures. Consequences can be meaningful without turning every environmental use into punishment.

Source drafts are not canon

The Lamp and the Ledger, Seven Trades, Book 2 notes and our recent spell atlases are exploratory records. New rules deserve testing before we fix them in the world.

V · The next refinements

What to test after the wonder.

A world becomes coherent not by closing ideas early, but by investigating the questions its most compelling inventions create.

Draw a power-growth sheet for one practitioner

Separate cultivated capacity, sensitivity to grain, repertoire, personal discoveries, coordination and the use of artifacts. Let two legendary characters reach greatness by different routes.

Test one spell by five routes

Have five masters achieve the same visible effect—one through endurance, one through alignment, one through design, one through a collective, one through transferred cost. Compare consequences.

Give artifacts visible provenance

Record inventor, original purpose, stored input, present user, beneficiaries, possible debt holders and verification methods. Make the reveal a mystery characters can solve.

Stage three disputed victories

One impressive victory should involve genuinely low resistance; another should involve an honestly shared burden; a third should conceal stolen costs. Let audiences discover which was which.

Follow discoveries through professions and generations

An apprentice trick becomes a martial technique, then infrastructure, then a method taught outside its founding school. Cross-training should generate new inventions, not merely bonuses.

Only then formalise costs and opposed casting

Retain Rank, Size, Tier and Exertion as distinct working dimensions. Test combat, collateral effects, consent and recovery on the spells we most want to keep.

The greatest power in PMU may not be the power to make the world obey. It may be the ability to discover a new relationship with it. But the world is full of people who would rather win first—and those people make excellent stories.
