The Planetary Works of the Seven Deep Grammars

The seven Schools are modern institutions perched atop recurrent structures that human cultures have independently discovered for thousands of years.

Not one genealogy. Recurrent discovery. Different cultures encounter the same underlying classes of relationship, elaborate them in radically different ways, and eventually carry surviving branches into the Straits.

Part one

The premise has changed.

The Great Schools are unusually intact modern institutions, each descended from — and continually recomposed out of — much older planetary discoveries.

Human societies encountered recurring problems: how to measure, how to bind trust, how to belong to place, how to transform material, how to coordinate cycles, how to preserve memory, how to carry burdens together. Wherever those problems were pursued deeply enough, practitioners converged toward one of a small number of stable magical grammars.

That makes the Straits significant by convergence. It is the place where surviving branches of all seven deep grammars became densely co-present — perhaps for the second time.

Source-derived: seven planetary lineages converging in the Straits New formalisation: grammars as recurrent attractors Open canon: historical genealogy kept deliberately ambiguous
problem class P ↓ culture c experiments across generations ↓ G_c → G_i ↓ a locally specific expression of a recurrent deep grammar

Part two

The seven grammars as attractors.

The lineages need not all descend from a secret prehistoric civilisation. The Axiomatic Stratum itself can explain recurrence.

Different cultures may independently converge toward the same deep grammar because the underlying relational structures are real. This keeps the magical physics universal while allowing the cultural sciences to remain genuinely distinct.

Parallax

Observation, reference, translation, measurement, position. Where is this true from?

Amanah

Trust, permission, obligation, witnessed relation. Under what terms may this hold?

Communion

Place, continuity, maintenance, belonging. What relation has been kept here?

Recomposition

Transformation, notation, setting, iterative making. How can this be set again?

Reciprocity

Cycles, timing, exchange, feedback, debt. What must move elsewhere if this changes here?

The Third Voice

Memory, identity, testimony, absence. What remains when the account is incomplete?

The Commons

Distributed agency, maintenance, shared burden. Who is actually carrying this?

seven recurrent grammars ≈ seven stable families of relation ≠ seven ethnic inheritances ≠ seven single-origin civilisations

Part four

The planetary works.

These are not “bigger spells.” Each represents a civilisation discovering that one class of relationship can be stabilised at planetary scale — usually incompletely, often accidentally, and never without consequences.

Meridian Orders · Parallax and the Survey

The World Measure

A planet becomes a single instrument.

Across unrelated civilisations, astronomers, surveyors, navigators and calendar-makers establish points of exceptional positional certainty. None knows the others are doing the same thing. Over centuries, the reference points begin to couple through the Axiomatic Stratum.

M⊕ = Σ observationᵢ

For three nights at a rare alignment, the whole network becomes coherent. Distances become exact. Lost islands locate themselves. Buried cities become triangulable. False cartographies fail. Nothing spatially hidden can remain perfectly hidden.

Historical seed

Ancient observatories, meridian surveys, astronomical bureaus, navigation traditions and geodetic networks across multiple civilisations.

Legend

Many cultures preserve stories of nights when “the world became sharp.” Scholars argue whether these are independent metaphors or records of the same planetary event.

Covenant Roads · Amanah

The Ocean of Trust

An ocean basin becomes partly made of agreements.

Ports that exchange fairly for centuries become more strongly related than their physical separation would imply. Aden, Gujarat, East Africa, island Southeast Asia and southern China cease to be merely points on a map; their repeated obligations carve a second topology into the ocean.

d_axiomatic(port A, port B) < d_geographic(port A, port B)

Empires cannot own this topology. It does not recognise conquest as equivalent to trust. Whole routes shorten for some merchants and lengthen for others.

Historical seed

Indian Ocean mercantile networks, caravan law, port cities, bonded exchange and long-duration commercial trust.

Legend

The first great rupture in global navigation was not caused by geography. It happened when enough covenants failed that an entire ocean became farther away.

Keepers of Ground · Communion and the Rooting

The Root Below Continents

Infrastructure becomes ecology itself.

The deepest Keeper traditions do not distinguish sharply between built and living systems. Forest, soil, water, migration, settlement and tending can accumulate enough relational density to behave as one maintained field.

Γ_land = f(watershed, forest, soil, species, migration, tending)

Fragments remain as sacred groves, root bridges, mangrove coasts, church forests, maintained springs and landscapes that collapse only after custodianship ends.

Historical seed

Sacred groves, long-managed forests, root bridges, keramat landscapes, church forests, coastal tending and place-based custodianship.

Legend

Before the seas rose, Sundaland may have been one continuous maintained landscape. The flooding drowned it and broke its relational continuity.

Houses of Setting · Recomposition and the Setting

The First Forme

The first representation that changed the world back.

Many traditions independently discover that symbols can stabilise relations: characters, diagrams, knots, moulds, numbers, plans, scores, code. The legendary claim is that there once existed an original symbolic grammar precise enough to reverse the usual direction between world and representation.

world → symbol becomes symbol → world

Every talisman, array, diagram, ritual notation and magical machine may be a corrupted descendant of this deeper executable grammar.

Historical seed

Writing systems, diagrams, alchemical notation, print, casting moulds, technical drawing, mathematical and ritual inscription.

Legend

Cultures disagree over its form: a tablet, a loom-pattern, a typecase, a divine diagram, a cave wall, a knot sequence. All may preserve fragments.

Weaving Calendars · Reciprocity and the Weave

The Planetary Loom

Climate understood as relation.

The ancient Weavers never “controlled the climate.” They discovered that ocean, ice, forest, soil, river, agriculture and settlement form one coupled system whose threads can be nudged across centuries.

ocean ↔ forest ↔ ice ↔ soil ↔ river ↔ land use ↔ atmosphere

One intervention in the Andes, another in monsoon Asia, another in the Sahel, another in island waters. No single civilisation sees the whole loom — yet together they alter the planet.

Historical seed

Terraces, khipu, calendar systems, subak, qanats, Angkorian waterworks, watershed governance and long-horizon agricultural coordination.

Legend

The Account of the Suns may record earlier planetary debt cycles. The terrifying possibility is that the Loom still exists, while modern societies pull threads without knowing the pattern.

Singing Lineages · The Third Voice and the Jeliya

The Unwritten World

Civilisation carried in people instead of things.

At its greatest extent, the Singing tradition may once have formed a distributed human infrastructure of epics, genealogies, law, migration routes, astronomical knowledge and ecological memory.

memory network = Σ living transmission × redundancy

No central archive existed. No master copy could be burned. The network endured precisely because the infrastructure was embodied in relationships among performers, communities and generations.

Historical seed

Oral epic traditions, genealogical custodians, sung law, apprenticeship, ritual memory and embodied navigation traditions.

Legend

“The world once remembered itself through us.” The tragedy in Kora teaching is that writing preserved fragments while weakening the living redundancy that made the whole network resilient.

Ring Assemblies · The Commons and the Joining

The Human Load-Bearing Network

A planetary catastrophe distributed into ordinary effort.

Village roof, dyke, irrigation, ship, harvest, disaster relief: many societies independently discover that burden can be distributed relationally. The legendary claim is that these networks once became coupled across the planet.

Γ_commons = Σ local reciprocal networks

During one global catastrophe, millions of people unknowingly carried tiny fractions of the same impossible load: a heavier basket, a creaking bridge, an inexplicable exhaustion, an extra meal required.

Historical seed

Communal labour, dykes, irrigation associations, village rebuilding, roof-thatching, work songs, reciprocal labour and disaster response.

Legend

The event survives under many names. In the Straits it is remembered simply as The Day Everyone Carried Something.

Part five

Why the Straits matters now.

The Straits becomes more compelling as the convergence chamber in which all seven traditions survive in unusually legible form.

Its importance comes from geography, migration, trade, empire, refuge, translation and institutional survival. It becomes the place where ancient planetary grammars are forced into proximity — perhaps for the second time.

The Great Schools are therefore not “the seven original orders.” They are modern archives of seven recurring discoveries, each carrying different fragments of a much older planetary record.

Keep the origin story unresolved.

The strongest version leaves several histories simultaneously plausible:

  • The seven grammars evolved independently across the world and merely met in the ancient port.
  • The ancient port was the first known place where all seven were deliberately integrated.
  • Fragments from the port later accelerated older traditions elsewhere without creating them.
  • The entire story of the port is a later Straits myth explaining why the same grammars recur globally.

Part six · the legend under the planet

The Port That Was Carried Away — reconsidered

The myth becomes stronger once the world is older than the Straits.

Every lineage in the Straits still tells the story of the vanished port. Now the story can be one origin among many.

The seven sciences were already old when they arrived there. Surveyors came with benchmarks from forgotten observatories. Covenant-keepers came with routes older than dynasties. Keepers brought roots from places whose names were lost. Setters carried diagrams copied so many times nobody remembered the originals. Weavers brought cords whose knots counted rains that no chronicle recorded. Singers arrived already carrying epics longer than kingdoms. Assemblies came with joints, rotas and rules for carrying things together.

The miracle of the port was not invention.

It was interoperability.

For the first time, perhaps, all seven grammars worked on the same city at once. The port became a demonstration that difference could remain difference and still form one operational whole.

· · ·

Then the lineages quarrelled over what the city was. A Meridian archive called it a perfect measurement system. Covenant texts called it a promise among strangers. Keeper songs called it a mangrove that learned masonry. Setting records described a machine. Calendar cords described an exchange system. Kora verses called it a memory that could float. Sund accounts insisted it was simply what happened when everyone carried their share.

Perhaps all seven accounts were correct.

When the city was dismantled, its pieces did not seed empty continents. They entered older traditions already waiting for them. A brass bolt entered a survey house. A seal entered a caravan court. A root joined a sacred grove. A slug of type entered a printer's case. A cord entered an irrigation archive. A verse joined an epic. A timber peg entered a village roof.

The pieces disappeared because they became useful.

· · ·

The oldest members of the seven Straits Schools still keep one object each in rooms that students are not shown. They disagree about whether the objects are truly from the port.

They agree on only one thing.

Every Regatta of Seven Hands ends with the seven Schools building one structure together, overnight, and dismantling it before dawn. The public calls it tradition. The oldest practitioners call it rehearsal.
