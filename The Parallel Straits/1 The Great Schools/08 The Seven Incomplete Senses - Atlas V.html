Prelude · powers as epistemology

Not superheroes. Different kinds of human.

The cast’s powers are not rewards, weapons or cosmetic upgrades. They are the logical extremes of the intellectual traditions that formed them. Every power makes something newly perceivable — and every power destabilises the boundary between ability, identity, duty and personhood.

A civilisation that treats observation as contingent eventually redesigns perception. A school obsessed with transformation eventually makes the body mutable. A custodial tradition binds a person to territory. A merchant culture discovers promises with persistence. An oral civilisation learns to hear memory in relationships. An ecological house produces people who are themselves habitat nodes. A commons tradition eventually makes ownership locally optional.

The sources of these abilities remain deliberately plural. Some are genetically engineered. Some emerge from symbiotic biology. Some require neural training. Some are infrastructural. Some look metaphysical. Some may involve dark-energy exposure or unknown cosmological conditions. The world refuses to collapse all extraordinary phenomena into a single explanatory technology.

That ambiguity is essential. A power that can be completely understood becomes engineering. A power that cannot be investigated becomes hand-waving. This world lives in the fertile middle: repeatable enough to produce institutions, strange enough to keep reopening ontology.

And the strongest characters are not those who perfectly embody their traditions. They are those whose abilities force their traditions into contradiction.

Their powers do not make them stronger than ordinary humans. They make them stranger kinds of human. Atlas V · character principle
01 · Elias Tan · TKI

The Observer
Who Cannot Stay SingularPolyperceptual Subject E-17 · “Many-Eyed”

He can hold multiple incompatible perceptual models without collapsing them into one reality.

Signature ability
Parallax

The observer becomes the experimental variable.

Elias is the product of neuroplastic training, synthetic neural lattices, engineered glial structures and resonance conditioning. He perceives visual space, magnetic gradients, electromagnetic noise, social attention fields, emotional resonance and probabilistic motion simultaneously.

Ordinary cognition resolves competing models into a single usable world. Elias can resist that collapse. He can see what someone is, what they are preparing to do, how nearby minds expect them to behave and which invisible infrastructures are structuring the space around them.

“It only appears impossible because you’re standing in the wrong reality.”

In dangerous situations, Parallax can resemble precognition. But Elias does not see “the future.” He sees multiple present structures whose interaction makes some futures more available than others.

The cost

He does not always know which perception belongs to reality — or whether several contradictory perceptions may all be true at different ontological levels.

Can perceive
Magnetism · intent vectors · attention fields · hidden systems · relational expectation.
Looks like
Telepathy, precognition, impossible spatial awareness.
Actually is
A refusal to privilege one observational frame too early.
Contradiction
The man with the most perceptions trusts perception least.
02 · Lin Yanshi 林衍时 · Lan Fang

The Woman Who
Can Rewrite FormAdaptive morphologist · architect of living systems

Her body remains biologically revisable — but every transformation leaves a residue.

Signature ability
Recomposition

Transformation becomes biological memory.

Yanshi’s engineered regulatory systems keep adult tissues unusually plastic. She can redistribute bone density, alter pigmentation and thermal behaviour, restructure musculature, adapt her visual spectrum, change respiratory capacity and gradually refit herself for unfamiliar gravity or atmosphere.

Her changes are slow. Recomposition is deliberate and costly, and every body-state leaves a trace. Past transformations remain as biological palimpsest: a silver line beneath the skin, altered hand proportions, a voice that sometimes slips into an abandoned respiratory configuration.

Change is possible. Return is an approximation.

Over time, Yanshi learns to sense analogous instability in architecture, institutions and living systems. She becomes able to intuit how complex forms may be reconfigured without collapse.

The cost

Her body remembers every previous self. Transformation does not erase history; it accumulates it.

Can alter
Body architecture · metabolism · thermal regulation · perception · structural systems.
Cannot do
Return perfectly to an earlier self.
Contradiction
She was trained to transform everything and begins wondering what should remain unchanged.
03 · Nur Iskandar · Temenggong

The Custodian
Bound to PlaceLegal custodian · ecological covenant bearer

When he represents a territory, the territory becomes capable of injuring him back.

Signature ability
Territorial Communion

Representation becomes reciprocal.

Through ritual training, neural symbiosis, microbiome engineering and mechanisms nobody fully understands, Nur undergoes Binding with the territory he legally represents. Soil stress, poisoned water, forest disease, microbial collapse and mass animal displacement enter his body as sensation.

A damaged watershed may produce fever. A blocked river can manifest as breathlessness. Burned forest can leave phantom pain. The legal fiction that a custodian “speaks for” a place becomes biologically uncomfortable, because the speaker now feels what happens to the represented.

Within deeply bound territory, Nur can sometimes influence ecological response: roots stabilise disturbed soil, migration patterns alter, microbial systems shift, water redistributes. He does not command.

He requests. Sometimes the territory answers.
The cost

After years of Binding, Nur increasingly refers to the watershed as “we.” Nobody knows whether this is metaphor, devotion — or a literal shift in personhood.

Feels
Water stress · soil damage · absence of species · ecological poisoning.
Can influence
Distributed ecological response inside bound territory.
Contradiction
Custodianship preserves the land by gradually destabilising the custodian’s singular self.
04 · Samira Bharucha al-Haddad · Merikani

The Keeper
of PromisesTemporal fiduciary · covenant architect

Some promises acquire persistence beyond the lives of those who made them.

Signature ability
Amanah

Trust acquires field strength.

Merikani researchers discover that sufficiently consequential vows — repeated across generations and embedded simultaneously in ritual, memory, law, architecture and economic behaviour — can behave like temporal attractors. They call these Covenant Fields.

Samira sees them almost as threads or architectures. Weak obligations fade. Ancient ones can fill entire rooms. She can trace inheritance, strengthen commitments, transfer responsibility and perform the feared act of Release: dissolving a covenant that no longer deserves continuation.

Some promises deserve to die.

Amanah is therefore not “truth magic.” It is a power over durable obligation — and over the moral sediment that promises accumulate through time.

The cost

Every covenant touched can touch her back. Shame, longing, grief and expectation from long-dead signatories can become part of her own emotional weather.

Can perceive
Broken vows · inherited debts · durable obligations · covenant density.
Most sacred act
Release.
Contradiction
Raised to protect trust; uniquely capable of ending it.
05 · Amadou Sane · Kora

The Man Who Hears
the Dead Between VoicesSecond Hearer · memory advocate · relational medium

Places, objects, rituals and relationships may carry memory that belongs to no surviving individual.

Signature ability
The Third Voice
SPEAKER · SECOND VOICE · RELATION

Memory persists in relation.

Kora’s oral sciences, neurobiology, acoustic resonance and ancestral archives converge into Memory Echo research. Certain places, objects and repeated practices retain structures of grief, expectation, fear and belonging that can be perceived by gifted Hearers.

Amadou enters these echoes and reconstructs fragments of experience. A courthouse remembers argument. A necklace carries generations of mourning. A public square contains the residue of protest. A song performed for four centuries becomes dense with accumulated emotional architecture.

Then he discovers something beyond speaker and contradiction:

The Third Voice — what the relationship itself remembers.

A marriage. A city. An institution. A species. A planet.

The cost

Memory does not respect ownership. Amadou increasingly carries emotions whose origin he cannot identify. “I don’t know whose grief this is.”

Can hear
Memory echoes · relational residue · unsaid history · distributed grief.
Not exactly
Ghosts.
Contradiction
The greatest memory advocate in the world can no longer be certain which memories are his.
06 · Mayu Quispe · Nine Rain

The
Habitat-BornOff-world ecological symbiont · living infrastructure node

Her body is one of the habitat’s operating systems.

Signature ability
Reciprocity
BODY · WATER · MICROBE · STRUCTURE · BREATH

The line between citizen and life-support system dissolves.

Mayu was born off-world after generations of ecological engineering. Her body contains modified microbial communities, radiation repair mechanisms, altered oxygen efficiency, reinforced bone systems, electrochemical symbionts and living interfaces with the habitat’s distributed biological control network.

She senses temperature gradients, water pressure, nutrient stress and structural strain. By touching living surfaces she can stimulate membranes, reroute water, grow fungal reinforcement and rebalance metabolic loops.

In emergency states, she can transfer part of her own metabolic reserve into habitat recovery.

“The habitat needs you.” Is that love — or exploitation?
The cost

The more indispensable Mayu becomes to the habitat, the harder it becomes to insist that she is a person first and infrastructure second.

Can sense
Habitat metabolism · water stress · nutrient imbalance · structural biology.
Can do
Grow · seal · reroute · stabilise · sacrifice.
Contradiction
A civilisation built on reciprocity may be asking one person to reciprocate too much.
07 · Erik Soren · Sund

The Man Who Can
Stop OwnershipExperimental civic neuro-field · non-appropriation bearer

Within his field, systems built around possession and command begin to lose their authority.

Signature ability
Commons Field

What happens when nobody owns the situation?

Sund’s distributed computation, legal encoding, cognitive consensus systems and neuromorphic infrastructure eventually produce temporary non-appropriation zones. Erik carries an experimental biological version.

Inside his Commons Field, smart weapons can forget their authorised owner. Locks may default open. proprietary systems lose exclusive permissions. coercive contract clauses suspend. Socially conditioned obedience can weaken.

The effect can feel liberating because people become newly conscious of hierarchy that previously operated automatically.

But a hospital, spacecraft or family sometimes needs somebody to be responsible.
The cost

Erik can suspend ownership and command more easily than he can replace them with legitimate coordination.

Disrupts
Property permissions · coercive systems · linked weaponry · conditioned obedience.
Cannot guarantee
What cooperative order emerges afterward.
Contradiction
The power to free a situation can also make it impossible to govern.
Beyond the seven schools

Some powers belong to history itself.

Centuries of cross-pollination, off-world exposure and uncontrolled emergence create lineages nobody can comfortably attribute to one institution.

01 · Deep-space anomaly

The Darkborn

Travellers exposed to vacuum-energy and gravitational experiments return with unstable mass, dream-sharing across distance, memories that precede events and microscopic distortions around emotional stress.

Nine Rain calls them Void-touched. TKI issues a much longer technical label.

Mutation, contamination, or contact with a layer of physics human beings were never built to inhabit?
02 · Gaia emergence

The Gaia-Bonded

Children born inside long-established Gaia Listening ecologies show seasonal immune changes, sensitivity to water chemistry and anomalous synchrony with animal populations.

“Perhaps the territory learned how to speak through a person.”
03 · Anti-archive anomaly

The Unwritten

Some people resist cognitive recording itself. Scans fail to stabilise. Memories of them blur. Witnesses remember an encounter and lose the identity.

They become spies, saints, criminals, monastics and philosophical nightmares.

What is personhood in a civilisation that cannot reliably archive you?
04 · Relational permeability

The Mirrors

After prolonged contact with another person, Mirrors unconsciously acquire aspects of gesture, speech, affective rhythm and sometimes memory tendency.

Are they exceptional imitators — or people whose identity boundaries are genuinely porous?
When powers become ordinary enough for bureaucracy

Superhuman ability produces paperwork.

The decisive sign that the extraordinary has become real is the paperwork: the emergence of law, insurance, medicine, etiquette, religion, sports, dating norms and administrative forms around it.

Cognitive consent

Resonance assault

Entering or manipulating another person’s shared cognitive state without permission becomes a distinct offence. Consent law now includes mental co-presence.

Territorial personhood

Ecological bodily harm

Destroying a bound territory may legally constitute injury to its custodian — while injuring the custodian may impair the ecosystem they carry.

Merikani courts

Covenant bankruptcy

People overwhelmed by inherited vows may petition for ceremonial and legal Release, raising the question of whether obligation can become pathological.

Kora medicine

Memory contamination

Specialists diagnose emotional and mnemonic material that may not originate in the patient, without assuming such material is false or delusional.

Lan Fang identity

Morphological passports

Biometric identity becomes unreliable when bodies can change. Legal identity shifts toward continuity of memory, covenant and authenticated self-declaration.

Sund regulation

Commons restrictions

Activating a non-appropriation field inside critical infrastructure may be prohibited even when the field itself is considered a protected civic technology.

Nine Rain personhood

Habitat refusal

If a living habitat exhibits cognition through people like Mayu, can it refuse passengers, reject expansion or demand compensation?

Everyday life

Dating, sport, insurance

People disclose resonance sensitivity before intimacy. Competitive leagues regulate perceptual augmentation. Insurers distinguish voluntary from inherited covenant exposure.

The mythic turn

The Pattern recognises them.

The planetary Pattern does not grant the seven their powers. It behaves as though their powers are interfaces already compatible with something larger.
