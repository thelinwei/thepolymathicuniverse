02 · Why Fong wins

It gives the school a history beyond its philosophy.

1. It separates the institutions by ear.

Toa Keng and Lam Keng are perfectly possible names in one city. Their shared syllable is not a realism error. However, for readers meeting seven schools, Toa Keng / Lan Fong is a cleaner distinction, especially in dialogue, captions and a lookbook.

2. It grounds Chineseness in the southern seas.

The PMU’s stated kongsi connection gives the school a Southeast Asian institutional lineage of its own. That suits the original premise of several Chinese modernities meeting in a port.

3. It supports the later material world.

The existing Lan Fong chapters connect shared enterprise, type-setting, craft and the recomposition of objects. The name’s narrative context supports those developments without having to announce “Transformation” on the gate.

4. It leaves room for contradiction.

A school with inherited patrons, competing communities and a public name can contain conservatives and reformers. Each student holds the institution’s governing concept in their own way.

One limit: a Hakka kongsi lineage can be a founding strand alongside the earlier Hokkien, Teochew, Cantonese and other contributors. My recommendation is to retain that plural institution. The historical reference deepens the school and keeps it plural.

The PMU’s historical inspiration and its fictional continuation are separate claims. This paper evaluates the archive’s storytelling choices; the real Lanfang polity keeps its own history, and PMU canon keeps its own chronology.

03 · The spelling decision

Lan Fong and Lam Fong are not interchangeable by accident.

Lan Fong has the stronger editorial case today: it is the spelling in the current homepage register and in the later material I checked. Retaining it avoids introducing an additional unexplained variant while settling the existing ones.

Lam Fong remains a legitimate fictional naming choice. If Lin Wei prefers its sound and visual form, the school’s inherited English spelling can be Lam Fong. But I would not claim that this is an authenticated Hakka romanisation simply because it sounds plausible, or silently infer its Chinese characters from the earlier 南景.

If the intended Chinese identity follows the Lanfang reference, 蘭芳書院 is my proposed Chinese institutional form, for Lin Wei’s approval. The website evidence checked here settles the Latin spelling it currently uses; the bilingual naming specification is the next step.

My vote is Lan Fong College. If the author’s vote is Lam Fong College, the author’s vote wins—and all future labels should follow it.
04 · Where I disagree with the earlier naming argument

Keep the useful distinctions. Drop the absolute rules.

“A name is a fossil, not a thesis” is a good prompt, not a law.

The Register of Names usefully gives institutions official, everyday and mocking names. But institutions can also be named after ideals, founders’ aspirations, literary quotations or religious commitments. A philosophical name does not automatically fail the realism test.

Jit Sin is a good idea, but I would not use it as the main school name.

The daily-renewal name creates a sharp contradiction: a school committed to change can become dogmatic about changing. That is good fiction. Yet the name foregrounds the school’s assigned epistemology so clearly that it risks making the institution feel designed to demonstrate a thesis. The register itself also acknowledges an existing Penang school bearing Jit Sin. For PMU, I find Lan Fong the more useful centre of gravity.

The renewal idea can still inform the school’s debates or motto, and becomes an official name only if Lin Wei wants that history.

A shared syllable does not make two school names impossible.

The register’s ban on shared syllables confuses an editorial convenience with a rule about cities. The reason to prefer Fong is reader clarity and the strength of its institutional story—not an assertion that Toa Keng and Lam Keng could never coexist.

Kora House does not become implausible because “a house has walls.”

“House” can refer to a lineage, community or institution. The refusal of that word could be an entertaining belief held by characters; it is not a linguistic necessity. I support Sané-Kora Institute for its fuller founding-lineage identity while retaining Kora in everyday use.

Neither Nine Rains nor Temenggong needed to be dismissed.

Nine Rain gains a specific fictional day-sign explanation; that is a positive reason to choose it. The plural was not inherently unreal. Likewise, Sri Temenggong Institution and Balai Pusaka can coexist as the public and internal names already described in the register. They need not compete as mutually exclusive replacements.

06 · A decision that will stay decided

Separate the name from the draft history.

  1. Lin Wei approves one master register. Every school gets a stable identifier, a formal name, a standard short label and any explicitly approved in-world aliases.
  2. Use that register across both AI workflows. A later draft cannot rename a school merely because another name seems more philosophically elegant.
  3. Mark older documents as earlier versions. Invent canonical renaming events only when the author wants those events in the story.
  4. Update the active lookbook and website labels after the decision. Preserve original historical documents as appropriate. No website files have been changed as part of this paper.

I agree with moving away from Lam Keng. I favour the Fong name because it supports the world that has developed around it. My remaining vote is for Lan Fong; Lin Wei’s preference for Lam Fong is the exact point still awaiting the author’s ruling.
