Design note · the format is the argument

A crest is a European sentence. Eight of these houses speak something else.

So the rule for this plate is simple and it does all the work: each mark takes the format its own tradition uses for authority. The shape of the mark is the epistemology. The contents are secondary.

The lazy version of this job is nine shields. Divide the field, add a keris here, a swallow there, a condor for the trans-Pacific house, and letter a motto on a ribbon underneath. It would look coherent, it would print well, and it would be a lie — because the shield carries its own grammar: hereditary land held by force, and only one house in this city comes from that grammar.

Work the other way instead. Ask what object each tradition already uses to mean this is binding. TKI: a shield, because it is a colonial foundation and should not be flattered out of it. Merican: a seal, because a merchant house's authority is an impression pressed into a document. Temenggong: a tumpal, the upward triangle of the textile border, because the mark belongs to cloth and ceremony before it belongs to a gate. Lam Keng: a square chop, because a southern Chinese institution signs by carving. Nine Rains: a woven band with a knotted record hanging from it, because the record is the mark. Kora: a seat, because authority is who is permitted to sit and be answered.

Then the three that break the set, which is where the plate earns its keep. Sund refuses insignia, so its mark is an assembly diagram — three timbers cross-lapped, each holding the others up. Sekolah Petang was never granted a mark, so its mark is what it actually had: a serial number in a rubber stamp, slightly crooked, ink-blotted. And Rumah Arus carries its knowledge as a route; so its mark is a navigation chart with a registry stamp struck across it.

Constraint held throughout: one colour, no gradients, fills without meaning, legible at 12 mm. A mark that needs two colours to be understood has become a logo.

One consequence is worth stating before the plate. Rendered together, the nine do not harmonise, and they should not. Anyone who wants the set to look like a set is asking for the atlas's original error in graphic form: the belief that seven incompatible theories of knowledge can share one visual system without one of them supplying it.

01 · Toa Keng Institution · 1821

The Shield

Format · European heraldry, unapologetically
Argent, a fess wavy sable in base; issuant therefrom a sun in splendour; the base hatched of mud. Motto: Ex Limo, Lumen.

The only genuine coat of arms in the city, and the plate leaves it a coat of arms. Its honesty is the wavy band, which is the drain, placed low and crossing the full width so it reads as a fact of the ground.

The sun rises from the ditch, sharing its compartment — the one compositional decision the school's own committee fought for three years. Below the band, hatching: the convention for mud, and also the convention for something a herald would rather leave unnamed.

02 · Al-Merican College · 1834

The Seal

Format · mercantile impression, struck twice
Within a milled roundel: a lateen sail of two courses; a star of eight rays in chief; a wave in base; dividers open, sinister.

A merchant house's authority is not carried on a shield into a field. It is pressed into wax at the bottom of a page. So this is a stamp, and the outer ring is deliberately printed twice, offset three units — the ghost impression of a seal used ten thousand times on real contracts.

The sail is drawn as two courses meeting at a single point, which is the house's whole doctrine: several routes, one promise. The dividers sit low and off-centre, because measurement serves the obligation.

03 · Sri Temenggong Institution

The Tumpal

Format · textile border motif
A tumpal, apex to heaven; in chief a crescent doubled; palewise a keris inverted; in base a root system of nine radicles.

The tumpal is the triangular border of woven cloth, and choosing it over a shield is the point: this house's authority precedes and outlives the building it currently occupies. Cloth travels. A gate does not.

The keris points down — power restrained into responsibility — and its blade is drawn with the wave of the true forged blade. Beneath it the roots take up a third of the field, more space than the crescent, because roots uphold the sky is a load-bearing statement about structure and should be drawn as load.

Cannot say · who was moved off the roots
04 · Lam Keng College · 南景书院 · 1847

The Chop

Format · carved square seal, corner chipped
A square chop; within, a swallow volant, wings recovered; the characters 南 and 景 opposed; the lower dexter corner broken away.

A school that signs by carving gets a carved mark. The square is heavy-stroked because a chop is cut, and the swallow is rendered in three strokes that could plausibly be made by a blade — leave, return, leave again.

The chipped corner is the important decision. Twice this college destroyed a generation of its own records while clearing space, and the chip is drawn as a real break with a dotted ghost of the missing stone. The school approved it; a chop that has been used for two centuries and shows no damage is a forgery.

Cannot say · what was in the minutes that were burned
05 · The House of Nine Rains

The Band

Format · woven band with a hanging record
A band stepped in three terraces; within, a condor displayed interlocked with a serpent; water in base. Pendant: five cords, knotted 2·1·3·1·2.

The house holds that a record can be a physical object as well as a page, so the mark both depicts the doctrine and contains a reading. Five cords hang below the band, knotted, and the knots are the current cohort's water figures. The mark is therefore out of date every year, and is recut every year.

The stepped top edge is the terrace, and the condor's wings are drawn as the same steps, so sky and earth share one geometry as equals.

Cannot say · who ties the cords
06 · Kora House

The Seat

Format · a stool, which is an office
A ceremonial seat, curved of plate; two ears affronté; the column strung with three courses; the base plate uninscribed.

Authority here is a seat: who may sit, who must answer. The ears are drawn as part of the seat, because listening is the office itself.

The base plate is left blank in every casting. No name has ever been cut into it, which is either the House's finest tradition or the neatest way ever devised of keeping the second voice unattributed. The plate declines to decide and draws the blank at full size.

Cannot say · who appoints the ears
07 · The Sund Commons

The Lap

Format · joinery detail
Three members of equal section, cross-lapped at three points, standing. No field, no charge, no motto. A single datum line in base.

Sund will not carry insignia, and pretending otherwise would falsify the house. So its mark is a working drawing: three identical timbers, each lapped over one neighbour and under the other, forming a structure with no keystone and no member that could be called principal.

Remove any one and the drawing is three sticks. That is the entire statement, it takes no motto, and it is the only mark on this plate that a reader can test by covering part of it with a thumb.

Cannot say · that the joint is expensive to maintain
Entry 00 · Sekolah Petang · No. 4

The Stamp

Format · rubber serial stamp · never granted a mark
A box, set crooked; legend SEK. PETANG; the numeral 04 broken by a stencil bridge; in base the two sessions, 0730 / 1300. Ink blotted at three points.

No commission ever designed a mark for the shift schools, so inventing an elegant one would be a fabrication. This is what they actually had: the school's identity was a number in a rubber stamp on the top-right of a form, and there were two hundred and forty of them.

Everything here is a real property of the object. It prints crooked because it was applied by hand at speed. The numeral is bridged because the stencil had to survive being cut by a clerk. The blots are where the pad was over-inked. It is the least dignified mark on the plate and it stamped more documents than the other eight combined.

Cannot say · anything about the pedagogy, because nobody was ever asked to draw it
Entry — · Rumah Arus · deregistered

The Chart

Format · navigation chart · a mark that cannot be a mark
Battens of swell and current, seven, crossing; nodes of shell, five, at the intersections; all drawn broken. Overstruck: a registry stamp, FIELD 4(B).

This is the design problem the plate exists to expose. A crest is a fixed object identifying a fixed body, and Rumah Arus was a set of routes held by moving people. So the mark is a stick chart — battens for swell and current, shell nodes for the islands — which is the tradition's own instrument. It teaches a passage. Only those inside the tradition can read it, and the plate leaves it that way.

Every line is drawn broken. Not for style: eleven passages survive in full and somewhere between forty and three hundred are gone, so a continuous line would overstate the holdings. Then the stamp — Field 4(b), permanent premises — set at an angle across the middle of the chart, in the one other colour this plate permits, because the deregistration is now the most legible thing about the school and pretending otherwise would be sentimental.

The plate's honest admission: this is a mark about Rumah Arus, made by people who can read heraldry. The house would not have made it, would not have needed it, and had no word for it.

The only test that matters

Twelve millimetres, one colour, cut in brass.

A mark lives on a blazer pocket, a stamped envelope, a bicycle plate, a hospital form. Here are all nine at the size they will actually be seen at, most of the time, by people who never attended any of them. Three of them win at this size, and the winners are the modest ones.

Wins at 12 mmSund's lap, Temenggong's tumpal, Kora's seat. All three are single silhouettes with one internal event. Sund wins hardest, which is a joke the school would enjoy: the house that refuses insignia has the best insignia in the city.
Fails at 12 mmMerican's seal collapses into a grey disc, and Nine Rains' knotted cords become fuzz — both because they encode information. Their marks are documents, and documents keep their size.
Wins for the wrong reasonPetang 04. A crooked box with a two-digit numeral is perfectly legible at any size, because it was designed by a stationery supplier for exactly this purpose. The city's most recognisable school mark is a serial number, and nobody framed one.
Design coda

Nine marks that refuse to be a set.

If a client asked for these nine as a unified identity system, the correct answer would be no. Harmonising them means choosing one visual grammar and translating the other eight into it, and every available grammar belongs to somebody. Make them all shields and the city is a colony. Make them all seals and it is a market. Make them all textile borders and it is a heritage campaign.

What can be unified is the discipline: one colour, no gradient, no fill that carries meaning, legible at twelve millimetres, and every mark drawn in the object its own tradition uses to mean binding. That is a system with no centre — which is the only kind this city can honestly have.

Two of the nine are not marks at all, and were included anyway. A rubber stamp and a broken chart. They are the two that a heraldic register would normally exclude on the grounds that there is nothing to draw, and they carry the plate.

A crest tells you who a school thinks it is.
The format tells you where it thinks authority comes from.
The format is the harder thing to fake.
