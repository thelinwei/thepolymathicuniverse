[The Seven Curricula]

(The macro view of the seven schools: how broad each one runs, how deep it goes, and who leads where.)

The seven schools divide the city’s learning unevenly, by design. Lan Fang runs both the broadest and the deepest school of making, and the city’s college of Chinese medicine. Toa Keng is the widest civic university. Sané-Kora holds the deepest music and the oldest archive. Al-Merikani and Nine Rain are narrow and very deep, the one in number, sky and sound, the other in water, earth and time. Sri Temenggong leads land, coast and the body. Sund is total in everything made to be shared. This edition follows the allocations of the Prospectus of the Seven Schools, drawn as a map.

I

Each bar runs across all 54 fields, from named schools at the left to fields learned elsewhere at the right. A long dark start means depth; a short grey end means breadth.

Toa KengThe Unfinished Institution
20 deep41 taught5 named · 15 faculty · 5 unit · 16 modules · 9 activity · 4 elsewhere

The widest civic university: law, policy, economics, management, journalism and civic theatre, most of them full faculties. Its music and dance live in clubs, and its categories are the ones this map is drawn in.

Al-MerikaniThe Turning Court
12 deep33 taught9 named · 3 faculty · 14 unit · 7 modules · 4 activity · 17 elsewhere

Narrow and very deep: mathematics, the sky, instruments, sound and ethical finance, on roads that run from the Coromandel coast to Andalus. It barely touches the body or the land, and does not need to.

Sri TemenggongThe Woven Garden
13 deep47 taught6 named · 7 faculty · 15 unit · 19 modules · 6 activity · 1 elsewhere

Ecology and land first, then the coast, then performance and cloth, carried by the sea peoples of the archipelago. Its knowledge is practised through the body and the ground.

Lan FangThe Rewritten Courtyard
21 deep47 taught9 named · 12 faculty · 12 unit · 14 modules · 5 activity · 2 elsewhere

Both the broadest and the deepest in making: art, design, fashion, film, materials, engineering, enterprise and Chinese medicine. It studies everything except governance, and almost nothing in the city is made or mended without passing through it.

Nine RainThe Rain Terraces
9 deep22 taught6 named · 3 faculty · 13 unit · 0 modules · 4 activity · 28 elsewhere

Few fields, each one total: water, earth, climate, the sky and land art. Everywhere else it sends its students across the city.

Sané-KoraThe Listening Village
16 deep36 taught11 named · 5 faculty · 12 unit · 8 modules · 2 activity · 16 elsewhere

The deepest music in the city, its oldest archive, and the schools of learning and community health that grow from listening.

SundThe Common House
10 deep33 taught5 named · 5 faculty · 20 unit · 3 modules · 7 activity · 14 elsewhere

Social design, welfare, cooperatives, education and repair: a narrow house, total in everything made to be shared.

Seven records

Toa Keng

The long record
The survey and the register: every lot, title and licence entered in one hand, the working habit of a colonial port.
The crossing
Arrives with the charter that made the port, and never leaves the waterfront.
In the Straits
Its categories are the ones this prospectus is written in.
The stain
Plantation science, and a register that turned shared ground into titled lots.

Al-Merikani

The long record
The Marakkayar, seafarers, shipbuilders and captains of the Coromandel coast, who wrote Tamil in an Arabic-derived script; merikani, the American cotton East Africa priced its goods in; and a navigator’s handbook from around 1490 behind every voyage.
The crossing
By monsoon from the Coromandel ports, family by family, carrying letters of credit instead of land.
In the Straits
The city hears the cloth and the house means the seafarers. Dar al-Amana’s oldest ledgers still count in bolts.
The stain
The same cloth was spun from slave-grown cotton and issued to enslaved porters on the caravan roads.

Sri Temenggong

The long record
The river letters: permits the Temenggong granted to Chinese planters, each naming a river and a term, kept in Johor’s registers from 1844 to 1902.
The crossing
None. Sri Temenggong was on the shore first, with the sea peoples who lived on its boats.
In the Straits
Dewan Adat still issues river letters, so the island keeps two registries and an old quarrel over which came first.
The stain
River masters fell into debt and sold their letters to city magnates, who held rivers they never worked.

Lan Fang

The long record
Centuries of Chinese star records, Zheng He’s star-measured crossings, and the Annals of Lanfang, the only written account any west Borneo kongsi left.
The crossing
1884, when the Dutch ended the kongsi.
In the Straits
Lan Fang admits without examination, elects every head, keeps the Annals in its oldest case of type, and runs the city’s free clinics. It studies everything except governance.
The stain
A president who helped the Dutch seize a rival kongsi’s leader. Lan Fang teaches the vote as the guard against that kind of bargain.

Nine Rain

The long record
Knotted cords that kept accounts, tables that tracked Venus for centuries, and silver: for three and a half centuries Mexico mined most of the world’s supply.
The crossing
Across the Pacific on the Manila galleons, as coin.
In the Straits
Its silver was the city’s money for a century, until Toa Keng’s registry demonetised it. The Silver Question never quite closes.
The stain
Andean silver at Potosí was dug under the mita, a forced labour draft. Nine Rain knows whose hands made the coin.

Sané-Kora

The long record
Timbuktu’s manuscripts, hundreds of thousands of them, on astronomy, mathematics, medicine and law; and Kaabu, where tradition says the kora was born and the Sanneh were one of two royal lines.
The crossing
Two falls: Timbuktu’s scholars exiled in the 1590s, and Kansala taken in 1867.
In the Straits
The jeli of Jeliya sing and the copyists of the Ajami House write, and the house argues over which memory is truer.
The stain
Kaabu’s nobility grew rich on war captives. Kuma recites that too, because a genealogy that leaves it out unpraises the captives.

Sund

The long record
The Sound Toll Registers: about 1.8 million ship passages recorded at Helsingør between 1497 and 1857, beside an assembly whose lawspeaker carried the law in his head.
The crossing
1857, when the toll ended.
In the Straits
Its folk school dates itself to 1844 and its assembly keeps a law-speaker.
The stain
A commons paid for by a toll on everyone else’s ships.

Four further cuts

What the map shows only sideways: how each school makes its people, where schools meet, what they owe, and what they never list.

iiWhere schools meet

The richest programmes sit between two schools, and each one opens a seam in the city.

The Songbook

Sané-Kora and Lan Fang

Printing songs made to be sung. The first edition was burned by its own composers.

Water Underwriting

Al-Merikani and Nine Rain

Insuring the rains. The contracts that run Basin Seven were drafted here.

Bridal and Costume

Sri Temenggong and Lan Fang

The cut meets the ceremony: wedding dress and stage costume for the city’s marriage hub.

The Monsoon Studio

Nine Rain and Sri Temenggong

Water science, landscape, oral testimony, public finance and public performance on one problem.

iiiWhat leading costs

The school that leads a field answers to the whole city for it and teaches any student who comes. Nine Rain leads water, so Basin Seven is its to explain. Toa Keng leads law, so the unregistered masters of Sané-Kora are its failure. Sané-Kora leads archives, so a genealogy lost anywhere in the city is its shame.

ivThe shadow curriculum

Each school also teaches one thing it never lists, and it is always its own method turned inward.

  • Toa KengPlantation scienceSurvey turned into ownership of other people’s land and labour.
  • Al-MerikaniThe binding debtTrust turned into a leash.
  • Sri TemenggongThe art of forgettingCustodians who decide which heirlooms are allowed to be lost.
  • Lan FangForgeryRemaking turned on the record itself.
  • Nine RainWithholdingReciprocity as a weapon: the water that stops.
  • Sané-KoraUnpraisingThe song that erases a name from every genealogy.
  • SundThe closed doorA commons that only opens to those already inside.
