Seven schools · Academic ecology · Discussion draft 01

Different ways
of knowing.

One university ecology. Seven institutional characters. Some fields warrant a full faculty; others a precise specialist school, a taught option, a student society—or no local provision.

Start with familiar university faculties. Then look inside them to see where PMU’s differences become meaningful.

All PMU allocations, institutional names and leadership orders are design proposals, not previously established canon. Naming follows the recent atlas: Toa Keng, Al-Merikani, Sri Temenggong, Lan Fang, House of Nine Rains, Sané-Kora and Sund Commons. Earlier working names included TKI and Lam Keng.

A conservatory is an institution, not an orchestra. It needs a coherent curriculum, sustained individual and ensemble tuition, composition/theory/history, assessment, practice and production facilities, and professional pathways. A campus choir or gamelan group alone is A. An assessed elective is M. A dedicated performance degree is S. A broad music institution spanning several pathways is F.

Music: three different anchors

Sané-Kora: the broad conservatory—performance, voice, composition, improvisation, music education and living musical lineages.

Al-Merikani: specialist composition and sound, joining musical structures, instrument-making, acoustics and computation.

Sri Temenggong: specialist degrees joining music, movement, theatre and material scenography.

Keep the internal exceptions

Sané-Kora can teach Western classical performance. Lan Fang can sustain experimental work with little commercial return. Sund can host a difficult, individualistic artist.

Cultural genealogy informs educational practice; it does not restrict who may study what. Institutional identities should influence approaches rather than become ethnic rules about ability.

04 · Institutional portraits

What each school feels like academically

Students in place first; disciplinary structures second.

TK · The Unfinished Institution

Toa Keng

A city hearing, a student newspaper and a public theatre share the same courtyard.

Academic character
Civic institutions, public reasoning and metropolitan systems.
Internal order of emphasis
Public policy → law → economics → urban systems
Productive tension
Professional status and formal procedure can crowd out informal knowledge.

AM · The Turning Court

Al-Merikani

A student moves from an optics bench to a translation seminar and an evening composition studio.

Academic character
Mathematics, instruments, translation and experimental inquiry.
Internal order of emphasis
Mathematics → engineering → translation → composition
Productive tension
An elegant formal system can miss how people actually live.

ST · The Woven Garden

Sri Temenggong

Fieldwork in a tidal garden becomes a material workshop and a movement rehearsal.

Academic character
Living landscapes, maritime worlds and embodied making.
Internal order of emphasis
Ecology → landscape → performance → maritime enterprise
Productive tension
Claims of harmony can conceal conflicts over land, labour and access.

LF · The Rewritten Courtyard

Lan Fang

A repair bench, an animation studio and a small venture test occupy adjacent rooms.

Academic character
Making, production, materials and entrepreneurial transformation.
Internal order of emphasis
Materials → design → production → enterprise
Productive tension
Speed and commercial success can outrun care, reflection and labour rights.

NR · The Rain Terraces

House of Nine Rains

Students check a rain gauge, compare long observation series and build an installation that changes with the season.

Academic character
Earth systems, water, time and long-horizon observation.
Internal order of emphasis
Water → earth systems → astronomy → spatial arts
Productive tension
Long horizons can become a reason to postpone urgent human needs.

SK · The Listening Village

Sané-Kora

An ensemble rehearsal becomes an oral-history session and a discussion with a neighbourhood health team.

Academic character
Listening, oral knowledge, music, education and community health.
Internal order of emphasis
Music → oral humanities → education → community health
Productive tension
Consensus and respected voices can silence dissent or less audible members.

SC · The Common House

Sund Commons

A student repairs a shared component, tests an accessible service and joins a cooperative budget meeting.

Academic character
Public welfare, cooperative systems and repairable design.
Internal order of emphasis
Social design → welfare → education → circular engineering
Productive tension
Agreement and standardisation can suppress singularity and productive friction.

06 · Other ways to think

Keep the faculty map. Add other lenses.

The conventional table remains the orientation layer. These interpretations explain why the divisions exist and how students move between them.

1 · Objects of study × ways of knowing

Two schools may study water through different practices: Nine Rains through observation and modelling; Sri Temenggong through living landscapes; Al-Merikani through instruments; Sané-Kora through community governance and health.

Are they teaching different subjects, or producing different kinds of knowledge about the same subject?

2 · Home school × teaching host

A student can belong to Toa Keng while taking composition at Al-Merikani or an auditioned performance pathway at Sané-Kora. Belonging need not dictate every academic option.

Cross-school access is normal, with transparent prerequisites, auditions, timetables and funded travel.

3 · Lead × steward × collaborator

A lead school maintains the degree. A steward maintains shared facilities or archives. Collaborators supply methods, staff and field sites.

Sané-Kora leads music degrees; Al-Merikani stewards acoustics facilities; Sri Temenggong hosts performance residencies.

4 · Shared foundations × scarce facilities

Share statistics, writing, computing, ethics and business literacy. Concentrate costly specialist provision instead of building seven complete copies.

One recording complex, one advanced materials facility, one clinical partnership network—with equitable access.

5 · Missions that cross faculties

Build temporary programmes around monsoon adaptation, ageing, food security, public memory or repair. Each school contributes a different method.

A Monsoon Studio combines water science, landscape, oral testimony, public finance and public performance.

6 · Depth × access × recognition

A later layer should show who can enter, how learning is recognised, and whether informal practitioners are paid and credited as teachers.

No local degree does not mean no knowledge; no credential does not mean no expertise.

7 · Precedence as responsibility

A lead school is responsible for sustaining a field across PMU. It supports access, shares resources and welcomes critique.

Leadership orders can change as faculty, student work and institutional needs evolve.

8 · Protect the unprogrammed

Keep some workshops, ensembles, gardens and discussion groups outside assessment. Students should be able to make music without turning it into a qualification.

CCA/ECA is a different form of participation, not an unfinished academic department.
