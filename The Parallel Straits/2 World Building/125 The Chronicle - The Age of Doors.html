(from the First Door to a.m. 108: the Threshold Accord, dozens of Doors kept as commons, the Gardener’s Rule, crossings made ordinary, and the Straits at the meeting of the worlds)

A world that never needed to conquer its neighbours found it had whole worlds to tend.

How to read itOne spine, two sides

Time runs down the spine. The Straits is on the left and the world on the right. Events that move both cross the spine and span the page.

The StraitsThe worldBothA blank to fill

For five centuries the world had learned to share its seas. The Concord licensed its harbours, the Sound Compact kept its strait, and the Straits stood where the monsoons met and kept the oceans stitched together. When a Door opened in the Evenki forest in 1983, the world already knew what to do with a passage: hold it on terms, and let the people at its edge keep it.

So the Threshold Accord of 1986 read like an old law in a new place. Every Door would be a commons, in the custody of the peoples whose land it opened on. Sund’s jurists helped write it, and they brought with them the seat the Guna had won at the Isthmus Stair, the seat with the power to say no.

Then the Doors multiplied. The houses learned to find them, each by its own art, and behind each lay a world of its own, unpeopled and unspoiled. Settlement stayed light, because a world that had spent a century learning to ask before taking had no reason to start grabbing now. The Gardener’s Rule said it plainly: take from a world only what it can give back.

By the 2010s, crossing between worlds was as ordinary as crossing the harbour. Door-ports grew up around the old harbours, and the Straits, already the meeting place of the oceans, became a meeting place of worlds. In a.m. 108 the seven houses still teach, the Congresses and Covenants still come round, and on the Ninth Night everyone, in every world, lights one lamp.

The Gardener’s Rule

Take from a world only what it can give back. The ethic grows from the houses’ oldest habits: Sri Temenggong’s asking, Nine Rain’s borrowing and returning, the Charter’s rule that every working names its sink.

Settlement stays light

The Haudenosaunee principle of thinking seven generations ahead.

The crossings渡

The Straits

The Straits becomes a crossroads of crossroads. Its harbour has kept the oceans stitched together for six centuries; now it keeps the worlds stitched together too.

The world

Travel between worlds becomes ordinary. Door-ports grow up around the old harbours, and a grandmother can visit her grandson in another world and be home for dinner.

2026
The Straits · a.m. 108

The Straits

Still the Concord’s seat. The Charter Hall, the Court of Workings, the Burden Ledger and the Teachers’ Shield sit in one port. The seven houses teach the world, Congresses and Covenants come round in their rhythm, and every member’s jar is opened in its season.

The world · a.m. 108

A patchwork of compacts

Two crowns rule outright where sixteen once did. The great shapes are the Great Lakes Concord, the Cem Anahuac Federation, the Union of the Four Quarters, the Mande–Songhai Federation and the Federated Provinces, and none of them could swallow its neighbours if it tried. The Atlantic Concert joins the Isles, France and the Low Countries without merging them. The Plains Commons and the Steppe Kurultai still rule by movement.
