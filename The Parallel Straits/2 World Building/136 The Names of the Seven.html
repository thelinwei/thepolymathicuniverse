01 / The method Muara gave us

Charter and street.

The Muara decision drew a line that every great institution eventually discovers. A school has the name on its seal and the name in people's mouths, and the two have different keepers. The charter is kept by the house: it is formal, slow, and written in the language of the people who founded it. The street name is kept by the city: it is quick, affectionate, sometimes rude, and spoken in whatever tongue the speaker happens to be using that afternoon.

So every house here gets two rules.

The charterSpeaks the house's own founding language and moves through three ages. It begins as a gathering of people, settles into a building on the Straits, and opens into an institution for everyone. Pertapaan → Balai → Taman set the pattern.
The streetSpeaks the city's language. It comes from what a passer-by actually meets: a place, a sound, a people, a date. Muara is simply where the school stands.

In a multipolar world this division does real work. In our history, a school that wanted to be taken seriously abroad often took an English name, because English travelled with an empire. In PMU, no empire set the terms, so every charter stays in its own tongue, and the city's lingua franca does the translating on the street. That is the achar principle showing up in how people talk.

There is also a practical gift. For five of the six houses, the name already used across PMU becomes the street name. Every earlier volume still reads naturally: the documents simply turn out to have been written in the city's voice all along.

On paper, seven charters in seven tongues. On the street, the names everyone already knew.The principle in one line
02 / Hakka · the maker house

Lan Fang: from fragrance to workshop.

Lan Fang needed the most rethinking, because the first proposal was the safest one, and safety is the wrong instinct for the house that makes things.

First proposal · the charter line兰芳公司 → 兰芳义学 → 兰芳书院

Partnership, then free school, then academy. It followed the house's legal paperwork: the kongsi became a school fund, the school fund became a free school, and the modern name reclaimed the classical 书院, as Hong Kong's 新亚书院 did in 1949. Coherent, and it continued the "Kongsi Free School" of the earlier gazette. It described the house's constitution and missed its hands.

Revised · the hundred crafts兰芳公司 → 兰芳堂 → 兰芳百工院

Partnership, then the hall of many uses, then the Academy of the Hundred Crafts. This line follows what Lan Fang actually does: it makes, it heals, it fights, and it treats all three as crafts with standards. This is the proposed canon.

I
West Borneo · the gold fields · 1786

兰芳公司 · Lanfang Kongsi

The house begins as a sworn partnership of Hakka miners who pooled their claims, voted their headmen and shared what the ground gave up. A kongsi is the act of holding in common, and the word travelled so far through the Straits that Malay absorbed it: berkongsi means to share. The house's first name already belonged to two languages.

II
The Straits · the hall

兰芳堂 · Lan Fang Tang

On the Straits the kongsi built a single hall, and 堂 is the one Chinese word that holds everything Lan Fang did under that roof. A 堂 is an ancestral hall, a pharmacy (as in the old medicine houses named 某某堂), a martial-arts hall, and a classroom (课堂). In this age you could walk in to buy a remedy, learn a form, settle a vote and have a tool mended, all at the same counter.

III
The modern house · official

兰芳百工院 · Academy of the Hundred Crafts

百工, the hundred crafts, is the classical name for every artisan under heaven, and it comes with a teacher already standing inside the house. Mozi, himself a craftsman, wrote that even the hundred crafts all work by standards: 百工为方以矩，为圆以规，直以绳，正以县. The square for corners, the compass for circles, the line for straightness, the plumb for true. The Mohist school sits at the heart of Lan Fang, and this name puts Mozi's standard over the whole house. Medicine becomes a craft with its measures. A martial form becomes a craft with its measures. A furnace, a brush, a needle and a fist are taught under one roof.

One house · three languages on the street

The city says Lan Fang three ways.

In English
Lan Fang

The name every PMU volume already uses. Unchanged.

In Chinese
兰坊

Same sound, different character. The city writes 芳, fragrance, as 坊, workshop. The orchid's scent became the orchid's workbench, and nobody can remember who changed it first.

In Malay
Kongsi

The oldest name, still alive in the bazaar, and it means to share.

The homophone carries the divergence. Chinese readers see the house drift from poetry to practice inside a single syllable, while English readers keep the name they know. Readers who hold both languages get the joke, and the history inside it.

A third reading, kept for later
The furnace, 炉, ties the house's three arts together in one object: the alchemist's elixir furnace, the smith's forge, and the martial idiom 炉火纯青, when the flame turns pure blue and the skill is finally perfected. It makes a fine name for the forge-and-pharmacy courtyard at the centre of the campus, and a fine word for what students call the place among themselves. As the house's public name it would narrow Lan Fang to fire, so it stays a nickname.
03 / The European houses

Toa Keng: guests at the ditch.

Toa Keng is the house where Europe's many interests at the Straits gathered: British, French, Germanic and Iberian merchants, notaries, lens-grinders, clerks and lawyers. In PMU they arrived as traders and never as conquerors. That one fact decides everything about the house's names.

The question behind the charter

If no European nation conquered, whose language names the shared house?

In our history, English became Europe's voice abroad because it travelled with an empire. In PMU there is no such empire. A British name would offend the French; a French name would offend the Germans. The one tongue every European scholar shared and no European nation owned was Latin.

Latin is the neutral ground. In a world without empire, it plausibly stayed Europe's scholarly language for much longer, the way it once held together universities from Bologna to Uppsala. So Toa Keng's charter speaks Latin, the language its quarrelling founders could all agree on.

I
The ditch · licensed trade

The Factories

The house begins plural. Each nation kept its own licensed trading house, its factory, comptoir, Kontor or feitoria, under the host city's law. Real history offers the model: the Hanseatic Kontors at Bergen and London, and the Thirteen Factories at Canton, where foreign merchants lived and traded inside rules they did not write. Toa Keng starts as several houses side by side, watching each other.

II
The pooling of clerks and scholars

The Collegium

Over time the factories' scholars pooled their libraries, lenses and law books into one college. A collegium is a body of colleagues bound by shared rules, and the root of the word means those chosen together. This is the age when Toa Keng learns to referee: a house founded by rivals has to become very good at fair procedure.

III
The modern house · official

Studium Generale of the Straits

In medieval Europe, a studium generale was a school open to students from anywhere, and its degrees carried the ius ubique docendi, the right to teach everywhere. For a house built on measurement, standards and fair judgment, that is the perfect inheritance. A Toa Keng certificate is honoured in every port, which suits the Straits as the place where the world's findings are collated and checked.

Why the street name is Hokkien

Toa Keng is the Hokkien 大坑, the big ditch. When guests arrive without conquering, the host decides where they live, and hosts tend to offer the edge of town: Canton gave the factories a strip outside its walls, and Nagasaki gave the Dutch an artificial island. The Straits gave its European guests the marshy ditch. The Hokkien traders next door named the quarter after it, and the name stuck long after the Europeans had drained, culverted and surveyed it into the best-measured ground in the city.

It also rhymes with Muara. The Malay house is named for the river mouth, and the European house for the ditch: the city names both by their water.

Chosen · officialStudium Generale

Open to all, valid everywhere. It names the house's purpose: standards that cross borders.

Considered · for a hallSpecula · Forum

Specula, the watchtower, from the root that gives us spectacles and perspective, is the perfect name for Toa Keng's great observatory and lens hall. Forum belongs to its moot court, where students argue and referees rule. Both stay as names of buildings inside the house.

04 / Arabic–Indic · the house of trust

Marikar: the boat-masters.

This house needed a new street name for a reason the alternative history forces on it. Merikani was the East African name for American mill cloth. In a world with no United States, that cloth never sails, and the word never forms.

What survives is older and better. In Tamil, marakkalam is a wooden ship, and the Marakkayar were its masters: seafaring merchant families of the Coromandel coast who traded across the Bay of Bengal into the Straits. Another account traces the name to the Arabic markab, also a ship. Two languages agree on the boat, which is exactly this house's lineage. The family name took many spellings along the way: Maricar, Marikkar, Marican, and in Penang, Merican. The city settles on Marikar.

FUNDUQ AL-AMANA → WAKALAT AL-AMANA → DAR AL-AMANA

The constant word is amana: a trust held on someone else's behalf. The house teaches finance, ledgers, the Chain of Keeping, and the art of making a stranger's promise good across an ocean, so every age of its charter is a different building for the same trust.

FunduqThe merchants' inn, where goods, letters and promises were lodged for the night. Venice borrowed the word as fondaco.
WakalaBoth a trading house, like the great wakalas of Cairo, and the contract of agency: trusting someone in a far port to act for you. One word for the building and the bond.
Dar al-AmanaThe House of Trust. The modern official name, and a name that already appears for this house in earlier volumes.
05 / Nordic · the house of joining

Sund: named for the water.

SUNDFÉLAG → SUNDVARVET → SUNDS HÖGSKOLA
I
The fellowship

Sundfélag

In Old Norse, a félag was a partnership in which members laid their property together, fé plus lag. It is where the English word fellow comes from. For the house of links and joining, the founding act is a pooling.

II
The yard

Sundvarvet

A varv is a shipyard. Sund's mastery of heat, metal and linkage was learned at the slipway, and the school grew inside the yard.

III
The modern house · official

Sunds Högskola

Högskola is the Nordic name for a school of higher learning, especially the technical ones. It fits the most modern of the seven houses.

The street name needs no change. Sund means strait. Nordic sailors knew exactly what a strait was worth: for four centuries every ship through the Øresund paid the Sound Dues. When they arrived at the Straits, they called it by their own word, and the city returned the favour by calling the Nordic school after the water the whole city stands on.

06 / Andean–Mesoamerican · the house of weather

Nine Rain: a day the city never forgot.

AYLLU → YACHAY WASI → HATUN YACHAY WASI

The charter is Quechua. The ayllu is the Andean kin community bound by ayni, reciprocity, and it is the right first age for a house whose magic is the give-and-take of moisture, friction and weather. The Yachay Wasi, the house of knowing, was the school in Cusco where the amautas taught. Hatun Yachay Wasi, the great house of knowing, is the word Quechua speakers use for a university today.

The street name keeps the other half of the house's lineage. Nine Rain, Chiucnahui Quiyahuitl, is a day in the Mesoamerican 260-day count, and people across Mesoamerica carried the names of the days they were born. The city remembers the house by the day it was founded. The Andes speak in the charter and Mexico speaks on the street, so the two halves of the Americas meet inside one school.

07 / Mande · the house of sound

Kora: the sound from the street.

SANÉ TON → SANÉ BLON → SANÉ BLONBA

Sané is the constant, the ruling lineage of Kaabu, held as this house's Budi Pusaka. A ton is a sworn association of peers; the state of Ségou itself grew out of one. The blon is the vestibule of a Mande house, the entrance hall where visitors are received, words are spoken and disputes are settled. At Kangaba, the sacred Kama Blon is re-roofed every seven years while the jelis of Kela recite the epic, a ritual of renewal that fits beside the Straits' own covenants. Blonba, the great vestibule, makes the hall of the word into an institution, and BlonBa is still the name of a cultural stage in Bamako today.

The street name is the easiest of all. You know you are near the house before you see it, because you hear the kora. GPT's drafts already call it Kora, and now the drift is canon.

08 / Across the seven

How the city names its schools.

Put the street names side by side and a pattern appears. The city has no single naming habit: it names houses by where they stand, who founded them, when they began and what they sound like.

MuaraThe river mouth.Malay · a place
Toa KengThe big ditch.Hokkien · a place
SundThe strait itself.Nordic · a place
MarikarThe masters of ships.Tamil · a people
Lan Fang · 兰坊 · KongsiThe orchid workshop, the sharing.Hakka, Chinese, Malay · a practice
Nine RainA founding day.Nahuatl · a time
KoraThe instrument you hear.Mande · a sound

Three houses are named for water. The three oldest settlers sit at the river mouth, the ditch and the strait, and the city's map of its schools reads like a map of its shoreline. The rest are named by the senses and the calendar, by what you meet when you walk past.

What this study proposes
  • Every house carries a three-age charter in its founding tongue and a street name in the city's.
  • Lan Fang's charter follows its crafts: 兰芳公司 → 兰芳堂 → 兰芳百工院, with 兰坊 as the city's written form.
  • Toa Keng's charter speaks Latin, the neutral tongue of rival European guests, and ends as a Studium Generale.
  • Al-Merikani's street name becomes Marikar, since the American cloth behind merikani never exists in PMU.
  • Sund, Nine Rain, Toa Keng, Lan Fang and Kora keep the names already used across PMU as their street names.

The Muara decision taught a simple thing: a school is written down by the people who found it and spoken by everyone else. Carried through the seven, it turns the Straits into a city where seven charters keep seven languages alive, while the bazaar hands out nicknames freely in all of them.

On paper: Taman Budi Pusaka, 兰芳百工院, Studium Generale, Dar al-Amana, Sunds Högskola, Hatun Yachay Wasi, Sané Blonba.
On the street: Muara, Lan Fang, Toa Keng, Marikar, Sund, Nine Rain, Kora.

Where the names come from

Lan Fang

  1. Mozi, 法仪 · Standards. The passage on the hundred crafts and their square, compass, line and plumb.
  2. The Lanfang Kongsi of west Borneo. The Hakka mining partnership the house is named after.
  3. New Asia College, 新亚书院. A modern school that kept the classical name.

Toa Keng

  1. Studium generale. The medieval open university and the right to teach everywhere.
  2. The Hanseatic Kontors and the Thirteen Factories of Canton. Foreign merchants living under host law.

Marikar

  1. The Marakkayar seafaring communities. The boat-master etymologies and the many spellings of the name.
  2. Marakkalam in Tamil. Boat, craft, ship, vessel.

Sund

  1. Fellow, from Old Norse félagi. The partner who lays property together.
  2. The Sound Dues. Four centuries of tolls on the Øresund.

Nine Rain

  1. The tonalpohualli. The 260-day count and its day signs, Rain among them.
  2. Peru's Constitutional Tribunal, press notes in Quechua. Hatun yachay wasi in use for a university.

Kora

  1. The Kaabu empire and the Ségou state, grown from a ton.
  2. Kangaba and the Kama Blon. The sacred vestibule and its seven-year renewal.
  3. BlonBa, Bamako. The great vestibule as a modern stage.
