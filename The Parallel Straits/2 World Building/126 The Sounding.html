The Polymathic Universe · Book Two · After the Veyr Retaliation
[The Sounding]雨露 · 一九三九
(the midsummer night the Straits came back to the Sound to sing over the water where it had made war: the burden it lifted from three ports and a province, the voices from across the world who carried it, the argument over Veyr, and the four songs sung every night after)

The hands that brought the thunder came back with the dew.

Claude5 November 2026 · canon draft from Linwei’s brief · from 93, 99, 120, 123, 124

The outcomeThe Sounding in seven lines

  1. Midsummer night, 1939, a.m. 21. On some four hundred boats in the Sound, where the Narrows Fleet was unbuilt, and on the beaches of three ports, a choir of thousands lifts the burden the war left behind.
  2. 雷霆, then 雨露. The Retaliation was the thunder. The Sounding is the dew. Together they complete 雷霆雨露, thunder and dew from one hand.
  3. What it lifts. The burden Veyr sank into Danzig, Riga, Visby and the Oder estates, and the Straits’ own war workings still in force.
  4. How. Every voice takes a sliver. The ports’ own people hold the drone, pitched on the last note of Nyara Koyaté, the jeli who died at Veyr.
  5. Who. Pol Robesi proposed it, Beek keeps the beat, and the voices include al-Sitt Kawthar, Mei Wan, Jango Reinholt, the castrato Granello and a net-mender of Riga.
  6. The argument. Whether to sing over Veyr. Tenin Koyaté, Nyara’s student, sings the last verse, and Veyr’s reservoirs fill.
  7. Dawn. Granello begins to sing the sleeping Warden awake: four songs, every night, for nine years, until he wakes in 1948 to stand before the Court of Workings.

Where it sitsBetween the Sound and the Covenant

The Chronicle’s 狭海, 1934–1939, ends with the Veyr Retaliation. The Sounding follows it that midsummer and opens “After the Sound”: the first Straits students to go abroad sail as its chorus.

VIThe nightVIIIThe voicesXThe WardenIXFrom our world

The Retaliation ended the war in one night, and fewer than three hundred people died. The doctrine of the Senyap Works had held: unmake, do not kill. But unmaking ends a war without clearing what the war left. When the fleet was gone and the Warden asleep, the burden Veyr had sunk into other people’s land was still lying there, and so were the Straits’ own workings, cast to starve an army and still doing it.

Weeks later, letters came from Riga about wells that tasted of iron and children who woke screaming. The houses could lift that burden only by sending it somewhere else, and harm sent where nobody had agreed to carry it was the very thing Veyr had done. So they did what the Straits has always done when the load is too heavy for one ship. They shared it out among everyone willing, and the way they shared it was a song.

This volume tells that night: how it was proposed and argued over, who came to sing and from how far, what each verse lifted, and the one verse almost nobody wanted to sing. It ends at dawn, with an old man walking up to a castle to sing to the one person the night could not reach.

Part One

What the war left 战后

The ledger after the Sound, and the name for what came next.

I
After the Sound · 1939

Four kinds of burden

Unmake, do not kill, left the dead few and the burden where it lay.

Sunk by Veyr
  • Reversed recipes cost three times over, and Veyr sent the cost into the old Oder estates.
  • Then into the ports it took: Danzig, Riga, Visby. Wells taste of iron, children sleep badly, nets come up empty.
Held by Veyr
  • Craftsmen and workshops carried off from the three ports, freed at the Sound and still far from home.
  • Its war council, awake and closed by Konggu, unable to speak an order.
Left by the Straits
  • Ground Says No, still holding the Oder estates barren.
  • The Drought Account on Veyr’s reservoirs.
  • The buoyed edges of the Hush in the Sound.
  • Veyr’s land, resting under the terms of the peace.
Asleep
  • The Warden of the Narrows. The garrisons woke a day after the battle and the council woke the next. He did not.
  • He had cast the reversed recipes with his own hands. When the Forgetting took them back, their burden settled on him, and it holds him under.
II
The name

雷霆, then 雨露

The Retaliation was the thunder. This is the dew.

雷霆雨露

An old court saying holds that 雷霆雨露，俱是君恩: thunder and dew alike are the sovereign’s grace. Punishment and mercy come from the same hand. The Straits gave the Retaliation the first two characters and the Sounding the second two, and the world read the pair exactly as the saying means it.

Gratitude and fear arrived together. The power that could unbuild a fleet in silence could also take a coast’s burden into its own singers’ bodies, and the ports that were healed that night understood both halves.

EnglishThe Sounding

After the Battle of the Sound. To sound is to make music, and also to measure how deep the water runs.

Rain and dew. Beside 雷霆, the Retaliation’s name, it completes 雷霆雨露.

LatinRos post Fulmen

Dew after the thunderbolt.

Arabicالطلّ

al-Ṭall, the light dew, enough to keep a garden green after the heavy rain has passed.

ii
Part Two

The making 成曲

Proposed in one sentence, organised by ledger, and argued over in every rehearsal.

III
The Convocation Hall · spring 1939

All of us, a little 众担

The idea came from a singer who had never seen the land his own grandparents left.

When the letters from Riga were read in the Convocation Hall, the houses argued for a day about where the burden could go. Every answer sent it somewhere. Pol Robesi, a bass of Sané-Kora who was there to sing at the evening service, stood up from the back and said: then we carry it ourselves, all of us, a little.

Matron Gonxhe had spent twenty years teaching exactly that: the Commons, many hands lifting one working, each according to what it can bear. Her method gave the idea its mechanics. A share small enough is worked off in a few days of tiredness, like hard labour, and enough shares can carry a province.

Ibn Muqaddim took the ledger. Every singer signed for a share. No share fell on anyone who had not signed, and the founders and the house masters took the heaviest. Beek took the console. He chose Midsummer, the shortest night on the Sound, when the sky barely goes dark, because the ports would be awake anyway.

The invitations went out by Long Song to the partner schools and beyond them, to anyone whose voice could hold a coast. They reached Cairo, Peking, Naples, a caravan camp outside Paris, and the beaches of the three ports themselves.

IV
The boathouse on the Sound

The door and the drone 门 · 长音

Every house brought its own scale, and nobody could share a chord.

Leave your house at the door.Beek’s sign on the boathouse door, in seven languages

Titles, house colours and instruments stayed outside. Only voices went in. Jango Reinholt, who has no house, read the sign, laughed, and carried his guitar in under his arm. It stayed: the one instrument in the room.

The tuning problemSeven houses, seven scales

Lan Fang sings in 律吕. Al-Merikani’s singers move between notes the tempered piano does not have. Nine Rain’s and Sri Temenggong’s gongs ring in scales no European ear had names for, the kora tunes its own way, and the Baltic coast sings to the kokle. Beek spent a week trying to write one chord they could all stand in, and gave up.

They could not share a chord. They could share a drone. One long held note sits under any scale, and each tradition floats its own above it. Beek chose the pitch: the last note Nyara Koyaté sang in the toll house at Veyr, which made every memory string forget what it held. The Forgetting at the Sound had been tuned to it. Now the whole coast would tune to it.

The drone went to the people of the ports, who could hold it humming. It asked nothing of them but breath, and the Commons made their slivers one working. The great voices sang over it. Ordinary people held the ground, and most of the burden went down into it.

V
Three weeks of rehearsal

Rehearsals 排练

The hardest part of the night was getting everyone into it.

The wrong language 错语

Munsyi Kadir wrote the refrain in Low German, the language of the Hansa merchants the Straits had traded with for five centuries, and the language the Straits had always heard the Baltic in. At the first rehearsal Ilze Kalniņa, a net-mender from Riga, stood up and said that was what the merchants spoke, and the merchants’ landlords. Then she sang the refrain in Latvian, to a tune her grandmother used for a daina about drawing in nets. Kadir’s Common Tongue could let a hall understand itself, but a song binds in one tongue. Hers is the version recorded, and every port sang its own verse in its own language.

Konggu’s line 一句

The working has to be opened, and opening is Konggu’s art. But the Kumatigi is a master of the word who never sings, and for a week he could not find the note. Al-Sitt Kawthar listened to him speak for an afternoon, then sang his line back to him in the rise and fall of his own speech. He spoke it on pitch the next morning.

The chairs 空椅

It was still the year of the Empty Desks, when every chair the dead had left stays empty. So seven chairs went onto the first boat, one for each teacher who died at Veyr. Nobody suggested filling them. Nyara’s was set at the front, where a lead singer would stand.

The verse nobody would rehearse 一节

Kadir wrote a verse for Veyr itself. It was on the program, and in three weeks nobody rehearsed it. Bala Koyaté, Nyara’s kinsman, would not have it sung in the boathouse. Beek left it in the score and said the night would decide.

The castrato arrives late 迟到

Granello came by sea from Naples four days before Midsummer, fifty-six years old, carrying nothing but a coat. At his first rehearsal he held one note through a whole circuit of the boathouse clock, and the room stopped arguing for the rest of the day.

iii
Part Three

The night 一夜

Midsummer on the Sound, from the names at dusk to the castle at sunrise.

VI
Midsummer night 1939 · a.m. 21

Dusk to dawn 黄昏至黎明

Times are as the Sané-Kora recorders marked them. The bar down the side is the drone, Nyara’s note, under everything.

21:40dusk
The boats gather

Some four hundred boats anchor over the deep water where the Narrows Fleet came apart. The buoys that marked the edge of the Hush still ride there. The Straits sailors pull them up first, one by one, and stack them in the bows.

Seven empty chairs stand on the first boat, Nyara’s in front.

22:05sunset
The names

Bala Koyaté sings the names of the seven teachers and the five students, each with their house, their subject and the town they came from. Nothing else is sung until he finishes.

22:30
The drone rises长音

Nyara’s last note, carried by the Long Song to the shores of Riga, Danzig and Visby, where tens of thousands of people are waiting on the beaches. They take it up, humming. Copenhagen joins, and then the villages of the Oder estates. Each person holds a sliver of the burden, small enough for anyone, and Matron Gonxhe’s Commons makes the slivers one working.

Everyone can do this. Most of the burden lifted that night went into the drone.

23:10
Konggu opens开

He speaks his line on the note al-Sitt Kawthar taught him, the first time anyone has heard him on pitch, and the working opens. So does the Close he laid on Veyr’s war council at the Sound. In a locked hall in Veyr, men who have not been able to give an order since the battle find their voices again. The first thing any of them says is to ask for water.

23:40
The three ports三港

Each port has its own verse, in its own tongue. Al-Sitt Kawthar holds Riga’s for forty minutes, one phrase turned and turned again until the beach is one body; the iron taste goes out of the wells. Mei Wan sings Danzig’s as a mother calling her children in from the shore. Visby’s fishermen sing theirs to Jango’s guitar. The stolen artisans, freed a week before, stand in the boats and hear their towns sing back.

00:30the darkest hour
The Oder estates地还

Ground Says No was the Straits’ own working: at the Sound it made the estates that fed Veyr’s army refuse to grow. Veyr had already sunk years of reversed burden into the same soil. Now the Straits releases its refusal and lifts Veyr’s burden in one verse, and its own singers take the heaviest shares. Pol Robesi sings the low line.

The founders carry more here than anyone, by Ibn Muqaddim’s ledger.

01:20
The argument争

The verse for Veyr is written and unsung. The terms of the peace say Veyr’s land rests until every stolen artisan is home, and the Straits’ Drought Account still holds its reservoirs low. Half the singers will not sing for the port that killed their teachers. Bala Koyaté says he will sing truth over Veyr and never praise. The boats wait, and the drone holds the whole time.

01:55
The last verse发

Tenin Koyaté stands and sings Nyara’s lineage over Veyr: whose daughter she was, what she taught, the note she sang last. The Drought Account lifts as she sings, and the reservoirs begin to fill. Veyr’s land keeps its rest. Veyr’s people will drink, and they will drink with her name.

From Copenhagen, the old toll families, who fled Veyr when the arsenal faction took the council, row out and join the drone for this verse. Most of their children have never seen the town.

Bala Koyaté does not sing it. He stands beside her the whole time.

03:30first light
The refrain合

Everyone, boats and beaches, sings the refrain in Ilze Kalniņa’s Latvian. Jango plays the light up. Granello takes the top line one last time, and holds the final note longer than anyone has breath for.

04:25sunrise
Up to the castle醒

Granello is rowed to the quay and walks up to the castle where the Warden of the Narrows has slept since the battle. At the hour the Sounding ended he sings four songs, and he will sing the same four at the same hour every night for nine years.

VII
Afterwards

As each side tells it 各说

A night like this is remembered in as many ways as there were people on the water.

The StraitsThunder and dew from one hand

Some in the houses felt the Sounding as a debt paid. Others felt it as the war finished properly. Beek, who kept the beat on both nights, said it was the same music.

The three portsSaved, and watching

The beaches sang with their whole hearts. They also learned that the people who can lift a burden from a coast can lay one, and they kept that knowledge.

Veyr’s peopleWater from the people who dried it

They drank, and they knew whose name was in the water. Some resented that more than the drought.

The toll familiesSinging for a town they left

They had sailed for Copenhagen when the arsenal faction took the council. Their children sang for a home they knew from stories, a little out of tune.

The dropped schoolsA pageant of power

The schools the Straits had dropped since the Congress called it theatre: the cartel singing because it can. The word cartel was said louder after Midsummer.

The families of the deadTruth, never praise

Bala refused and Tenin sang, and both were right. The Koyaté family still tells it both ways.

Part Four

The voices 众声

Fourteen of the voices, and the people in our world they are drawn from.

VIII
The cast

Fourteen voices

Founders already in the canon have solid borders. Voices proposed in this volume have dashed ones.

Sané-Kora · bass

Pol Robesi

after Paul Robeson

Born in the Straits to a Mande trading family, he has never seen the lands his grandparents left. He sings in twenty-three languages and learned most of them from the people whose songs they are.

He proposed it, in the Convocation Hall, in one sentence: then we carry it ourselves, all of us, a little.

Sané-Kora · Keeper of the Third Voice

Sebastian Beek

after Johann Sebastian Bach

From two unlike voices he composes a third that binds. At the Sound he held Veyr’s army between two beats.

He takes the console, hangs the sign on the boathouse door, and keeps the beat for the whole night. The same hand, both nights.

Sund · Rector

Matron Gonxhe

after Mother Teresa

Her method is the Commons: how many ordinary hands join one working, each lifting the share it can bear.

The drone is her Commons sung. She walks the shore at Riga all night among the people holding it.

Al-Merikani · Nāẓir

Ibn Muqaddim

after Ibn Khaldun

He reads the bonds that hold a group together, and the moment they begin to weaken.

He keeps the ledger of shares, so that every singer signs for what they carry and no share falls on anyone who did not agree to it.

Sané-Kora · Kumatigi

Konggu空谷子

after 鬼谷子 Guiguzi

A master of the word who never sings. At the Sound he closed Veyr’s war council, and it could give no order until he opened it again.

The working has to be opened, and opening is his art. He cannot find the note until al-Sitt Kawthar gives it to him.

Sané-Kora · Jeli kuntigi

Bala Koyaté

after Balla Fasséké

The chief of the griots, who sings history until it acts. Nyara Koyaté was his kinswoman.

He sings the names of the seven teachers and the five students at dusk. He will not sing praise over Veyr, and says so.

Sri Temenggong · Keeper of Letters

Munsyi Kadir

after Munshi Abdullah

A son of the Straits who carries meaning between languages. His Common Tongue lets a hall understand itself.

He writes the refrain, in the wrong language. A song binds in one tongue, so his Common Tongue cannot save him, and a net-mender does.

Cairo · paired with Al-Merikani

al-Sitt Kawthar

after Umm Kulthum

The Lady. On the first Thursday of the month whole cities stop to hear her, and she can repeat a single line until a crowd is one body.

She holds Riga’s verse for forty minutes on one phrase, and teaches Konggu his opening by singing it in the cadence of his own speech.

Peking · paired with Lan Fang

Mei Wan梅畹

after 梅兰芳 Mei Lanfang

The great dan of his age, a man whose women on stage are more exact than life. His name shares two characters with the house of Lan Fang, and the house never lets him forget it.

He sings Danzig’s verse as a mother calling her children in from the shore, and Danzig’s mothers answer him.

No house · guitar

Jango Reinholt

after Django Reinhardt

A Romani guitarist who lost two fingers in a caravan fire and found a way to play around the wound. He belongs to no school and no state.

He reads the sign on the door, laughs, and carries his guitar in. It is the one instrument in the room, and it plays in every tuning.

Naples · with Toa Keng’s partners

Granello

after Farinelli (Carlo Broschi), and Alessandro Moreschi

Born in Naples in 1883 and cut as a boy, before the Kindling. After it, Lan Fang and Sané-Kora learned to hold a boy’s voice unbroken without a knife, so he is the last voice of his kind. His breath seems to have no end.

He sings the top line the whole burden rises through. At dawn he is carried up to the castle above the quay, and begins.

Riga · net-mender

Ilze Kalniņa

after the women who kept the dainas

She mends nets on the Daugava and knows a few thousand four-line songs by heart, as her grandmother did.

She stands up in rehearsal and sings the refrain in Latvian. Hers is the version recorded.

Sané-Kora · student

Tenin Koyaté

Nyara’s niece and student, one of the seven students Veyr sent home in the spring as a message.

She sings the last verse, over Veyr.

Sané-Kora · jeli and teacher

Nyara Koyaté

in memory

She sang her lessons into memory strings. When Veyr took the strings, she sang one last note, and every string forgot what it held. She died in the toll house on the quay.

Her chair on the first boat stays empty. Her last note is the drone.

IX
Precedents

From our world

Nine people, places and nights behind the Sounding. Each card unfolds.

When
The night of 28 January 1985
Where
A&M Studios, Hollywood

We Are the World

Forty-six of the biggest names in American music, one room, one night, one sign on the door.

Harry Belafonte wanted Black artists to answer the Ethiopian famine the way Band Aid had in Britain. Ken Kragen, Lionel Richie’s manager, turned the idea into a single. Michael Jackson and Richie wrote the song, and Quincy Jones produced it. The singers came straight from the American Music Awards and recorded until morning.

Read moreFold

Quincy Jones taped a sign at the door: check your ego at the door. Bob Dylan could not find his line until Stevie Wonder sat at the piano and sang it back to him in Dylan’s own voice. Stevie also proposed a line in Swahili. Waylon Jennings reportedly left, and someone pointed out that Ethiopians speak Amharic, so the line was dropped. Prince never came.

Released in March 1985, the single became one of the best-selling records ever made, and USA for Africa raised tens of millions of dollars for famine relief. The room was full of people singing for a place most of them had never seen, and the song outlived every argument about it. The 2024 documentary The Greatest Night in Pop tells the night from the inside.

Farinelli and the sleepless king

The most famous voice in Europe, booked for one listener, every night, for nearly ten years.

Carlo Broschi, called Farinelli, was the greatest castrato of his age, trained in Naples under Nicola Porpora and famous for breath that seemed to have no end. In 1737 Queen Elisabeth Farnese brought him to Spain to reach her husband, Philip V, sunk in a melancholy that kept him from his bed, his barber and his council.

Read moreFold

By the old account Farinelli sang the same few songs to the king every night until Philip died in 1746. Whatever the exact count, the effect was real enough that the court kept him on. The king began to wash, to sleep and to sign papers again, and Farinelli became one of the most trusted people in Spain.

Under Ferdinand VI he ran the royal opera and the festivals at Aranjuez. He left Spain in 1759, retired to Bologna, and died there in 1782, visited by the music historian Charles Burney and by the fourteen-year-old Mozart. The story of the voice prescribed like medicine is the seed of Granello’s nine years.

Rome and the Sistine Chapel

The last castrato on record

The only castrato voice anyone alive has heard comes off a crackling wax disc.

Alessandro Moreschi (1858–1922) sang in the Sistine Chapel choir for three decades, the most celebrated soprano in Rome. In 1902 and 1904 the Gramophone Company recorded him in Rome. Those discs are the only recordings of a castrato ever made.

Read moreFold

By then the practice was ending. Italy had outlawed castration, and in 1903 Pope Pius X’s reform of church music closed the papal choir to new castrati. Moreschi retired in 1913 as the last of a line that had shaped European singing for three hundred years.

The recordings are hard listening: an older singer, primitive equipment, a style of sobbing ornament the century had forgotten. They are also the closest anyone can come to the sound Farinelli made. In PMU, Granello is drawn from both men, the healer and the last of his kind.

Paul Robeson, the bass in twenty languages

Athlete, lawyer, actor, bass, and a man who learned other peoples’ languages so he could sing their songs to them.

Paul Robeson was the son of a man who had escaped slavery. He was a star athlete and a law graduate before his bass voice made him famous in Show Boat and The Emperor Jones. In 1930s London he studied African languages at the School of Oriental Studies and began singing folk songs in Russian, Yiddish, Chinese, Welsh and many more.

Read moreFold

He rewrote the words of Ol’ Man River from resignation into defiance, and in 1938 he sang to the International Brigades at the front in Spain. Welsh miners took him as one of their own. He sang for workers in their own languages because, he said, the songs of the world’s peoples shared one root.

His politics cost him his passport in the 1950s, and he sang to Wales over a transatlantic telephone line when he could not travel. He is the model for Pol Robesi: a diaspora voice whose ancestry lay on one continent, singing for peoples on another.

Umm Kulthum’s Thursdays

On the first Thursday of the month, the streets of the Arab world emptied for one woman.

Umm Kulthum, Kawkab al-Sharq, the Star of the East, grew up singing religious songs in a Nile Delta village, dressed as a boy at her father’s side. Her monthly concerts, broadcast live from Cairo, ran for hours: a single song could last most of an evening.

Read moreFold

Her art was tarab, the ecstatic state a singer and a listening crowd reach together. She would repeat one line again and again, changing it each time in response to the audience until the hall was in a kind of trance. Her songs with Mohammed Abdel Wahab, above all Enta Omri in 1964, are still sung across the region.

When she died in 1975, millions followed her funeral through Cairo. In PMU she becomes al-Sitt Kawthar, the voice that can hold a whole city still. Al-Sitt, the Lady, is what Egyptians called her.

Mei Lanfang abroad

The greatest dan of Peking opera taught the West’s theatre makers a new way to be on stage.

梅兰芳 Mei Lanfang (1894–1961) played women’s roles, 旦, in Peking opera, and he reshaped the form. His 1930 tour of the United States sold out, and two American universities gave him honorary doctorates.

Read moreFold

In Moscow in 1935 his performances were watched by Stanislavski, Meyerhold, Eisenstein and Bertolt Brecht. Brecht’s essay on Chinese acting, written after seeing him, became one root of his theory of distance on stage. A man playing a woman with perfect control showed Europe’s directors that a performer can stand beside a role and still move an audience.

When Japan occupied Shanghai and Hong Kong he grew a moustache so that he could not be made to perform for the occupiers, 蓄须明志, and he did not sing in public for eight years. His courtesy name was 畹华 Wanhua. In PMU he becomes 梅畹 Mei Wan, who shares two characters with the house of Lan Fang.

Django, two fingers and a new music

A fire took two of his fingers, so he invented a way to play the guitar with the rest.

Django Reinhardt was born into a Manouche Romani family on the road in Belgium. In 1928 a fire in his caravan badly burned his left hand, and two fingers never worked again. He relearned the guitar around the injury and created a style no one could copy.

Read moreFold

In 1934 he and the violinist Stéphane Grappelli formed the Quintette du Hot Club de France, string jazz with no drums and no horns, now called gypsy jazz. Nuages, recorded in 1940, became a song of occupied Paris. His people had no state and were persecuted across Europe; he survived the war playing.

His name, Django, is often glossed as Romani for I awake. In PMU he becomes Jango Reinholt, the guitarist who belongs to no house and plays in every tuning, and his name runs through a night that ends with a sleeper beginning to wake.

The singing Baltic

Two hundred thousand songs kept by fishers and farmers, and a revolution won by singing.

Latvian peasants lived for centuries under German-speaking merchants and landlords and kept their own world in dainas, four-line songs about work, the sun, weddings and the dead. Krišjānis Barons gathered them into Latvju dainas, published 1894–1915, with more than two hundred thousand texts.

Read moreFold

The first Latvian Song Festival met in Riga in 1873, and tens of thousands still sing together at it. On Jāņi, midsummer night, people stay up singing līgo songs until the sun returns. In 1987–1991 Estonians, Latvians and Lithuanians sang their way out of the Soviet Union, and on 23 August 1989 about two million people held hands in a line across all three countries, the Baltic Way.

This is the coast the Straits came to sing for, and in PMU’s Swahili moment the Straits writes its refrain in the merchants’ German. Ilze Kalniņa, a net-mender of Riga, answers with the language the dainas were kept in.

The Sound Dues at Elsinore

For four centuries every ship into the Baltic stopped at one castle and paid.

Eric of Pomerania began collecting a toll on every ship passing the Øresund, the narrow Sound between Denmark and Sweden, around 1429. Ships anchored under the guns of Kronborg at Helsingør, declared their cargo and paid. The toll funded the Danish crown for centuries.

Read moreFold

The dues were abolished by the Copenhagen Convention of 1857, and their registers survive as one of the great records of early modern trade. Kronborg is Shakespeare’s Elsinore, where Hamlet’s father walks the battlements.

In PMU, Veyr is the port that lived on these dues and was ruined when wind-roads let ships sail past without stopping. Its Warden sleeps in the castle above the quay, and Granello sings to him there.

1939–1948 · a.m. 21–30

Nine years of four songs 九年四曲

The one person the night could not reach.

Hua He’s Long Sleep was meant to last a day. On the Warden of the Narrows it did not lift, and Hua He said plainly that the sleep was no longer his. The Warden had cast the reversed recipes himself, and when the Forgetting took them back, their burden came home to the one body that had sent it out. It held him under.

The houses would not reach into his mind to pull him up. The rule they were already writing for the Charter forbade working any mind without consent. A song asks nothing and compels nothing; it can only be there when the sleeper starts to climb. So Granello sang.

He sang four songs every night at the hour the Sounding ended, in a room in the castle above the quay where the teachers had died: a Neapolitan lullaby, an aria from his youth, the Sounding’s refrain in Ilze’s Latvian, and last, Nyara’s note, held for as long as his breath would go. In nine years he missed eleven nights, all of them in one winter when he was ill.

The Warden woke in the spring of 1948, a.m. 30, weeks after the Monsoon Charter was signed. The Court of Workings had its first case. Granello sang the four songs once more, that evening, to an empty room, and sailed home to Naples.

XI
After Midsummer

The Midsummer Record 长夏之录

They left something the world kept singing.

The record 录

Sané-Kora’s phonic devices recorded the whole night. Copies went to every port that sang. Artefacts of the time lasted six months, so each port renews its copy at Midsummer and Midwinter by singing along with it, and the record has never stopped working. Riga still sings it on Jāņi.

The chorus abroad 游学

The first Straits students ever sent abroad sailed as the Sounding’s chorus. When it ended, they stayed with the partner schools around the Baltic, and the two-way exchange the Congress had promised began.

Home by boat 归

The week after Midsummer, the Sounding’s boats carried the stolen artisans of Danzig, Riga and Visby home with their tools.

The Covenant table 发馨合盟

In 1946, when the first circle came home and the jars were opened, the refrain was sung at the table before anyone ate.

The Ninth Night 九夜

When Clean Paper fell due around 1947, Sané-Kora’s choirs woke the sleeping islands, and millions of households each gave one almost imperceptible measure. The Ninth Night is the Sounding done with lamps.

The Charter 季风宪章

No mind worked without consent; land may refuse and is never poisoned; no Hush on a whole people. The Sounding was the first time the Straits lived by those rules, nine years before it wrote them.
