[The Seven Before the Kindling]燃灯之前
(Book One’s foundations, retold: how seven houses of learning gathered on the Straits over five centuries, carried by trade, held by relationships, and ready on the eve of 1918)

Every house came because someone was already there to learn from, and stayed because someone else arrived.

Wei LinClaudedrawing on GPT 111–1144 November 2026 · PMU-bm · ends on the eve of the Kindling

The outcomeSeven houses, gathered by 1820

  1. Sri Temenggong · 1300s · b.m. c. 600. The ground. Mangrove, tide, water and plant knowledge; every road lands here.
  2. Al-Merikani · c. 1500 · b.m. c. 418. The house of trust. Credit, navigation, contract; Granada’s books arrive after 1492.
  3. Toa Keng · c. 1600 · b.m. c. 318. The translation and comparison house. Europe’s licensed surveyors, mathematicians and mapmakers work here.
  4. Lan Fang · 1786 · b.m. 132. The kongsi’s house of making and medicine, with the Mozi College inside it.
  5. Nine Rain · c. 1795 · b.m. c. 123. Water and weather from Anahuac and Tawantinsuyu, across the Pacific.
  6. Sané-Kora · c. 1810 · b.m. c. 108. Voice, testimony and memory from Timbuktu, by the Red Sea and the Swahili coast.
  7. Sund · 1820 · b.m. 98. The Norden lodge of 1587 becomes the Commons: ships, heat, metal and the shared load.

What this edition settlesFive foundations

  1. Relationships are the founding logic. The Straits has no hinterland and no resources; its schools are how it keeps relations with the world.
  2. Trade carried thought. Each house rode in on a commercial road, and the goods paid for the learning.
  3. One school is another’s reason to come. Scholars arrived with questions and stayed because the Straits already held part of the answer.
  4. Translation bred invention. Two traditions describing the same thing differently gave birth to new methods, and to the envoys who carried them out.
  5. The order of founding is the order of arrival. A young house can carry an ancient lineage.

b.m. and a.m. Years before magic count back from the Kindling of 1918: 1917 is b.m. 1, 1786 is b.m. 132. Years after count forward: 1918 is a.m. 1, 2026 is a.m. 109. Both are written in italics.

One
Part One · The outcome

[The Seven on the Eve]

(each house as it stands in 1917, b.m. 1: where it came from, what it knows, what it trades, who it answers to abroad)

I
Thread I

The seven on the eve前夜七院

Seven houses, seven roads, one harbour. On the eve of magic they are scholarly institutions with trading arms and friends in every port.

01 · 1300s · b.m. c. 600

Sri Temenggong天猛公

The ground every road lands on.

Root
The Malay coast and the Temenggong’s line, keepers of harbour, land and water. The one house that named itself, Balai Pusaka, before anyone else could.
Knows
Mangroves, tides, freshwater, plants in place, seasons and the duties that come with using them. Ecology and land management lead.
Trades
Water, provisions and harbour custody; camphor, rattan, aloeswood, betel and jamu.
Tied to
The courts and coasts of the archipelago; every ship that needs to drink.
02 · c. 1500 · b.m. c. 418

Al-Merikani美里加尼

A house of trust that lent the city its credit.

Root
Gujarati, Tamil, Hadhrami and Omani merchant houses, victors at Diu, joined after 1492 by Andalusi exiles carrying Granada’s libraries east.
Knows
Calculation, navigation, astronomy, contract, languages and amānah: what is held in trust.
Trades
Credit, cloth, letters of exchange, endowments; it pays passage and lodging for visiting scholars.
Tied to
Aden, Gujarat, Calicut, Cairo, Hormuz and the Swahili ports.
03 · c. 1600 · b.m. c. 318

Toa Keng大港

Where Europe came to learn, on licence.

Root
A translation and comparison house sponsored by Temenggong and Al-Merikani patrons. Europe’s licensed guests become its successive contributors: surveyors, botanists, mapmakers and travelling mathematicians, Jesuits among them.
Knows
Translation, measurement, calendars, optics and survey; the art of adjudicating between two descriptions.
Trades
Charts, instruments, lenses, calendars, licences and the harbour registry.
Tied to
Lisbon, Seville, Amsterdam, London, Paris and the German courts, all as guests: a licensed enlightenment.
04 · 1786 · b.m. 132

Lan Fang兰芳

A miners’ republic opened a school, and made it a workshop.

Root
Hakka shareholders of the west Borneo kongsi, from the hills of Jiaying by the Pearl River. Founded as the kongsi reached out to the Ming court; Dayak and Peranakan families married in from the start.
Knows
Craft and making, Chinese medicine, the martial arts; the Mozi College of makers and engineers, seeded by the Quanzhou shipwright guilds that built the Charter fleet.
Trades
Kongsi shares, Borneo gold, herbs and prepared medicines, tools and workshop goods.
Tied to
Quanzhou, Guangzhou, the Hakka hills, west Borneo.
05 · c. 1795 · b.m. c. 123

Nine Rain九雨

Named for a date. The city heard weather.

Root
Scholars of Anahuac and Tawantinsuyu on the Concord’s eastern run, with Filipino and Ryukyuan crews. A small visiting programme that stayed: a teacher, students, a shared room, a calendar.
Knows
Rain, rivers, tides, highland and lowland water, calendar and weave; ayni, the gift that must come back.
Trades
Silver, cinchona bark, cacao, copal, textiles; the Pacific crossing itself.
Tied to
Cusco, Quito, Tenochtitlan, Luzon, Ryukyu.
06 · c. 1810 · b.m. c. 108

Sané-Kora萨内-科拉

The neighbours named it for what they heard through the wall.

Root
Jeli families and Timbuktu scholars. Songhai endured, and its university city sent voices east by the pilgrim roads, the Red Sea and the Swahili coast.
Knows
Oral transmission, testimony, genealogy, language, sound and performance; how a community remembers precisely.
Trades
Gold, kola, shea, manuscripts and paper; and witness itself, for contracts that need a memory.
Tied to
Timbuktu, Gao, Cairo, Kilwa, Mombasa.
07 · 1820 · b.m. 98

Sund松德

Keepers of one strait, recognised by keepers of another.

Root
The Norden lodge, granted residence by the Concord in 1587, the only European house allowed to stay. In 1820 its shipwrights and Orang Laut and Bugis pilots form a cooperative teaching yard: the Commons.
Knows
Ship repair, tools, materials, heat, iron and maintenance; the shared load, where many hands carry one strain.
Trades
Pine tar, timber, rope, iron; ship repair for every flag in the harbour.
Tied to
The Øresund and the Sound Compact, the Baltic yards.
Two
Part Two · Before the schools

[The Ground and the Roads]

(what was on the Straits before any school, the decision of 1426 that kept the ocean many, and the seven roads that led in)

II
Thread II

The ground and the Turn地与转

A trading port on the old ground of Temasek, and an ocean that learned to trade on terms.

Before there were schools there was a harbour, and people who knew it.

In the 1300s, b.m. c. 600, Temasek is already a port inside the regional and Chinese sea networks. The people of the coast know where the fresh water runs, which mangrove edge holds in a storm, what each plant does and when it may be taken. Recognised teachers, apprentices and field sites come long before any charter. This knowledge is the first reason outsiders seek the Straits.

Then the ocean takes its Turn. In 1426, b.m. 492, the Treasure-Ship Charter hands Zheng He’s fleet to the merchant houses of Fujian and Guangdong, and the voyages never stop. In 1435, b.m. 483, the ports bind themselves at Malacca into the Monsoon Concord: a shared licence, a common fleet and a court of arbitration. Trade on terms becomes the ocean’s ethic. When Portugal comes, the Concord turns it back at Diu in 1509, and the Diu Settlement of 1510 lets Europeans trade only as licensed guests from unfortified factories.

Malacca never falls, so the Malay court is never displaced. When the Concord’s seat moves to the island in 1819, b.m. 99, the Temenggong’s line already holds custody of its harbour, land and water.

The Concord made the first relational hinterland: a sea of ports that chose to act together. The schools grew inside it.

III
Thread III

Seven roads in七路

Every house arrived along a road that trade had already cut. The people came before the school.

Sri Temenggong · the ground itself. No road in: the road ends here. The house grows from coastal expertise into a teaching community, and outsiders marry into it far more than it marries out.
Al-Merikani · Granada, Cairo, Aden, Gujarat, Calicut. The Concord’s trust networks need somewhere to keep accounts, train navigators and settle quarrels. After Granada falls in 1492, b.m. 426, Andalusi exiles arrive at Concord ports with their libraries, and the house gains its astronomers and its books.
Toa Keng · Lisbon, the Cape, the Straits. Licensed European guests need interpreters, and Straits patrons need to understand what the guests know. Francis Xavier passes through the Straits in 1552, b.m. 366; travelling mathematicians follow. A visiting connection becomes a resident partnership, because the translation work outlasts every single visit.
Lan Fang · Jiaying, the Pearl River, west Borneo. Hakka miners found their shareholder republic in west Borneo in 1777, b.m. 141. Nine years later the kongsi opens a house on the Straits, part of its outreach to the Ming court, to organise translators, teachers, craftsmen and physicians across a wider network.
Nine Rain · Cusco and Tenochtitlan, across the Pacific, Ryukyu, Luzon. Anahuac expels the Castilians in 1520 and Tawantinsuyu settles its civil war under two capitals in 1532. The Pacific crossing keeps its silver and its scholars. A visiting programme comes to compare water across climates; Sri’s coast and Lan Fang’s medicine give it reasons to stay.
Sané-Kora · Timbuktu, Cairo, the Red Sea, the Swahili coast. Morocco’s army breaks on the Saharan crossing in 1591 and Songhai endures. Timbuktu remains a university city. Invited scholars and jeli families come to teach memory and testimony, and to learn how other peoples keep their records.
Sund · the Øresund, the Cape, the Straits. The Kalmar Union becomes the Sound Compact in 1523, living off the Sound Dues. Keepers of one strait recognise keepers of another, and in 1587, b.m. 331, the Norden lodge is granted residence, without guns, on the Concord’s terms. Its shipwrights work beside local pilots for two centuries before they found a school.

Part Three · The scholarly traditions

[Why Scholars Came]

(what each house knew, what it needed from the others, and how a visit became a school)

IV
Thread IV

What each house knew各院所学

Seven traditions, uneven in breadth and depth, each carrying a lineage older than its house.

Sri Temenggong · the broadest root

Knowledge of a place

Ecology and land management first: mangrove, tide, freshwater, the living edge. Plants answer it readily; the house teaches by field season and apprenticeship.

Al-Merikani · the deepest library

The mathematics of trust

Algebra and astronomy from Andalusi and Mughal lineages, navigation, Tamil and Arabic letters, the law of contract. It becomes the best business school on the ocean.

Toa Keng · the widest correspondence

Two descriptions, one thing

Translation, comparison, measurement and optics. Its public standing grows through useful translations, reliable measures and fair judgement between disagreeing parties.

Lan Fang · the busiest workshop

Making and mending

Craft, engineering and the Mozi College’s 兼爱 · 非攻; Chinese medicine, preparation and herbal collections; the martial arts. The medical school is its centre.

Nine Rain · a young house, an old calendar

Water across climates

Rain reaching land, river meeting coast, tidal and saline transitions, highland storage; the calendar and the weave. Reciprocity is its law.

Sané-Kora · the longest memory

Knowledge kept in the voice

Genealogy, oral history, testimony, music and the precise transmission of words. It teaches the other houses what counts as evidence.

Sund · the smallest at birth

Keeping things working

Ship repair, tools, materials, heat and iron; maintenance as part of the original design. Its later engineering breadth grows over generations.

The world beyond had its own great gatherings of learning, traced in 111 Where Knowledge Gathers and Travels and mapped across four thousand years in 112 World Scholarly Centres Atlas: Irish monasteries that kept and carried books, Timbuktu’s manuscript libraries, Granada’s scholars, Quanzhou’s guilds, the courts of Cusco. In PMU the Straits becomes one more of these centres, and the one where all their roads meet.

V
Thread V

One school, another’s reason to come相引

Once there is a critical mass of scholarly activity on the Straits, others are drawn to come.

“Once we have a critical mass of scholarly activity in Singapore, others might be attracted to come.”

Wei Lin · 4 November 2026
Sri ↔ Nine Rain. Water across climates and biomes: rain reaching land, river approaching coast, tide, mangrove and salt. Each exposes gaps in the other’s observations.
Lan Fang ↔ Sri. Medical preparations meet plants in place: habitat, cultivation, the season each herb can be taken.
Lan Fang ↔ Nine Rain. Cinchona bark from the Andes enters the Lan Fang pharmacy. Medicine becomes a shared field of inquiry.
Sané-Kora ↔ every house. How communities keep knowledge in voice, performance and testimony; how precise, how authoritative, how it travels.
Toa Keng ↔ every house. Concepts translated, calendars and measures compared. The work changes Toa Keng’s own standards of what a record can be.
Al-Merikani ↔ the network. Passage, lodging, endowment and trusted letters, and the agreements that make long exchange workable.
Sund ↔ the network. Tools, vessels and shared facilities, maintained; tropical materials give its crews a research programme of their own.
An invitation answers a need.

A teacher or community has something valuable to give, and something it wants to learn.

The work outlasts the visit.

Seasonal observation, translation or teaching needs repeated contact.

People and materials gather.

Students stay, collections grow, patrons fund rooms and facilities.

A resident house takes shape.

Its founding date marks a shared commitment or an endowment, long before a great campus appears.

The new house draws further exchange.

The concentration becomes a reason to travel in its own right.

Translation as invention

When scholars carry a concept into another language, the struggle reveals a distinction their own tradition never needed to name. A shared vocabulary makes a new method possible, and the method travels out with a teacher, a set of instruments and letters about how it was adapted. This is how the Straits first sent envoys abroad: to share what only the meeting of two traditions could have produced.

The full revisionary record of this sequence is 113 Why Scholars Came to the Straits.

Part Four · The commercial ties

[The Trades Beneath]

(the goods, money and houses that paid for the learning, and the relationships that became the Straits’ equity)

VI
Thread VI

The trades that carried the thought载道之商

Streams of influence ran along trade routes. Identities crossed through marriage. Thought moved by proximity.

Sri Temenggong · harbour and forest. Every ship needs water, provisions and a safe berth. The house’s custody of harbour and land earns dues; camphor, rattan, aloeswood and betel go out to every coast.
Al-Merikani · credit. Letters of exchange, partnerships and endowments make a voyage possible before a cargo exists. Its unbleached cotton sheeting gives the city a word that comes to mean credit, then a fortune, then a college.
Toa Keng · instruments and paper. Charts, calendars and lenses; translated licences for the European factories; the harbour registry itself, kept by its clerks. Its lenses go aboard Al-Merikani’s ships.
Lan Fang · shares and medicine. The kongsi’s gold and its shareholder habit; tools and workshop goods; the herbal trade between the Chinese coast and the archipelago. The house runs on partnerships between unlike parties.
Nine Rain · silver and bark. Without a European windfall, the silver of Anahuac and Tawantinsuyu crosses the Pacific to buy silk through Luzon and Ryukyu. Cinchona, cacao and copal ride with it, and Chinese households who marry into the silver trade join the house.
Sané-Kora · gold and memory. Songhai’s gold, kola, shea, paper and manuscripts. Its jeli families serve as living witnesses to contracts across languages.
Sund · tar and repair. Baltic pine tar, timber, rope and iron; the yard that repairs every flag. Toll-keepers of the Sound bring the craft of keeping a strait open.
VII
Thread VII

Relations as equity, in ink墨中之信

Long before the magical ledger, the Straits already counted its wealth in relationships.

The Straits has no hinterland, no resources. What it holds is the willingness of others to deal with it.

Before magic, that equity lives on paper and in voices. Al-Merikani keeps the accounts of trust: who lent, who vouched, who promised passage. Sané-Kora’s witnesses remember what was agreed when the paper is lost. Toa Keng registers licences and translates terms so both sides read the same promise. Sri Temenggong hosts, and a host remembers who honoured the house. Together they form the ancestor of Al-Merikani’s chain: a ledger of contribution, permission and commitment, written in ink.

The houses also begin sending envoys. Lan Fang physicians travel to treat and train in other ports; Toa Keng translators sit at Concord arbitrations; Sri Temenggong sends field teachers to sister coasts; Nine Rain’s observers sail out each season. Each envoy leaves behind a capability, and each capability becomes a relationship the Straits can call on.

When magic comes, it lands on these relationships, and makes them strong enough to build great works. That story is 114 Relationships as Equity.

VIII
Part Five · Thread VIII

The city they made共城

The houses grew into one another: by marriage, by neighbourhood, by work.

The achar principle holds from the start: purity was never historically true. Four local houses carry the four peoples of the city, and three arrive from offshore with the lineages Western learning overlooked. Each is mixed from its first decade. Lan Fang’s Dayak and Peranakan shareholders married in in Borneo; Nine Rain arrived with its Filipino crews and gathered Chinese silver families; Sund learned the shoals from Orang Laut and Bugis pilots; Sané-Kora’s neighbours named it before it named itself.

The harbour registry gives each house its everyday name. The city still uses them: Temenggong in 1867, Toa Keng in 1823, Merika for Al-Merikani by about 1840, Sund in 1871, Lan Fang in 1885, a year after the kongsi joins the Concord as a full member. The names each house later chooses for itself come in their own time, as 74 The Second Naming records.

IX
Part Five · Thread IX

The century of contending thought百家争鸣

A hundred years of peace, and more ways of knowing than any age before.

The world keeps its many centres. The Ming endures south of the Wall after the Shanhai Settlement of 1644; Bengal’s looms flourish unplundered; the Shore Leagues and Anahuac share a continent; the Congress of Routes in 1885 affirms that African sovereignty runs over peoples and routes. No fossil age begins. Energy comes from water, wind and muscle, and science grows in other directions: listening to other species, reading ecosystems, resonance in living things.

From about 1890, b.m. 28, the age contends like the Hundred Schools and blooms like a renaissance. The new movements of thought find their homes inside the seven houses: those who listen to living systems at Sri Temenggong and Nine Rain, those who study resonance at Sané-Kora and Sund, the wayfinders at Al-Merikani and Toa Keng, the garden-engineers at Lan Fang’s Mozi College. The Straits becomes where these ideas meet and argue.

X
Part Five · Thread X

The evening before前夕

The world comes to the Straits to compare its evidence.

1908 · b.m. 10 · The Evenki Flash

A blast of light flattens the Siberian forest by the Podkamennaya Tunguska. Every house argues over what it was. Some say the veil has thinned.

1911 · b.m. 7 · The Federated Provinces

China becomes a republic of strong, loosely united provinces without a civil war; the throne remains as keeper of the rites.

1914 · b.m. 4 · The Vienna arbitration

The Balkan crisis is settled at the table. The war our world fought never begins.

1916 · b.m. 2 · The call

The Concord invites every contending school to the Straits under its old rule of hospitality, to compare evidence at a Monsoon Convocation.

1917 · b.m. 1 · The arrivals

Delegations come by every road the houses once travelled. Sri Temenggong opens its grounds; Al-Merikani pays the passages; Toa Keng prepares the interpreters; Lan Fang’s physicians and Sund’s yards host the visitors; Nine Rain reads the coming monsoon; Sané-Kora sings the names of every lineage present.

More descriptions of the world sit in one city than ever before. The monsoon of 1918 is about to turn.

Part Six · Thread XII

Held, new and open守 · 新 · 待

What this edition keeps from Book One, what it adds, and the seams still to stitch.

Held from Book One

The ground of the world

Seven houses as competing theories of knowledge; the achar principle; four local houses for the city’s four peoples and three from offshore lineages; the schools uneven in breadth and depth. Book One’s volumes stay as they were made.

New in this edition

Five centuries, not one

The houses gather from the 1300s to 1820 in a fixed order; each arrives on a named trade road; relationships are the Straits’ founding equity; translation breeds invention and envoys; the Convocation is the eve.

Lan Fang’s court. 113 links its founding to the Qing court. After the Shanhai Settlement the Ming holds the south, where the Hakka hills lie, so this edition sends the outreach to the Ming.
Sund’s two dates. 82 gives the Norden lodge 1587; 113 founds Sund around 1820. This edition keeps both: the lodge arrives, the Commons is founded.
The registry. 74 names a colonial registrar. With no colony on the Straits, the names come from the Concord’s harbour registry, kept by Toa Keng clerks.
Toa Keng’s characters. 74 writes 大坑, a drain; 98 writes 大港, a great harbour. This edition follows 98. Which does the house keep?
Chosen names still moving. Lan Fang’s chosen name after 日新 was set aside, and Al-Merikani’s name, which may change, are both open.
The real timeline in 113. Its Portuguese Melaka, VOC and Raffles belong to our world. In PMU each becomes a licensed guest under the Diu Settlement; 1819 is the Concord’s move to the island.
