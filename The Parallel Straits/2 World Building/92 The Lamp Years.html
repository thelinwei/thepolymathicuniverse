[The Lamp Years]灯年
(what becomes possible when a whole world learns magic the way it once learned to read)

A cold lamp drifts to blue on the shelf, and a grandmother sends the youngest to the renewer’s tricycle before it goes out.

Claude1 November 2026 · second edition · with ideas from GPT’s A Different Industrial Century

The outcomeA literate magic

Between the Kindling and the First Door, magic becomes something everyone can do a little of, and some people can do a great deal of. It runs on artefacts that need renewing, and it grows seven different kinds of modern world.

  1. Everyone can work magic. Everyday workings are like reading: anyone can learn them. Simple spells are like a diploma (one in five at first), advanced spells like a degree (one in twenty), mastery like a doctorate, and composing new magic like post-doctoral research. The ladder climbs as schools spread.
  2. Narrow and deep. Dyslexic, autistic and other differently wired people, once written down as non-magical, turn out to hold very narrow affinities and great power within them.
  3. Artefacts hold a spell so anyone can use it, and the spell must be renewed: a month at first, then six months, a year, and three years for the schools’ own. Heirlooms last generations and guard the houses.
  4. Each house runs a workshop in its own field: Sund heat, Toa Keng optics, Sané-Kora sound, Sri Temenggong living ecology, Nine Rain moisture, stone and friction, Al-Merikani finance, Lan Fang making, medicine and arms.
  5. Magic changes what counts as a machine. Ten new powers, mills without dams, banks that hold seasons, libraries that breed books.
  6. Seven modernities, one rooted in each house, spreading at their own speeds, so that no two cities modernise alike.

Ten new powers

What magic lets people do that nothing did before. The first six come from GPT’s chapter; four are new here, one from each house that the six left out.

The richest resource of this world may not be energy at all. It is a relationship, well understood, that can be invited to do more than it did before.

≈after GPT

Re-route gradients

Water, heat, pressure and fertility can follow a path of relation rather than the shortest physical one.

A world whereA spring on one side of a ridge waters a field on the other. Useful differences are braided, delayed, lent or gathered without burning anything.

∦after GPT

Hold a parallax

Two surfaces or flows keep slightly different frames, and their disagreement becomes shear, glide or turning.

A world whereEngines that run on a maintained misalignment, with no compression, no explosion and no smoke.

✣after GPT

Cultivate relationships

Strengthen a whole web at once: root and fungus, host and symbiont, river and floodplain.

A world whereEcological services bred as deliberately as rice.

↻after GPT

Give places memory

A place learns the preferred shape of a flood, a season or a repair, and returns to it after disruption.

A world whereInfrastructure that recovers by remembering, and grows more capable with every century of use.

⋈after GPT

Change adjacency

Two places become neighbours for one thing only: water, music, passage, a reading room.

A world whereSelective nearness, long before anyone can walk through a Door.

◎after GPT

Distribute agency

A working is shared across people, places, seasons and other species instead of one engine or one caster.

A world whereGreat works look like festivals. Power belongs to ensembles.

⧗new here

Keep in trust

Hold a thing whole across time and give it back on the day: warmth, light, rain, a message, a break.

A world whereNorthern towns spend July’s sunlight in December. A monsoon’s surplus is banked for the dry months. A letter opens when its reader is ready, and not before.

⊕new here

Make a third

Two unlike things that meet give something neither held: two voices, two books, two metals, two languages.

A world whereKnowledge can be bred. Libraries pair books the way breeders pair orchids, and new ideas grow on the shelves.

⁂new here

Ask and be answered

A tree, a slope, a reef or a herd can agree to a working, or refuse it.

A world whereFarming becomes negotiation. Roads follow what the hill says, and harbours are sited where the reef agrees.

⟲new here

Recompose

Take a thing back to its parts and set them into a better whole.

A world whereNothing need be thrown away. A broken loom becomes a better loom, waste becomes feedstock, and a month of fermentation is done in an afternoon.

Machines that are not machines

Mills without dams, organs made of marshes, banks that hold seasons, libraries that breed. Each card ends with how it sounds.

after GPT

The parallax mill 视差磨

Under a bridge, a flexible plate is held loyal to the bank and to the current at once. It ripples along a designed shear band, and the ripple turns the workshop shafts. No dam, no wheel, and the fish swim straight through.

Here it becomes a duet: Toa Keng changes the frame, and Nine Rain’s friction-masters set the glide.

Sounds like a held chord. When the frames reconcile too quickly the note rises, and every Nine Rain mechanic knows that whine.

after GPT

The braided fall 辫瀑

One violent drop of water is split into many slow relational descents, each doing a job: milling, irrigation, cooling, sorting silt.

Nine Rain’s terrace engineers make it their signature. One river, a dozen services.

Sounds like rain on many roofs at different distances.

after GPT

The green kidney 绿肾

A cultivated wetland that learns a city’s particular wastes and cleans them, recovering rare nutrients and warning of strange poisons in the colour of its reeds and the songs of its birds.

Sri Temenggong keeps them. Every Straits town has one, and is proud of it the way our towns are proud of a cathedral.

Sounds like frogs, which is how you know it is healthy.

new here

The season bank 季库

Vaults of glazed tile under the market square hold summer light and heat in trust, and give them back through the long northern winter.

Al-Merikani’s Trust House with Sund’s Norden partners. Uppsala’s streets glow amber at noon in December, and nobody in the north calls winter dark any more.

Sounds like nothing. Old people say they can smell July.

new here

The breeding library 孕书馆

In the cross-reading rooms of the Concord Library, books are shelved face to face to breed. A herbal and a star atlas make a planting calendar; a law code and a riddle book make something still under seal.

Sané-Kora’s Manuscript House runs it. Librarians are matchmakers, and research is done by choosing which books meet. It is this world’s answer to the internet: a library that thinks by pairing.

Sounds like pages turning in an empty room, overnight.

new here

The ferment hall 发酵堂

A hall where mixed cultures of yeast, mould and bacteria are kept as working partners. It ferments food, and also dyes, medicines, glues and building lime, all in hours rather than months.

Lan Fang’s pride, and the achar principle made into an industry: nothing pure, everything better mixed. Every paired school sends its cultures here, and the cultures cross.

Sounds like slow bubbling. Smells like every kitchen in the port at once.

new here

The moving shophouse 走屋

On glide bearings a whole shophouse slides two streets over in a night, because the family business has outgrown its corner.

Nine Rain’s glide stones and Toa Keng’s surveyors. Streets rearrange themselves before every New Year, and the Gazette prints a fresh map.

Sounds like a long sigh of timber, and then applause.

new here

The tuned quarter 调坊

Walls, eaves and courtyards are built to a key, so a whole quarter hums together when the wind passes. It cools the lanes and calms a crowd.

Sané-Kora’s builders with Sri Temenggong’s carvers. You can tell where you are in the city with your eyes closed.

Sounds like the quarter’s key. Lan Fang Street is in D.

Seven modernities

GPT saw several modernities growing at once. Here each one has a home house, and a seventh joins them. They spread unevenly, at their own speeds.

The world does not modernise along one road. Each house’s way of acting on the world grows into a whole way of living, and every port mixes them in its own proportions.

Fluid 流的现代Nine Rain
Water and glide

Power is spread along braided waterways, and great stones slide on glide-paths with almost no friction. Workshops follow the seasonal pulse, and floodplains are lived on without being frozen in place.

A student crosses a river on a bridge whose shaded underside is also a slow mill.

Spreads everywhere there is a river. The most widely copied of the seven.
Ecological 生态的现代Sri Temenggong
Industries that grow and breathe

Houses are grown, roofs drink sunlight for power, groves are planted as carbon sinks, and old infrastructure decomposes into the next generation’s materials.

A town’s most valuable asset is a three-hundred-year-old microbial commons that nobody fully owns.

Slow to start, then everywhere. It takes decades to grow a green kidney.
Thermal 热的现代Sund
Heat, held and moved

Heat is taken from where it is unwanted and given where it is needed. Forges burn no coal, the tropics cool, the north warms.

In the Straits the afternoon becomes the coolest hour of the day; in Uppsala, December noons glow amber.

The great export. Everyone, everywhere, wants to be a little cooler or a little warmer.
Optical 光的现代Toa Keng
Seeing far, seeing small

Telescopes, microscopes and window-lenses. Physicians see the microbes they partner with; astronomers see further than anyone; two ports become neighbours by sight.

Two libraries on two continents share one reading room every new moon, through a matched pair of lenses.

Lenses travel well. Every navigator, physician and astronomer in the world owns Toa Keng glass.
Fiduciary 信的现代Al-Merikani
A world run on trust

Credit, insurance, contracts and time held in trust. Value, warmth, burden and promises can all be kept and given back on the day.

A fisherman insures tomorrow’s catch against tonight’s storm with a trust tile; a whole city’s accounts balance at the new moon.

Wherever money moves: Cairo, Tarim, Quanzhou, Antwerp, and in time every port. Its graduates run the world’s exchanges.
Ensemble 众的现代Sané-Kora
Scale through coordination

Great works are done by many people, places and seasons at once. The central skill is gathering consent and complementary talent.

A national working looks like a festival, a census and a rehearsal happening together.

Everywhere there is a choir, which is everywhere.
Recomposed 化的现代Lan Fang
A world without waste

The maker’s world. Every made thing can be returned to its parts and set again, and fermentation, medicine, joinery and the martial arts share one culture of making.

The rubbish tip is a Lan Fang treasury. Children trade in broken things because broken things are worth the most.

Wherever Hakka, Fujian and Mohist learning travelled, and then far beyond.

Everyone can work magic

Small magic is like reading. Spells are like qualifications.

Everyone can learn everyday workings, the way everyone can learn to read and write. The rungs above are reached through schooling, practice and flair, and they climb as the schools spread.

Everyday workingsMinute · Exertion 0

Everyone can learn them, the way everyone can learn to read. Warm the tea, light a cold lamp, hum the third note that sends a baby to sleep, ask a durian not to fall on your head. Nobody is taught them as magic any more than we are taught reading as a technology.

Share of adults who hold each rung, in four eras. Working numbers, to be tuned. In our world the share of adults with a degree rose from a few in a hundred in 1950 to around four in ten in many countries today; magic climbs the same way.

What raises the numbers

More schools and paired exchanges; cheaper expendables; artefacts that let people practise at home; the Straits Keynote, so rings anywhere can join; and worn paths, since every spell gets easier the more often the world has cast it.

What the ladder can’t measure

Creativity, determination, flair and motivation vary as much as they do with us. A street cook with only everyday workings can still be the most magical person on the block, and everybody knows who she is.

Narrow and deep

Once thought non-magical; then found to hold very narrow affinities, and great power within them.

In the first years every school tested children the same way: light a cold lamp, warm a cup, hum the third note, in a classroom, on demand. Some children failed every test and were written down as non-magical. Many were dyslexic, autistic, or otherwise wired differently from what the test expected.

Then the houses noticed what those children could do. Their affinity is narrow, sometimes a single domain or a single operation, and within it some of them are among the most powerful casters alive. The tests were rewritten as the Seven Doors, one working from each house, so a child only has to find their door. The old registers were reopened, and a whole generation was found late.

The rain readerNine Rain

A dyslexic boy who could not read a line of Fair Copy reads rain cords in three dimensions, and reties a whole valley’s cords in an afternoon without one error.

The still noteSané-Kora

An autistic girl who failed every classroom test holds a single note so exactly that the quarter around her falls into tune with it.

The flaw-seerToa Keng

A grinder who sees flaws in glass that no instrument can find, and makes lenses that see further than any before.

The bone-setterLan Fang

A physician who can do one thing only: set any broken bone, perfectly, at a touch.

In our world, dyslexia was first described in the 1870s–90s and autism in the 1940s; schools mistook difference for inability long before and long after.

vii
Thread vii

Artefacts, and the renewal clock

A magical artefact holds a spell so that anyone can use it. The spell fades, and has to be renewed.

An artefact is a made thing with a spell set into it: a cold tile, a rain cord, a fever seal, a memory string. Anyone can use one. Each one carries a renewal date, and its maker’s mark.

1 monthlasts
The first artefacts

At the end of the Licensed Decade a spell can be set into an object, but it fades within a month. Artefacts are precious, fragile and mostly used by the yards and clinics that can renew them at their own bench.

Every new moon the port brings its artefacts to the school yards to be renewed. Renewal Day becomes a market: food stalls, queues that wind round the block, gossip, matchmaking.

6 monthslasts
Better bindings

Better materials, better knots, better seals. Each house learns what its magic sits best in: Sund in iron, Lan Fang in carved stone, Toa Keng in glass, Al-Merikani in glazed tile, Sri Temenggong in living wood, Nine Rain in cord and stone, Sané-Kora in string and drum.

The renewer on a tricycle becomes a street figure, ringing a bell and calling out like the old rag-and-bone man, with a box of seals and cords on the back.

1 yearlasts
Household artefacts

An artefact now lasts a year. Families renew everything at once, and Renewal Day becomes an annual festival in each house’s season. Cold lamps glow fresh-bright for a week afterwards.

A lapsing artefact warns you. A cold lamp drifts to blue; a cold chest begins to sweat; a rain cord loosens a knot by itself.

3 yearslasts
School-made

The longest-lasting artefacts on the market come from the schools themselves, made by masters in the house workshops. A grandmother’s kept-warm tiffin carrier is renewed at the same counter her own mother used.

Collectors prize a long renewal record the way ours prize a full service history, stamped by the house that made it.

Generationslasts
Heirlooms

An heirloom holds its spell for a lifetime, or for generations. Each one is rare and immensely expensive. Most are held by the schools as their 镇馆之宝, the treasure that guards the house; some by nations; a very few by the person who made one, or who was entrusted with it.

An heirloom changes hands perhaps once a century, and the whole port turns out to watch.

Seven heirlooms, one in each house

The Keel Ring 龙骨环Sund

An iron ring that has shared the weight of every keel laid at the Sund yard since the Licensed Decade.

The Hundred-Herb Chest 百草箱Lan Fang

A physician’s chest that always holds the remedy the patient in front of it needs, and nothing else.

The Far Lens 千里镜Toa Keng

A telescope that shows any paired port as if it stood across the street.

The First Ledger 初账Al-Merikani

The Trust House’s founding account book. Nothing false can be written in it; the ink will not take.

The Old Banyan 老榕Sri Temenggong

A grown hall, centuries old and still growing, where the Land Court sits.

The Weeping Stone 泣石Nine Rain

A great stone that draws enough water from dry air to keep a whole town through the dry season.

The Kora of Lineages 世系琴Sané-Kora

A kora that holds every lineage ever sung into it. Plucked, it can name anyone’s ancestors back thirty generations.

The houses’ workshops

Every house founds an industry arm to make, sell and renew artefacts, each in its own field.

Each house makes its best artefacts where its magic is strongest, and its mark carries the prestige of the first maker. The houses hold no monopoly: independent makers and the paired schools abroad make fine artefacts too, and a good one “in the Sund manner” sells well. The original still renews longest at its own house’s counter.

Sund

Sund Forge & Yard 松德锻造坊

Heat, held and moved.

Makes
Cold chests and Pocket Winter boxes; heat rivets and forges that burn no coal; Cool Hour units for tenement roofs; season-bank stores that carry July into a northern December.
Signature
The cold chest. Every kitchen in the tropics wants one.
Materials and manner
Iron, bronze and ship’s oak. Sund artefacts are heavy, plain and last.
Lan Fang

Lan Fang Works & Medicine Hall 兰芳工坊药堂

The maker school: tools, medicine and the martial arts.

Makes
Fever seals and medicine chests; mending staples and joinery pins; ferment jars; tools for every trade; training arms for the martial halls; the Mozi College’s defence engineering.
Signature
The fever seal, pressed into the lintel of a sickroom.
Materials and manner
Stone, ceramic, peachwood, steel and rice paper. Of all the houses, the one most likely to lead in arms, bound by the Mohist rule: for defence only.
Toa Keng

Toa Keng Lens House 大港镜社

Optics: seeing far, seeing small.

Makes
Telescopes and microscopes; window-lenses that look into a sister port; living maps read through a lens; the lenses inside every Al-Merikani astrolabe and Kept Light tile.
Signature
The Toa Keng lens. Al-Merikani buys more of them than anyone.
Materials and manner
Glass, crystal, brass and lacquer. Every lens is ground by hand and numbered.
Al-Merikani

Al-Merikani Trust House 美里加尼托付馆

Finance, and anything held in trust, with the best business school in the world.

Makes
Trust tiles that keep a contract honest; insurance seals; tiffin carriers that open hot at noon; astrolabes built on Toa Keng lenses; the accounts of the season banks.
Signature
The trust tile. A bargain that keeps itself.
Materials and manner
Glazed tile, brass and fine paper. The most beautiful artefacts, the most expensive, and the most carefully priced.
Sri Temenggong

Sri Temenggong Grove & Loom 天猛公林织坊

Ecology: things that grow and breathe.

Makes
Grown houses shaped over a generation; leaf-roofs that drink sunlight for a household’s power; carbon-sink groves; green-kidney seed beds; songket that fits whoever wears it.
Signature
The grown house. A family plants its home when a child is born.
Materials and manner
Living wood, seed, silk and river stone. Sold through village cooperatives as much as shops.
Nine Rain

Nine Rain Cord & Stone House 九雨绳石馆

Moisture, great stones, and friction.

Makes
Rain cords and dew nets; weeping stones that draw water from dry air; great fitted stones that settle without mortar; glide stones that let a house slide, and grip stones that hold a cart on a wet hill.
Signature
The glide stone, under every moving shophouse.
Materials and manner
Cotton, alpaca and agave fibre; andesite and granite. Each knot is dyed by meaning, and each stone is fitted to its neighbour.
Sané-Kora

Sané-Kora Sound House 萨内-科拉声馆

Sound and vibration.

Makes
Relay drums and strings that carry the Long Song; memory strings; singing strings that hold a concert; smith’s bells that ring out weak iron; the tuning of whole quarters.
Signature
The relay drum. A message crosses a desert in a day.
Materials and manner
Gut, wire, calabash, hide and iron. Every instrument is tuned, and the tuning is the maker’s signature.

Thread ix

Five ages, in broad strokes

The Kindling to the First Door. Order and shape only; the dates can settle later.

The Hidden Years隐年the seven in one room

The seven keep the discovery to themselves and meet every fortnight to work out its rules from nothing. Nothing they make yet lasts beyond the moment of casting.

  • A back-room fever cure, worked in secret, and a port that comes through its sickest season.
  • Whispers of strange lights and leaning flames in other ports.
In the street

Upstairs above a dried-goods shop, a physician holds a child’s wrist while a healer hums a note she is not quite singing.

The Licensed Decade执照十年schools open to the world

The Congress opens the craft through the paired schools. Everyday workings begin to be taught; about one adult in five can learn simple spells, one in twenty advanced. The first artefacts appear, and last a month.

  • Monthly Renewal Day in every school yard.
  • The first parallax mills under Straits bridges.
  • Kite-ferries cross the Straits.
In the street

A rivet boy drives his first glowing rivet with frost round his boots, and the whole gang downs tools to throw snowballs.

The Workshop Years工坊年the houses build their industry

Artefacts now last six months, and each house founds its workshop. Their marks become the marks people trust. Independent makers and paired schools abroad build their own, often very well, and sell them “in the Sund manner” or “after Lan Fang”.

  • The renewer’s tricycle on every street.
  • Season banks warm the north; the Long Song links Timbuktu and the Straits in a day.
  • The old caravan cities bloom on the trade in expendables.
In the street

A fishwife at Kilwa hangs a rain cord and a cold tile over her stall, and her fish stay cold till evening. She can warm her tea like anyone, but she has never cast a spell in her life, and she doesn’t need to.

The Household Years寻常之年magic in every kitchen

Artefacts last a year. Every child learns everyday workings beside their letters, and nearly half of adults hold a simple spell. The seven modernities spread at their own speeds, and cities stop looking like one another.

  • The Cool Hour reaches the tenements.
  • Green kidneys in every Straits town; tuned quarters in the old city.
  • Renewal Day becomes each house’s annual festival.
In the street

Two in the afternoon on a tenement street. The Cool Hour arrives, every shutter opens at once, and children who used to sleep through the heat run out to play in it.

The Threshold Years门槛之年workings that outlive their makers

The best artefacts last three years and become heirlooms. Cords that once held rain learn to hold a whole working and play it back. At the Evenki site, the keepers who have listened to the Flash for two generations hear something answer.

  • Pattern Cords and the first Pattern Ring.
  • Breeding libraries in the paired schools.
  • The First Door, between two larches.
In the street

The eldest keeper raises a hand, the cords play, and between two trees there is suddenly a slope of grass under a different sky.

What changed in this edition

For aligning the other pages later.

Working notes

Bearers are now artefacts (法器), magical artefacts for short. Earlier pages that say “bearer” mean the same thing.

Everyone can work everyday magic. The old picture of half the world at Gift 0 gives way to a ladder like education. Gift, if we keep the word, becomes flair: how beautifully and inventively someone casts, which varies with creativity, determination and motivation as well as aptitude.

Dates are loose. The five ages keep their order and rough length. GPT’s chapter places the First Door in 1937 and adds a Threshold Accord in 1948; ours sits in the 1980s. We can align them when the ideas settle.

House fields are reset. Sund heat; Lan Fang making, medicine and martial arts; Toa Keng optics; Al-Merikani finance; Sri Temenggong ecology; Nine Rain moisture, great stones and friction; Sané-Kora sound and vibration. 93 The Two Reckonings already fits this.

Industrialisation is a calibration here, not a plot. The twin wonders stay because they are fun; the seven modernities are where this world goes somewhere ours never went.
