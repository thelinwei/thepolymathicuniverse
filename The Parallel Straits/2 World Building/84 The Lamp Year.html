Part One · The outcome

[The Lamp Year]

(What happened in 1918, the three decades that built up to it, and the three that poured out of it.)

I
Thread I

1918 at a glance 燃灯

The world came to compare what it knew, and left able to do something no one had done before.

By 1918 PMU had lived a century without a world war and without a fossil age. Knowledge had kept climbing even as energy stayed modest, and the contending schools had multiplied in every port. In 1908 an unexplained flash in the Evenki forest set them all arguing. In 1916 the Concord did what it had always done with a quarrel: it called the parties to the Straits under its licence of hospitality. On the ninth night of the lull between monsoons in 1918, seven practitioners from seven traditions worked together on a single relation, as they had for weeks without result. This time something gave. The lamps in the hall leaned toward them; the harbour water stood still for the length of a breath; every compass in the port swung and came back. They had pierced the veil. Around the world, in their own ways, people felt it: fishermen off Kilwa felt the sea pause, a choir in Uppsala lost its pitch at the same instant, children woke crying in Fujian, and those with the Gift felt something open behind their eyes that never quite closed again. That night is the Kindling, 燃灯, and it is a.m. 1. The Straits count their years from it: a.m., after magic, so the year our calendars call 2026 is a.m. 109.

II
Thread II

Before the lamp · the roads to the Straits 百家争鸣

The many ways of knowing travelled inside the seven houses, along roads of trade, money, industry and thought, and landed together in one port.

SundThe Norden lodge, from 1587

Timber, iron and the cooperative movements of the North came with the shipwrights. The idea that cooperation is nature’s engine lived inside its yards and welfare halls.

Lan FangThe Hakka road, Jiaying to the Pearl River

Kongsi mining capital, clan banks and makers’ guilds. Physicians who treated the body as a weather of relations travelled with them.

Toa KengThe European houses at the Straits

Licensed under the Concord, British, French, Germanic and Iberian merchants and scholars never conquered, but they kept trading, and their counting houses, courts, newspapers and lecture halls gathered in Toa Keng. The urge to measure, record and reframe everything came with them.

Al-MerikaniThe Hadhrami and Gulf routes

Trusts, finance, astronomy and instruments. A science of keeping and timing came with the merchants of Tarim and Cairo.

Sri TemenggongThe courts of Riau and the archipelago

Land, cloth, forest and coast. The craft of reading whole landscapes, of moving through an ecosystem as a navigator moves through swell, was already theirs.

Nine RainThe Pacific road from Anahuac and the Andes

Water engineering, terraces, khipu and the long calendars. Gardening whole ecosystems was an Andean and Anahuac habit long before it was a school.

Sané-KoraThe Swahili coast and the road from Timbuktu

Griots, archives, music and medicine. Listening to other voices, including those of other species, was carried in their songs.

The approach

PMU-bm · 1860–1918
  1. 1860s
    The ceiling settles

    With no fossil age, energy comes from water, wind, muscle and wood. Knowledge keeps climbing; power stays modest. Electricity is known, drawn from rivers and falls, and never from coal.

  2. 1885
    The Congress of Routes

    At Mbanza Kongo, sovereignty over peoples and routes is affirmed for Africa. It becomes the template for the age: rule by agreement, not by line.

    Our worldThe Berlin Conference partitions Africa.

  3. 1890s
    The Contending Schools begin 百家争鸣

    A century of peace and a world of many centres produce more ways of knowing than any age before, argued out inside the seven houses and their partner schools across the Concord ports.

  4. 1898
    The Wuxu Settlement 戊戌之约

    A constitutional throne with provincial assemblies; reform becomes a public argument across the provinces.

    Our worldThe Hundred Days’ Reform fails.

  5. 1908
    The Evenki Flash

    A blast of light flattens forest by the Podkamennaya Tunguska. No one can explain it. Evenki keepers say the forest “opened and closed”. For a decade every school tries to account for it, and none can.

    Our worldThe Tunguska event.

  6. 1911
    The Federated Provinces 联省共和

    Declared without civil war. The provinces send their best scholars abroad, and many go to the Straits.

  7. 1914
    The Vienna arbitration

    The Balkan crisis is settled at the table. The world keeps its peace, and its scholars keep their correspondence.

    Our worldThe First World War, which ends the real 1900s flowering.

  8. 1916
    The Concord calls a convocation

    After eight years of rival explanations for the Flash, the Concord invites every contending school to the Straits to compare evidence in one place, under its old rule of hospitality: any guest who accepts the licence may speak.

III
Thread III

After the lamp 灯年

Three years of secret work, then a congress that opened the craft to the world on terms.

The outpouring

PMU-am · from 1918, a.m. 1
  1. 1918
    The Monsoon Convocation and the Kindling 季风会讲 · 燃灯

    On the ninth night of the lull between monsoons, seven practitioners of seven traditions pierce the veil together, and the world feels it. The convocation ends in astonishment and confusion: no one, the seven included, can say exactly what they did, or do it again at will.

    Our worldThe influenza pandemic.

  2. 1918–1919
    The Fortnight Circle 半月会

    The seven return to their houses and swear their closest colleagues to secrecy. Every two weeks they meet, in turn, in one of the seven houses to compare failures. It takes months to repeat the working once, and longer to understand why it worked. With no grammar and no spell book, they begin to write the first.

  3. 1919
    The reunion of the seven

    A year after the Kindling the seven meet in full. They can repeat a handful of small workings reliably and have begun to map the rules beneath them, but they cannot agree whether to tell the world.

  4. 1919–1921
    Whispers from other shores

    Word arrives through the Concord’s networks: a lamp that lit itself in a Bengal school, a weaver in Cusco whose cords held back a storm. Others are finding the veil on their own.

  5. 1921
    The Congress of the Open Lamp 启灯大会

    To keep the lead and to shape what follows, the seven call the world to the Straits and reveal the craft. Membership comes with rules: a licence, a teacher, an oath never to work on a person without consent, and a duty to report every discovery. What the seven worked out in three years becomes the first syllabus.

  6. 1921 onward
    The paired schools 结对

    Each Straits house invites schools of its own lineage abroad, often several: Sund with Uppsala, Copenhagen and the Norden yards; Lan Fang with the Hakka academies of Jiaying and the workshops of Guangzhou and Quanzhou; Toa Keng with Europe’s universities, from Edinburgh and Leiden to Paris, Göttingen and Salamanca; Al-Merikani with Tarim, Cairo and Isfahan; Sri Temenggong with the courts of Riau, Java and the Mekong; Nine Rain with Texcoco and Cusco; Sané-Kora with Timbuktu, Kilwa and Kano. Students and teachers cross both ways. The Straits schools become the most sought-after in the world, and the clearing-house where discoveries from every shore are collated.

  7. 1921–1931
    The Licensed Decade 执照十年

    The first decade of the Lamp Years, the long era from the Kindling to the First Door (1918–1983). The craft spreads carefully, through apprenticeship and licence. Each house writes its first grammar and songbook; physicians learn to tune the body’s relations; the first listening councils with other species meet, river by river. A generation grows up learning workings alongside its letters. Like industrialisation in our world, the spread of the craft remakes economies, cities and families wherever it lands, port by port and school by school.

  8. 1927
    The Concordat

    The Concord’s old ethic carried into magic: many grammars licensed under no single sovereign. Seven grammars are recognised, each kept distinct, none allowed to rule the others. Disciplined incompleteness becomes law.

  9. 1931
    The Silesian Breaking

    A prince tries to hold a rebellious province by workings and is broken by the load. The world learns what holding a people by magic costs.

  10. 1983
    The First Door

    Workers return to the site of the Evenki Flash and open on purpose what opened by accident in 1908. Behind it lies an unpeopled world. Evenki keepers hold its custody.

  11. 1986
    The Threshold Accord 门约

    Every Door becomes a commons in the custody of the peoples whose land it opens on. Sund’s jurists help draft it.

  12. 1990s–2000s
    The opening

    Dozens of Doors open. Settlement stays light under the Gardener’s Rule.

  13. 2010s onward
    The crossings

    Travel between worlds becomes ordinary; Door-ports grow up around the old harbours.

  14. 2026
    The present, a.m. 109

    A world of many powers and many worlds. The Straits remain the Concord’s seat; the lamp has burned for 109 years.

IV
Thread IV

Why the world chose not to conquer

Magic makes conquest possible again. The world had already learned to want other things.

i

Five centuries of accords

From the Concord’s licence onward, the world learned that wealth comes on terms. Hospitality became habit long before it became law.

ii

Regard beyond the human

The listening and landscape traditions carried by the houses, and the Gaia movements, taught generations to treat rivers, forests and other species as parties to the world, not property in it.

iii

Magic as exertion

A spell costs its caster what hard work costs: strain, then rest. A working with the grain and with consent goes easier; coercion goes harder, but stays possible.

iv

Recipes are shared

Once a spell is composed it enters a songbook and can be taught. No house keeps a monopoly for long, so no house can rule by magic alone.

v

Who chose otherwise

Some pay for their magic with bodies that never agreed: no limit, double weight, a Scar on the world. These are PMU’s villains, and they are powerful exactly because they refuse the bill.

!

The danger

A spell that changes what people want would make coercion feel like consent. It is the most dangerous recipe in PMU, and the reason the Concordat forbids will-workings: natural ground for the Revisionist and the Second Concordance.

When power finally came, the world had better uses for it.

The real roots

Our own world had a contending-schools moment around 1900. The First World War ended it. PMU lets it run.

Calcutta, 1900sPlants that answer

Jagadish Chandra Bose measured how plants respond to stimuli and argued for continuity between plant and animal life.

Hamburg, 1909Every creature’s world

Jakob von Uexküll proposed the Umwelt: each species lives in its own perceived world.

1902Cooperation as evolution

Peter Kropotkin’s Mutual Aid argued that cooperation, not only struggle, drives survival.

1926The living film of the earth

Vladimir Vernadsky’s The Biosphere treated life as a geological force.

Edinburgh, 1915Cities as ecology

Patrick Geddes’ Cities in Evolution read the city as a living region.

Santiniketan, 1901Schools under trees

Rabindranath Tagore founded a school that taught in the open, joining many traditions.

Kyoto, 1911A philosophy of pure experience

Nishida Kitarō’s An Inquiry into the Good began a new philosophy from Asian and Western roots together.

1913Intuition from Kumbakonam

Srinivasa Ramanujan wrote to Cambridge with results that came from a way of knowing others could not follow.

Thread VII

What this changes

Moving the lamp to 1918 makes Book One the age before magic and Book Two the age after it.

  • The schools found themselves in 1780–1860, as Atlas IV says, in a world before magic, and meet the lamp a lifetime later. Book One becomes PMU-bm; Book Two becomes PMU-am.
  • Volumes 82 and 83 now carry the 1918 Kindling, the Evenki Flash, the First Door and the Threshold Accord (to be moved to 1983 and 1986) and the renames.
  • The no-world-wars premise now rests on the Concord’s politics alone until 1918; after 1918 it rests on those accords holding while magic makes conquest possible again.
  • Atlas IV keeps its own steam, industrial war and “Concord Era”, under the Annex rule. The world beneath is the newer iteration.
