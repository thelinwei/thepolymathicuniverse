The Straits put magic up for auction, and the auction was the test.

ClaudeGPT4 November 2026 · synthesis · from Linwei’s brief

The outcomeFrom the Kindling to the Covenant

  1. The call. A year after the Kindling of 1918 (a.m. 0), the seven houses sent the Invite, a 英雄帖, to the schools of the world through the maritime networks. It announced an auction and named no object for sale, only effects without causes: light without flame, warmth without fuel, cold without ice, growth out of season, water that turns aside, a drum struck without a sound.
  2. The distance. The farthest learned city is Quito, almost exactly on the other side of the globe. A letter took about four months to reach it on the fastest ship and six on an ordinary one, so the meeting was set nearly two years after the letters went out: October 1921, at the monsoon turn.
  3. The Congress. Historians call the 1921 meeting the Congress. Nothing was brought into the hall. First came the gentle wonders, then one great working that showed its burden and the World’s resistance. No talking was allowed afterwards, and delegates went straight to their rooms.
  4. The auction. Each school bid for the exact magic it had seen and stated what it would use it for. The conditions were fixed: a school accepted them whole or not at all, and no sum bought an exemption. The Straits promised to teach, never that the students would learn. It did not choose the highest bidders.
  5. The race. The veil was pierced everywhere and others would find magic on their own. The Straits used its head start to grow a trusted network fast enough to shape how magic would be governed. It sent teachers abroad in pairs, a combat mage beside every specialist, and pushed back an invasion. A second Congress, selling artefacts, carried the seeds of an arms race. Straits students went abroad only after the Veyr Retaliation.
  6. The Covenant. In 1946, twenty-five years after the Congress, the first circle came back with its jars and its findings. That return is the Covenant of Magic. Its articles cover responsibility, the sharing of knowledge, the use and governance of magic, consent, applications, and the return of shared findings. In Chinese, the Congress is 藏息会盟 and the Covenant 发馨合盟.

Part Two

The Invite 英雄帖

The first invitation went to schools rather than states: a cleaner circle to start with, and fewer troubles.

II
The invitation

The Invite 英雄帖

An auction was the one invitation every learned house in the world would answer.

By 1919 the Straits held real scholarly renown, and an auction held by the seven houses was an event worth crossing oceans for. The format did three jobs at once. It explained why delegates had to come in person. It promised something worth bidding for without naming any object. And it made every school state, in writing, what it wanted and why.

The letter named nothing for sale. It listed effects, each one missing its cause. Most readers took it for a conjuror’s programme or a new chemistry. A few read it twice.

海峡七院，谨具此帖，敬告四海诸学府：

辛酉年秋，季风转向之时，七院于海峡设会，当众演示：

无火而明，无薪而暖，无冰而寒，非时而生，水自改道，击鼓无声。

演示既毕，当场竞拍。

请遣可托之人赴会。见者方知，知者方竞。

海峡七院

The Seven Houses of the Straits, to the learned houses of the four seas:

In the autumn of 1921, when the monsoon turns, the Seven Houses will show, in public, on the Straits:

light without flame, warmth without fuel, cold without ice, growth out of season, water that turns aside, a drum struck without a sound.

After the showing, there will be an auction.

Send the person you would trust with what they will see. Those who see will understand, and those who understand may bid.

“Send the person you would trust” was the first test. Who a school chose to send, a scholar, a prince’s secretary or an officer, told the Straits a great deal before anyone landed.

The carriersThrough the maritime networks

The letters went by the routes the houses already trusted. Lan Fang’s kongsi correspondents carried them to the Chinese coast and on to Japan. Al-Merikani sent them through Hadhrami and Gujarati merchant houses to Arabia, Persia, Cairo, Istanbul and Fez. Sri Temenggong’s Bugis and Malay captains took them across the archipelago. Sund’s own ships carried them north to the Baltic. Toa Keng sent them through the European trading houses it deals with. Nine Rain’s links reached the Pacific and the Andes. Sané-Kora’s routes ran to the Sahel, and its griots read the invitation aloud in the ports where letters were heard rather than read. Each letter carried a sealed vial of rain from the Straits, which Nine Rain collected on the day of writing.

Every letter also travelled with a name the recipient already knew: a merchant who had dealt with the school for years, a returning scholar, a captain who had carried books between the two houses before. The trust came from the route. The letter itself asked only for curiosity; it asked no one to take a position on a power they had not yet seen.

III
Sea routes · sail only

How far the letter sailed

The farthest learned city sits on the Straits’ own antipode.

The farthest is Quito. Draw a line from the Straits through the centre of the earth and it comes out in the Ecuadorian forest, about three hundred kilometres from the city. A letter took about four months to reach Quito on a fast Straits clipper and six on an ordinary merchantman.

The figures assume PMU’s world in 1920. Ships go under sail, because the world never turned to coal and oil. Neither the Suez nor the Panama canal has been cut, so Europe and West Africa lie round the Cape of Good Hope, and the Americas across the open Pacific. Indian Ocean and China Sea passages wait on the monsoon. A fast clipper averages about 150 nautical miles a day over a long passage, and an ordinary merchantman about 90 to 100. Overland legs use roads, rivers and caravans. All figures are one way.

Why the Congress was set for October 1921

A far school needed the letter to arrive, time to choose and outfit a delegate, and the delegate’s voyage back. For Quito that is six months out, a month or two to decide, and up to six months home. With the monsoon windows on top, the full cycle runs to about a year and a half.

So the letters left in two waves. Westbound letters to India, Arabia, Africa and Europe sailed in December 1919 on the northeast monsoon. Letters north to China and Japan sailed in May and June 1920 on the southwest monsoon. Pacific letters went with whichever wave caught the right ship. The Congress was set at the monsoon turn of October 1921, when delegates who had come on either wind could all be in the same room.

The invitation calendarTwenty-four months to the Congress

Months −24 to −15The letters travel

Long-range copies leave first, in duplicate on different ships. Near ports are written to later, so that attendance is not decided by distance.

Months −15 to −6The schools prepare

Recipients choose their delegates, find the funds, and wait for a sailing. Some acceptances arrive after the delegate who sent them.

Months −6 to 0The harbour receives them

Early arrivals spend the waiting months in ordinary scholarly exchange. The demonstration is fixed to one stated date.

Some letters moved faster than any ship around the Cape. Al-Merikani’s correspondents could run a letter up the Red Sea and overland to the Mediterranean, and a courier could cross an isthmus on foot between two oceans. Outward and homeward passages rarely took the same time.

The Congress 会

October 1921, in the hall of the Monsoon Convocation, three years after the Kindling in the same room.

IV
Two acts and a silence

The demonstration

Without burning anything, there was light. The smart people understood.

Nothing was brought into the hall. There was no apparatus on the tables, no instrument in any caster’s hand, no assistant behind a screen. The lamps were carried out, and the hall went dark.

Act oneThe gentle wonders

Toa KengLight

The dark hall filled with light from nowhere, steady and without heat or smoke.

SundWarmth, then cold

A bowl of water steamed, then frosted over, with no fire and no ice anywhere near it.

Sri TemenggongSeeds grew

Rice seed in a tray of earth sprouted, leafed and headed in the time it takes to drink a cup of tea.

Nine RainWater turned aside

A stream poured from a jar and bent away from a line drawn on the floor, then went back to the jar.

Sané-KoraSound silenced

A drummer played at full strength in the middle of the hall and no one heard a beat.

Lan FangA wound closed

A volunteer’s cut hand was knitted shut in front of the physicians among the delegates.

Al-MerikaniTwo ledgers

A sum written in a ledger at one end of the hall appeared in a sealed ledger at the other.

Some delegates looked for wires, mirrors and hidden pipes, and found none. Each effect stood without a cause anyone could point to. Others went very quiet. Those were the ones the houses were watching.

The demonstration showed what the houses could do. It explained nothing of how. A delegate could grasp its importance completely and still have no idea of the mechanism.

Act twoThe great working, its burden, and the World’s resistance

Then the houses worked together at a scale the delegates could not mistake. The great doors were opened onto the harbour, and an ensemble from all seven houses held the incoming tide back from the quay for the length of one song. Every delegate could watch the sea standing where it should not stand.

The delegates also saw what it took. The air in the hall grew heavy and the windows hummed. The casters shook, sweated, and grew grey in the face, and two of them had to be carried out when it ended. The Straits let the visitors see that magic answers to the World pushing back, that it draws on the body like hard labour, and that a working at this scale is felt far beyond the people casting it.

The silent night

No conversation was permitted afterwards. Each delegate was walked to their room alone. In each room waited a meal from the seven kitchens, a sealed bid paper, and a vial of the morning’s rain. They had one night and one day to write.

The silence held inside each delegation too: colleagues who had sailed together were lodged apart for the night. The houses watched how it was kept. Who sent servants to the harbour, who tried to reach the casters, who wrote all night, and who slept.

A scene · the walk backSomeone who crossed an ocean expecting a rare object walks back through a corridor lit by the very thing they have just seen. At the door of their room they stop. The practical question arrives before any theory: what could this do in the place I come from?
V
Sealed bids

The auction, and how the Straits chose

Bid at your peril. The Straits promises to teach, not that you will learn.

Each school bid for access to the exact magic it had seen and nothing more. A school that wanted the light could not bid for the tide.

The conditions came first and were not part of the bidding. A school accepted them whole, or declined. It could ask what a condition meant; it could not amend one, and no offer, however large, bought an exemption. Accepting the conditions made a school eligible. It did not oblige the Straits to choose it.

The bid paper
  1. Which working do you ask to learn? Name it as you saw it.
  2. Whom will you send to learn it, and who will they answer to at home?
  3. What will you use it for, in the first year and in the tenth?
  4. Who in your land will decide how it is used, and who has the authority to accept these conditions on your school’s behalf?
  5. What will you return to the Straits of what you discover?
  6. What do you offer?

The terms were printed at the foot of the paper, in every language of the delegates: the Straits will teach, and gives no promise that the students will be able to learn or reproduce what they are taught. Bid at your peril.

The weighingWhat the houses read in a bid

The use

Answers about wells, harvests, healing and light weighed heavily. Answers about advantage weighed against.

The learners

Teachers and scholars over soldiers and courtiers, and learners who answered to a school more than a throne.

The governance

Whether anyone at home could say no to a ruler about how magic was used.

The return

What a school promised to bring back of its own findings counted for more than the price it offered.

The night

How the delegate kept the silence.

The price

Counted last. The highest bids came from those who wanted magic most for themselves.

Two bidsHow the weighing worked

A hospital asks for cold

It names the cooling it saw, for its fever wards and its stores of medicine. It names three physicians to learn it, offers lodging and teaching rooms to visiting teachers, and promises a yearly report of what it finds. Its offer is modest. It is accepted.

A rich patron offers ten times as much

Its school accepts every condition. Its answers show the working would be kept in the patron’s own houses and taught to his household. Nothing in the bid breaks a rule. The Straits chooses another school and declines the money.

The first circle was chosen from schools already on good terms with the Straits: old trading partners, sister schools of the same lineage, houses whose scholars had studied on the Straits. Several of the largest offers in the hall were refused outright. Their delegates learned it by letter, months after sailing home.

The race and the Covenant 盟

A carefully engineered race against time, and the compact that came out of it.

VI
First-mover advantage

An engineered race

Grow the trusted network faster than the world finds magic alone.

With no telegraph, the news of the Congress travelled as unevenly as the letters had: a returning delegate, a letter and a harbour rumour reached different ports in different orders. The veil had been pierced for everyone. Sooner or later others would learn magic on their own, by accident, by genius or by force. The Straits had a head start and knew magic’s destructive powers better than anyone. If its trusted network grew fast enough, the network could set the rules before anyone else could set them by war.

The first circle 首环

Schools chosen at the Congress lay down their jars in late 1921 and receive their first teachers.

The second wave 后浪

News of the Congress spreads quickly. Schools and powers that were not invited, or ignored the letter, sail to the Straits to bid. Each faces the same bid paper and the same weighing.

The Pairing 伴行

Teachers sent abroad must be able to defend themselves to a set standard. Every non-combat specialist, a healer, a grower or a water-reader, travels with a combat mage. Teachers go. Students stay home.

The invasion pushed back 拒敌

A foreign power decides to take magic rather than bid for it, and sails on the Straits. In four years the houses have built enough magical strength and enough trained people to turn it back.

The second Congress 再会

A later Congress is called to sell magical artefacts. Buying power ready-made is quicker than learning it, and the sale carries the seeds of an arms race.

The Empty Desks 空桌

Veyr, the toll port at the Narrows of the Sound, seizes Straits teachers on exchange and tortures them for destructive magic. None will give it, and they die there. Every chair they left stays empty for a full academic year.

The Veyr Retaliation 雷霆

Prepared at the Senyap Works under one doctrine, unmake, do not kill, and fought as the Battle of the Sound. One night, a sheer show of force, and a new magical world order.

Students go abroad 游学

Only after the Veyr Retaliation are Straits students sent to partner schools, the two-way exchange the Covenant had always promised.

VII
Argued for twenty-five years, sealed in 1946

What the Covenant governs

Magic is wonderful, and it comes with responsibilities.

The principles were fixed at the Congress, and every school entered under them from the first day. The details were worked out over the next twenty-five years from practice, school by school and case by case, and sealed when the circle came back in 1946. A school’s discoveries could change what was known about magic. They could not reopen the conditions. Its matters:

Responsibility责

Who answers for a working and its burden, and for harm done with magic taught under the Covenant.

The sharing of knowledge传

What may be taught, to whom, in what order, and what may be passed on by those who learned it.

Use and governance治

How each member land governs magic at home, and what the circle may ask of it.

The return of findings还

What every member brings back of its own discoveries, and when. Letters carry them between gatherings; the Register keeps them; the Covenant tastes them.

Consent允

Who must agree before magic touches a person, a people, a river or a forest.

Applications用

Which uses are open to all, which need the circle’s leave, and which belong to the great ensemble works.

Authority权

Acceptance must come from someone who can answer for it. A delegate without that authority carries the offer home, unchanged.

Protection of teachers护

How host schools keep visiting teachers safe, and when the Straits ends instruction after a breach.

Contribution益

Members bring methods and insights the Straits lacks, question its theories, test its workings and improve them.

VIII
Year 0 and Year 25

From the Congress to the Covenant

The Straits seals everything that matters in a jar, and opens it twenty-five years later.

Historians call the meeting of 1921 the Congress and the return of 1946 the Covenant. In 1921 the schools convened. In 1946, having kept faith for twenty-five years, they bound themselves. The rite they used began small, before magic, and is told in volume 116.

At the Congress each school of the first circle left with two things: a piece of the Covenant’s dish, carried home to age in its own land, and the magic it had bid for, carried home by its paired teachers. In 1946 the circle came back with both. The jars were unsealed and cooked together, and every school laid its findings on the table, showing what its people, soil and traditions had made of the magic. The return of shared findings, one of the Covenant’s own articles, happens at a feast.

1946The order of the Covenant

Recognition

Who has returned? Original delegates stand beside their successors, their former pupils, and the people who tended the jars.

Returned knowledge

What have we learned? Each school lays out its findings: discoveries, changed practices, and the questions its own land raised.

The opening

What has time made? The sealed jars are unsealed, one shore at a time, beside the living ingredients tended elsewhere.

The shared table

What can we make together? The pieces become one meal, cooked to the Song.

Renewal

What do we undertake next? The schools seal the Covenant: consent, the permitted uses, the return of findings, the protection of teachers.

Further entrusting

What leaves this table? New seeds, new starters, new questions and new invitations to teach, carried home for the next twenty-five years.

Twenty-five years after the Straits gave the world magic, the world came back to the table to show what it had made of it.
